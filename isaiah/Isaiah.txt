Isaiah 26:8

  Psalms 134:13-14 — name, remembrance and judgment parallel: Isaiah joins the Lord's way of judgment with hope in His name and remembrance. Isaiah 26:9
which our soul desires. From night my spirit rises early toward Thee, God, because Thine ordinances are light upon the earth. Learn righteousness, you dwelling upon the earth!

Isaiah 26:10

Isaiah 26:11

Isaiah 26:12

  Numbers 6:26 — prayer for the Lord's peace: Isaiah addresses the Lord directly with the plea to give His people peace. The correspondence rests on the requested divine gift, not on replacing Isaiah's statement about recompense with wording about God doing all human works.

Isaiah 26:13

  Hosea 13:4 — exclusive acknowledgment of God: Isaiah confesses knowing no other besides the Lord and naming His name. Hosea likewise declares that Israel is to know no god except the Lord and that no other saves. The confession answers the same exclusive allegiance that Hosea states as God's claim upon His people.

Isaiah 26:14

  Isaiah 26:19 — same-chapter contrast in resurrection power: Isaiah first says that physicians cannot raise the dead. Isaiah 26:15

Isaiah 26:16

Both connect distress with renewed remembrance of God; the psalm's following warning about insincere hearts is not suppressed or applied automatically to Isaiah's speaker.

Isaiah 26:17

Isaiah 26:18

Isaiah 26:19

Isaiah 26:21

  Job 16:18 — uncovered blood parallel: Isaiah promises that the earth will uncover blood and cease concealing the slain. Job appeals directly to the earth not to cover his flesh's blood.   Job 26:13 — divine killing of the dragon: Isaiah's sword judgment targets the fleeing and crooked serpent described as a dragon.
