Joshua 1:1

  Deuteronomy 34:5-9 — Moses' death followed by Joshua's succession: Joshua's opening after Moses' death follows the conclusion recording that death and Joshua's reception of understanding and Israel's obedience; the two records retain their different introductory details.
  Exodus 33:11 — Joshua son of Nun as Moses' attendant: Both explicitly identify Joshua son of Nun as Moses' attendant; Exodus describes his remaining at the tent, while Joshua opens with the Lord addressing him after Moses' death.

  Deuteronomy 17:18-19 — Written law kept continually by a leader: Joshua must keep the law in his mouth and meditate continually; the royal instruction likewise requires retaining and reading a law scroll, preserving the distinct offices and descriptions.

They buried him by the borders of his lot at Thamnathasachara in the mountain of Ephraim, north of Mount Gaash.
  Judges 2:9 — Burial accounts sharing Ephraim and north-of-Gaash details: Both place Joshua's burial in his inheritance in Ephraim's mountain north of Gaash; Thamnathasachara and Timnath-heres remain in their supplied forms without independent name identification.

Joshua 24:31a
There they placed with him in the tomb in which they buried him the stone knives with which he circumcised the sons of Israel at Gilgal, when he brought them out of Egypt, as the Lord commanded them. They are there to this very day.
  Joshua 5:2-3 — Buried circumcision knives and their earlier commanded making: The continuation places the stone circumcision knives in Joshua's tomb; the earlier narrative records the command to make sharp stone knives and Joshua making and using them.

Joshua 24:32
The sons of Israel brought Joseph’s bones up from Egypt and buried them at Shechem in the portion of the field that Jacob acquired from the Amorites living at Shechem for one hundred ewe lambs; he gave it to Joseph as a portion.
  Exodus 13:19 — Joseph's bones carried from Egypt and finally buried: Joshua records the bones brought up from Egypt and buried at Shechem; Exodus expressly describes Moses taking those bones in fulfillment of Joseph's oath instruction.
  Genesis 33:19 — Shechem field purchase with preserved seller and price wording: Both describe Jacob acquiring a Shechem field for a hundred lambs; Joshua says ewe lambs and Amorite inhabitants, while Genesis names Hamor father of Shechem, without harmonizing those descriptions.

Joshua 24:33
After these things Eleazar son of Aaron, the high priest, died and was buried at Gabaath of Phinehas his son, which he had given him in the mountain of Ephraim.
  Joshua 24:33a — Same-chapter Eleazar's death and Phinehas' subsequent priestly service: Eleazar dies and is buried at his son's Gabaath; the continuation records Phinehas succeeding him and eventually being buried at his own Gabaath, preserving both notices.

Joshua 24:33a
On that day the sons of Israel took the ark of God and carried it about among themselves. Phinehas served as priest in place of Eleazar his father until he died and was buried at his own Gabaath.
  Numbers 25:11-13 — Phinehas' priestly line and covenant before his succession: The continuation names Phinehas succeeding Eleazar; Numbers identifies him as Eleazar's son in Aaron's line and grants him and his seed an enduring priesthood covenant.

Joshua 24:33b
The sons of Israel went away, each to his own place and his own city. The sons of Israel revered Astarte and Ashtaroth and the gods of the nations around them. The Lord handed them into the hands of Eglom king of Moab, and he ruled over them for eighteen years.
  Judges 2:13-14 — Other-god service followed by delivery to enemies: Both connect serving other gods with the Lord handing Israel to enemies; Joshua names Astarte, Ashtaroth and Eglom, while Judges names Baal and Astartes with a broader enemy description.
  Judges 3:12-14 — Moabite ruler's eighteen-year dominion in distinct narrative placements: Both record eighteen years under Moab's ruler after Israel's evil; the names Eglom and Eglon and the different placement of the notices are preserved without chronology harmonization.
