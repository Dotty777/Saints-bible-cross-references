Judges 1:1
After Joshua’s death, the sons of Israel inquired of the Lord, saying, “Who shall go up for us against the Canaanites, to lead the battle against them?”
  Judges 20:18 — Repeated inquiry about the leading tribe: Both accounts have Israel inquire who should lead a battle, with Judah named in the response; the earlier opponents are Canaanites and the later opponents Benjamin, so the campaigns are kept distinct.

Judges 1:2
The Lord said, “Judah shall go up. Behold, I have given the land into his hand.”
  Judges 20:18 — Judah chosen to lead in distinct campaigns: The Lord appoints Judah to go up in each account; Judges1 additionally promises the land into Judah's hand, while Judges20 concerns the different conflict against Benjamin.

Judges 1:3
And Judah said to Simeon his brother, “Go up with me into my allotted territory, and let us fight against the Canaanites; and I also will go with you into your allotted territory.” And Simeon went with him.
  Joshua 19:1-9 — Judah and Simeon acting within related allotments: Judah invites Simeon into reciprocal fighting in their allotted territories; Joshua explicitly places Simeon's inheritance amid Judah's lot and explains its derivation from Judah's larger portion.

Judges 1:4
Judah went up, and the Lord gave the Canaanites and the Perizzites into his hand. He struck them at Bezek: ten thousand men.
  Judges 1:2 — Same-chapter promised delivery and the Bezek victory: The Lord's promise to give the land into Judah's hand is followed by the Lord giving the Canaanites and Perizzites into that hand and Judah's stated defeat of ten thousand at Bezek.

Judges 1:5
They found Adoni-bezek at Bezek, fought against him, and struck the Canaanites and the Perizzites.
  Judges 1:6-7 — Same-chapter Adoni-bezek encounter and its outcome: The encounter with Adoni-bezek at Bezek leads to his flight, capture, mutilation and own explanation of repayment; the later units identify what became of this particular defeated ruler.

Judges 1:6
Adoni-bezek fled, but they pursued him, seized him, and cut off the extremities of his hands and his feet.
  Judges 1:7 — Same-chapter injury and Adoni-bezek's repayment confession: The cutting of Adoni-bezek's hand and foot extremities is the precise treatment he says he formerly inflicted on seventy kings; his own speech interprets his corresponding treatment as God's repayment.

Judges 1:7
Adoni-bezek said, “Seventy kings, with the extremities of their hands and feet cut off, used to gather what was beneath my table. As I have done, so God has repaid me.” They brought him to Jerusalem, and he died there.
  Judges 1:6 — Same-chapter repayment speech explains the reported injury: Adoni-bezek's confession about mutilating kings and being repaid directly explains the preceding report that his own hand and foot extremities were cut; no legal endorsement of either action is inferred.

Judges 1:8
The sons of Judah fought against Jerusalem and captured it. They struck it with the edge of the sword and burned the city with fire.
  Joshua 15:63 — Jerusalem's capture distinguished from Jebusite removal: Judges reports Judah capturing and burning Jerusalem; Joshua reports Judah's inability to destroy its Jebusites and their continued residence, retaining the different actions without supplying an unstated chronology.

Judges 1:9
After these things the sons of Judah went down to fight against the Canaanites dwelling in the hill country, the south, and the plain.
  Judges 1:19 — Same-chapter hill-country campaign and later possession report: Judah's campaign includes the hill country, south and plain; the later notice specifically reports mountain inheritance and failure in the valley, preserving its unusual Rechab explanation without importing iron chariots.

Judges 1:10
Judah went against the Canaanites dwelling in Hebron, and Hebron came out against him. Hebron’s former name was Kariatharboksepher. He struck Sheshai, Ahiman, and Talmai, descendants of Anak.
  Judges 1:20 — Same-chapter Hebron conflict and Caleb's Anakite removal: The Hebron account names three Anak descendants struck; the later Caleb grant again describes removing three sons of Anak, while retaining its additional three-cities wording and not resolving outside name variants.

Judges 1:11
From there they went against the inhabitants of Debir. Debir’s former name was City of Letters.
  Joshua 15:15 — Debir's explicit former-name notice in the parallel campaign: Both campaign accounts turn against Debir's inhabitants and explicitly call its former name City of Letters; Joshua names Caleb as the advancing actor, while Judges continues its preceding narrative.

Judges 1:12
And Caleb said, “Whoever strikes the City of Letters and takes possession of it, I will give him Achsah my daughter as wife.”
  Joshua 15:16 — Caleb's Achsah marriage offer for taking City of Letters: Both report Caleb promising his daughter Achsah as wife to the person taking City of Letters, with each passage's particular capture and mastery wording preserved.

Judges 1:13
Othniel son of Kenaz, Caleb’s younger brother, took possession of it, and Caleb gave him Achsah his daughter as wife.
  Joshua 15:17 — Othniel's capture and Achsah marriage: Both identify Othniel son of Kenaz as Caleb's younger brother, report his taking the city and record Caleb giving him Achsah as wife.

Judges 1:14
And when she entered, he urged her to ask her father for the field. She murmured on the beast of burden and cried out from it, “You have given me away into a southern land.” And Caleb said to her, “What do you desire?”
  Joshua 15:18 — Achsah's field request scene with differing initiative wording: Both describe her arrival, a proposed request to her father for a field, her cry from the riding animal and Caleb's question; Judges says he urged her, while Joshua reports her consulting him and speaking.

Judges 1:15
And Achsah said to him, “Give me a blessing; for you have given me away into a southern land. Give me a redemption of water.” And Caleb gave her, according to her heart, the redemption of the heights and the redemption of the low places.
  Joshua 15:19 — Achsah's requested blessing and upper-lower grant: Both describe Achsah seeking a blessing after being given into a southern land and Caleb granting an upper-lower provision; Judges says redemption of water and heights/low places, while Joshua preserves Golathmaim names.

Judges 1:16

Judges 1:17

Judges 1:18

Judges 1:19
The Lord was with Judah, and he inherited the mountain, because he could not inherit the inhabitants of the valley, because Rechab had separated it.
  Judges 1:9 — Same-chapter mountain inheritance within the earlier campaign frame: The later report of Judah inheriting the mountain develops the earlier hill-country campaign notice and contrasts failure in the valley; its Rechab clause is retained without replacing it with another military explanation.

Judges 1:20
He gave Hebron to Caleb, as Moses had said, and Caleb inherited the three cities from there and removed from there the three sons of Anak.
  Joshua 14:13-14 — Caleb's Hebron grant in the allotment account: Both expressly give Hebron to Caleb; Joshua names Joshua's blessing and Caleb's enduring lot for following the Lord, while Judges cites Moses and adds the three cities and three Anak sons.

Judges 1:21
The sons of Benjamin did not remove the Jebusites dwelling in Jerusalem. The Jebusites dwelt with the sons of Benjamin to this day.
  Joshua 15:63 — Jerusalem's continuing Jebusites under different tribal statements: Both describe Jebusites remaining in Jerusalem to the narrated day; Judges attributes non-removal to Benjamin, while Joshua names Judah's inability to destroy them, without merging the tribes or their actions.

Judges 1:22
The sons of Joseph also went up to Bethel, and Judah was with them.

Judges 1:23

Judges 1:24

Judges 1:25

  Judges 1:24 — Same-chapter release completes the promised entrance bargain: The man's provision of the route and his household's release fulfill the watchers' stated exchange of entrance information for mercy, distinguishing his release from the city's destruction.

Judges 1:26
The man went to the land of the Hittites, built a city there, and called its name Luz. That is its name to this day.
  Judges 1:23 — Same-chapter old Luz designation and a newly built Luz: The rescued man names his new Hittite-country city Luz after the preceding notice that Bethel was formerly called Luz; the narrative keeps the former city and newly built city distinct.

Judges 1:27
Manasseh did not possess Beth-shan, which is the City of the Scythians, or its dependent towns or surrounding lands; nor Ekthanaad and its dependent towns, the inhabitants of Dor and its dependent towns, the inhabitants of Balaam and its dependent towns, the inhabitants of Magedon and its dependent towns, or the inhabitants of Jeblaam and its dependent towns. Judges 1:28
When Israel became strong, it subjected the Canaanites to tribute, but did not completely remove them.
  Joshua 17:13 — Israel's growing strength and subjection without complete removal: Both state that Israel grew strong and subjected the Canaanites while not completely removing or destroying them; Judges specifies tribute and Joshua retains its more general subjection wording.

Judges 1:29
Ephraim did not remove the Canaanites dwelling in Gezer. The Canaanites dwelt among them in Gezer and became subject to tribute.
  Joshua 16:10 — Ephraim's unremoved Canaanites at Gezer: Both explicitly say Ephraim did not remove or destroy Gezer's Canaanites, who remained among them; Judges adds tribute, while Joshua supplies its separate later Pharaoh expansion.

Judges 1:30
Zebulun did not remove the inhabitants of Kedron or the inhabitants of Enaala. The Canaanites dwelt among them and became subject to tribute.
  Judges 1:28 — Same-chapter Zebulun's particular case of tribute without removal: The general Israel-wide notice of Canaanite tribute without complete removal has a concrete tribal example in Zebulun's Kedron and Enaala report; no alternate identities for those towns are asserted.

Judges 1:31
Asher did not remove the inhabitants of Acco, and they became subject to his tribute; nor the inhabitants of Dor, the inhabitants of Sidon, the inhabitants of Aalaph, Achazib, Helba, Aphek, or Roob.
  Judges 1:32 — Same-chapter Asher's named failures and their stated consequence: The list of places Asher did not remove is followed by Asher dwelling among the Canaanites because it could not remove them; the latter gives the explicit reason for this particular territorial outcome.

Judges 1:32
Asher dwelt among the Canaanites inhabiting the land, because he was unable to remove them.
  Judges 1:31 — Same-chapter Asher's coexistence explained by its named failures: Asher's residence among unremoved Canaanites follows its specific list from Acco through Roob; the source itself gives inability to remove them as the reason rather than an outside geographic explanation.

Judges 1:33
Naphtali did not remove the inhabitants of Beth-shemesh or the inhabitants of Beth-anath. Israel dwelt among the Canaanites inhabiting the land, but the inhabitants of Beth-shemesh and Beth-anath became subject to their tribute.
  Judges 1:28 — Same-chapter Naphtali's tribute within the non-removal pattern: Naphtali's Beth-shemesh and Beth-anath inhabitants become tributary despite non-removal, matching the earlier Israel-wide tribute-without-complete-removal notice while retaining the specific tribal and city statements.

Judges 1:34
The Amorites pressed the sons of Dan into the mountain, for they did not allow them to come down into the valley.
  Joshua 19:47a — Dan confined to the hill country by Amorites: The actual Joshua continuation and Judges both state that Amorites pressed Dan in the hill country and did not allow descent into the valley; Joshua additionally describes pressure on the allotted boundary.

Judges 1:35
The Amorites began to dwell on the Mountain of the Myrtle Grove, where the bears and the foxes are. The hand of the house of Joseph became heavy upon the Amorites, and they became subject to tribute.
  Judges 1:34 — Same-chapter Amorite pressure and later tributary subjection: The Amorites who constrain Dan are then described in their supplied mountain setting and subjected to tribute under Joseph's heavy hand; Dan's constraint and Joseph's action remain distinct tribal statements.

Judges 1:36
The Amorite boundary—the Idumean—was above Akrabin, at the Rock and above.
  Judges 1:34-35 — Same-chapter Amorite boundary closes the pressure-and-tribute account: The explicit Amorite/Idumean boundary notice closes the adjacent account of Amorite pressure on Dan and their subjection under Joseph; Akrabin and the Rock remain unlocated supplied boundary names.

Judges 2:1

He said to them, “The Lord, the Lord brought you up from Egypt and brought you into the land that He swore to your fathers to give you. He said to you, ‘I will never scatter My covenant with you.
  Exodus 6:6-8 — Egyptian deliverance and the sworn ancestral land: The messenger recalls the Lord bringing Israel out of Egypt into the fathers' sworn land; Exodus promises that same sequence of rescue and the land pledged to Abraham, Isaac and Jacob, preserving the distinct covenant formulations.

Judges 2:2
You shall make no covenant with those dwelling in this land, nor shall you bow down to their gods; but you shall shatter their graven images and pull down their altars.’ Yet you did not obey My voice when you did these things.
  Exodus 23:32-33 — Prohibited covenants and service of inhabitants' gods: Judges recalls the prohibition of covenants with the land's inhabitants and worship of their gods; Exodus explicitly forbids those covenants and warns that serving their gods becomes a stumbling block.
  Deuteronomy 7:2-5 — No covenant and destruction of cult objects commanded: Both forbid a covenant with the land's peoples and command destruction of their altars and images; Deuteronomy additionally addresses intermarriage, pillars and groves rather than being treated as an identical quotation.

Judges 2:3

  Joshua 23:12-13 — Retained nations and their gods as a threatened snare: Both warn that the Lord will cease removing remaining nations; Joshua calls those nations traps and stumbling blocks, while Judges calls the peoples constraints and their gods a stumbling block, preserving the distinct subjects and images.

Judges 2:4
When the messenger of the Lord spoke these words to all Israel, the people raised their voice and wept.

Judges 2:5

Judges 2:7
The people served the Lord all the days of Joshua and all the days of the elders who outlived Joshua, who knew all the great work the Lord had done for Israel.

Judges 2:8
Joshua son of Nun, the servant of the Lord, died at the age of one hundred ten years.

Judges 2:15

Judges 2:16

  Judges 2:18 — Same-chapter raised judges and their saving role elaborated: The announcement that the Lord raised judges to save Israel from plunderers is expanded by His presence with each judge, rescue through the judge's lifetime and compassion for the afflicted people's groaning.

Judges 2:17

I too will no longer remove before them anyone from the nations that Joshua left and let remain.”
