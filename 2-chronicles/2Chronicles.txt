2 Chronicles 1:1
Solomon son of David grew strong over his kingdom. The Lord his God was with him and made him exceedingly great.
  1 Chronicles 29:23-25 — Narrative continuation: The preceding account places Solomon on David's throne, describes Israel submitting to him, and says the Lord made him exceedingly great. This opening resumes that established kingship and divine exaltation.

2 Chronicles 1:2
Solomon spoke to all Israel: the commanders of thousands and hundreds, the judges, and all the rulers before Israel, the ancestral rulers.
  1 Chronicles 28:1 — Royal assembly parallel: The source lists Solomon's commanders of thousands and hundreds among Israel's leaders. David's earlier assembly includes commanders bearing those same numerical ranks and rulers over Israel; the different kings, occasions, and descriptions of other offices remain distinct.

2 Chronicles 1:3
Solomon and the whole assembly with him went to the high place in Gabaon, where God’s Tent of Testimony was, which Moses, the Lord’s servant, had made in the wilderness.
  1 Chronicles 21:29 — Historical sanctuary setting: Both identify the Lord's tent made by Moses in the wilderness at Gabaon. The earlier location statement explains the sanctuary to which Solomon and the assembly now go.

2 Chronicles 1:4
But David had brought God’s ark up from the city of Kariathiarim, because he had prepared a tent for it in Jerusalem.
  1 Chronicles 16:1 — Narrative antecedent: David's prepared tent for the ark is recalled here. The earlier account narrates bringing the ark into the tent David pitched, supplying the antecedent for its separate location from the wilderness tent.

2 Chronicles 1:5
The bronze altar that Beseleel son of Ouriou son of Or had made was there before the Lord’s tent, and Solomon and the assembly sought it out.
  1 Chronicles 21:29 — Historical altar setting: The bronze altar stands before the Lord's tent in Solomon's Gabaon visit. The earlier account explicitly places both the wilderness tent and the whole-burnt-offering altar at Gabaon; this connection does not resolve the builder's differently written names elsewhere.

2 Chronicles 1:6
Solomon offered there upon the bronze altar before the Lord, the one in the tent; he offered a thousandfold whole burnt offering upon it.
  1 Kings 3:4 — Narrative parallel: Both describe Solomon offering at the high-place altar with a stated thousand: Chronicles calls it a thousandfold whole burnt offering and Kings a thousand whole burnt offerings. The differing formulations are retained.

2 Chronicles 1:7
That night God appeared to Solomon and said to him, “Ask what I should give you.”
  1 Kings 3:5 — Narrative parallel: Both place God's appearance to Solomon at night and present the invitation to ask for a gift. Kings explicitly calls its appearance sleep; Chronicles' wording is preserved without adding that detail.

2 Chronicles 1:8
Solomon said to God, “Thou didst show great mercy to David my father, and Thou didst make me king in his place.
  1 Kings 3:6-7 — Narrative prayer parallel: Solomon acknowledges God's mercy to David and his own appointment as king in David's place in both prayers. Kings expands the account of David's conduct and the son's lack of experience.

2 Chronicles 1:9
Now, Lord God, let Thy name be confirmed upon David my father, for Thou hast made me king over a people as numerous as the dust of the earth.
  1 Kings 3:7-8 — Narrative prayer parallel: Both prayers join Solomon's appointment in his father's place to the immense size of God's people. Chronicles uses the dust comparison, while Kings says the people cannot be numbered.

2 Chronicles 1:10
Now give me wisdom and understanding, and I shall go out before this people and come in; for who can judge this great people of Thine?”
  1 Kings 3:9 — Narrative prayer parallel: Solomon asks for the capacity to judge God's great people in both accounts. Wisdom and understanding here correspond to the target's requested hearing heart and discernment for just judgment.

2 Chronicles 1:11
God said to Solomon, “Because this came into your heart, and you did not ask for wealth of possessions, glory, or the life of opponents, and did not ask for many days, but did ask for wisdom and understanding to judge My people over whom I made you king,
  1 Kings 3:11 — Narrative response parallel: Both divine replies contrast Solomon's request for judicial understanding with the wealth, long life, and opponents' lives he did not request. The stated reason for granting the request is shared.

2 Chronicles 1:12
I give you wisdom and understanding. I will also give you wealth, possessions, and glory, so that none like you has existed among the kings before you, nor will it be so after you.”
  1 Kings 3:12-13 — Narrative promise parallel: Both replies grant unusual wisdom and also unrequested wealth and glory. Kings states the wisdom and royal-wealth promises in separate sentences; Chronicles combines these gifts in its response.

2 Chronicles 1:13
Solomon came from the bama in Gabaon to Jerusalem, from before the Tent of Testimony, and he reigned over Israel.
  1 Kings 3:15 — Narrative return parallel: After the divine encounter Solomon comes to Jerusalem in both accounts. Kings continues with offerings and a feast, while Chronicles closes by stating that he reigned over Israel.

2 Chronicles 1:14
Solomon gathered chariots and horsemen. He had a thousand four hundred chariots and twelve thousand horsemen. He left them in the chariot cities, and the people were with the king in Jerusalem.
  1 Kings 10:26 — Royal military comparison: Both records give twelve thousand horsemen and place the royal force in chariot cities and Jerusalem. Chronicles separately counts fourteen hundred chariots; Kings counts four thousand mares, and those different categories and figures are not harmonized.

2 Chronicles 1:15
The king made gold and silver in Jerusalem like stones, and cedars in Judea as abundant as the sycamores in the plain.
  1 Kings 10:27 — Verbal royal-abundance parallel: Both compare Jerusalem's gold and silver to stones and its plentiful cedars to the sycamores of the plain. The paired material comparisons describe Solomon's abundance in the same terms.

2 Chronicles 1:16
Solomon’s horses came out of Egypt, and there was the price of the king’s merchants; they bought them to trade,
  1 Kings 10:28 — Royal trade parallel: Both describe Solomon's horses coming from Egypt through royal merchants. Kings additionally names Thekoue; the comparison does not turn Chronicles' price wording into that place-name.

2 Chronicles 1:17
and they went up and exported from Egypt one chariot for six hundred in silver and a horse for a hundred fifty. Likewise they brought them through their hands for all the kings of the Chettites and the kings of Syria.
  1 Kings 10:29 — Trade parallel with differing prices: Both describe Egyptian chariot and horse exports and onward trade for foreign kings, including Syria. Chronicles gives six hundred for a chariot and one hundred fifty for a horse; Kings gives one hundred for a chariot and fifty for a horse. The differing prices remain unreconciled.

2 Chronicles 1:18
Solomon resolved to build a house for the Lord’s name and a house for his kingdom.
  1 Kings 5:19 — Temple-building intention: Both state Solomon's intention to build a house for the Lord's name. Kings connects that intention to the word given to David; Chronicles also mentions the house for Solomon's kingdom.

2 Chronicles 2:1
Solomon gathered seventy thousand burden-bearing men and eighty thousand quarrymen in the mountain, with three thousand six hundred overseers over them.
  1 Kings 5:29-30 — Labor-force parallel: Both accounts number seventy thousand burden bearers, eighty thousand mountain stoneworkers, and three thousand six hundred overseers for Solomon's building work. Each stated group and count has an explicit counterpart.

2 Chronicles 2:2
Solomon sent to Chiram king of Tyre, saying, “As you dealt with my father David and sent him cedars to build himself a house to live in,
  1 Chronicles 14:1 — Explicit historical recollection: Solomon recalls Chiram king of Tyre supplying David with cedar for a residence. The target narrates that earlier supply and the builders sent to build David's house.

2 Chronicles 2:3
behold, I his son am building a house for the name of the Lord my God, to consecrate it to Him, to burn incense before Him, with continual presentation and the continual offering of whole burnt offerings morning and evening, on sabbaths, new moons, and the feasts of the Lord our God. This is an everlasting obligation upon Israel.
  Numbers 28:3-11 — Prescribed worship and temple service: Solomon names continual morning and evening burnt offerings, sabbaths, and new moons among the temple's duties. Numbers prescribes those daily, sabbath, and new-moon offerings; this pair does not claim that the target also states every other rite listed here.

2 Chronicles 2:4
The house I am building is great, for our God is greater than all the gods.
  1 Chronicles 16:25-26 — Explicit divine-greatness theme: Solomon grounds the great house in God's greatness above all gods. The earlier praise likewise declares the Lord great and above the gods, then distinguishes Him as maker of heaven from the nations' idols.

2 Chronicles 2:5
Who will be able to build Him a house, since heaven and the heaven of heaven cannot bear His glory? Who am I to build Him a house, except to burn incense before Him?
  1 Kings 8:27 — Explicit sanctuary limitation: Both Solomon passages say heaven and the heaven of heaven cannot contain or suffice for God, so a constructed house cannot do so. The earlier request limits the house's purpose to worship rather than containing God's glory.

2 Chronicles 2:6
Now send me a wise and knowledgeable man to work in gold, silver, bronze, and iron, in purple, scarlet, and hyacinth blue, skilled at engraving, with the skilled men with me in Judah and Jerusalem whom David my father prepared.
  1 Chronicles 22:15-16 — Prepared workforce antecedent: Solomon requests an additional skilled worker to join the craftsmen David prepared. David's earlier address identifies the abundant stoneworkers, carpenters, and artisans available to Solomon and names gold, silver, bronze, and iron work.

2 Chronicles 2:7
Send me cedar, juniper, and pine timbers from Lebanon, for I know your slaves know how to cut timber from Lebanon. Behold, your servants will be with my servants
  1 Kings 5:20 — Timber-request parallel: Both requests seek Lebanon timber, acknowledge the other workforce's timber-cutting skill, and arrange cooperation between the two sets of servants. The distinctive working arrangement connects the passages.

2 Chronicles 2:8
and will go to prepare abundant timber for me, for the house I am building is great and glorious.
  1 Kings 5:22-24 — Request and timber provision: Solomon's request continues with abundant timber for the great house. The target gives the answering promise of cedar and pine timber and records its supply, connecting the requested building material with the response.

2 Chronicles 2:9
Behold, I give the workers cutting timber grain for food, as gifts to your servants: twenty thousand kors, and twenty thousand kors of barley, twenty thousand measures of wine, and twenty thousand measures of oil.”
  1 Kings 5:25 — Building-supply exchange comparison: Both records join the timber arrangement to Solomon's provision of twenty thousand kors of grain and an oil supply. Chronicles lists food for workers and adds barley and wine; Kings describes Hiram's household and different oil terminology, so the allocations are not treated as identical.

2 Chronicles 2:10
Chiram king of Tyre replied in writing and sent it to Solomon: “Because the Lord loves His people, He has appointed you king over them.”
  1 Kings 10:9 — Royal appointment and divine love: Chiram's letter says the Lord appointed Solomon because He loves His people. The queen of Sheba likewise attributes Solomon's appointment over Israel to the Lord's love; the speakers and occasions remain distinct.

2 Chronicles 2:11
Chiram said, “Blessed is the Lord, the God of Israel, who made heaven and earth, who gave King David a wise son, skilled in understanding and knowledge, who will build a house for the Lord and a house for his kingdom.
  1 Kings 5:21 — Royal correspondence parallel: Both replies bless God for giving David a wise or discerning son. Chronicles expands the blessing with creation language and Solomon's building purpose, while Kings reports Hiram's rejoicing at the message.

2 Chronicles 2:12
Now I have sent you a wise man, possessing understanding: Chiram, my father,
  2 Chronicles 2:6 — Same-chapter request and response: Solomon requested a wise and knowledgeable craftsman, and the answering letter now says a wise man with understanding has been sent. This connection follows the explicit request and reply without redefining the phrase my father.

2 Chronicles 2:13
his mother is from Dan’s daughters, and his father is a Tyrian man. He knows how to work in gold, silver, bronze, iron, stone, and timber; to weave in purple, hyacinth blue, fine linen, and scarlet; to engrave engravings and devise every design, whatever you give him, with your skilled men and the skilled men of David my lord, your father.
  2 Chronicles 2:6 — Same-chapter craftsmanship specifications: The supplied craftsman's skills include the gold, silver, bronze, iron, colored fabrics, and engraving named in Solomon's request. The reply answers those particular specifications; it does not resolve his maternal identity against another account.

2 Chronicles 2:14
Now let my lord send his servants the grain, barley, oil, and wine of which he spoke.
  2 Chronicles 2:9 — Same-chapter offer and acceptance: The reply asks for the grain, barley, oil, and wine Solomon had offered to the timber workers. The four named provisions identify the earlier offer being accepted.

2 Chronicles 2:15
We will cut timber from Lebanon according to all your need and bring it in rafts to Joppa’s sea. You shall bring it to Jerusalem.”
  1 Kings 5:23 — Timber-transport parallel: Both replies arrange to bring Lebanon timber to the sea as rafts for Solomon to receive. Chronicles specifically names Joppa and the onward journey to Jerusalem; Kings leaves the delivery place to Solomon's instructions.

2 Chronicles 2:16
Solomon gathered all the resident foreign men in Israel’s land after the numbering by which David his father had numbered them. They were found to be a hundred fifty-three thousand six hundred.
  1 Chronicles 22:2 — Resident-foreigner labor antecedent: The census here explicitly follows David's numbering of resident foreigners. The target records David gathering Israel's resident foreigners and appointing quarrymen for God's house; it supplies the earlier workforce setting without claiming to state the later total.

2 Chronicles 2:17
He assigned seventy thousand of them as burden bearers, eighty thousand as quarrymen, and three thousand six hundred as taskmasters over the people.
  2 Chronicles 2:16 — Same-chapter assignment and census: The assigned burden bearers, quarrymen, and taskmasters are the resident foreigners counted immediately before. Their three stated totals sum to the preceding hundred fifty-three thousand six hundred, clarifying the assignment's scope.

2 Chronicles 3:1
Solomon began building the Lord’s house in Jerusalem on Mount Amoria, where the Lord appeared to David his father, at the place David had prepared, at the threshing floor of Orna the Jebusite.
  1 Chronicles 21:28 — Temple-site antecedent: The source locates the building at Orna the Jebusite's threshing floor, where the Lord appeared to David. The target names that same floor as the place where the Lord answered David and David sacrificed; the shared site connects the accounts while their different verbs remain intact.

2 Chronicles 3:2
He began building in the second month, in the fourth year of his reign.
  1 Kings 6:1c — Building-date parallel: Both place the beginning or foundation of the Lord's house in Solomon's fourth year and the second month. Kings adds the month's name; the shared year and month are explicit.

2 Chronicles 3:3
These were the measurements when Solomon began building God’s house: the length in cubits, by the first measurement, was sixty cubits, and the width twenty cubits.
  1 Kings 6:2 — Temple-dimension comparison: Both give the Lord's house a width of twenty cubits. Chronicles gives a length of sixty by the first measurement, while Kings gives forty and adds a height; the figures are compared without supplying an unverified reconciliation.

2 Chronicles 3:4
The ailam before the house extended twenty cubits across the house’s width, and its height was a hundred twenty cubits. He gilded it inside with pure gold.
  1 Kings 6:3 — Temple-frontage comparison: The structure before the house spans twenty cubits across the house's width in both accounts. Chronicles additionally gives the ailam's height and gilding, while Kings gives the porch's depth; those different dimensions are not equated.

2 Chronicles 3:5
He paneled the great house with cedar wood, gilded it with pure gold, and carved palm trees and hanging ornaments upon it.
  1 Kings 6:29 — Temple-decoration parallel: Both include palm trees in the house's decoration. Chronicles expressly describes carved palms on the paneled and gilded great house, while Kings lists palms in the inner and outer rooms after describing engraved cherubim.

2 Chronicles 3:6
He adorned the house with precious stones for glory, and with gold from Pharouaim,
  1 Chronicles 29:2 — Temple-material preparation and use: The temple is adorned here with precious stones and gold. David's earlier preparation for God's house explicitly includes gold and every precious stone; the comparison does not identify the source of Pharouaim gold with an unnamed location in the target.

2 Chronicles 3:7
and he gilded the house, the walls, gateways, ceilings, and doorways with gold, and carved cherubim upon the walls.
  1 Kings 6:29 — Temple-wall carving parallel: Both explicitly place carved cherubim on the house's walls. Chronicles also enumerates surfaces gilded with gold, while Kings includes palm-tree decoration in its wall description.

2 Chronicles 3:8
He made the House of the Holy of Holies. Its length across the width was twenty cubits, and its width twenty cubits. He gilded it with pure gold for cherubim, amounting to six hundred talents.
  1 Kings 6:20 — Inner-sanctuary dimension parallel: Both describe the inner sanctuary as twenty cubits in length and width and covered with refined or pure gold. Chronicles additionally specifies six hundred talents, which the target does not quantify.

2 Chronicles 3:9
As for the weight of the nails, the weight of one was fifty shekels of gold. He gilded the upper room with gold.
  1 Kings 7:36 — Temple gold-fittings parallel: Chronicles specifies gold nails and their weight; the temple utensil list in Kings explicitly includes nails of refined gold. The target supplies a particular material-and-object parallel without confirming Chronicles' weight or upper-room detail.

2 Chronicles 3:10
He made two cherubim in the House of the Holy of Holies, work made from wood, and he gilded them with gold.
  1 Kings 6:23-28 — Inner-sanctuary cherubim parallel: Both accounts place two cherubim inside the inner sanctuary and cover them with gold. Kings gives their measurements and positioning; Chronicles here specifies wooden workmanship.

2 Chronicles 3:11
The cherubim’s wings extended twenty cubits in length. One wing, five cubits, touched the house’s wall, and the other wing, five cubits, touched the other cherub’s wing.
  1 Kings 6:24-27 — Cherub-wing arrangement parallel: Chronicles describes five-cubit wings extending between the wall and the other cherub. Kings gives five cubits for each wing and the same wall-to-wing contact arrangement within the house.

2 Chronicles 3:12
One wing of the other cherub, five cubits, touched the house’s wall, and the other wing, five cubits, touched the wing of the other cherub.
  1 Kings 6:24-27 — Cherub-wing arrangement parallel: The second cherub's five-cubit wings touch the wall and the other cherub's wing here. Kings states that the second cherub has the same measure and describes the wings touching walls and one another.

2 Chronicles 3:13
The cherubim’s wings were spread twenty cubits. They stood upon their feet, with their faces toward the house.
  1 Kings 6:27 — Spread-wing arrangement parallel: The wings are spread across twenty cubits here. Kings describes spread wings reaching the two walls and meeting in the middle, the arrangement also detailed in Chronicles 3:11-12; only Chronicles here adds the standing posture and direction of the figures.

2 Chronicles 3:14
He made the curtain of hyacinth blue, purple, scarlet, and fine linen, and wove cherubim into it.
  Exodus 26:31-33 — Sanctuary-curtain pattern: Both curtains combine blue, purple, scarlet, fine linen, and woven cherubim at the inner sanctuary. Exodus prescribes the veil dividing the Holy Place from the Holy of Holies; Chronicles describes the temple curtain's corresponding materials and figures.

2 Chronicles 3:15
He made two pillars before the house, thirty-five cubits in height, and their capitals five cubits.
  1 Kings 7:3-4 — Pillar and capital comparison: Both describe two pillars before the house and capitals five cubits high. Chronicles states thirty-five cubits for the pillars, while Kings states eighteen for a pillar; the different height wording is preserved without reconciliation.

2 Chronicles 3:16
He made serseroth in the dabir and placed them upon the pillars’ capitals. He made a hundred pomegranates and placed them upon the hanging ornaments.
  1 Kings 7:5-6 — Pillar-capital ornament parallel: Both place hanging ornament and pomegranates at the pillars' capitals. The comparison does not define the untranslated serseroth or supply a matching total for Chronicles' hundred pomegranates from the target's two rows.

2 Chronicles 3:17
He erected the pillars before the temple, one on the right and one on the left. He named the one on the right Establishment and the one on the left Strength.
  2 Chronicles 3:15 — Same-chapter construction and installation: The two pillars made before the house are now erected before the temple, one on each side, and named. The earlier construction notice supplies the explicit two-pillar antecedent without requiring an etymological equation with names elsewhere.
