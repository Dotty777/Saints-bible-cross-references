2 Chronicles 1:1
Solomon son of David grew strong over his kingdom. The Lord his God was with him and made him exceedingly great.
  1 Chronicles 29:23-25 — Narrative continuation: The preceding account places Solomon on David's throne, describes Israel submitting to him, and says the Lord made him exceedingly great. This opening resumes that established kingship and divine exaltation.

2 Chronicles 1:2
Solomon spoke to all Israel: the commanders of thousands and hundreds, the judges, and all the rulers before Israel, the ancestral rulers.
  1 Chronicles 28:1 — Royal assembly parallel: The source lists Solomon's commanders of thousands and hundreds among Israel's leaders. David's earlier assembly includes commanders bearing those same numerical ranks and rulers over Israel; the different kings, occasions, and descriptions of other offices remain distinct.

2 Chronicles 1:3
Solomon and the whole assembly with him went to the high place in Gabaon, where God’s Tent of Testimony was, which Moses, the Lord’s servant, had made in the wilderness.
  1 Chronicles 21:29 — Historical sanctuary setting: Both identify the Lord's tent made by Moses in the wilderness at Gabaon. The earlier location statement explains the sanctuary to which Solomon and the assembly now go.

2 Chronicles 1:4
But David had brought God’s ark up from the city of Kariathiarim, because he had prepared a tent for it in Jerusalem.
  1 Chronicles 16:1 — Narrative antecedent: David's prepared tent for the ark is recalled here. The earlier account narrates bringing the ark into the tent David pitched, supplying the antecedent for its separate location from the wilderness tent.

2 Chronicles 1:5
The bronze altar that Beseleel son of Ouriou son of Or had made was there before the Lord’s tent, and Solomon and the assembly sought it out.
  1 Chronicles 21:29 — Historical altar setting: The bronze altar stands before the Lord's tent in Solomon's Gabaon visit. The earlier account explicitly places both the wilderness tent and the whole-burnt-offering altar at Gabaon; this connection does not resolve the builder's differently written names elsewhere.

2 Chronicles 1:6

  1 Kings 3:4 — Narrative parallel: Both describe Solomon offering at the high-place altar with a stated thousand: Chronicles calls it a thousandfold whole burnt offering and Kings a thousand whole burnt offerings. The differing formulations are retained.

2 Chronicles 1:7

  1 Kings 3:5 — Narrative parallel: Both place God's appearance to Solomon at night and present the invitation to ask for a gift. Kings explicitly calls its appearance sleep; Chronicles' wording is preserved without adding that detail.

2 Chronicles 1:8

  1 Kings 3:6-7 — Narrative prayer parallel: Solomon acknowledges God's mercy to David and his own appointment as king in David's place in both prayers. Kings expands the account of David's conduct and the son's lack of experience.

2 Chronicles 1:9

  1 Kings 3:7-8 — Narrative prayer parallel: Both prayers join Solomon's appointment in his father's place to the immense size of God's people. Chronicles uses the dust comparison, while Kings says the people cannot be numbered.

2 Chronicles 1:10
