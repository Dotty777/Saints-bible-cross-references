Malachi 1:1
A pronouncement of the word of the Lord concerning Israel through His messenger. Take it to your hearts!
  Haggai 1:12-13 — prophetic-messenger-parallel: Malachi introduces the Lord's word delivered through His messenger; Haggai is explicitly called the Lord's messenger and speaks the Lord's assurance to the people. Both passages identify the messenger's speech as commissioned divine communication.

Malachi 1:2
“I have loved you,” says the Lord. But you said, “How hast Thou loved us?” “Was not Esau Jacob’s brother?” says the Lord. “Yet I loved Jacob,
  Romans 9:13 — explicit-quotation: Romans quotes the declaration that Jacob was loved and Esau hated. Malachi 1:2 supplies the first half of that contrast, which Romans places within its discussion of God's purpose concerning Isaac's children.
  Genesis 25:24-26 — genealogical-background: Malachi asks whether Esau was Jacob's brother. Genesis narrates their birth as twins and names Esau first and Jacob his brother, supplying the family relationship presupposed by the question.

Malachi 1:3
but Esau I hated. I appointed his territories for desolation and his inheritance as gifts of a wilderness.”
  Romans 9:13 — explicit-quotation: Romans repeats the words about Esau being hated, completing the Jacob-Esau contrast begun in Malachi 1:2. The quotation concerns that declaration; it does not repeat Malachi's further description of Esau's desolated territories.
  Isaiah 34:9-13 — prophetic-judgment-parallel: Malachi describes Esau's territories and inheritance as desolate wilderness. Isaiah's oracle, identified as judgment on Idumea in 34:5-6, likewise portrays deserted land and ruined fortifications occupied by wilderness creatures.

Malachi 1:4
Because Idumea will say, “It has been overthrown; let us return and rebuild the deserted places,” thus says the Lord Almighty: “They will build, but I will overthrow. They will be called ‘Territories of Lawlessness’ and ‘A People Against Whom the Lord Has Drawn Up His Forces Forever.’”
  Obadiah 1:8-10 — prophetic-judgment-parallel: Malachi says Idumea's rebuilding will be overthrown and describes enduring divine opposition. Obadiah announces the removal of understanding and warriors from Idumea and Mount Esau, ending with removal forever for violence toward Jacob.

Malachi 1:5
Your eyes will see, and you yourselves will say, “The Lord has been magnified beyond Israel’s territories!”
  Psalms 46:8-10 — divine-kingship-parallel: Malachi anticipates recognition that the Lord is magnified beyond Israel's territories. The psalm declares God King of all the earth, reigning over nations whose rulers gather with Abraham's God, expressing the same reach beyond Israel.

Malachi 1:6
“A son honors his father, and a slave his master. If I am a father, where is My honor? If I am a master, where is the fear of Me?” says the Lord Almighty. You priests who belittle My name! But you said, “How have we belittled Thy name?”
  Isaiah 1:2-3 — filial-indictment-parallel: Malachi's divine Father and Master asks for honor and fear from priests who belittle His name. Isaiah similarly indicts children who reject the God who raised them and contrasts Israel's failure with animals recognizing their owner and lord.
  Exodus 20:12 — ethical-analogy: Malachi begins his accusation with the expectation that a son honors his father. Exodus explicitly commands honor for father and mother; Malachi uses that familiar filial duty as the premise for asking why God receives no honor as Father.

Malachi 1:7
“By bringing polluted loaves to My altar.” But you said, “How have we polluted them?” “By your saying, ‘The Lord’s table is despised, and the foods placed upon it are despised.’”
  Leviticus 22:2-3 — priestly-law-background: Malachi accuses priests of bringing polluted food and despising the Lord's table. Leviticus instructs Aaron's sons to guard consecrated things and not profane God's name, excluding an unclean priest who approaches them.

Malachi 1:8
“For if you bring a blind animal for sacrifice, is that not evil? If you bring one that is lame or sick, is that not evil? Bring it, then, to your ruler! Will he accept it? Will he show you favor?” says the Lord Almighty.
  Deuteronomy 15:21 — sacrificial-law-background: Malachi condemns presenting blind or lame animals as sacrifices. Deuteronomy explicitly forbids sacrificing an animal with lameness, blindness or another grievous blemish, providing the rule violated by these offerings.

Malachi 1:9
“And now appease the face of your God and plead with Him. These things have happened in your hands. Will I show favor to any of you?” says the Lord Almighty.
  Isaiah 1:15-16 — rejected-supplication-parallel: Malachi calls the offerers to plead with God while questioning whether their deeds will receive favor. Isaiah says multiplied supplication will not be heard from bloodstained hands and calls for cleansing and removal of evil deeds.

Malachi 1:10
“For even among you doors will be shut, and you will not kindle My altar for nothing. I have no desire for you,” says the Lord Almighty, “and I will not accept a sacrifice from your hands.”
  Amos 5:21-23 — rejected-worship-parallel: Malachi declares that God has no desire for the offerers and will not accept their sacrifice. Amos likewise rejects festivals, burnt offerings and sacrifices and refuses to hear their music, showing divine rejection of outward worship.

Malachi 1:11
“For from the sun’s rising to its setting, My name has been glorified among the nations, and in every place incense is brought to My name, together with a pure sacrifice, because My name is great among the nations,” says the Lord Almighty.
  Psalms 112:3-4 — verbal-thematic-parallel: Malachi locates the glorifying of God's name from sunrise to sunset and among nations. The psalm uses the same span from the sun's risings to settings to praise the Lord's name and declares Him high above all nations.
  Isaiah 56:6-7 — worship-among-nations-parallel: Malachi speaks of a pure sacrifice and the greatness of God's name among nations. Isaiah describes foreigners loving the Lord's name and bringing acceptable sacrifices to His altar in a house of prayer for all nations.

Malachi 1:12
“But you profane it by your saying, ‘The Lord’s table is polluted, and the foods placed upon it are despised.’
  Malachi 1:7 — same-chapter-literary-parallel: Both verses repeat dismissive speech about the Lord's table and food: the table is called despised in 1:7 and polluted in 1:12, while the food is despised in both. The repeated complaint connects 1:12's profaning charge with the earlier polluted offerings.

Malachi 1:13
“And you said, ‘These things are from hardship,’ and I blew them away,” says the Lord Almighty. “You were bringing things seized by force, the lame and the afflicted. If you bring the sacrifice, shall I accept these things from your hands?” says the Lord Almighty.
  Sirach 34:18-20 — sacrificial-ethics-parallel: Malachi includes goods seized by force among the unacceptable offerings. Sirach calls sacrifice from unjust gain a blemished offering and condemns sacrificing the property of the poor, explaining why wrongful acquisition cannot become an acceptable gift.

Malachi 1:14
“Cursed is one who was able, and had a male in his flock and a vow concerning it, yet sacrifices a damaged animal to the Lord. For I am a great King,” says the Lord Almighty, “and My name is manifest among the nations.”
  Leviticus 22:18-21 — vow-offering-law-background: Malachi curses the person who has a male pledged by a vow but sacrifices a damaged animal. Leviticus requires acceptable males without blemish for pledged offerings and insists that a sacrifice fulfilling a vow be unblemished.

Malachi 2:1
“And now this command is for you, priests.
  Malachi 1:6 — same-book-addressee-link: The command in 2:1 is expressly directed to priests. The earlier accusation in 1:6 names the priests who belittle God's name, identifying the group whose conduct the following command addresses.

Malachi 2:2
If you do not listen and do not take it into your hearts to give glory to My name,” says the Lord Almighty, “I will send out the curse upon you, and I will curse your blessing, and I will curse it. I will scatter your blessing, and it will not be among you, because you do not take it into your hearts.
  Deuteronomy 28:15-20 — covenant-sanction-parallel: Malachi threatens a curse upon the priests and their blessing if they refuse to listen and honor God's name. Deuteronomy likewise makes failure to listen and obey the trigger for curses that reach the people's stores, produce and work.

Malachi 2:3
Look, I am setting apart the shoulder for you, and I will scatter the stomach on your faces, the stomach of your festivals, and I will take you to the same place.
  Deuteronomy 18:3 — priestly-portion-reversal: Malachi's threat names the shoulder and the stomach of the festivals, with the stomach scattered on the priests' faces. Deuteronomy assigns the foreleg and stomach among the priests' sacrificial portions; familiar priestly portions become instruments of humiliation in Malachi.

Malachi 2:4
You will recognize that I have sent out this command to you, so that My covenant might be with the Levites,” says the Lord Almighty.
  Deuteronomy 33:8-10 — levitical-covenant-background: Malachi says the command serves God's covenant with the Levites. Moses' blessing of Levi praises keeping God's oracles and covenant and assigns declaration of the law and service at the altar, setting out that covenantal responsibility.

Malachi 2:5
“My covenant with him was of life and peace, and I gave it to him in fear, to fear Me and to shrink back before My name.
  Numbers 25:12-13 — priestly-covenant-parallel: Malachi describes the priestly covenant as life and peace, received in reverence. Numbers explicitly grants Phinehas a covenant of peace and an enduring priesthood for his zeal, a particular priestly covenant corresponding to Malachi's description.

Malachi 2:6
A law of truth was in his mouth, and injustice was not found on his lips. Directing his way in peace, he walked with Me and turned many back from injustice.
  Deuteronomy 33:10 — levitical-instruction-parallel: Malachi's faithful priest carries true law in his mouth and turns people from injustice. Deuteronomy assigns Levi the task of declaring God's ordinances and law to Jacob and Israel, the teaching responsibility faithfully exercised in Malachi.

Malachi 2:7
For a priest’s lips will guard knowledge, and people will seek law from his mouth, because he is the messenger of the Lord Almighty.
  Leviticus 10:10-11 — priestly-teaching-law: Malachi says a priest's lips guard knowledge and that people seek law from his mouth. Leviticus requires Aaron's priests to distinguish holy from profane and instruct Israel in the statutes delivered through Moses.
  Deuteronomy 17:8-11 — priestly-judgment-law: Malachi describes people seeking law from a priest. Deuteronomy directs difficult legal questions to the Levitical priests and requires obedience to the judgment and law they declare, giving a concrete setting for that consultation.

Malachi 2:8
But you have turned aside from the way and weakened many through the law; you have corrupted the covenant of Levi,” says the Lord Almighty.
  Hosea 4:6-9 — corrupt-priesthood-parallel: Malachi charges priests with abandoning the way, weakening others through law and corrupting Levi's covenant. Hosea condemns rejection of knowledge and forgetting God's law, linking priestly failure with the people's sins and shared punishment.

Malachi 2:9
“And I have made you despised and neglected among all the nations, because you did not guard My ways but showed partiality in the law.”
  Deuteronomy 16:19 — impartial-judgment-law: Malachi names partiality in the law as a reason for the priests' humiliation. Deuteronomy forbids perverting judgment or showing partiality, supplying a direct legal standard for the conduct Malachi condemns.

Malachi 2:10
Did not one God create you? Is there not one Father of you all? Why have you each abandoned your brother, so as to profane the covenant of your fathers?
  Deuteronomy 32:6 — divine-father-creator-parallel: Malachi appeals to one Creator and Father as the reason not to abandon a brother and profane the covenant. Deuteronomy likewise challenges an unfaithful people by naming the Lord as the Father who acquired, made and created them.
  Ephesians 4:6 — shared-divine-fatherhood: Malachi's appeal to one God and one Father grounds responsibility toward a brother. Ephesians similarly names one God and Father of all within its account of the community's unity.

Malachi 2:11
Judah has been abandoned, and an abomination has occurred in Israel and Jerusalem, because Judah profaned the Lord’s holy things, in which he delighted, and devoted himself to foreign gods.
  Deuteronomy 7:4-6 — foreign-worship-law-background: Malachi charges Judah with profaning the Lord's holy things and devoting himself to foreign gods. Deuteronomy warns against turning away to other gods and contrasts their worship with Israel's identity as God's holy, chosen people.

Malachi 2:12
The Lord will destroy the person who does these things, until he is also humbled away from Jacob’s dwellings and from those who bring a sacrifice to the Lord Almighty.
  Leviticus 20:3 — exclusion-for-profanation-parallel: Malachi threatens removal from Jacob's dwellings after the profanation described in 2:11. Leviticus also announces destruction from among the people for defiling God's holy things; its particular offense of giving offspring to a ruler is distinct from Malachi's wording.

Malachi 2:13
And you were doing these things that I hated: you covered the Lord’s altar with tears, with weeping and groaning from hardships. Is it still fitting to look toward a sacrifice or accept it from your hands?
  Isaiah 1:15-16 — worship-and-conduct-parallel: Malachi questions accepting sacrifices despite tears and groaning, with abandonment of a spouse identified in the next verse. Isaiah likewise refuses multiplied prayers from people whose hands carry wrongdoing and requires their conduct to change.

Malachi 2:14
And you said, “For what reason?” Because the Lord bore witness between you and the wife of your youth, whom you abandoned, though she is your partner and the wife of your covenant.
  Genesis 2:23-24 — marital-bond-background: Malachi calls the abandoned wife a partner and wife of covenant, with God as witness. Genesis describes the man cleaving to his wife and the two becoming one flesh, grounding the enduring marital bond that abandonment violates.

Malachi 2:15
And no other did it, and there was a remnant of his spirit. And you said, “What else does God seek but offspring?” So guard your spirit, and do not abandon the wife of your youth.
  1 Corinthians 7:10-11 — marital-faithfulness-parallel: The closing command of Malachi 2:15 is to guard one's spirit and not abandon the wife of youth. Paul similarly instructs a husband not to dismiss his wife and a wife not to separate, with reconciliation if separation occurs.

Malachi 2:16
But if, after hating her, you send her away, says the Lord, the God of Israel, impiety will cover your thoughts, says the Lord Almighty. So guard your spirit, and you must not abandon.
  Matthew 19:8-9 — divorce-and-wrongdoing-parallel: Malachi links sending a hated wife away with impiety and commands against abandonment. Jesus connects divorce with hardness of heart and warns that divorce followed by remarriage, apart from the stated exception, commits adultery.

Malachi 2:17
You who provoke God with your words! And you said, “How have we provoked Him?” By your saying, “Everyone who does evil is good before the Lord, and He has delighted in them,” and, “Where is the God of righteousness?”
  Isaiah 5:20-23 — moral-reversal-indictment: Malachi condemns saying that evildoers are good before God and questioning His righteousness. Isaiah pronounces woe on calling evil good and on justifying the ungodly while denying the righteous their right, indicting the same reversal of moral judgment.

Malachi 3:1
“Look, I am sending out My messenger, and he will look to a way before My face. Suddenly the Lord whom you seek will come to His own sanctuary, and the messenger of the covenant whom you desire. Look, he is coming,” says the Lord Almighty.
  Isaiah 40:3-5 — preparing-the-divine-way: Malachi announces a messenger attending to a way before the Lord's face. Isaiah calls for preparing the Lord's way before His glory and salvation are revealed, supplying a closely related image of preparation for divine arrival.
  Matthew 11:9-10 — explicit-quotation-and-identification: Jesus identifies John as the prophet about whom the sending of the messenger before the face and preparation of the way was written. The quoted messenger announcement corresponds to Malachi's opening promise, with the quotation's pronouns preserved in Matthew.
  Mark 1:2-4 — composite-citation: Mark places the messenger-before-the-face announcement beside Isaiah's wilderness voice and then introduces John baptizing. The first announcement corresponds to Malachi's messenger promise, within a combined citation headed by Isaiah in Mark's actual text.
  Luke 7:26-27 — explicit-quotation-and-identification: Luke records Jesus identifying John by the written promise of a messenger sent before the face to prepare the way. This repeats Malachi's messenger theme while preserving Luke's wording about preparing the way ahead of the addressed figure.

Malachi 3:2
And who will endure the day of his entrance? Or who will stand at his appearance? For he enters like a smelting fire and like launderers’ herb.
  Nahum 1:6 — divine-appearance-question-parallel: Malachi asks who can endure the coming figure's entrance or stand at his appearance. Nahum similarly asks who can stand before the Lord's anger, with melting and crushing imagery emphasizing the overwhelming encounter.
  Isaiah 1:25 — purifying-fire-parallel: Malachi compares the entrant to a smelting fire. Isaiah describes God's hand bringing a people through burning to purity and removing lawlessness, a corresponding image of divine purification rather than mere outward observance.

Malachi 3:3
He will sit, smelting and purifying as with silver and gold. He will cleanse the sons of Levi and melt them like gold and silver, and they will be those who bring a sacrifice to the Lord in righteousness.
  Zechariah 13:9 — metal-refining-parallel: Malachi pictures the sons of Levi cleansed like silver and gold so they can offer rightly. Zechariah likewise describes a remnant refined as silver and tested as gold, followed by restored recognition between God and His people.
  Jeremiah 6:29-30 — refining-outcome-contrast: Malachi's refiner succeeds in cleansing the priestly sons for righteous sacrifice. Jeremiah describes a silversmith working in vain because wickedness remains, leading to rejected silver; the shared refining image has contrasting outcomes.

Malachi 3:4
And the sacrifice of Judah and Jerusalem will please the Lord, as in the days of old and as in the former years.
  Psalms 50:20-21 — restored-acceptable-sacrifice: Malachi promises that Judah and Jerusalem's sacrifice will again please the Lord. The psalm asks for Zion's good and Jerusalem's rebuilding, then anticipates God's delight in a sacrifice of righteousness and offerings on His altar.

Malachi 3:5
“I will draw near to you in judgment, and I will be a swift witness against sorceresses, adulteresses and those who swear falsely by My name, against those who withhold a hired worker’s wages, those who oppress a widow, those who strike orphans with their fists, those who turn aside the judgment of a sojourner, and those who do not fear Me,” says the Lord Almighty.
  Leviticus 19:12-13 — ethical-law-background: Malachi's judgment list includes false swearing by God's name and withholding workers' wages. These adjacent Levitical commands prohibit both unjust swearing by God's name and retaining a hired worker's wages overnight.
  Deuteronomy 24:14-17 — protection-of-vulnerable-people: Malachi names withheld wages and wrongs against widows, orphans and sojourners. Deuteronomy requires prompt wages for the needy and prohibits perverting judgment for the sojourner, orphan or widow, matching these particular accusations.
  James 5:4 — judgment-for-withheld-wages: Malachi promises swift divine testimony against those withholding a hired worker's pay. James says withheld harvest wages cry out and that the harvesters' cries reach the Lord Sabaoth, likewise placing wage injustice before divine judgment.
  Exodus 22:20-23 — divine-defense-of-the-vulnerable: Malachi threatens judgment for harming widows, orphans and sojourners. Exodus prohibits afflicting these groups and says God hears their cries and responds in wrath, supplying the protective principle behind the threatened judgment.

Malachi 3:6
“For I am the Lord your God, and I have not changed. But you, sons of Jacob, do not refrain
  Psalms 101:27-28 — divine-constancy-parallel: Malachi's opening declaration says the Lord has not changed. The psalm contrasts changing, perishing creation with God who remains the same and whose years do not fail, expressing the same divine constancy.
  James 1:17 — divine-constancy-parallel: Malachi declares that the Lord has not changed. James says the Father of lights has no variation or shadow from turning, applying divine constancy to the source of good gifts.

Malachi 3:7
from the injustices of your fathers. You have turned aside from My ordinances and have not guarded them. Return to Me, and I will return to you,” says the Lord Almighty. And you said, “How shall we return?”
  Zechariah 1:3-4 — verbal-call-to-return: Both passages give God's invitation, 'Return to Me, and I will return to you,' in the setting of ancestors who departed from His ways. Zechariah's warning against repeating the fathers' practices matches Malachi's indictment of inherited disobedience.

Malachi 3:8
“Will a human defraud God? Yet you are defrauding Me.” And you will say, “How have we defrauded Thee?” “Because the tithes and the firstfruits are with you.
  Leviticus 27:30-32 — tithe-law-background: Malachi identifies retained tithes and firstfruits as defrauding God. Leviticus declares the land's tithe and the tenth animal holy and belonging to the Lord, explaining why withholding the tithe concerns what is God's.
  Numbers 18:21-24 — levitical-provision-background: Malachi charges the people with withholding dues belonging to God. Numbers assigns Israel's tithes, set apart for the Lord, to the Levites for their service, showing the divinely appointed destination of those dues.

Malachi 3:9
And you keep looking away, yet you are defrauding Me. The nation has been brought to an end.
  Malachi 3:8 — same-chapter-literary-continuation: Verse 3:9 repeats the charge of defrauding God and adds the nation's disastrous condition. Verse 3:8 identifies the disputed practice as withholding tithes and firstfruits, making the accusation's concrete subject explicit.

Malachi 3:10
And you brought all the produce dues into the storehouses, and his plunder will be in his house. Examine, then, by this,” says the Lord Almighty, “whether I will not open heaven’s floodgates for you and pour out My blessing for you until there is enough.
  Nehemiah 13:10-13 — tithe-storehouse-practice: Malachi speaks of produce dues brought into the storehouses. Nehemiah recounts Levites deprived of their portions, his rebuke over abandonment of God's house, and the people's subsequent bringing of grain, wine and oil tithes into stores.
  Deuteronomy 28:12 — heavenly-provision-parallel: Malachi promises opening heaven's floodgates and pouring out blessing. Deuteronomy promises that the Lord opens His heavenly treasure to give seasonal rain and bless the people's work, linking heaven's opening with material provision.

Malachi 3:11
And I will give you instructions for food, and I will certainly not destroy the fruit of your land, and your vine in the field will certainly not grow weak,” says the Lord Almighty.
  Haggai 2:18-19 — renewed-agricultural-blessing: Malachi promises not to destroy the land's fruit or weaken its vine. Haggai turns attention to formerly fruitless vine and trees and announces blessing from that day, presenting a corresponding reversal toward fruitful land.

Malachi 3:12
“All the nations will call you blessed, because you will be a desirable land,” says the Lord Almighty.
  Deuteronomy 28:9-11 — blessing-recognized-by-nations: Malachi says nations will call the people blessed and their land desirable. Deuteronomy describes nations recognizing the Lord's name upon His people alongside multiplied produce and livestock, joining public recognition with blessing in the land.

Malachi 3:13
“You have made your words heavy against Me,” says the Lord. And you said, “How have we spoken against Thee?”
  Malachi 2:17 — same-book-disputation-parallel: Both passages accuse the people of provoking God through speech and record their counterquestion asking how they have done so. The renewed disputation introduces the complaint in 3:14-15, following the earlier challenge to divine righteousness in 2:17.

Malachi 3:14
You said, “The one who serves God is futile. What advantage is there because we guarded His observances and because we walked as supplicants before the Lord Almighty?
  Psalms 72:13-14 — futile-faithfulness-complaint: Malachi reports the claim that serving God and keeping His observances brings no advantage. The psalmist similarly says he made his heart righteous in vain while enduring daily affliction, voicing the same temptation to regard faithfulness as profitless.

Malachi 3:15

  Psalms 72:3-12 — prosperity-of-the-lawless: Malachi reports that those practicing lawlessness are built up and survive opposition to God. The psalmist envies sinners' peace and describes their arrogant speech and prosperity, giving a fuller account of the same troubling observation.

Malachi 3:16
Those who feared the Lord spoke these things, each to his neighbor. The Lord paid attention and listened, and He wrote a book of remembrance before Him for those who feared the Lord and revered His name.
  Daniel 12:1 — divine-book-and-recognition: Malachi says a remembrance book is written before God for those who fear and revere Him. Daniel describes people found inscribed in the book receiving exaltation during affliction; both connect a divine record with favorable recognition, without identifying the books as the same object.
  Exodus 32:32-33 — divine-record-comparison: Malachi's book records those who revere God's name. In Exodus Moses appeals concerning God's written book, and God speaks of blotting out sinners. Both present moral accountability in terms of a book before God, with remembrance and removal as distinct actions.

Malachi 3:17
“They will be Mine,” says the Lord Almighty, “for a day that I am making for an acquisition, and I will choose them as a man chooses his son who serves him.
  Exodus 19:5-6 — covenant-possession-parallel: Malachi says the reverent will belong to God on the day of His acquisition. Exodus promises that those hearing His voice and keeping His covenant will be His treasured people among the nations, expressing the same claim of belonging to God.

Malachi 3:18
And you will return and see the distinction between the righteous and the lawless, and between one who serves God and one who does not serve.
  Matthew 25:31-46 — judgment-distinguishes-service: Malachi promises a visible distinction between righteous and lawless people and between serving God and not serving. Matthew portrays final separation in which service or failure to serve the needy is addressed as service or failure toward the King, ending in different outcomes for the righteous and the others.

Malachi 3:19
For look, the day of the Lord is coming, burning like an oven, and it will burn them. All the foreigners and all who practice lawlessness will be straw, and the coming day will ignite them,” says the Lord Almighty, “and neither root nor branch will remain from them.
  Isaiah 5:24-25 — judgment-by-consuming-fire: Malachi compares the lawless to straw burned on the coming day, leaving neither root nor branch. Isaiah likewise describes a stalk consumed by flame and roots becoming chaff because the people rejected the Lord's law.
  Obadiah 1:18 — fire-and-stubble-judgment: Malachi describes the lawless as straw ignited by the coming day. Obadiah depicts Jacob and Joseph as fire and flame consuming Esau as stubble, using the same image of combustible plant matter for those under judgment.

Malachi 3:20
But for you who fear My name, a sun of righteousness will rise, and healing in its wings; you will go out and leap like little calves released from bonds.
  Luke 1:78-79 — saving-light-imagery: Malachi promises the rising sun of righteousness with healing for those who fear God's name. Zechariah's prophecy in Luke describes a visiting dawn from above that shines on those in darkness and directs them into peace; both portray divine deliverance through rising light.
  Isaiah 60:1-3 — restoration-as-rising-light: Malachi's rising sun brings healing and release for the reverent. Isaiah calls Jerusalem to shine because the Lord's glory has risen upon her despite surrounding darkness, with nations walking by that light. The connection is the restorative light image, not an assertion that every detail is identical.

Malachi 3:21
You will trample the lawless, because they will be ashes beneath your feet on the day that I am making,” says the Lord Almighty.
  Isaiah 26:5-6 — judgment-beneath-the-feet: Malachi promises that the lawless will be ashes under the feet of the addressed people. Isaiah describes God bringing high fortified cities to the ground to be trampled by the feet of the meek and lowly, similarly placing the judged power beneath the feet of those God vindicates.

  Matthew 17:10-13 — elijah-promise-interpreted: Malachi promises Elijah before the Lord's day. Matthew records the expectation that Elijah must come first and Jesus' explanation that Elijah has already come, which the disciples understand as referring to John the Baptist.
  Luke 1:16-17 — elijah-role-applied-to-john: Malachi announces Elijah before the coming divine day. Luke describes John going before the Lord in Elijah's spirit and power to prepare a ready people, applying Elijah's preparatory role while naming John as the child in context.
  Sirach 48:9-10 — elijah-appointed-role: Malachi assigns Elijah a future preparatory mission before the Lord's great day. Sirach's praise of Elijah recalls his fiery departure and his recorded appointment to still anger before wrath and restore Jacob's tribes, a corresponding description of his appointed role.

Malachi 3:23
who will restore a father’s heart toward a son and a person’s heart toward his neighbor, lest I come and strike the land utterly.”
  Luke 1:17 — elijah-heart-restoration-allusion: Malachi says the promised Elijah will restore a father's heart toward a son and a person's heart toward a neighbor. Malachi 3:24

  Deuteronomy 4:9-14 — horeb-law-remembrance: Malachi closes by commanding remembrance of Moses' law, ordinances and judgments given at Horeb for Israel. Deuteronomy warns against forgetting the Horeb encounter and recounts the command to teach its ordinances and judgments to the people and their children.
