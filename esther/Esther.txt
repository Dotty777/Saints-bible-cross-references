Esther 1:1
In the second year of the reign of Artaxerxes the Great, on the first of Nisa, Mordecai son of Jair, son of Shimei, son of Kish, of the tribe of Benjamin, saw a dream.
  Esther 10:3b — Dream recalled before its interpretation: The opening identifies Mordecai as the dreamer; near the end he explicitly recalls the dream concerning these events before interpreting its figures. No external chronology is supplied for the regnal date.

Esther 1:1c
He was among the captives whom Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon had taken captive from Jerusalem with Jeconiah king of Judea.
  Jeremiah 24:1 — Deportation with Jeconiah: Both passages name Nebuchadnezzar and the deportation of Jeconiah from Jerusalem. Jeremiah supplies the historical setting of the captivity described here, without establishing Mordecai's age or resolving the later royal chronology.

Esther 1:1e
Look! Two great dragons came forward, both ready to wrestle, and their cry became loud.
  Esther 10:3d — Dragons explicitly interpreted: Mordecai later identifies the two dragons of his dream as Haman and himself; this is the narrative's own interpretation of the paired figures.

Esther 1:1f
At their cry every nation prepared for war, to wage war against a nation of righteous people.
  Esther 10:3e — Hostile nations explicitly interpreted: The dream's nations preparing war against the righteous nation are later identified as those gathering to destroy the name of the Jews.

Esther 1:1h
The whole righteous nation was troubled, fearing its own calamities. They prepared to perish and cried out to God.
  Esther 10:3f — The endangered crying nation identified: The dream depicts the righteous nation fearing destruction and crying to God; the interpretation identifies the nation as Israel, who cried to God and were saved.

Esther 1:1i
From their cry there came, as though from a small spring, a great river, much water.
  Esther 10:3c — Spring and river explicitly interpreted: The small spring becoming a great river is expressly interpreted as Esther, whom the king married and made queen.

Esther 1:1k

  Esther 8:15-16 — Dream light and the reversal of public standing: The dream joins rising light with the exaltation of the lowly; later Mordecai appears in royal clothing and the Jews have light and joy. This compares the narrative's reversal and light imagery without claiming a literal cosmic event.

Esther 1:1l

  Esther 10:3b-3f — Sought understanding followed by interpretation: Mordecai initially keeps the dream in his heart and seeks its meaning; he later recalls it and explains its river, dragons, nations and saved people.

Esther 1:1n
