Wisdom of Solomon 1:1
Love righteousness, you who judge the earth; think of the Lord in goodness, and seek Him in simplicity of heart.
  Micah 6:8 — parallel union of justice and devotion: Love of righteousness and sincere seeking of the Lord correspond to Micah's requirement to do justice and walk with God.

Wisdom of Solomon 1:2
For He is found by those who do not put Him to the test, and He makes Himself known to those who do not distrust Him.
  Deuteronomy 6:16 — parallel rejection of testing God: Wisdom connects finding God with refusing to test Him; Deuteronomy expressly forbids putting the Lord to the test.

Wisdom of Solomon 1:3
For crooked reasonings separate from God, and power, when put to the test, exposes the foolish.
  Isaiah 59:2 — parallel moral separation from God: Crooked reasoning separates people from God here, while Isaiah describes offenses standing between the people and God.

Wisdom of Solomon 1:4
For wisdom will not enter a soul that devises evil, nor will she dwell in a body indebted to sin.
  Wisdom of Solomon 7:25 — internal parallel of wisdom's purity: Wisdom's refusal to inhabit an evil-devising soul corresponds to the later declaration that nothing defiled enters her pure outflow.

Wisdom of Solomon 1:5
For a holy spirit of discipline will flee deceit and depart from senseless reasonings, and will be put to shame when injustice comes upon it.
  Ephesians 4:30-31 — related wrongdoing against the holy Spirit: The spirit of discipline flees deceit and is put to shame when injustice comes upon it; Ephesians likewise places its warning against grieving the Holy Spirit beside the removal of malice and slander.

Wisdom of Solomon 1:6
For wisdom is a spirit that loves humanity, and she will not acquit a blasphemer of what his lips have said; for God is a witness of his kidneys, a true overseer of his heart, and a hearer of his tongue.
  Jeremiah 17:10 — close heart-and-kidneys examination motif: Both passages portray God examining the heart and kidneys, grounding accountability in knowledge of a person's interior life.

Wisdom of Solomon 1:7
For the Spirit of the Lord has filled the inhabited world, and that which holds all things together has knowledge of utterance.
  Psalms 138:7-10 — parallel universal presence of God's Spirit: The Spirit filling the inhabited world corresponds to the psalm's inability to escape God's Spirit in heaven, Hades or the distant sea.

Wisdom of Solomon 1:8
Therefore no one who speaks unjust things will escape notice, nor will convicting justice pass him by.
  Matthew 12:36-37 — parallel accountability for speech: Unjust speech cannot escape convicting justice; Matthew similarly makes spoken words subject to accounting and condemnation.

Wisdom of Solomon 1:9
For there will be an examination of the ungodly person’s deliberations, and the report of his words will come to the Lord to expose his lawless deeds.
  Psalms 138:2-5 — related divine knowledge of thought and speech: The examination of deliberations and report of words correspond to God's knowledge of the psalmist's reasonings and tongue.

Wisdom of Solomon 1:10
For an ear of jealousy listens to all things, and the murmur of grumblings is not hidden.
  Numbers 14:27-28 — narrative example of heard grumbling: Wisdom says grumbling is not hidden; Numbers explicitly reports the Lord hearing the congregation's grumbling and their words in His ears.

Wisdom of Solomon 1:11
Therefore guard yourselves against useless grumbling, and restrain your tongue from slander; for a secret utterance will not pass away without effect, and a lying mouth destroys a soul.
  Ephesians 4:29-31 — parallel restraint of harmful speech: Restraint of slander and destructive utterance corresponds to the prohibition of rotten speech and the removal of slander.

Wisdom of Solomon 1:12
Do not pursue death through the wandering of your life, nor draw destruction upon yourselves through the works of your hands;
  Ezekiel 18:30-31 — parallel appeal to abandon death-bringing wrongdoing: Wisdom warns against drawing destruction through one's works; Ezekiel urges the people to cast away impieties and asks why they are dying.

Wisdom of Solomon 1:13
For God did not make death, neither does He delight in the destruction of the living.
  Ezekiel 18:32 — close denial of delight in death: God's lack of delight in the destruction of the living corresponds to His declaration that He does not desire the dying person's death.

Wisdom of Solomon 1:14
For He created all things that they might exist, and the created things of the world are life-preserving; there is no poison of destruction in them, nor is Hades’ dominion upon the earth.
  Genesis 1:29-31 — related goodness and sustenance of creation: Wisdom calls created things life-preserving; Genesis describes plants given as food and judges the whole creation very good, without making Wisdom's additional claims about Hades.

Wisdom of Solomon 1:15
For righteousness is immortal.
  Proverbs 12:28 — parallel association of righteousness with life: Wisdom's immortality of righteousness corresponds to life in the ways of righteousness, without treating the proverb as a full account of immortality.

Wisdom of Solomon 1:16
But the ungodly summoned him by their hands and words; considering him a friend, they wasted away, and they made a covenant with him, because they are worthy to belong to his portion.
  Isaiah 28:15 — close covenant-with-death motif: The ungodly make a covenant with death here; Isaiah exposes rulers who claim agreements with death and a covenant with Hades.

Wisdom of Solomon 2:1
For they said among themselves, reasoning wrongly: “Our life is short and sorrowful, and there is no healing when a human being dies, and no one has been known to return from Hades.
  Job 14:1-12 — related mortal lament within different arguments: The ungodly begin with short, sorrowful life and irreversible death; Job's lament describes short-lived humanity and the dead lying asleep until heaven is no longer stitched together. Wisdom subsequently rejects the ungodly conclusion.

Wisdom of Solomon 2:2
For we came into being by chance, and afterward we will be as though we had not existed; for the breath in our nostrils is smoke, and reason is a spark in the movement of our heart;
  Ecclesiastes 12:7 — contrast concerning the human spirit's origin and end: The ungodly attribute existence to chance and reduce breath to smoke; Ecclesiastes instead describes the spirit as given by God and returning to Him.

Wisdom of Solomon 2:3
when it is extinguished, the body will become ashes, and the spirit will disperse like insubstantial air.
  Ecclesiastes 12:7 — contrast concerning the spirit after death: The ungodly predict dispersion into air, whereas Ecclesiastes says the spirit returns to God; the relation is a contrast, not agreement with the quoted speakers.

Wisdom of Solomon 2:4
And our name will be forgotten in time, and no one will remember our works; our life will pass like the traces of a cloud, and will disperse like mist, driven away by the sun’s rays and weighed down by its heat.
  Job 7:9-10 — shared disappearing-cloud and lost-place imagery: The ungodly compare forgotten life to a vanishing cloud; Job likewise compares the dead to a cleared-away cloud whose former place no longer recognizes him.

Wisdom of Solomon 2:5
For our allotted time is the passing of a shadow, and there is no turning back from our end, because it has been sealed, and no one returns.
  Job 14:2-5 — shared shadow and bounded-life imagery: The quoted speakers describe a passing shadow and sealed allotted time; Job similarly depicts a fleeing shadow and a divinely fixed limit to life.

Wisdom of Solomon 2:6
Come, then, and let us enjoy the good things that exist, and eagerly make use of creation as in youth.
  Isaiah 22:13 — parallel condemned response to mortality: The ungodly urge present enjoyment after denying hope beyond death; Isaiah condemns the feast justified by the claim that tomorrow brings death.

Wisdom of Solomon 2:7
Let us fill ourselves with costly wine and perfumes, and let no spring blossom pass us by.
  Isaiah 5:11-12 — parallel indulgence in wine: The ungodly resolve to fill themselves with costly wine; Isaiah condemns prolonged wine drinking that neglects the Lord's works.

Wisdom of Solomon 2:9
Let none of us be without a share in our revelry; let us leave tokens of our enjoyment everywhere, for this is our portion, and this our lot.
  Isaiah 22:13 — parallel revelry claimed under approaching death: The speakers claim revelry as their portion in the argument begun with death; Isaiah's condemned revelers likewise make enjoyment their answer to impending death.

Wisdom of Solomon 2:10
Let us oppress the poor righteous person; let us not spare a widow, nor show regard for an old man’s many years of gray hair.
  Exodus 22:21-24 — direct moral contrast over vulnerable people: The ungodly propose oppressing the poor and sparing no widow; Exodus forbids mistreating widows and pressing poor debtors.

Wisdom of Solomon 2:11
Let our strength be the law of righteousness, for what is weak is shown to be useless.
  Psalms 81:2-4 — contrast between might and righteous judgment: The speakers make strength their law and dismiss weakness; the psalm requires judges to vindicate and rescue the poor and needy instead.

Wisdom of Solomon 2:12
Let us lie in wait for the righteous person, because he is troublesome to us and opposes our works; he reproaches us for sins against law and charges us with sins against our upbringing.
  Psalms 36:12-14 — parallel persecution of the righteous: The ungodly plot against the righteous critic; the psalm similarly describes sinners watching the righteous and drawing weapons against the upright.

Wisdom of Solomon 2:13
He professes to have knowledge of God and calls himself a child of the Lord.
  John 5:17-18 — related claim of divine parentage under hostility: The righteous person calls himself the Lord's child; John records Jesus calling God His Father amid murderous opposition. This is a thematic parallel, not an asserted identification of the speakers.

Wisdom of Solomon 2:14
He has become a reproach to our thoughts; even the sight of him weighs upon us,
  Amos 5:10 — parallel hatred of a moral reprover: The righteous person's presence reproaches the plotters' thoughts; Amos describes hatred of one who reproves at the gates.

Wisdom of Solomon 2:15
because his life is unlike that of others, and his paths are different.
  Psalms 1:1-2 — parallel separation of a righteous way of life: The righteous person's different paths correspond to the blessed person's refusal to walk in ungodly counsel and devotion to the Lord's law.

Wisdom of Solomon 2:16
He has reckoned us counterfeit, and keeps away from our ways as from impurities; he calls the final lot of the righteous blessed, and boasts that God is his father.
  2 Corinthians 6:17-18 — shared separation and divine-fatherhood motifs: Keeping away from impurities and calling God father correspond to the instruction to separate from uncleanness and the promise of God as Father.

Wisdom of Solomon 2:17
Let us see whether his words are true, and test what will happen at his departure.
  Matthew 27:42-43 — parallel hostile test of a righteous person's claims: The plotters propose testing whether his words prove true; the mockers demand rescue as a test of belief in Jesus and His trust in God.

Wisdom of Solomon 2:18
For if the righteous person is a son of God, He will help him and rescue him from the hand of his opponents.
  Matthew 27:43 — close sonship-and-rescue taunt: Both hostile speeches connect claimed divine sonship with the demand that God rescue the persecuted person.

Wisdom of Solomon 2:19
Let us examine him with insult and torture, so that we may know his gentleness and test his endurance of wrong.
  Matthew 27:27-31 — related abusive testing of the persecuted: The proposed examination through insult and torture corresponds to the soldiers' mocking, spitting and striking of Jesus before crucifixion.

Wisdom of Solomon 2:20
Let us condemn him to a shameful death, for, according to his words, he will be visited with care.”
  Matthew 27:39-43 — related shameful execution and expected divine care: The ungodly condemn a person whose claim of divine care they challenge; Matthew places the same rescue challenge around the crucified Jesus.

Wisdom of Solomon 2:21
They reasoned these things and went astray, for their wickedness blinded them;
  Ephesians 4:17-19 — parallel moral blindness of understanding: Wickedness blinds the plotters here; Ephesians links darkened understanding and ignorance to hardness of heart and impure conduct.

Wisdom of Solomon 2:22
they did not know the mysteries of God, nor hope for a reward of holiness, nor recognize a prize for blameless souls.
  Wisdom of Solomon 3:4-9 — internal contrast of denied and promised reward: The ungodly fail to recognize holiness's reward; the following account explicitly promises immortality, benefits and mercy to the faithful.

Wisdom of Solomon 2:23
For God created the human being for incorruption and made him an image of His own eternity;
  Genesis 1:27 — creation-image parallel: Wisdom's claim that humanity is God's image recalls Genesis's creation in God's image; Wisdom adds its explicit interpretation in terms of eternity and incorruption.

Wisdom of Solomon 2:24
but through the devil’s envy death entered the world, and those who belong to his portion experience it.
  Genesis 3:13-19 — related deception-and-death narrative: Wisdom interprets death's entry through the devil's envy; Genesis supplies the serpent's deception and humanity's return to earth, without itself naming envy or the devil.

Wisdom of Solomon 3:1
But the souls of the righteous are in the hand of God, and torment will surely not touch them.
  John 10:28-29 — shared divine-hand security and life: The righteous souls are secure in God's hand; Jesus likewise promises life and declares that no one can snatch His people from His or the Father's hand.

Wisdom of Solomon 3:2
In the eyes of the foolish they seemed to have died, and their departure was reckoned a calamity,
  Isaiah 57:1 — parallel misunderstood death of the righteous: Foolish observers misread the righteous person's departure; Isaiah likewise says no one understands the removal of the righteous.

Wisdom of Solomon 3:3
and their going away from us a destruction; but they are in peace.
  Isaiah 57:2 — close righteous-death and peace parallel: Wisdom contrasts apparent destruction with peace; Isaiah describes the righteous person's burial in peace.

Wisdom of Solomon 3:4
For even if they are punished in the sight of human beings, their hope is full of immortality;
  1 Corinthians 15:53-54 — related hope of immortality beyond mortality: The righteous hope in immortality despite apparent punishment; Paul likewise anticipates mortality putting on immortality and death's defeat.

Wisdom of Solomon 3:5
and after being disciplined a little, they will receive great benefits, because God tested them and found them worthy of Himself.
  Hebrews 12:10-11 — parallel beneficial divine discipline: A little discipline brings great benefits here; Hebrews describes God's discipline as beneficial and producing righteousness afterward.

Wisdom of Solomon 3:6
Like gold in a crucible He tested them, and like a sacrificial whole burnt offering He accepted them.
  Proverbs 17:3 — close gold-furnace and divine-testing image: Wisdom likens God's testing to gold in a crucible; Proverbs similarly compares the testing of gold and silver to hearts chosen before the Lord.

Wisdom of Solomon 3:7
And at the time of their visitation they will shine out, and like sparks among stubble they will dart about.
  Daniel 12:3 — parallel future radiance of the faithful: The righteous shine at their visitation; Daniel likewise depicts the understanding and faithful as heavenly lights and stars.

Wisdom of Solomon 3:8
They will judge nations and rule over peoples, and the Lord will reign over them unto the ages.
  Daniel 7:22-27 — parallel judgment and kingdom of the holy ones: Wisdom promises the righteous judgment over nations under the Lord's reign; Daniel assigns judgment and everlasting kingship to the holy people.

Wisdom of Solomon 3:9
Those who trust in Him will understand truth, and the faithful will remain with Him in love, for grace and mercy are for His chosen ones.
  John 15:9-10 — parallel remaining in divine love: The faithful remain with God in love here; Jesus similarly calls His disciples to remain in His love through keeping His commandments.

Wisdom of Solomon 3:10
But the ungodly will have punishment in keeping with their reasoning— those who neglected what is righteous and departed from the Lord.
  Romans 2:5-9 — parallel judgment according to unrighteous conduct: Wisdom connects punishment to ungodly reasoning and departure from righteousness; Romans contrasts reward for good with wrath upon those obeying injustice.

Wisdom of Solomon 3:11
For the one who despises wisdom and discipline is wretched; their hope is empty, their labors unprofitable, and their works useless.
  Proverbs 1:7 — close rejection of wisdom and discipline: Wisdom calls the despiser of wisdom and discipline wretched; Proverbs identifies despising both as the behavior of the ungodly.

Wisdom of Solomon 3:13
For blessed is the undefiled barren woman, who has not known a bed of transgression; she will have fruit at the visitation of souls,
  Isaiah 54:1 — related reversal of barrenness: Wisdom promises fruit to the undefiled barren woman; Isaiah tells a barren woman to rejoice in abundant children, using the image for a restored community rather than an identical individual situation.

Wisdom of Solomon 3:14
and the eunuch who has done no lawless deed with his hand and has not conceived evil against the Lord; for a choice favor will be given him for his faithfulness, and a more delightful portion in the Lord’s temple.
  Isaiah 56:4-5 — close faithful-eunuch and temple-inheritance parallel: Both promise faithful eunuchs an honored portion in God's house; Isaiah expressly adds an everlasting name better than sons and daughters.

Wisdom of Solomon 3:15
For the fruit of good labors is glorious, and the root of understanding does not fail.
  Proverbs 11:30 — parallel righteous fruit as life: The glorious fruit of good labor and unfailing root of understanding correspond to a tree of life growing from righteousness's fruit.

Wisdom of Solomon 3:16
But the children of adulterers will not reach fulfillment, and offspring from an unlawful bed will disappear.
  Wisdom of Solomon 4:3-5 — internal development of unfruitful unlawful offspring: The failure of offspring from an unlawful bed is developed in the following chapter's illegitimate shoots and useless fruit.

Wisdom of Solomon 3:17
For even if they become long-lived, they will be reckoned as nothing, and their old age will be without honor at the end;
  Wisdom of Solomon 4:8-9 — internal contrast between age and moral honor: Long life does not secure honor for the offspring described here; the next chapter explains honorable age through understanding and an unstained life rather than years.

Wisdom of Solomon 3:18
and if they die quickly, they will have no hope, nor consolation on the day of decision.
  Wisdom of Solomon 3:4 — explicit same-chapter contrast of hope after death: The offspring described here have no hope if they die quickly, in contrast to the righteous whose hope is full of immortality despite their apparent affliction.

Wisdom of Solomon 3:19
For the ends of an unrighteous generation are harsh.
  Wisdom of Solomon 4:19-20 — internal elaboration of the unrighteous end: The harsh end of an unrighteous generation is elaborated as dishonor, anguish, lost remembrance and a reckoning of sins.

Wisdom of Solomon 4:1
Childlessness with virtue is better, for immortality is in its remembrance, because it is recognized both by God and by human beings.
  Isaiah 56:5 — parallel enduring honor beyond children: Wisdom values childlessness with virtue through immortal remembrance; Isaiah promises faithful eunuchs an everlasting name better than sons and daughters.

Wisdom of Solomon 4:2
When it is present, people imitate it, and when it has departed, they long for it; throughout the age it marches crowned in triumph, having won the contest for undefiled prizes.
  1 Corinthians 9:24-25 — shared contest and enduring-crown imagery: Virtue is pictured winning undefiled prizes and marching crowned; Paul likewise uses disciplined athletic competition for an incorruptible crown.

Wisdom of Solomon 4:3
But the prolific multitude of the ungodly will be of no use; from illegitimate shoots it will not send a root deep down, nor establish a secure foundation.
  Psalms 1:3-4 — contrast of rooted righteous life and unstable ungodliness: Wisdom denies the ungodly a deep root; the psalm contrasts the righteous tree beside water with ungodly chaff carried away.

Wisdom of Solomon 4:4
For even if it flourishes in branches for a time, standing insecurely, it will be shaken by wind and uprooted by the force of winds.
  Psalms 1:4 — shared wind-driven instability of ungodliness: The insecure branches are uprooted by winds; the psalm similarly depicts ungodly people as chaff driven away by wind, with a different plant image.

Wisdom of Solomon 4:5
Unfulfilled branches will be broken off, and their fruit will be useless, unripe for eating, and fit for nothing.
  Matthew 7:19 — parallel removal of fruitless growth: Wisdom's useless, broken branches correspond to the tree without good fruit being cut down; both use failed fruit to depict moral failure.

Wisdom of Solomon 4:7
But if a righteous person dies early, he will be at rest.
  Isaiah 57:1-2 — parallel righteous death as peaceful removal: Wisdom grants rest to the righteous who die early; Isaiah presents the righteous person's removal from injustice and burial in peace.

Wisdom of Solomon 4:8
For honorable old age is not length of time, nor is it measured by a number of years;
  Job 32:7-9 — parallel denial that years guarantee worth: Wisdom denies that honorable age is measured by years; Elihu similarly denies that the long-lived necessarily possess wisdom or judgment.

Wisdom of Solomon 4:9
rather, understanding is gray hair for human beings, and an unstained life is the maturity of old age.
  Job 32:8-9 — parallel wisdom rather than years as maturity: Wisdom substitutes understanding and an unstained life for gray hair; Elihu locates instruction in God's breath rather than mere length of life.

Wisdom of Solomon 4:10
Having become pleasing to God, he was loved, and while living among sinners he was transferred.
  Genesis 5:24 — close pleasing-God and transfer motif: The unnamed righteous person pleases God and is transferred, closely recalling Enoch's pleasing God and being transferred; the Wisdom verse itself does not name Enoch.

Wisdom of Solomon 4:11
He was snatched away, lest evil change his understanding or deceit mislead his soul;
  Isaiah 57:1 — parallel removal from surrounding evil: Wisdom describes removal lest evil alter the righteous mind; Isaiah says the righteous man is taken from the presence of injustice.

Wisdom of Solomon 4:12
for the fascination of wickedness obscures what is good, and the wandering of desire alters an innocent mind.
  James 1:14-15 — parallel corrupting action of desire: Desire alters an innocent mind here; James describes desire enticing a person and giving birth to sin and death.

Wisdom of Solomon 4:13
Having been brought to completion in a short time, he fulfilled long years;
  Wisdom of Solomon 4:8-9 — explicit same-chapter explanation of mature short life: A short life can fulfill long years because the earlier lines define honorable maturity by understanding and an unstained life rather than elapsed time.

Wisdom of Solomon 4:14
for his soul was pleasing to the Lord; therefore he hastened from the midst of wickedness. Yet the peoples saw and did not understand, nor set such a thing in their minds:
  Isaiah 57:1 — close misunderstood removal of the righteous: Both describe the righteous person's removal from wickedness and observers who fail to understand or take it to heart.

Wisdom of Solomon 4:15
that grace and mercy are among His chosen ones, and visitation among His holy ones.
  Wisdom of Solomon 3:9 — internal recurrence of mercy for the chosen: This statement repeats the earlier assurance that grace and mercy belong to God's chosen, explaining the apparent loss of the righteous.

Wisdom of Solomon 4:16
But a righteous person who has died will condemn the living ungodly, and youth brought swiftly to completion will condemn the many years of an unrighteous person’s old age.
  Matthew 12:41-42 — related exemplary condemnation at judgment: The righteous dead expose the living ungodly here; Matthew similarly presents earlier repentant or wisdom-seeking people rising to condemn a later generation.

Wisdom of Solomon 4:17
For they will see a wise person’s end and will not understand what the Lord planned concerning him or for what purpose He kept him safe.
  Isaiah 57:1 — parallel failure to understand a righteous person's end: Wisdom's observers do not understand God's purpose in the wise person's end; Isaiah likewise stresses the uncomprehended removal of the righteous.

Wisdom of Solomon 4:18
They will see and despise, but the Lord will laugh them to scorn.
  Psalms 36:13 — close divine-laughter reversal: The Lord laughs at those who despise the righteous person's end; the psalm likewise portrays the Lord laughing at the sinner because his day is coming.

Wisdom of Solomon 4:19
And afterward they will become a dishonored corpse and an object of outrage among the dead for ever; for He will dash them down headlong, speechless, and shake them from their foundations; they will be laid utterly waste, and will be in anguish, and their remembrance will perish.
  Psalms 9:6-7 — parallel destruction of the ungodly's remembrance: Wisdom joins the ungodly's destruction to vanished remembrance; the psalm likewise describes their erased name and destroyed memory.

Wisdom of Solomon 4:20
They will come in dread at the reckoning of their sins, and their lawless deeds will confront and convict them.
  Ecclesiastes 12:14 — parallel reckoning of all deeds: Lawless deeds confront their authors at examination here; Ecclesiastes likewise places every deed, even what was overlooked, under divine judgment.

Wisdom of Solomon 5:1
Then the righteous person will stand with great boldness before those who afflicted him and those who disregarded his labors.
  Proverbs 28:1 — parallel confidence of the righteous: The righteous person's bold stance before former tormentors corresponds to the proverb's righteous person being confident like a lion.

Wisdom of Solomon 5:2
When they see him, they will be troubled with terrible fear and astonished at his unexpected salvation.
  Isaiah 66:5 — related reversal of persecutors' expectations: Those who afflicted the righteous are astonished at his salvation; Isaiah likewise anticipates shame for those who hate the people addressed by the Lord.

Wisdom of Solomon 5:3
They will speak among themselves in repentance, and in anguish of spirit they will groan and say:
  Wisdom of Solomon 2:21-22 — internal recognition of former error: The anguished repentance of the persecutors realizes the narrator's earlier verdict that their reasoning was astray and ignorant of God's reward.

Wisdom of Solomon 5:4
“This was the one whom we fools once made a laughingstock and a byword of reproach. We reckoned his life madness and his end dishonorable.
  Wisdom of Solomon 2:19-20 — internal reversal of contempt and shame: The former mockers recall their insulting verdict on the righteous person's life and end, reversing the earlier proposal to torture him and condemn him to shameful death.

Wisdom of Solomon 5:5
How was he counted among the sons of God, and how is his portion among the saints?
  Wisdom of Solomon 2:13-18 — internal vindication of disputed divine sonship: The persecutors now recognize the righteous person's portion among God's sons and saints after previously treating his claim to divine sonship as something to disprove.

Wisdom of Solomon 5:6
So we strayed from the way of truth, and the light of righteousness did not shine upon us, and the sun did not rise upon us.
  Proverbs 4:18-19 — parallel moral paths of light and darkness: The persecutors confess that righteousness's light did not shine upon their way; Proverbs contrasts the shining righteous path with the dark, stumbling way of the ungodly.

Wisdom of Solomon 5:7
We were filled with paths of lawlessness and destruction, and traveled through trackless deserts, but the way of the Lord we did not know.
  Proverbs 4:14-19 — parallel lawless paths opposed to the righteous way: Their admission of traveling destructive paths corresponds to the prohibition of ungodly ways and the contrast between righteous light and ungodly darkness.

Wisdom of Solomon 5:8
What did arrogance profit us? And what did wealth with boasting contribute to us?
  Luke 12:19-21 — parallel exposure of wealth's inability to save: The persecutors question the benefit of boastful wealth; the rich fool's accumulated goods similarly fail to help when his life is demanded.

Wisdom of Solomon 5:9
All those things passed like a shadow and like a report rushing by;
  Job 8:9 — shared fleeting-shadow image: The vanished advantages of the ungodly pass like a shadow; Bildad likewise calls human life on earth a shadow, within a different argument about limited knowledge.

Wisdom of Solomon 5:10
like a ship passing through surging water, whose trace cannot be found after it has crossed, nor the path of its keel among the waves;
  Proverbs 30:19 — shared elusive path of a seagoing ship: Wisdom's ship leaves no discoverable wake; Proverbs names a seagoing ship's path among tracks the speaker cannot comprehend.

Wisdom of Solomon 5:11
or like a bird that has flown through the air: no sign of its passage is found. It crossed the light air, lashed by the beat of its pinions and split by the force of its rushing wings; and afterward no sign of its passage was found in it;
  Proverbs 30:19 — shared elusive aerial track: Wisdom describes a bird's vanished path through the air; Proverbs likewise names the tracks of a flying eagle among elusive things.

Wisdom of Solomon 5:13
so we also were born and vanished, and had no sign of virtue to show, but were consumed in our wickedness.”
  Wisdom of Solomon 2:9 — internal failure of the promised lasting mark: Those who planned to leave tokens of enjoyment everywhere now confess that they vanished without a sign of virtue; their proposed legacy is exposed as empty.

Wisdom of Solomon 5:14
For the hope of an ungodly person is like dust borne by wind, and like fine frost driven by a storm; like smoke it was dispersed by wind, and like the memory of a guest who stayed one day it passed away.
  Psalms 1:4 — parallel wind-driven disappearance of ungodliness: The ungodly person's hope is wind-borne dust and dispersed smoke; the psalm similarly pictures the ungodly as chaff flung away by wind.

Wisdom of Solomon 5:15
But the righteous live for ever, and their reward is with the Lord, and the Most High takes care of them.
  John 10:28-29 — parallel enduring life under divine care: The righteous live forever under the Most High's care; Jesus likewise promises eternal life and security in His and the Father's hand.

Wisdom of Solomon 5:16
Therefore they will receive the royal crown of splendor and the diadem of beauty from the Lord’s hand, because with His right hand He will cover them and with His arm He will shield them.
  Isaiah 62:3 — close royal-crown and divine-hand imagery: The righteous receive a crown and diadem from the Lord's hand; Isaiah calls restored Zion a crown of beauty and royal diadem in God's hand, with a different bearer of the image.

Wisdom of Solomon 5:17

Wisdom of Solomon 18:13

  Exodus 4:22-23 — close Israel-as-son and firstborn-judgment connection: Wisdom ties recognition of God's son to the firstborn's destruction; Exodus already links Israel as God's firstborn son with the threatened death of Pharaoh's firstborn.

Wisdom of Solomon 18:14
For while quiet silence surrounded all things and night was midway through its swift course,
  Exodus 12:29 — midnight-judgment allusion: Wisdom's silence halfway through the night sets the judgment scene that Exodus explicitly locates at midnight.

Wisdom of Solomon 18:15
Thine all-powerful word leaped from heaven, from royal thrones, a stern warrior, into the midst of the doomed land, carrying Thine unfeigned command as a sharp sword;
  Hebrews 4:12-13 — related divine-word and sword imagery: Wisdom portrays God's commanded judgment through an armed, sword-bearing word; Hebrews likewise likens God's active, judging word to a penetrating sword, without identifying this Egyptian judgment as the incarnation.

Wisdom of Solomon 18:16
standing there, it filled all things with death; it touched heaven while standing upon the earth.
  Exodus 12:29 — firstborn judgment behind the cosmic-warrior image: The word filling the land with death interprets the Lord's midnight striking of Egypt; Exodus does not narrate the heaven-touching warrior's stature.

Wisdom of Solomon 18:20
But a trial of death touched the righteous too, and a breaking of the multitude occurred in the wilderness; yet the wrath did not remain long.
  Numbers 17:10-13 — Israelite outbreak and halted wrath allusion: The righteous people's brief death trial recalls the outbreak among Israel that ceased after priestly atonement.

Wisdom of Solomon 18:21

  Numbers 17:11-13 — Aaron's incense intercession allusion: The minister hastening with incense to oppose wrath recalls Aaron running into the congregation with the fire pan and making atonement.

Wisdom of Solomon 18:22
He conquered the anger, not by bodily strength, not by the activity of weapons, but by a word he subdued the punisher, reminding him of the fathers’ oaths and covenants.
  Exodus 32:11-14 — related intercession invoking the fathers' promises: The priestly appeal to ancestral oaths parallels Moses's distinct intercession recalling God's oath to Abraham, Isaac and Jacob; the two mediators and episodes are not conflated.

Wisdom of Solomon 18:23

  Numbers 17:13 — close standing-between-dead-and-living allusion: Wisdom's mediator stands between the fallen and the living and checks wrath; Numbers explicitly says Aaron stood between dead and living and the outbreak subsided.

Wisdom of Solomon 18:24
For upon his ankle-length garment was the whole world, and the glories of the fathers upon the engraving of four rows of stones, and Thy majesty upon the diadem of his head.
  Exodus 28:17-21 — priestly four-row stones background: The fathers' glories engraved in four rows correspond to the breastpiece's four rows of stones bearing Israel's tribal names; Wisdom supplies its larger cosmic interpretation of the garment.

Wisdom of Solomon 18:25

  Numbers 17:13-15 — halted-outbreak narrative conclusion: The destroyer's yielding corresponds to the outbreak subsiding after Aaron's intercession; Numbers does not itself attribute this to fear of the priestly ornaments.

Wisdom of Solomon 19:1

Wisdom of Solomon 19:2

  Exodus 14:5-9 — close change-of-heart and pursuit allusion: Both recount Egypt reversing its release of Israel and pursuing the departing people.

Wisdom of Solomon 19:3

  Exodus 12:30-33 — background of mourning and urgent expulsion: Wisdom stresses the folly of pursuing those just begged to leave; Exodus supplies the mourning cry and urgent Egyptian demand for Israel's departure before the subsequent pursuit.

Wisdom of Solomon 19:4

  Exodus 14:17-18 — parallel divine purpose in the final pursuit: Wisdom interprets the compulsion toward further punishment; Exodus likewise places the Egyptians' renewed entry into danger within God's announced purpose to display His glory, without mentioning forgetfulness.

Wisdom of Solomon 19:5

  Exodus 14:27-29 — close contrasting outcomes at the sea: The people's astonishing passage and enemies' death correspond to Israel walking on dry ground while the returning sea covers Egypt's forces.

Wisdom of Solomon 19:6

  Genesis 1:9-10 — creation background for the sea-crossing renewal image: Wisdom calls the elements' service a new fashioning of creation; Genesis's appearance of dry land from gathered water supplies the original creation pattern behind that comparison.

Wisdom of Solomon 19:7

  Exodus 14:21-22 — dry-road-through-the-sea allusion: Wisdom's dry way out of the sea corresponds to the divided waters and dry ground of Israel's crossing; the grass-bearing plain is Wisdom's additional description.

Wisdom of Solomon 19:8
through it those sheltered by Thy hand passed as an entire nation, having seen marvelous wonders.
  Exodus 14:29-31 — collective passage under God's protecting hand: The sheltered nation passes after seeing wonders; Exodus likewise describes Israel's safe passage and recognition of the Lord's great hand.

Wisdom of Solomon 19:9

  Isaiah 63:13-14 — close horse-like Exodus guidance image: Wisdom likens the rescued people to grazing horses; Isaiah likewise compares God's guidance through the abyss to a horse in the wilderness and livestock on a plain, without the same leaping-lamb image.

Wisdom of Solomon 19:10

  Exodus 8:1-13 — close frogs-from-water and gnats-from-earth allusions: Wisdom recalls abnormal production of frogs from the river and gnats from earth; Exodus describes both the frogs rising from Egypt's waters and the dust becoming gnats.

Wisdom of Solomon 19:11
Later they also saw a new production of birds, when, urged on by desire, they asked for luxurious foods;
  Numbers 11:4-6 — background of desire for richer food: The request for luxurious foods recalls the people's intense craving for meat and dissatisfaction with manna.

Wisdom of Solomon 19:12
for as a consolation quail came up to them from the sea.
  Numbers 11:31 — close quail-from-the-sea allusion: Wisdom's quail coming from the sea correspond to the wind carrying quail across from the sea into Israel's camp.

Wisdom of Solomon 19:13

  Exodus 9:23-24 — earlier storm-signs background: The remembered violent thunderbolts correspond to the thunder, hail and fire that earlier struck Egypt; the target does not itself state Wisdom's comparative judgment about hostility to strangers.

Wisdom of Solomon 19:14

  Exodus 1:8-14 — background of the later enslavement of benefactors: The latter group enslaving formerly beneficial strangers corresponds to the new Pharaoh who did not know Joseph and Egypt's oppression of Israel. The former hostile hosts remain the distinct Sodom example.

Wisdom of Solomon 19:15

  Genesis 19:24-25 — judgment on the former hostile hosts: The announced visitation on the former group corresponds to Sodom's overthrow after the hostile treatment of visitors narrated earlier in Genesis 19, not to Egypt's later enslavement.

Wisdom of Solomon 19:16

Wisdom of Solomon 19:18

  Wisdom of Solomon 16:24-25 — internal explanation of creation's altered service: The rearranged elements recapitulate creation stretching or relaxing to serve God's purposes and changing to provide His nourishing gift.

Wisdom of Solomon 19:20
