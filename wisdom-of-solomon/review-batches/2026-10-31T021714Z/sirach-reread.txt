Sirach 1:1
SOURCE: All wisdom is from the Lord and is with Him for ever.
TARGET Proverbs 2:6:
Proverbs 2:6: For the Lord gives wisdom, and from His presence come knowledge and understanding.
RELATIONSHIP: Wisdom teaching parallel
RATIONALE: Sirach locates all wisdom with the Lord; Proverbs likewise names the Lord as giver of wisdom and the source of knowledge and understanding.

Sirach 1:2
SOURCE: Who can count the sand of the seas, the drops of rain, and the days of eternity?
TARGET Job 38:37:
Job 38:37: Who counts clouds with wisdom and inclined heaven toward earth?
RELATIONSHIP: Rhetorical image parallel
RATIONALE: Sirach asks who can count rain and other quantities beyond human reckoning; God's question in Job asks who counts clouds with wisdom. Both challenge human mastery of the heavens' quantities.

Sirach 1:3
SOURCE: Who can trace the height of heaven, the breadth of the earth, the abyss, and wisdom?
TARGET Job 11:7-9:
Job 11:7: Will you find a trace of the Lord, or have you reached the furthest things the Almighty made?
Job 11:8: Heaven is high—what will you do? What do you know of things deeper than those in Hades,
Job 11:9: or longer than the measure of earth, or than the breadth of the sea?
RELATIONSHIP: Rhetorical image parallel
RATIONALE: Sirach links inaccessible cosmic dimensions with wisdom. The speech in Job likewise challenges human understanding through heaven's height, depths, and the earth and sea's extent; its accusation against Job is not endorsed here.

Sirach 1:4
SOURCE: Wisdom was created before all things, and discerning understanding from eternity.
TARGET Proverbs 8:22-25:
Proverbs 8:22: The Lord created me as the beginning of His ways, for His works.
Proverbs 8:23: Before the age He founded me, in the beginning,
Proverbs 8:24: before making the earth and before making the deeps, before the springs of the waters came forth,
Proverbs 8:25: before the mountains were established, and before all hills He gives me birth.
RELATIONSHIP: Personified wisdom parallel
RATIONALE: Wisdom's creation before all things corresponds to personified Wisdom's account of being created before earth, depths and mountains in Proverbs. The shared claim concerns wisdom's priority in creation.

Sirach 1:6
SOURCE: To whom was the root of wisdom revealed? Who knew her skillful works?
TARGET Job 28:20-23:
Job 28:20: But where was wisdom found, and what is the place of understanding?
Job 28:21: It has escaped every human and was hidden from the birds of heaven.
Job 28:22: Destruction and Death said, ‘We have heard its renown.’
Job 28:23: God established its way well, and He knows its place.
RELATIONSHIP: Wisdom teaching parallel
RATIONALE: Sirach asks to whom wisdom's root was revealed; Job asks where wisdom is found, says it escapes human sight, and contrasts this with God's knowledge of its place.

Sirach 1:8
SOURCE: There is One who is wise, greatly to be feared, seated upon His throne,
TARGET Romans 16:27:
Romans 16:27: to the only wise God, through Jesus Christ, to whom be the glory throughout the ages. Amen.]
RELATIONSHIP: Divine attribute parallel
RATIONALE: Sirach identifies One who is wise, subsequently named as the Lord; Romans' doxology praises the only wise God. The connection concerns divine wisdom, without adding throne imagery to Romans.

Sirach 1:9
SOURCE: the Lord Himself. He created her, saw her, counted her out, and poured her upon all His works,
TARGET Job 28:27:
Job 28:27: Then He saw wisdom and expounded it; having prepared it, He traced it out.
RELATIONSHIP: Personified wisdom parallel
RATIONALE: Sirach describes God seeing and ordering wisdom before pouring her upon His works; Job likewise describes Him seeing, preparing and tracing out wisdom within His ordering of creation.

Sirach 1:10
SOURCE: with all flesh according to His giving; He supplied her to those who love Him.
TARGET James 1:5:
James 1:5: But if any of you lacks wisdom, let him ask God, who gives to all generously and without reproach, and it will be given to him.
RELATIONSHIP: Wisdom as divine gift
RATIONALE: Wisdom is supplied according to God's giving in Sirach; James directs those lacking wisdom to the God who gives generously. Both locate the gift's availability in God.

Sirach 1:11
SOURCE: The fear of the Lord is glory and a boast, gladness and a crown of rejoicing.
TARGET Psalms 127:1-2:
Psalms 127:1: A song of the ascents. Happy are all who fear the Lord, who walk in His ways.
Psalms 127:2: You will eat the labors of your wrists; happy are you, and it will be well with you.
RELATIONSHIP: Reverence and gladness
RATIONALE: Sirach associates fear of the Lord with rejoicing; the psalm calls those fearing the Lord happy and promises their well-being. Reverence and happiness are joined in both.

Sirach 1:12
SOURCE: The fear of the Lord will delight the heart and give gladness, joy, and length of days.
TARGET Proverbs 10:27:
Proverbs 10:27: The fear of the Lord adds days, but the years of the impious will become few.
RELATIONSHIP: Reverence and longevity
RATIONALE: Sirach promises length of days through fear of the Lord; Proverbs explicitly says that fear of the Lord adds days. The longevity promise closely corresponds.

Sirach 1:13
SOURCE: It will go well at the end with the one who fears the Lord, and on the day of his death he will be blessed.
TARGET Ecclesiastes 8:12:
Ecclesiastes 8:12: The sinner has done evil from then, and there is length for him; for I know that it will be good for those who fear God, so that they may fear before Him.
RELATIONSHIP: Reverence and well-being
RATIONALE: Sirach says the Lord-fearing person's end will be good; Ecclesiastes affirms that it will be good for those fearing God despite the sinner's apparent prolongation.

Sirach 1:14
SOURCE: To fear the Lord is the beginning of wisdom; she was created together with the faithful in the womb.
TARGET Proverbs 9:10:
Proverbs 9:10: The beginning of wisdom is the fear of the Lord, and the counsel of holy ones is understanding.
RELATIONSHIP: Verbal wisdom parallel
RATIONALE: Both verses explicitly identify fear of the Lord as the beginning of wisdom. Sirach's additional womb imagery is not attributed to Proverbs.

Sirach 1:15
SOURCE: Among human beings she nested on an eternal foundation, and among their offspring she will be trusted.
TARGET Sirach 4:16:
Sirach 4:16: If he trusts, he will possess her as an inheritance, and his descendants will hold her in possession.
RELATIONSHIP: Generational wisdom parallel
RATIONALE: Wisdom is trusted among people's offspring in Sirach 1; chapter 4 explains her possession as an inheritance held by descendants. Both describe wisdom continuing across generations.

Sirach 1:16
SOURCE: To fear the Lord is the fullness of wisdom, and she intoxicates them with her fruits.
TARGET Sirach 24:19-21:
Sirach 24:19: Come to me, you who desire me, and be filled from my produce.
Sirach 24:20: For my memory is sweeter than honey, and my inheritance than a honeycomb.
Sirach 24:21: Those who eat me will still hunger, and those who drink me will still thirst.
RELATIONSHIP: Personified wisdom imagery
RATIONALE: Wisdom intoxicates with her fruits in chapter 1; in chapter 24 she invites people to eat her produce and drink. Both portray participation in wisdom through consumption imagery.

Sirach 1:17
SOURCE: She will fill their whole house with desirable things and their storehouses with her produce.
TARGET Proverbs 24:3-4:
Proverbs 24:3: With wisdom a house is built, and with understanding it is raised upright;
Proverbs 24:4: with discernment store-rooms are filled from every precious and good wealth.
RELATIONSHIP: Wisdom and household abundance
RATIONALE: Sirach's wisdom fills a house and storehouses with desirable produce; Proverbs joins a house built through wisdom to store-rooms filled with precious wealth through discernment.

Sirach 1:18
SOURCE: The fear of the Lord is the crown of wisdom, making peace and the health of healing flourish.
TARGET Proverbs 3:7-8:
Proverbs 3:7: Do not be prudent in your own estimation; fear God and turn away from every evil.
Proverbs 3:8: Then there will be healing for your body and care for your bones.
RELATIONSHIP: Reverence and healing
RATIONALE: Sirach connects fear of the Lord with healing health; Proverbs connects fearing God and avoiding evil with healing for body and bones. Both describe reverence through bodily well-being.

Sirach 1:19
SOURCE: He saw her and counted her out; He rained down skill and discerning knowledge and raised high the glory of those who hold her fast.
TARGET Job 28:27:
Job 28:27: Then He saw wisdom and expounded it; having prepared it, He traced it out.
RELATIONSHIP: Divine ordering of wisdom
RATIONALE: Sirach's divine seeing and distribution of wisdom corresponds to Job's account of God seeing, preparing and expounding wisdom. Job's wording is not represented as an identical counting formula.

Sirach 1:20
SOURCE: To fear the Lord is the root of wisdom, and her branches are length of days.
TARGET Proverbs 10:27:
Proverbs 10:27: The fear of the Lord adds days, but the years of the impious will become few.
RELATIONSHIP: Reverence and longevity
RATIONALE: Sirach pictures long days as branches growing from fear of the Lord; Proverbs states directly that fear of the Lord adds days. The image develops the same longevity teaching.

Sirach 1:22
SOURCE: Unjust anger cannot be justified, for the weight of a man’s anger brings about his fall.
TARGET James 1:19-20:
James 1:19: Know this, my beloved brothers: let every person be quick to hear, slow to speak, slow to anger,
James 1:20: for a man’s anger does not accomplish the righteousness of God.
RELATIONSHIP: Anger and righteousness
RATIONALE: Sirach denies justification to unjust anger; James says human anger does not accomplish God's righteousness. Both reject anger as a reliable means of right conduct.

Sirach 1:23
SOURCE: A patient person will hold out until the right time, and afterward gladness will spring up for him.
TARGET James 5:11:
James 5:11: Look, we call those who endured blessed. You heard of Job’s endurance and saw the outcome from the Lord, because the Lord is full of compassion and merciful.
RELATIONSHIP: Endurance and its outcome
RATIONALE: Sirach promises gladness after patient endurance; James calls those who endure blessed and invokes Job's outcome from the compassionate Lord. Both look beyond present hardship.

Sirach 1:24
SOURCE: Until the right time he will conceal his words, and the lips of many will recount his understanding.
TARGET Proverbs 17:27-28:
Proverbs 17:27: One who refrains from sending forth a harsh word is discerning, and a patient man is intelligent.
Proverbs 17:28: Wisdom will be reckoned to a senseless person who asks for wisdom, and someone who makes himself mute will seem intelligent.
RELATIONSHIP: Wise restraint of speech
RATIONALE: Sirach's wise person conceals words until the right time; Proverbs commends withholding a harsh word and associates silence with intelligence. Speech restraint displays discernment in both.

Sirach 1:25
SOURCE: In wisdom’s treasuries are sayings of knowledge, but reverence for God is an abomination to a sinner.
TARGET Proverbs 1:7:
Proverbs 1:7: The fear of God is the beginning of wisdom, and good understanding belongs to all who practice it. Reverence toward God is the beginning of perception, but the ungodly will despise wisdom and discipline.
RELATIONSHIP: Wisdom and rejected reverence
RATIONALE: Sirach contrasts wisdom's knowledge with the sinner's aversion to reverence; Proverbs joins reverence with perception and says the ungodly despise wisdom and discipline.

Sirach 1:26
SOURCE: If you desire wisdom, keep the commandments, and the Lord will supply her to you.
TARGET Psalms 110:10:
Psalms 110:10: The beginning of wisdom is the fear of the Lord; good understanding belongs to all who practice it. His praise remains for an age of an age.
RELATIONSHIP: Practiced reverence and understanding
RATIONALE: Sirach tells wisdom-seekers to keep commandments; the psalm joins fear of the Lord with wisdom and good understanding for those who practice it. Both connect understanding with obedient practice.

Sirach 1:27
SOURCE: For wisdom and instruction are the fear of the Lord, and faithfulness and gentleness are His good pleasure.
TARGET James 3:17:
James 3:17: But the wisdom from above is first pure, then peaceable, considerate, open to persuasion, full of mercy and good fruits, impartial, without hypocrisy.
RELATIONSHIP: Wisdom's moral character
RATIONALE: Sirach associates wisdom with faithfulness and gentleness pleasing to God; James describes wisdom from above as peaceable, considerate and merciful. Both describe wisdom through conduct toward others.

Sirach 1:28
SOURCE: Do not disobey the fear of the Lord, and do not approach Him with a divided heart.
TARGET James 4:8:
James 4:8: Draw near to God, and He will draw near to you. Cleanse your hands, sinners, and purify your hearts, you of divided mind.
RELATIONSHIP: Undivided approach to God
RATIONALE: Sirach forbids approaching God with a divided heart; James commands those of divided mind to purify their hearts as they draw near to God.

Sirach 1:29
SOURCE: Do not play the hypocrite in people’s talk, and keep watch over your lips.
TARGET Matthew 23:27-28:
Matthew 23:27: Woe to you, scribes and Pharisees, hypocrites, because you resemble whitewashed tombs, which outwardly appear beautiful, but inwardly are full of dead people’s bones and all uncleanness!
Matthew 23:28: So you too outwardly appear righteous to people, but inwardly you are full of hypocrisy and lawlessness.
RELATIONSHIP: Hypocrisy condemned
RATIONALE: Sirach forbids hypocritical conduct in people's speech; Jesus condemns an outward appearance of righteousness concealing inward hypocrisy. Both reject a deceptive public presentation of one's character.

Sirach 1:30
SOURCE: Do not exalt yourself, lest you fall and bring dishonor upon your soul. The Lord will uncover your hidden things and cast you down in the midst of the assembly, because you did not approach the fear of the Lord and your heart is full of deceit.
TARGET Luke 14:7-11:
Luke 14:7: He told a parable to those who had been invited, noticing how they chose the foremost places at table. He said to them,
Luke 14:8: “When someone invites you to a wedding feast, do not recline in the foremost place. Someone more honored than you may have been invited by him,
Luke 14:9: and the one who invited you both will come and say to you, ‘Give this man your place.’ Then you will begin, with shame, to occupy the last place.
Luke 14:10: But when you are invited, go and recline in the last place, so that when the one who invited you comes, he will say to you, ‘Friend, move up higher.’ Then you will have honor before all who recline at table with you.
Luke 14:11: For everyone who exalts himself will be humbled, and the one who humbles himself will be exalted.”
RELATIONSHIP: Self-exaltation and public shame
RATIONALE: Sirach warns that self-exaltation ends in humiliation before the assembly; Jesus' banquet parable depicts shame before fellow guests and concludes that self-exalters will be humbled.

Sirach 2:1
SOURCE: Child, if you come to serve the Lord, prepare your soul for testing.
TARGET James 1:2-4:
James 1:2: Count it all joy, my brothers, whenever you encounter trials of various kinds,
James 1:3: knowing that the testing of your faith produces endurance.
James 1:4: And let endurance have its complete work, so that you may be complete and whole, lacking in nothing.
RELATIONSHIP: Testing in faithful service
RATIONALE: Sirach prepares a new servant of the Lord for testing; James tells believers to expect trials that test faith and produce endurance. Testing belongs within faithful life in both.

Sirach 2:2
SOURCE: Set your heart straight and endure; do not rush in a time of calamity.
TARGET Psalms 26:14:
Psalms 26:14: Wait for the Lord; act courageously, and let your heart be strong, and wait for the Lord.
RELATIONSHIP: Steadfast heart in distress
RATIONALE: Sirach commands a steady heart and endurance in calamity; the psalm tells the waiting worshipper to act courageously and let the heart be strong while waiting for the Lord.

Sirach 2:3
SOURCE: Cling to Him and do not withdraw, so that you may grow in your last days.
TARGET Deuteronomy 10:20:
Deuteronomy 10:20: You shall fear the Lord your God, and you shall serve Him, and you shall cleave unto Him, and you shall swear by His name.
RELATIONSHIP: Clinging to the Lord
RATIONALE: Sirach's instruction to cling to the Lord without withdrawing closely corresponds to Deuteronomy's combined commands to fear, serve and cleave to Him.

Sirach 2:4
SOURCE: Accept whatever is brought upon you, and be patient amid the changes of your humiliation.
TARGET Hebrews 12:5-11:
Hebrews 12:5: and you have forgotten the encouragement that addresses you as sons: “My son, do not make light of the Lord’s discipline, nor faint when reproved by Him.
Hebrews 12:6: For the Lord disciplines the one He loves, and scourges every son He receives.”
Hebrews 12:7: Endure for discipline. God deals with you as sons; for what son is there whom a father does not discipline?
Hebrews 12:8: But if you are without discipline, in which all have become partakers, then you are illegitimate children and not sons.
Hebrews 12:9: Furthermore, we had our fathers in the flesh to discipline us, and we respected them. But shall we not much more submit to the Father of spirits and live?
Hebrews 12:10: For they disciplined us for a few days according to what seemed right to them, but He for our benefit, so that we may share in His holiness.
Hebrews 12:11: Now every discipline seems for the present to be a matter of sorrow, not joy, but afterward it yields the peaceful fruit of righteousness to those trained through it.
RELATIONSHIP: Enduring humbling hardship
RATIONALE: Sirach calls for patience in humiliation; Hebrews urges endurance of painful discipline for its later benefit. The comparison concerns patient reception of hardship, without identifying every adversity as the same event.

Sirach 2:5
SOURCE: For gold is tested in fire, and acceptable people in the furnace of humiliation.
TARGET 1 Peter 1:6-7:
1 Peter 1:6: In this you rejoice, though now, for a little while, if necessary, you have been grieved by various trials,
1 Peter 1:7: so that the tested genuineness of your faith—more precious than gold that perishes, though tested by fire—may be found to result in praise and glory and honor at the revelation of Jesus Christ.
RELATIONSHIP: Testing by fire imagery
RATIONALE: Sirach compares acceptable people tested in humiliation with gold tested in fire; Peter likewise compares faith tested through trials with fire-tested gold. Both use refining to explain human testing.

Sirach 2:6
SOURCE: Trust Him, and He will support you. Set your ways straight and hope in Him.
TARGET Proverbs 3:5-6:
Proverbs 3:5: Trust God with your whole heart; do not exalt yourself in your own wisdom.
Proverbs 3:6: In all your ways acknowledge her, that she may cut your ways straight; and your foot will surely not stumble.
RELATIONSHIP: Trust and straight paths
RATIONALE: Sirach combines trust in God with setting one's ways straight; Proverbs commands wholehearted trust in God and attendance to wisdom for straight paths, rather than self-reliance.

Sirach 2:7
SOURCE: You who fear the Lord, wait for His mercy, and do not turn aside, lest you fall.
TARGET Psalms 32:18-22:
Psalms 32:18: Behold, the Lord’s eyes are upon those who fear Him, those who hope in His mercy,
Psalms 32:19: to rescue their souls from death and nourish them in famine.
Psalms 32:20: Our soul waits for the Lord, for He is our helper and defender.
Psalms 32:21: For our heart will rejoice in Him, and we have hoped in His holy name.
Psalms 32:22: May Thy mercy, Lord, be upon us, just as we have hoped in Thee.
RELATIONSHIP: Waiting for divine mercy
RATIONALE: Sirach addresses those fearing the Lord and waiting for mercy; the psalm describes precisely those who fear Him, hope in His mercy and wait for His help.

Sirach 2:8
SOURCE: You who fear the Lord, trust Him, and your reward will certainly not fail.
TARGET Psalms 24:2-3:
Psalms 24:2: In Thee I have trusted; may I not be put to shame, and let my enemies not laugh at me.
Psalms 24:3: For indeed, all who wait for Thee will certainly not be put to shame; let all who act lawlessly without cause be ashamed.
RELATIONSHIP: Trust that will not fail
RATIONALE: Sirach assures the Lord-fearing that trust will not lose its reward; the psalm declares that those waiting for God will not be put to shame. Both oppose trust to final disappointment.

Sirach 2:9
SOURCE: You who fear the Lord, hope for good things, for eternal gladness and mercy.
TARGET Romans 15:13:
Romans 15:13: And may the God of hope fill you with all joy and peace in believing, so that you may abound in hope in the power of the Holy Spirit.
RELATIONSHIP: Hope and believing joy
RATIONALE: Sirach urges hope for gladness and mercy; Romans asks the God of hope to fill believers with joy and peace so that hope abounds. Both join reliance on God with joy.

Sirach 2:10
SOURCE: Look at the generations of old and see: Who trusted in the Lord and was put to shame? Who remained in His fear and was abandoned? Who called upon Him and was disregarded by Him?
TARGET Psalms 21:5-6:
Psalms 21:5: In Thee our fathers hoped; they hoped, and Thou didst rescue them.
Psalms 21:6: To Thee they cried out and were saved; in Thee they hoped and were not put to shame.
RELATIONSHIP: Ancestral testimony of trust
RATIONALE: Sirach asks whether earlier generations trusted and were ashamed or called without response; the psalm says the fathers hoped, cried to God, were rescued and were not ashamed.

Sirach 2:11
SOURCE: For the Lord is compassionate and merciful; He forgives sins and saves in a time of affliction.
TARGET Psalms 102:8-10:
Psalms 102:8: The Lord is compassionate and merciful, patient and abundant in mercy.
Psalms 102:9: He will not be angry to the end, nor maintain wrath for ever.
Psalms 102:10: He has not dealt with us according to our sins, nor repaid us according to our lawless deeds,
RELATIONSHIP: Compassion and forgiveness
RATIONALE: Sirach explains trust through the Lord's compassion and forgiveness; the psalm calls Him compassionate and merciful and says He has not repaid people according to their sins.

Sirach 2:12
SOURCE: Woe to cowardly hearts and slack hands, and to the sinner who walks on two paths!
TARGET James 1:6-8:
James 1:6: But let him ask in faith, doubting nothing, for the one who doubts is like a wave of the sea, driven and tossed by the wind.
James 1:7: For that person must not suppose that he will receive anything from the Lord,
James 1:8: a man of divided mind, unstable in all his ways.
RELATIONSHIP: Divided allegiance and instability
RATIONALE: Sirach condemns a sinner walking two paths; James describes the person of divided mind as unstable in all his ways. Both use divided direction to depict failed trust.

Sirach 2:13
SOURCE: Woe to the slack heart, because it does not trust; therefore it will not be sheltered.
TARGET Hebrews 10:38-39:
Hebrews 10:38: But My righteous one will live by faith; and if he draws back, My soul takes no pleasure in him.”
Hebrews 10:39: But we are not people who draw back to destruction, but people of faith for the preservation of the soul.
RELATIONSHIP: Faithlessness and lost protection
RATIONALE: Sirach's slack heart lacks trust and shelter; Hebrews contrasts faithful preservation of the soul with drawing back to destruction. The warnings connect abandoned trust with peril.

Sirach 2:14
SOURCE: Woe to you who have lost endurance! What will you do when the Lord visits?
TARGET Hebrews 10:35-39:
Hebrews 10:35: Therefore do not throw away your confidence, which has a great reward.
Hebrews 10:36: For you need endurance, so that, having done the will of God, you may receive the promise.
Hebrews 10:37: “For yet a very little while, the coming One will come and will not delay.
Hebrews 10:38: But My righteous one will live by faith; and if he draws back, My soul takes no pleasure in him.”
Hebrews 10:39: But we are not people who draw back to destruction, but people of faith for the preservation of the soul.
RELATIONSHIP: The need for endurance
RATIONALE: Sirach warns those who lose endurance before the Lord's visitation; Hebrews urges endurance to receive the promise and warns against drawing back while awaiting the coming One.

Sirach 2:15
SOURCE: Those who fear the Lord will not disobey His words, and those who love Him will keep His ways.
TARGET John 14:21:
John 14:21: The one who has My commandments and keeps them is the one who loves Me. And the one who loves Me will be loved by My Father, and I will love him and reveal Myself to him.”
RELATIONSHIP: Love expressed in obedience
RATIONALE: Sirach identifies love for the Lord with keeping His ways; Jesus identifies the person keeping His commandments as the one who loves Him. Both make obedience the expression of love.

Sirach 2:16
SOURCE: Those who fear the Lord will seek His good pleasure, and those who love Him will be filled with the law.
TARGET Psalms 118:47-48:
Psalms 118:47: And I was meditating on Thy commandments, which I loved greatly.
Psalms 118:48: And I raised my hands toward Thy commandments, which I loved, and I was pondering Thine ordinances.
RELATIONSHIP: Love directed toward God's law
RATIONALE: Sirach says those loving the Lord are filled with the law; the psalm's speaker loves, meditates on and raises his hands toward God's commandments. Both depict affectionate engagement with divine instruction.

Sirach 2:17
SOURCE: Those who fear the Lord will prepare their hearts and humble their souls before Him.
TARGET James 4:8-10:
James 4:8: Draw near to God, and He will draw near to you. Cleanse your hands, sinners, and purify your hearts, you of divided mind.
James 4:9: Be wretched and mourn and weep. Let your laughter be turned into mourning and your joy into dejection.
James 4:10: Humble yourselves before the Lord, and He will exalt you.
RELATIONSHIP: Preparing and humbling the heart
RATIONALE: Sirach's preparation of hearts and humbling of souls corresponds to James' commands to purify hearts and humble oneself before the Lord.

Sirach 2:18
SOURCE: Let us fall into the hands of the Lord and not into human hands, for as His greatness is, so also is His mercy.
TARGET 2 Samuel 24:14:
2 Samuel 24:14: David said to Gad, “I am greatly hemmed in on every side. I will fall into the Lord’s hand, for His compassions are very many; I certainly will not fall into human hands.” David chose death for himself.
RELATIONSHIP: Close verbal parallel
RATIONALE: Sirach prefers falling into the Lord's hands because of His mercy; David likewise chooses the Lord's hand rather than human hands because His compassions are many.

Sirach 3:1
SOURCE: Listen to me, your father, children, and act accordingly, so that you may be saved.
TARGET Proverbs 4:1-4:
Proverbs 4:1: Listen, children, to a father’s discipline; pay attention to gain discernment,
Proverbs 4:2: for I give you a good gift; do not abandon my law.
Proverbs 4:3: For I too became a son obedient to a father and loved in a mother’s presence,
Proverbs 4:4: who used to speak to me and teach me: Let our word take hold in your heart;
RELATIONSHIP: Paternal instruction formula
RATIONALE: Sirach addresses children as a father asking them to listen and act; Proverbs likewise calls children to hear a father's discipline and retain his teaching.

Sirach 3:2
SOURCE: For the Lord has honored a father over his children and confirmed a mother’s authority over her sons.
TARGET Exodus 20:12:
Exodus 20:12: Honour your father and your mother, that it may be well with you, and that your days may be long upon the good land which the Lord your God gives you.
RELATIONSHIP: Command to honor parents
RATIONALE: Sirach grounds parental honor and authority in the Lord; Exodus commands honor for both father and mother. Both present parental honor as a divine obligation.

Sirach 3:3
SOURCE: The one who honors a father atones for sins,
TARGET Sirach 3:14:
Sirach 3:14: For mercy toward a father will not be forgotten; in place of sins it will be built up for you.
RELATIONSHIP: Same-chapter filial-mercy parallel
RATIONALE: Honoring a father is connected with atonement here; verse 14 develops this through mercy toward a father being remembered in place of sins. The two statements frame filial care's moral significance.

Sirach 3:4
SOURCE: and the one who glorifies his mother is like someone storing up treasure.
TARGET Sirach 7:27-28:
Sirach 7:27: Glorify your father with your whole heart, and do not forget your mother’s labor pains.
Sirach 7:28: Remember that through them you were born; what will you repay them to match what they have given you?
RELATIONSHIP: Filial honor parallel
RATIONALE: The mother's honor is valued like stored treasure here; chapter 7 commands remembrance of her labor pains and asks what repayment could match the parents' gift. Both stress the weight of filial gratitude.

Sirach 3:6
SOURCE: The one who glorifies a father will have length of days, and the one who obeys the Lord will give his mother rest;
TARGET Exodus 20:12:
Exodus 20:12: Honour your father and your mother, that it may be well with you, and that your days may be long upon the good land which the Lord your God gives you.
RELATIONSHIP: Parental honor and long life
RATIONALE: Sirach promises long days to one glorifying a father; Exodus attaches long days and well-being to honoring father and mother. The stated benefit closely corresponds.

Sirach 3:7
SOURCE: he will serve those who brought him into being as masters.
TARGET Colossians 3:20:
Colossians 3:20: Children, obey your parents in all things, for this is well pleasing in the Lord.
RELATIONSHIP: Filial obedience
RATIONALE: Sirach describes service to those who brought one into being; Colossians tells children to obey parents because this pleases the Lord. Both connect filial obedience with religious duty.

Sirach 3:8
SOURCE: Honor your father in deed and word, so that a blessing may come upon you from him.
TARGET Exodus 20:12:
Exodus 20:12: Honour your father and your mother, that it may be well with you, and that your days may be long upon the good land which the Lord your God gives you.
RELATIONSHIP: Parental honor and blessing
