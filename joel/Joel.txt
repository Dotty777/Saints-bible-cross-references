Joel 1:1
The word of the Lord that came to Joel, the son of Bathuel.
  Acts 2:16-17 — later express attribution of divine prophetic speech to Joel: The heading identifies the recipient of the Lord's word as Joel son of Bathuel. Peter introduces words spoken through the prophet Joel and quotes God's Spirit promise, matching Joel 3:1. That explicit quotation connects the book's named recipient with the later attribution; Acts does not repeat the father's name.

Joel 1:2
Hear these things, elders, and give ear, all who inhabit the land! Have such things happened in your days or in your fathers’ days?
  Exodus 10:6 — parallel appeal to ancestral experience in describing extraordinary calamity: Joel asks whether the elders or their fathers have experienced such things. Exodus announces locusts filling Egyptian houses as neither fathers nor forefathers have seen. Both use ancestral experience to express the calamity's extraordinary character, without identifying the two events.

Joel 1:3
Tell your children about these things, and let your children tell their children, and their children a further generation.
  Exodus 10:2 — parallel commands to transmit the report through successive generations: Joel commands telling children, their children and a further generation. God tells Moses to recount His dealings with Egypt and His signs to children and grandchildren. The common instruction preserves the memory of the events across generations; Exodus explicitly names Egypt and God's signs.

Joel 1:4
What the caterpillar left, the locust devoured; what the locust left, the bruchus locust devoured; what the bruchus locust left, the blight devoured.
  Exodus 10:5 — parallel successive destruction of what an earlier calamity left: Joel describes each named devourer consuming what its predecessor left. Exodus describes locusts consuming what the hail left and the trees upon the land. The repeated destruction of the remainder connects the passages, while Joel's four named agents differ from the hail-and-locust sequence.

Joel 1:5
Sober up, you drunkards, from their wine, and weep! Lament, all who drink wine to intoxication, because gladness and joy have been removed from your mouths.
  Isaiah 24:11 — close calls to lament over wine and the loss of gladness: Joel summons drunkards and wine drinkers to weeping and lament because gladness and joy have been removed from their mouths. Isaiah commands wailing over wine and says the earth's gladness has ceased. The shared reversal is from wine-associated gladness to lament; Joel does not here say that wine itself has been removed.

Joel 1:6
For a nation has come up against My land, strong and uncountable. Its teeth are a lion’s teeth, and its grinding teeth a young lion’s.
  Jeremiah 4:7 — parallel lion imagery for destructive invasion: Joel depicts the invading nation with a lion's teeth and a young lion's grinding teeth. Jeremiah describes a lion leaving its enclosure, destroying nations and making the land desolate. Both portray destructive invasion through lion imagery, with teeth specified in Joel and the lion's movement specified in Jeremiah.

Joel 1:7
It laid My vine waste and shattered My fig trees; searching, it searched it out and cast it away; it whitened its branches.
  Jeremiah 5:17 — parallel devastation of vines and fig cultivation by an invading nation: Joel's invading nation lays the vine waste and shatters the fig trees. Jeremiah's distant nation devours vineyards, fig orchards and olive groves along with other produce. Both name vines and figs among the invasion's agricultural losses; Joel additionally describes whitened branches.

Joel 1:8
Lament to Me more than a bride wrapped in sackcloth for her first husband.
  Jeremiah 6:26 — parallel sackcloth lament expressed through grief for a beloved person: Joel commands lament greater than that of a bride in sackcloth for her first husband. Jeremiah calls the daughter of the people to put on sackcloth and make mourning for a beloved one. The commands combine sackcloth with bereavement imagery; the bride and first husband are particular to Joel.

Joel 1:9
Sacrifice and drink offering have been removed from the Lord’s house. Mourn, priests who minister at the altar,

Hosea says they did not pour wine to the Lord, describes defiling sacrifices, and says their loaves will not enter His house. The connection concerns disrupted food-and-drink worship, while removal and defilement remain different descriptions.

Joel 1:10

  Haggai 1:10-11 — parallel judgment affecting grain, wine and oil: Joel describes suffering grain, dried wine and diminished oil. Haggai announces withheld dew and produce and a sword upon the land, grain, wine and oil. The same three agricultural staples appear within judgment on the land; Haggai's explicit sword is not replaced with a drought statement.

Joel 1:11

Jeremiah says the land's works failed for lack of rain and the farmers were ashamed and covered their heads. Agricultural failure affects the cultivators in both, while their stated conditions differ.

Joel 1:12

  Joel 2:22 — paired failure and renewed fruitfulness of vine and fig tree: The vine dries up and fig trees diminish in the lament. The later reassurance says the tree bore fruit and vine and fig tree gave their strength. These named plants connect the desolation with the announced renewal, without replacing this source's separate statement that people put joy to shame.

Joel 1:13

  Joel 1:9 — same-chapter expansion of the priests' mourning over removed offerings: Both units address ministers at the altar and connect their mourning with sacrifice and drink offering kept from God's house. This later summons adds wrapping themselves, beating their breasts and sleeping in sackcloth to the earlier instruction to mourn.

Joel 1:14

The repeated commands connect the communal responses while preserving the added participants.

Joel 1:15

Isaiah likewise commands wailing because the Lord's day is near and shattering will come from God. The shared warning does not replace Joel's stated comparison with Isaiah's explicit naming of God as the source of shattering.

Joel 1:16

Deuteronomy directs the people to bring offerings to the chosen place, eat before the Lord and rejoice. The loss reverses the conjunction of nourishment and glad worship described in the command.

Joel 1:18

Jeremiah describes deer abandoning their young for lack of pasture and wild donkeys whose eyes fail because there is no grass. Both extend the land's calamity to animals, while the species and their responses differ.

Joel 1:19

The advancing force is later preceded by consuming fire and followed by flame, leaving a desolate plain. The connection concerns the shared devastation imagery without specifying an unstated natural cause or identifying the force.

Joel 1:20

Both connect animal life with water under God's provision, while Joel describes its loss.

Joel 2:2

Zephaniah uses the same four atmospheric descriptions within its account of the Lord's day of wrath. Ezekiel's restoration makes devastated land like a garden of delight and restores the ruined cities. The same garden comparison marks opposite movements: devastation in Joel and renewal in Ezekiel.

The connection is the sound and movement of chariots in battle imagery; Joel presents a comparison rather than naming actual chariots in the force.

Joel 2:6

The connection concerns disciplined collective movement; Proverbs explicitly names locusts, while this Joel unit describes the force through fighters and soldiers.

Joel 2:13

The gathering and seated judgment connect the scenes; the named setting and Joel's round-about qualification remain distinct.

Joel 4:13
Send out sickles, because the vintage is at hand! Enter and tread, because the winepress is full; the vats overflow, because their evils have multiplied.
  Isaiah 63:2-3 — related winepress imagery for judgment on evil: Joel commands sending out sickles and treading the full winepress because evil has multiplied. Isaiah describes garments as from a trodden winepress, followed by trampling in wrath and blood brought to the earth. Both associate pressing and trampling with judgment, while the garments and explicit blood belong to Isaiah's description.

Joel 4:15
The sun and moon will grow dark, and the stars will sink their brightness.
  Joel 2:10 — repeated darkening of the sun, moon and stars: Both passages describe the sun and moon growing dark and the stars sinking their brightness. The earlier unit accompanies the advancing force with shaking earth and heaven; this later unit places the repeated celestial darkening in the judgment scene among the nations.

Joel 4:16
The Lord will cry out from Zion and give His voice from Jerusalem; heaven and earth will shake. But the Lord will spare His people, and the Lord will strengthen the sons of Israel.
  Amos 1:2 — close pairing of the Lord's voice from Zion and Jerusalem: Joel says the Lord will cry out from Zion and give His voice from Jerusalem. Amos pairs the same places with the Lord speaking and giving His voice. The resulting scenes differ: Joel describes cosmic shaking and care for His people, while Amos describes mourning pastures and Carmel's dried summit.

Joel 4:17
You will recognize that I am the Lord your God, dwelling in Zion on My holy mountain. Jerusalem will be a holy city, and foreigners will no longer pass through her.
  Zechariah 14:20-21 — related holiness of Jerusalem with an exclusion from sacred space: Joel describes Jerusalem as a holy city through which foreigners will no longer pass. Zechariah describes objects in Jerusalem and Judah as holy to the Lord and says no Canaanite will remain in His house. Holiness and exclusion connect the passages, while Zechariah's named figure and temple scope differ from Joel's city-wide statement.

Joel 4:18
On that day mountains will drip sweetness, hills will flow with milk, and all Judah’s water outlets will flow with water. A spring will come out of the Lord’s house and water the Wadi of Rushes.
  Ezekiel 47:1-2 — parallel water flowing out by the sanctuary: Joel promises a spring issuing from the Lord's house to water the Wadi of Rushes. Ezekiel sees water flowing beneath the house's open court and from its right side; the vision later states its origin in holy places at 47:12. The shared sanctuary flow does not establish that their named courses are the same.
  Amos 9:13 — close promise of mountains dripping sweetness: Both passages promise mountains dripping sweetness as part of renewed abundance. Joel adds hills flowing with milk and Judah's water outlets; Amos adds overlapping agricultural seasons and hills planted together. The precise mountain image is shared without replacing sweetness with a more specific substance.

Joel 4:19
Egypt will become desolate, and Idumea a desolate plain, because of injustices concerning the sons of Judah, because they poured out righteous blood in their land.
  Obadiah 1:10 — parallel punishment of Idumea for violence against Jacob's people: Joel says Egypt and Idumea will become desolate for injustice toward Judah's sons and righteous blood shed in the land. Obadiah announces shame and removal for slaughter and impiety toward brother Jacob, within its address to Idumea. Idumea's violence and ensuing judgment connect them; Joel also names Egypt.

Joel 4:20
But Judea will be inhabited forever, and Jerusalem for generations upon generations.
  Zechariah 14:11 — parallel promises of Jerusalem's future habitation: Joel promises Judea will be inhabited forever and Jerusalem through generations. Zechariah says people will dwell there, no accursed thing will remain, and Jerusalem will dwell confidently. Both promise inhabited Jerusalem; Joel specifies its duration, while Zechariah emphasizes security and removal of what is accursed.

Joel 4:21
“I will avenge their blood, and I will certainly not acquit.” And the Lord will dwell in Zion.
  Deuteronomy 32:43 — parallel divine vengeance for blood shed: Joel closes with God's promise to avenge blood and not acquit. Deuteronomy says He avenges the blood of His sons and repays His enemies, alongside cleansing His people's land. The explicit avenging of blood is the connection; Deuteronomy additionally names the victims as His sons.
  Zechariah 2:14 — shared assurance of the Lord dwelling in Zion: Joel's closing statement places the Lord dwelling in Zion. Zechariah tells daughter Zion to rejoice because the Lord is coming to dwell within her. The shared location and divine presence connect Joel's final assurance with Zechariah's invitation to joy.
