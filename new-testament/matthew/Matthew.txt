Matthew — Saints Holy Bible cross-references
195 completed source verses; 226 retained pairs; 201 source units read; 6 deferred units.
First unfinished: Matthew 1:14. Next new source review: Matthew 8:1.
Completed means an initial curated cross-reference pass: the complete supplied English source unit and each retained target passage were read, and each pair has a specific explanation. It does not mean exhaustive coverage or independent specialist approval.
Original Saints numbering is retained.

Matthew 1:1
A book of the origin of Jesus Christ, son of David, son of Abraham.
  Romans 1:3 — genealogy: Matthew introduces Jesus Christ as David's son; Romans likewise identifies God's Son as coming from David's seed according to the flesh. The shared claim concerns Davidic descent.
    Romans 1:3 concerning His Son, who came from David’s seed according to the flesh,
  Galatians 3:16 — promise and fulfillment: Matthew's title calls Christ Abraham's son. Galatians interprets the promises to Abraham's seed as concerning Christ, providing an explicit apostolic connection between Abraham's lineage and Christ.
    Galatians 3:16 Now the promises were spoken to Abraham and to his seed. It does not say, “And to seeds,” as concerning many, but as concerning one, “And to your seed,” who is Christ.

Matthew 1:2
Abraham begot Isaac; and Isaac begot Jacob; and Jacob begot Judah and his brothers;
  Genesis 25:19 — genealogy: Genesis explicitly says that Abraham begot Isaac, the first father-son relationship in Matthew's verse.
    Genesis 25:19 And these are the generations of Isaac, Abraham’s son: Abraham begot Isaac.
  Genesis 25:26 — genealogy: Genesis records Jacob's birth to Isaac and Rebekah, supplying the narrative behind Matthew's statement that Isaac begot Jacob.
    Genesis 25:26 And afterward his brother came out, and his hand held Esau’s heel; and he was named Jacob. And Isaac was sixty years old when Rebekah bore them.
  Genesis 29:35 — genealogy: Genesis describes Leah bearing Judah to Jacob, supplying the birth narrative for Matthew's Jacob-Judah link.
    Genesis 29:35 And she conceived again and bore a son, and said, “This time I will give thanks to the Lord.” Therefore she called his name Judah; and she ceased bearing.

Matthew 1:3
Judah begot Perez and Zerah by Tamar; Perez begot Hezron; Hezron begot Ram;
  Genesis 38:27–38:30 — genealogy: Genesis recounts the twins Perez and Zerah and the scarlet-thread incident. In the surrounding Tamar narrative this is the birth episode Matthew compresses into his identification of Judah's sons by Tamar.
    Genesis 38:27 And when she gave birth, behold, there were twins in her womb.
    Genesis 38:28 And as she was giving birth, one put out his hand; and the midwife took it and bound a scarlet thread upon his hand, saying, “This one shall come out first.”
    Genesis 38:29 But as he drew back his hand, immediately his brother came out; and she said, “Why has a barrier been broken through because of you?” And his name was called Perez.
    Genesis 38:30 And afterward his brother came out, upon whose hand was the scarlet thread; and his name was called Zerah.
  Ruth 4:18–4:19 — genealogy: Ruth's closing genealogy follows Perez to Hezron and then Arran; Matthew follows Perez to Hezron and Ram. The sequences are comparable while the edition's different name forms remain visible.
    Ruth 4:18 Now these are the generations of Perez: Perez begot Hezron,
    Ruth 4:19 and Hezron begot Arran, and Arran begot Amminadab,

Matthew 1:4
Ram begot Amminadab; Amminadab begot Nahshon; Nahshon begot Salmon;
  Ruth 4:19–4:20 — genealogy: Ruth follows Arran through Amminadab and Nahshon to Salman; Matthew gives Ram, Amminadab, Nahshon and Salmon. The overlapping succession supports comparison without silently replacing the Saints edition's name forms.
    Ruth 4:19 and Hezron begot Arran, and Arran begot Amminadab,
    Ruth 4:20 and Amminadab begot Nahshon, and Nahshon begot Salman,

Matthew 1:5
Salmon begot Boaz by Rahab; Boaz begot Obed by Ruth; Obed begot Jesse;
  Ruth 4:13–4:17 — genealogy: Ruth narrates Boaz taking Ruth as wife and her bearing the child subsequently named Obed, whose descendants are Jesse and David. Matthew compresses those relationships into the genealogy; Ruth does not identify Rahab as Boaz's mother.
    Ruth 4:13 Boaz took Ruth, and she became his wife. He went in to her, and the Lord gave her conception, and she bore a son.
    Ruth 4:14 The women said to Naomi, “Blessed be the Lord, who has not left you without the near kinsman today; and may he call your name in Israel.
    Ruth 4:15 He shall be a restorer of life to you and a nourisher of your old age, for your daughter-in-law who loved you has borne him, and she is better to you than seven sons.”
    Ruth 4:16 And Naomi took the child and laid him in her bosom, and became his nurse.
    Ruth 4:17 The neighbouring women gave him a name, saying, “A son is born to Naomi.” They called his name Obed. He was the father of Jesse, the father of David.
  Ruth 4:21–4:22 — genealogy: Both genealogies connect Salman/Salmon, Boaz, Obed and Jesse in succession. The Ruth passage also continues to David, but does not independently supply Matthew's statement about Rahab.
    Ruth 4:21 and Salman begot Boaz, and Boaz begot Obed,
    Ruth 4:22 and Obed begot Jesse, and Jesse begot David.

Matthew 1:6
Jesse begot David the king. David begot Solomon by the wife of Uriah;
  Ruth 4:22 — genealogy: Ruth's final verse explicitly says Jesse begot David, the first relationship stated in Matthew's verse.
    Ruth 4:22 and Obed begot Jesse, and Jesse begot David.
  2 Samuel 12:24 — genealogy: Samuel narrates David and Bathsheba having Solomon, corresponding to Matthew's David-Solomon link. Matthew calls the mother the wife of Uriah, while this Samuel verse names her Bathsheba and calls her David's wife.
    2 Samuel 12:24 David comforted Bathsheba his wife. He went in to her and lay with her. She conceived, bore a son, and named him Solomon. The Lord loved him.

Matthew 1:7
Solomon begot Rehoboam; Rehoboam begot Abijah; Abijah begot Asaph;
  1 Chronicles 3:10 — genealogy: Chronicles gives Solomon, Rehoboam, Abia and Asa in the royal succession. Matthew has Solomon, Rehoboam, Abijah and Asaph: the overlapping sequence is clear, but the Asa/Asaph wording difference is retained and not treated as an exact name match.
    1 Chronicles 3:10 Solomon’s sons: Rehoboam; Abia his son, Asa his son, Jehoshaphat his son,

Matthew 1:8
Asaph begot Jehoshaphat; Jehoshaphat begot Joram; Joram begot Uzziah;
  1 Chronicles 3:10–3:12 — genealogy: Chronicles supplies the succession Asa, Jehoshaphat, Joram, Ahaziah, Joash, Amasias, Azariah and Joathan. Compared with Matthew's Asaph, Jehoshaphat, Joram and Uzziah, it exposes both a name-form difference and Matthew's compressed generations; it does not make every Matthew link an immediate father-son relationship.
    1 Chronicles 3:10 Solomon’s sons: Rehoboam; Abia his son, Asa his son, Jehoshaphat his son,
    1 Chronicles 3:11 Joram his son, Ahaziah his son, Joash his son,
    1 Chronicles 3:12 Amasias his son, Azariah his son, Joathan his son,

Matthew 1:9
Uzziah begot Jotham; Jotham begot Ahaz; Ahaz begot Hezekiah;
  1 Chronicles 3:12–3:13 — genealogy: Chronicles names Azariah, Joathan, Ahaz and Hezekiah, the royal succession corresponding to Matthew's Uzziah, Jotham, Ahaz and Hezekiah. The different name forms are shown rather than harmonized in the source text.
    1 Chronicles 3:12 Amasias his son, Azariah his son, Joathan his son,
    1 Chronicles 3:13 Ahaz his son, Hezekiah his son, Manasseh his son,

Matthew 1:10
Hezekiah begot Manasseh; Manasseh begot Amos; Amos begot Josiah;
  1 Chronicles 3:13–3:14 — genealogy: Chronicles continues through Hezekiah, Manasseh, Amon and Josiah. Matthew gives Hezekiah, Manasseh, Amos and Josiah; the Amon/Amos difference remains explicit while the surrounding royal sequence agrees.
    1 Chronicles 3:13 Ahaz his son, Hezekiah his son, Manasseh his son,
    1 Chronicles 3:14 Amon his son, Josiah his son.

Matthew 1:11
Josiah begot Jechoniah and his brothers at the deportation to Babylon.
  1 Chronicles 3:15–3:16 — genealogy: Chronicles places Joakim among Josiah's sons and Jechonias in Joakim's line. This is a useful comparison with Matthew's compressed Josiah-Jechoniah transition; Chronicles includes the intervening generation and does not corroborate an immediate father-son link between those two names.
    1 Chronicles 3:15 Josiah’s sons: Joanan the firstborn, Joakim the second, Sedekias the third, Salloum the fourth.
    1 Chronicles 3:16 Joakim’s sons: Jechonias his son, Sedekias his son.

Matthew 1:12
After the deportation to Babylon, Jechoniah begot Shealtiel; Shealtiel begot Zerubbabel;
  1 Chronicles 3:17–3:19 — genealogy: The Saints Chronicles record places Salathiel among Jechonia-asir's sons and lists Zerubbabel among Salathiel's sons. This parallels Matthew's Jechoniah, Shealtiel and Zerubbabel sequence while retaining the edition's own spelling and parentage text.
    1 Chronicles 3:17 The sons of Jechonia-asir: Salathiel his son,
    1 Chronicles 3:18 Melchiram, Phadaias, Sanesar, Jekemia, Osamo, and Denethi.
    1 Chronicles 3:19 Salathiel’s sons: Zerubbabel and Semei. Zerubbabel’s sons: Mosollamos and Ananias, and Salomith their sister,
  Luke 3:27 — genealogy: Luke also places Zerubbabel next to Shealtiel, but continues to Neri rather than Matthew's Jechoniah. The link records the shared names and the differing ancestry instead of claiming that the two genealogies are identical.
    Luke 3:27 of Joanan, of Rhesa, of Zerubbabel, of Shealtiel, of Neri,

Matthew 1:13
Zerubbabel begot Abiud; Abiud begot Eliakim; Eliakim begot Azor;
  Luke 3:27 — genealogy: Both genealogies include Zerubbabel. Matthew follows him toward Abiud, Eliakim and Azor, whereas Luke's line toward Jesus goes through Rhesa and Joanan. This is a comparison at the shared ancestor, not independent confirmation of Matthew's three subsequent names.
    Luke 3:27 of Joanan, of Rhesa, of Zerubbabel, of Shealtiel, of Neri,

Matthew 1:16
and Jacob begot Joseph, the husband of Mary, of whom was born Jesus, who is called Christ.
  Luke 1:27 — narrative parallel: Both genealogies connect Jesus with Joseph while qualifying the connection: Matthew identifies Joseph as Mary's husband and says Jesus was born of Mary, while Luke says Jesus was Joseph's son as was supposed. Matthew calls Joseph's father Jacob while Luke names Heli; the difference is not resolved by this cross-reference.
    Luke 1:27 to a virgin betrothed to a man named Joseph, of David’s house. The virgin’s name was Mary.
  Luke 3:23 — genealogy: Both genealogies connect Jesus with Joseph while qualifying the connection: Matthew describes Joseph as Mary's husband, of whom Jesus was born, and Luke says Jesus was Joseph's son as was supposed. Matthew calls Joseph's father Jacob while Luke names Heli; the difference is not resolved by this cross-reference.
    Luke 3:23 Jesus Himself was about thirty years old when He began His ministry. He was the son, as was supposed, of Joseph, of Heli,

Matthew 1:17

Its inclusion of Ahaziah, Joash, Amasias and Joakim makes Matthew's selectivity visible; this passage does not independently verify Matthew's three numerical groups of fourteen.

Matthew 1:18
The origin of Jesus Christ was like this. When His mother Mary was betrothed to Joseph, before they came together she was found to be with child from the Holy Spirit.
  Luke 1:34–1:35 — narrative parallel: Matthew attributes Mary's pregnancy before marital relations to the Holy Spirit. Luke has Mary ask how conception will occur without knowing a man and has the angel answer that the Holy Spirit will come upon her.
    Luke 1:34 Mary said to the angel, “How will this be, since I do not know a man?”
    Luke 1:35 The angel answered her, “The Holy Spirit will come upon you, and the power of the Most High will overshadow you; and so the holy One to be born will be called Son of God.

Matthew 1:19
Joseph her husband, being righteous and unwilling to expose her to public disgrace, decided to divorce her quietly.
  Deuteronomy 24:1 — thematic parallel: Joseph's proposed divorce belongs to the legal world in which Deuteronomy describes a husband giving a wife a divorce certificate and sending her away. The law supplies divorce background; it does not narrate Joseph's case or prescribe his plan to act quietly.
    Deuteronomy 24:1 And if a man takes a wife and dwells with her as husband, and she finds no favour in his sight, because he has found some unseemly thing in her, and he writes her a certificate of divorce, and gives it into her hands, and sends her forth out of his house,

Matthew 1:20
As he considered these things, behold, an angel of the Lord appeared to him in a dream and said, “Joseph, son of David, do not fear to take Mary your wife home, for what has been conceived in her is from the Holy Spirit.
  Luke 1:35 — narrative parallel: The angel's assurance to Joseph that Mary's conception is from the Holy Spirit corresponds to Gabriel's explanation to Mary that the Holy Spirit will come upon her. These are announcements to different recipients about the same conception, not the same reported angelic encounter.
    Luke 1:35 The angel answered her, “The Holy Spirit will come upon you, and the power of the Most High will overshadow you; and so the holy One to be born will be called Son of God.

Matthew 1:21
She shall bear a Son, and you shall call His name Jesus, for He shall save His people from their sins.”
  Luke 1:31 — narrative parallel: Both angelic announcements direct that Mary's Son be named Jesus. Matthew addresses Joseph and explains the name through salvation from sins; Luke's instruction is addressed to Mary.
    Luke 1:31 And behold, you will conceive in your womb and bear a Son, and you shall name Him Jesus.
  Luke 1:76–1:77 — thematic parallel: Matthew explains Jesus' mission as saving His people from their sins. Zechariah's prophecy describes the child's preparation of the Lord's way as giving His people knowledge of salvation in the forgiveness of their sins; the shared connection is salvation and forgiveness, while the child addressed in Luke is John.
    Luke 1:76 And you also, child, will be called prophet of the Most High, for you will go before the Lord to prepare His ways,
    Luke 1:77 to give His people knowledge of salvation in the forgiveness of their sins,

Matthew 1:22
All this has happened so that what was spoken by the Lord through the prophet might be fulfilled:
  Isaiah 7:14 — promise and fulfillment: Matthew's fulfillment introduction leads directly into the following virgin-and-Emmanuel quotation. Isaiah supplies that identifiable oracle; the connection depends on reading Matthew's introduction together with its continuation in 1:23.
    Isaiah 7:14 Therefore the Lord Himself will give you a sign: behold, the virgin will conceive and bear a son, and you will call his name Emmanuel.

Matthew 1:23
“Behold, the virgin shall conceive and bear a son, and they shall call His name Emmanuel”; which means “God with us.”
  Isaiah 7:14 — quotation: Matthew quotes Isaiah's virgin conceiving and bearing a son named Emmanuel and adds the explanation 'God with us.' The Saints Isaiah text uses 'you will call his name' where Matthew has 'they shall call His name'; the supplied wording is preserved.
    Isaiah 7:14 Therefore the Lord Himself will give you a sign: behold, the virgin will conceive and bear a son, and you will call his name Emmanuel.
  Matthew 28:20 — thematic parallel: Within Matthew itself, the opening Emmanuel explanation, 'God with us,' corresponds to Jesus' closing assurance that He is with His disciples all the days. This is an internal literary and theological correspondence, not a second independent birth account.
    Matthew 28:20 teaching them to keep all that I commanded you; and behold, I am with you all the days, until the consummation of the age.”

Matthew 1:24
When Joseph awoke from sleep, he did as the angel of the Lord commanded him and took his wife home.
  Matthew 2:13–2:14 — narrative parallel: Matthew later repeats Joseph's response to an angelic dream: an instruction concerning the Child and His mother is followed by Joseph rising and carrying it out. This same-book narrative parallel connects Joseph's two acts of obedience without treating the dreams as one event.
    Matthew 2:13 After they withdrew, behold, an angel of the Lord appeared to Joseph in a dream, saying, “Rise, take the Child and His mother, and flee to Egypt. Stay there until I tell you, for Herod is about to seek the Child to destroy Him.”
    Matthew 2:14 He rose, took the Child and His mother by night, and withdrew to Egypt.

Matthew 1:25
He did not have relations with her until she bore a Son; and he called His name Jesus.
  Luke 2:21 — narrative parallel: Both accounts report the actual giving of the name Jesus in accordance with an angel's prior instruction. Luke supplies the naming at circumcision; it does not independently state Matthew's qualification about Joseph's relations with Mary.
    Luke 2:21 When eight days were completed for His circumcision, He was named Jesus, the name given by the angel before He was conceived in the womb.

Matthew 2:1
After Jesus was born in Bethlehem of Judea in the days of King Herod, behold, magi from the east arrived in Jerusalem,
  Luke 2:4–2:7 — narrative parallel: Luke locates Joseph and Mary in Bethlehem when Mary bears her Son, corresponding to Matthew's statement that Jesus was born in Bethlehem of Judea. Luke does not describe the magi in this passage.
    Luke 2:4 Joseph also went up from Galilee, from the city of Nazareth, into Judea, to David’s city, which is called Bethlehem, because he belonged to David’s house and family,
    Luke 2:5 to be registered with Mary, who was betrothed to him and was pregnant.
    Luke 2:6 While they were there, the time came for her to give birth.
    Luke 2:7 She bore her firstborn Son, wrapped Him in swaddling cloths, and laid Him in a manger, because there was no place for them in the lodging place.

Matthew 2:2
saying, “Where is the One born King of the Jews? For we saw His star at its rising and have come to bow before Him.”
  Numbers 24:17 — allusion: Balaam's oracle joins a rising star from Jacob with a ruler arising from Israel. Matthew's star and newborn King invite a royal-star comparison; Matthew does not explicitly quote the oracle, and Numbers does not describe the magi's astronomical observation.
    Numbers 24:17 I will show him, but not now; I pronounce him blessed, but he does not draw near. A star shall rise from Jacob, and a man shall arise from Israel. He shall crush Moab's leaders and plunder all the sons of Seth.

Matthew 2:3
When King Herod heard this, he was troubled, and all Jerusalem with him.
  Matthew 2:16 — narrative parallel: Within this same chapter, Herod's alarm at the report of a newborn king develops into his angry order to kill Bethlehem's boys. This is an internal narrative development of the same threat, not independent confirmation of Herod's reaction.
    Matthew 2:16 Then Herod, seeing that the magi had outwitted him, became exceedingly angry. He sent men and killed all the boys in Bethlehem and throughout its district who were two years old and under, according to the exact time he had learned from the magi.

Matthew 2:4
He gathered all the chief priests and scribes of the people and asked them where the Christ was to be born.
  John 7:41–7:42 — thematic parallel: Herod asks the religious authorities where the Christ is to be born. John's crowd likewise debates the Christ's origin and appeals to Scripture for Davidic descent and Bethlehem; the passages share the specific scriptural birthplace question, not the same conversation.
    John 7:41 Others said, “This is the Christ.” But some said, “Surely the Christ does not come from Galilee?
    John 7:42 Has not the Scripture said that the Christ comes from the offspring of David and from Bethlehem, the village where David was?”

Matthew 2:5
They told him, “In Bethlehem of Judea, for this is what has been written through the prophet:
  Micah 5:1 — quotation: The authorities identify Bethlehem as the birthplace on the basis of a prophet. The following quotation is grounded in Micah's Bethlehem ruler oracle, numbered 5:1 in this Saints edition, rather than silently using another edition's 5:2.
    Micah 5:1 And you, Bethlehem, house of Ephrathah, are small to be among Judah’s thousands; from you one will come out for Me to be a ruler in Israel, and his goings out are from the beginning, from days of old.

Matthew 2:6
‘And you, Bethlehem, land of Judah, are by no means least among the rulers of Judah; for out of you will come a ruler who will shepherd My people Israel.’”
  Micah 5:1 — quotation: Both passages identify Bethlehem in Judah as the place from which Israel's ruler comes. Matthew's 'by no means least' reformulates Micah's description of Bethlehem as small, and Matthew's shepherd wording extends beyond this one Micah verse.
    Micah 5:1 And you, Bethlehem, house of Ephrathah, are small to be among Judah’s thousands; from you one will come out for Me to be a ruler in Israel, and his goings out are from the beginning, from days of old.
  2 Samuel 5:2 — allusion: Matthew's ruler 'who will shepherd My people Israel' closely parallels the Lord's commission of David to shepherd His people Israel and be ruler over Israel in Samuel. It contributes the Davidic shepherd-ruler language alongside the Bethlehem oracle.
    2 Samuel 5:2 In days past, when Saul was king over us, you were the one who led Israel out and brought them in. The Lord said to you, ‘You will shepherd My people Israel, and you will be ruler over Israel.’”

Matthew 2:7
Then Herod secretly called the magi and learned from them the exact time when the star appeared.
  Matthew 2:16 — narrative parallel: The same chapter explicitly says Herod's age limit for the killings was set according to the exact time learned from the magi. That later statement explains the narrative significance of this secret inquiry; it is an internal causal link.
    Matthew 2:16 Then Herod, seeing that the magi had outwitted him, became exceedingly angry. He sent men and killed all the boys in Bethlehem and throughout its district who were two years old and under, according to the exact time he had learned from the magi.

Matthew 2:8
He sent them to Bethlehem, saying, “Go and inquire carefully about the Child. When you find Him, report to me, so that I too may come and bow before Him.”
  Matthew 2:16 — narrative parallel: Herod says he wishes to bow before the Child, but his subsequent order to kill the boys exposes the hostile purpose of his inquiry. This same-chapter comparison identifies the contrast between his stated pretext and his later action.
    Matthew 2:16 Then Herod, seeing that the magi had outwitted him, became exceedingly angry. He sent men and killed all the boys in Bethlehem and throughout its district who were two years old and under, according to the exact time he had learned from the magi.

Matthew 2:9
After hearing the king, they went on their way. And behold, the star they had seen at its rising went ahead of them until it came and stood over the place where the Child was.
  Matthew 2:2 — narrative parallel: The magi identify the guiding star as the star they had already seen at its rising. The same-chapter reference to their earlier report establishes the recurrence of this particular sign; it supplies no independent account of the star's motion.
    Matthew 2:2 saying, “Where is the One born King of the Jews? For we saw His star at its rising and have come to bow before Him.”

Matthew 2:10
When they saw the star, they rejoiced with exceedingly great joy.
  Luke 2:10–2:11 — thematic parallel: The magi's exceedingly great joy at the sign of the Child corresponds to Luke's announcement of great joy because the Savior, Christ the Lord, has been born. The observers and signs differ, so this is a shared response to Jesus' birth rather than a merged visit.
    Luke 2:10 The angel said to them, “Do not fear, for behold, I announce to you good news of great joy, which will be for all the people:
    Luke 2:11 today in David’s city a Savior has been born for you, who is Christ the Lord.

Matthew 2:11
They entered the house and saw the Child with Mary His mother. Falling down, they bowed before Him; and opening their treasures, they offered Him gifts: gold, frankincense, and myrrh.
  Isaiah 60:5–60:6 — allusion: Isaiah pictures wealth coming from the nations and visitors carrying gold and frankincense. Matthew's visitors offer gold and frankincense to the Child, with myrrh added. The shared gift imagery supports an allusion, while Isaiah's address to Jerusalem is not erased.
    Isaiah 60:5 Then you will see and fear and be astonished in heart, for the wealth of sea, nations, and peoples will turn toward you. There will come to you
    Isaiah 60:6 herds of camels, and camels from Madiam and Gaipha will cover you. They will all come from Saba, bringing gold and carrying frankincense, and they will proclaim the Lord’s salvation as good news.
  Psalms 71:10–71:11 — thematic parallel: The Greek-numbered royal Psalm 71 pictures foreign kings bringing gifts and bowing before the king. Matthew's magi bow and offer treasures to the Child; Matthew calls them magi, and this comparison does not turn them into named or numbered kings.
    Psalms 71:10 kings of Tarshish and the islands will offer gifts; kings of the Arabians and Saba will bring gifts.
    Psalms 71:11 And all kings will bow before him; all nations will serve him.

Matthew 2:12
Having been warned in a dream not to return to Herod, they withdrew to their country by another way.
  Matthew 2:22 — narrative parallel: Matthew twice has a dream warning redirect a journey away from danger associated with Herod's dynasty: the magi avoid Herod here, and Joseph avoids Judea under Archelaus later. This is a same-book repeated protection motif involving different recipients.
    Matthew 2:22 But hearing that Archelaus reigned over Judea in place of his father Herod, he feared to go there. Warned in a dream, he withdrew to the regions of Galilee.

Matthew 2:13
After they withdrew, behold, an angel of the Lord appeared to Joseph in a dream, saying, “Rise, take the Child and His mother, and flee to Egypt. Stay there until I tell you, for Herod is about to seek the Child to destroy Him.”
  Exodus 1:16–1:22 — narrative parallel: Herod's threat to destroy the Child recalls Pharaoh's orders to kill Hebrew boys. This is a comparison of royal threats against male children, not a claim that the two rulers or historical events are identical.
    Exodus 1:16 And he said, “When you attend the Hebrew women in childbirth, and they are about to give birth, if the child is male, kill him; but if the child is female, preserve her.”
    Exodus 1:17 But the midwives feared God. They did not do as the king of Egypt had commanded them, and they kept the boys alive.
    Exodus 1:18 And the king of Egypt called the midwives and said to them, “Why have you done this thing, and kept the male children alive?”
    Exodus 1:19 The midwives said to Pharaoh, “The Hebrew women are not like the Egyptian women, for they give birth before the midwives come to them.” And they bore children.
    Exodus 1:20 God dealt well with the midwives, and the people multiplied and became very strong.
    Exodus 1:21 And because the midwives feared God, they made houses for themselves.
    Exodus 1:22 And Pharaoh commanded all his people, saying, “Cast every male child born to the Hebrews into the river, but keep every female child alive.”

Matthew 2:14
He rose, took the Child and His mother by night, and withdrew to Egypt.
  Genesis 46:2–46:4 — narrative parallel: Joseph takes his family into Egypt in response to the preceding dream instruction. Genesis similarly places Jacob's descent into Egypt under a divine night vision and a promise of return. The narratives concern different families and circumstances; their shared feature is a divinely directed family descent into Egypt.
    Genesis 46:2 And God spoke to Israel in a vision of the night, saying, “Jacob, Jacob.” And he said, “What is it?”
    Genesis 46:3 And He said, “I am the God of your fathers. Do not fear to go down into Egypt, for I will make you a great nation there.
    Genesis 46:4 “And I will go down with you into Egypt, and I will bring you up at last; and Joseph shall lay his hands upon your eyes.”

Matthew 2:15
He remained there until Herod’s death, so that what was spoken by the Lord through the prophet might be fulfilled: “Out of Egypt I called My Son.”
  Hosea 11:1 — quotation: Matthew applies the Egypt-call saying to Jesus' return in its fulfillment account. The pinned Saints Hosea text speaks of Israel as an infant and says 'out of Egypt I summoned his children,' whereas Matthew has 'I called My Son.' This is an identifiable quotation relationship with a real wording difference and Matthew's typological application, not an exact English match.
    Hosea 11:1 Because Israel was an infant, I loved him, and out of Egypt I summoned his children.

Matthew 2:16
Then Herod, seeing that the magi had outwitted him, became exceedingly angry. He sent men and killed all the boys in Bethlehem and throughout its district who were two years old and under, according to the exact time he had learned from the magi.
