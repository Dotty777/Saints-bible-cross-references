Ephesians 1:1
Paul, an apostle of Christ Jesus through the will of God, to the saints who are [in Ephesus] and faithful in Christ Jesus:
  2 Corinthians 1:1 — salutation parallel: Both introduce Paul as Christ Jesus' apostle through God's will and address saints. Corinthians names Timothy and its Corinth/Achaia recipients; Ephesians has no named co-sender and preserves the original brackets around in Ephesus.
    2 Corinthians 1:1 Paul, an apostle of Christ Jesus through the will of God, and Timothy the brother, to the Church of God in Corinth, together with all the saints in the whole of Achaia:

Ephesians 1:2
grace to you and peace from God our Father and the Lord Jesus Christ.
  Galatians 1:3 — salutation parallel: Both give grace and peace from God our Father and the Lord Jesus Christ. This is a shared greeting formula addressed to different communities, not an additional event.
    Galatians 1:3 grace to you and peace from God our Father and the Lord Jesus Christ,

Ephesians 1:3
Blessed be the God and Father of our Lord Jesus Christ, who blessed us with every spiritual blessing in the heavenly places in Christ,
  1 Peter 1:3 — doxological parallel: Both bless the God and Father of our Lord Jesus Christ. Ephesians names every spiritual blessing in the heavenly places in Christ; Peter's letter names merciful new birth and living hope through Jesus' resurrection.
    1 Peter 1:3 Blessed be the God and Father of our Lord Jesus Christ, who, according to His great mercy, has given us new birth into a living hope through the resurrection of Jesus Christ from the dead,

Ephesians 1:4
just as He chose us in Him before the foundation of the world, so that we should be holy and without blemish before Him in love,
  2 Timothy 1:9 — teaching parallel: Both connect divine purpose in Christ with a holy calling or life and a time before the present ages. Ephesians says chosen before the world's foundation; Timothy's letter names purpose and grace before the ages. The temporal expressions remain distinct.
    2 Timothy 1:9 who saved us and called us with a holy calling, not according to our works but according to His own purpose and grace, which was given to us in Christ Jesus before the times of the ages,

Ephesians 1:5
having predestined us for adoption as sons through Jesus Christ to Himself, according to the good pleasure of His will,
  Galatians 4:4–4:6 — teaching parallel: Both relate adoption as sons to God's action through Jesus Christ. Galatians describes sending and redeeming through the Son and then the Spirit's cry; Ephesians emphasizes predestination according to God's good pleasure rather than repeating that sequence.
    Galatians 4:4 But when the fullness of time came, God sent forth His Son, born of a woman, born under law,
    Galatians 4:5 to redeem those under law, that we might receive adoption as sons.
    Galatians 4:6 And because you are sons, God sent forth the Spirit of His Son into our hearts, crying, “Abba, Father!”

Ephesians 1:6
to the praise of the glory of His grace, with which He favored us in the Beloved.
  Ephesians 1:12 — internal doxological parallel: The praise-of-His-glory refrain recurs after the inheritance and prior-hope statements. Here it specifically names the glory of His grace and favor in the Beloved. This is an internal doxological pattern, not an identical additional benefit.
    Ephesians 1:12 so that we who had hoped beforehand in Christ should be to the praise of His glory.

Ephesians 1:7
In Him we have redemption through His blood, the forgiveness of transgressions, according to the riches of His grace,
  Colossians 1:14 — teaching parallel: Both join redemption with forgiveness. Ephesians says transgressions, through His blood and according to grace's riches; the actual Colossians unit says sins and does not include an added through-His-blood clause.
    Colossians 1:14 in whom we have redemption, the forgiveness of sins.

Ephesians 1:8
which He lavished on us in all wisdom and understanding,
  Colossians 1:9 — teaching parallel: Ephesians describes grace lavished in wisdom and understanding; Colossians prays for knowledge of God's will in spiritual wisdom and understanding. The related intellectual vocabulary belongs to a declaration in one and a petition in the other.
    Colossians 1:9 For this reason we too, since the day we heard, have not ceased praying for you and asking that you may be filled with the knowledge of His will in all spiritual wisdom and understanding,

Ephesians 1:9
having made known to us the mystery of His will, according to His good pleasure which He purposed in Him,
  Colossians 1:26–1:27 — teaching parallel: Both describe a divine mystery made known to believers. Ephesians names God's will and good pleasure; Colossians describes prior hiding, present disclosure and Christ in you as hope of glory. Their stated content and emphasis remain distinct.
    Colossians 1:26 the mystery hidden from the ages and from the generations, but now revealed to His saints,
    Colossians 1:27 to whom God willed to make known what are the riches of the glory of this mystery among the nations, which is Christ in you, the hope of glory.

Ephesians 1:10
for an administration of the fullness of the times, to sum up all things in Christ, the things in the heavens and the things on the earth, in Him.
  Colossians 1:20 — teaching parallel: Both place all things in the heavens and on earth in relation to Christ. Ephesians speaks of summing them up in Him at the fullness of times; Colossians speaks of reconciliation and peace through the cross's blood. The actions are not made identical.
    Colossians 1:20 and through Him to reconcile all things to Himself, having made peace through the blood of His cross, [through Him], whether the things upon the earth or the things in the heavens.

Ephesians 1:11
In Him we were also allotted an inheritance, having been predestined according to the purpose of the One who works all things according to the counsel of His will,
  Colossians 1:12 — teaching parallel: Both describe an inheritance connected with divine provision. Ephesians names being allotted an inheritance according to predestining purpose; Colossians thanks the Father for enabling a share in the saints' inheritance in light. The formulations differ.
    Colossians 1:12 giving thanks to the Father who made you fit for a share of the inheritance of the saints in the light,

Ephesians 1:12
so that we who had hoped beforehand in Christ should be to the praise of His glory.
  Ephesians 1:6 — internal doxological parallel: Praise of His glory repeats the earlier refrain about the glory of His grace. Here it follows we who hoped beforehand in Christ. The comparison preserves the additional prior-hope wording without assigning the we group an unprinted ethnic identity.
    Ephesians 1:6 to the praise of the glory of His grace, with which He favored us in the Beloved.

Ephesians 1:13
In Him you also, having heard the word of truth, the gospel of your salvation, and having believed in Him, were sealed with the promised Holy Spirit,
  2 Corinthians 1:21–1:22 — teaching parallel: Both describe divine sealing and the Spirit. Corinthians also names establishing, anointing and the Spirit's pledge in hearts; Ephesians names hearing the gospel, believing and the promised Holy Spirit. Their recipients and expressed sequence remain distinct.
    2 Corinthians 1:21 Now the One who establishes us with you in Christ and anointed us is God,
    2 Corinthians 1:22 who also sealed us and gave the pledge of the Spirit in our hearts.

Ephesians 1:14
who is the pledge of our inheritance, toward the redemption of the possession, to the praise of His glory.
  2 Corinthians 5:5 — teaching parallel: Both name the Spirit as a pledge. Ephesians relates the pledge to inheritance and redemption of the possession; Corinthians relates it to God's preparing the speakers in the bodily-life argument. The accompanying images and objects are preserved.
    2 Corinthians 5:5 Now the One who prepared us for this very thing is God, who gave us the pledge of the Spirit.

Ephesians 1:15
For this reason I too, having heard of your faith in the Lord Jesus and your love toward all the saints,
  Colossians 1:4 — teaching parallel: Both report hearing of the readers' faith in Jesus and love toward all the saints. Ephesians names the Lord Jesus while Colossians names Christ Jesus; the correspondence does not identify their readerships as one congregation.
    Colossians 1:4 having heard of your faith in Christ Jesus and the love you have toward all the saints,

Ephesians 1:16
do not cease giving thanks for you, remembering you in my prayers,
  Colossians 1:3 — teaching parallel: Both join thanksgiving for the readers with continuing prayer. Ephesians uses the writer's first-person singular remembrance; Colossians describes we giving thanks and praying. The letter situations and pronouns remain distinct.
    Colossians 1:3 We thank God, the Father of our Lord Jesus Christ, always praying for you,

Ephesians 1:17
so that the God of our Lord Jesus Christ, the Father of glory, may give you a spirit of wisdom and revelation in the knowledge of Him,
  Colossians 1:9 — teaching parallel: Both prayers seek divinely given understanding. Ephesians asks the God of the Lord Jesus Christ, Father of glory, for a spirit of wisdom and revelation in knowing Him; Colossians asks for knowledge of His will in spiritual wisdom and understanding.
    Colossians 1:9 For this reason we too, since the day we heard, have not ceased praying for you and asking that you may be filled with the knowledge of His will in all spiritual wisdom and understanding,

Ephesians 1:18
with the eyes of your heart enlightened, so that you may know what is the hope of His calling, what are the riches of the glory of His inheritance among the saints,
  Romans 8:17–8:18 — teaching parallel: Both connect divine inheritance and hoped-for glory. Ephesians asks that the heart know the calling's hope and His inheritance's glory among the saints; Romans calls God's children heirs with Christ and anticipates revealed glory. The possessive wording and Romans’s suffering qualification remain distinct.
    Romans 8:17 And if children, then also heirs: heirs of God and joint heirs with Christ, if indeed we suffer with Him so that we may also be glorified with Him.
    Romans 8:18 For I reckon that the sufferings of the present time are not worth comparing with the glory that is to be revealed in us.

Ephesians 1:19
and what is the surpassing greatness of His power toward us who believe, according to the working of the might of His strength,
  Ephesians 3:20 — internal prayer parallel: The later doxology again names power working in us. Here the prayer concerns the surpassing greatness of God's power toward believers; later God's ability exceeds asking or thinking. This is a recurring internal power theme rather than a second miracle report.
    Ephesians 3:20 Now to the One who is able to do far more abundantly than all we ask or think, according to the power working in us,

Ephesians 1:20
which He exercised in Christ when He raised Him from the dead and seated Him at His right hand in the heavenly places,
  Romans 8:34 — teaching parallel: Both describe Christ raised from the dead and at God's right hand. Ephesians explicitly describes God exercising power and seating Him in heavenly places; Romans also names His death and intercession for us. Their additional claims remain distinct.
    Romans 8:34 Who is the one who condemns? Christ Jesus is the One who died—more than that, was raised—who is also at God’s right hand, who also intercedes for us.

Ephesians 1:21
far above every rule and authority and power and dominion and every name that is named, not only in this age but also in the one to come.
  Philippians 2:9–2:11 — teaching parallel: Both describe Christ's exaltation above every name. Philippians develops universal bending of knees and confession to God the Father's glory; Ephesians lists rule, authority, power and dominion across this age and the coming one. The lists and formulations differ.
    Philippians 2:9 Therefore God also highly exalted Him and graciously gave Him the name above every name,
    Philippians 2:10 so that at the name of Jesus every knee should bend, of those in heaven and on earth and under the earth,
    Philippians 2:11 and every tongue confess that Jesus Christ is Lord, to the glory of God the Father.

Ephesians 1:22
And He subjected all things under His feet and gave Him as head over all things to the Church,
  Psalms 8:7 — scriptural allusion: The all-things-under-His-feet clause corresponds to the Psalm's subjection formula. Ephesians applies it to Christ and adds His headship for the Church; that Church clause is not supplied as part of the Psalm's creation-language setting.
    Psalms 8:7 and didst appoint him over the works of Thy hands; Thou didst subject everything beneath his feet:

Ephesians 1:23
which is His body, the fullness of the One who fills all things in all.
  Colossians 1:18 — teaching parallel: Both explicitly call the Church Christ's body. Colossians names Him as its head and firstborn from the dead; Ephesians describes its fullness relation to the One who fills all things. The additional fullness wording is not silently inserted into the selected Colossians unit.
    Colossians 1:18 And He is the head of the body, the Church; He is the beginning, the firstborn from the dead, so that He Himself might have first place in all things,

Ephesians 2:1
And you were dead in your transgressions and sins,
  Colossians 2:13 — teaching parallel: Both describe the recipients as dead in transgressions. Colossians also names uncircumcision and being made alive with forgiveness; the Ephesians sentence develops its own former-life description before reaching made-alive language in2:5.
    Colossians 2:13 And you, who were dead in transgressions and the uncircumcision of your flesh, He made you alive together with Him, having forgiven us all the transgressions,

Ephesians 2:2
in which you once walked according to the age of this world, according to the ruler of the authority of the air, of the spirit now working in the sons of disobedience—
  2 Corinthians 4:4 — teaching parallel: Both describe hostile power associated with the age and disobedience or unbelief. Ephesians names the ruler of the air's authority and a working spirit; Corinthians names the god of this age blinding minds. The titles and actions are not treated as one verbatim formula.
    2 Corinthians 4:4 among whom the god of this age has blinded the minds of the unbelieving, so that the light of the good news of the glory of Christ, who is the image of God, might not shine upon them.

Ephesians 2:3
among whom we too all once lived in the desires of our flesh, carrying out the wishes of the flesh and of our thoughts, and we were by nature children of wrath, like the rest.
  Titus 3:3 — teaching parallel: Both look back on a former life directed by desires. Titus also lists deception, malice, envy and hatred; Ephesians speaks of flesh and thought and children of wrath by nature. The lists and the nature statement are not made identical.
    Titus 3:3 For we too were once foolish, disobedient, led astray, enslaved to various desires and pleasures, living in malice and envy, hateful, hating one another.

Ephesians 2:4
But God, being rich in mercy, because of His great love with which He loved us,
  Titus 3:4–3:5 — teaching parallel: Both locate salvation in divine mercy and love rather than the recipients' prior worthiness. Titus names God's kindness and love for humanity and saving mercy; Ephesians names God's abundant mercy and great love before its made-alive statement.
    Titus 3:4 But when the kindness of God our Savior and His love for humanity appeared,
    Titus 3:5 He saved us, not on the basis of works done by us in righteousness, but according to His mercy, through the washing of regeneration and renewal by the Holy Spirit,

Ephesians 2:5
even when we were dead in transgressions, made us alive together with Christ—by grace you have been saved—
  Colossians 2:13 — teaching parallel: Both speak of people dead in transgressions being made alive together with Christ. Ephesians explicitly adds by grace you have been saved; Colossians also describes forgiving all transgressions. The additional clauses stay in their own passages.
    Colossians 2:13 And you, who were dead in transgressions and the uncircumcision of your flesh, He made you alive together with Him, having forgiven us all the transgressions,

Ephesians 2:6
and raised us together and seated us together in the heavenly places in Christ Jesus,
  Colossians 3:1 — teaching parallel: Both refer to being raised together with Christ. Ephesians also says seated together in heavenly places in Christ; Colossians commands seeking what is above where Christ is seated at God's right hand. It does not explicitly say the readers are seated there in that unit.
    Colossians 3:1 If, then, you were raised together with Christ, seek the things above, where Christ is, seated at the right hand of God.

Ephesians 2:7
so that in the ages to come He might show the surpassing riches of His grace in kindness toward us in Christ Jesus.
  Ephesians 1:7 — internal thematic parallel: The riches-of-His-grace wording recurs. Earlier it describes redemption through Christ's blood and forgiveness; here the future ages display surpassing grace's riches in kindness in Christ. The present benefit and future display remain distinct.
    Ephesians 1:7 In Him we have redemption through His blood, the forgiveness of transgressions, according to the riches of His grace,

Ephesians 2:8
For by grace you have been saved through faith, and this is not from yourselves; it is the gift of God,
  Romans 3:24 — teaching parallel: Both attribute salvation or justification to divine grace. Romans names being justified freely by His grace through redemption in Christ; Ephesians says saved by grace through faith and adds that this is not from yourselves but God's gift. The terms justification and salvation remain distinct.
    Romans 3:24 being justified freely by His grace through the redemption that is in Christ Jesus,

Ephesians 2:9
not from works, so that no one may boast.
  Romans 3:27 — teaching parallel: Both exclude boasting in an argument about works and faith. Ephesians says not from works so no one boasts; Romans asks where boasting is and contrasts a law of works with a law of faith. The different law wording is preserved.
    Romans 3:27 Where, then, is boasting? It was excluded. Through what kind of law? Of works? No, but through a law of faith.

Ephesians 2:10
For we are His workmanship, created in Christ Jesus for good works, which God prepared beforehand, so that we should walk in them.
  Titus 2:14 — teaching parallel: Both describe a divinely formed or redeemed people directed toward good works. Titus names Christ's self-giving to purify a people zealous for them; Ephesians names God's workmanship and prior preparation of the works to walk in. The actions differ.
    Titus 2:14 who gave Himself for us, so that He might ransom us from all lawlessness and purify for Himself a people for His own possession, eager for good works.

Ephesians 2:11
Therefore remember that once you, the Gentiles in the flesh, called “uncircumcision” by what is called “circumcision,” made by hands in the flesh—
  Colossians 2:11 — teaching parallel: Both discuss circumcision and flesh or hands. Ephesians recalls Gentile labels and circumcision made by hands in flesh; Colossians describes circumcision not made by hands in Christ. The physical description and Christ-centered metaphor remain distinct.
    Colossians 2:11 in whom you were also circumcised with a circumcision not made by hands, in the stripping off of the body of the flesh, in the circumcision of Christ,

Ephesians 2:12
that at that time you were without Christ, alienated from the commonwealth of Israel and strangers to the covenants of the promise, having no hope and without God in the world.
  Romans 9:4 — teaching parallel: Israel, covenants and promise are explicitly connected in both. Romans lists Israelite privileges; Ephesians recalls Gentile alienation from Israel's commonwealth and promise covenants before the following nearness statement.
    Romans 9:4 who are Israelites. Theirs are the adoption as sons and the glory and the covenants and the giving of the law and the worship and the promises;

Ephesians 2:13
But now in Christ Jesus, you who once were far away have become near by the blood of Christ.
  Hebrews 10:19 — teaching parallel: Both relate access or nearness to Jesus' blood. Ephesians describes formerly distant recipients made near by Christ's blood; Hebrews describes confidence to enter the holy places by Jesus' blood. The spatial images and addressed circumstances remain distinct.
    Hebrews 10:19 Therefore, brothers, since we have confidence for entry into the holy places in the blood of Jesus,

Ephesians 2:14
For He Himself is our peace, who made both one and broke down the dividing wall of the barrier, the hostility, in His flesh,
  Colossians 1:20 — teaching parallel: Christ as our peace and the breaking of hostility correspond to making peace through the cross's blood in the reconciliation account. Ephesians focuses on both groups becoming one; Colossians names things on earth and in heaven. The scopes differ.
    Colossians 1:20 and through Him to reconcile all things to Himself, having made peace through the blood of His cross, [through Him], whether the things upon the earth or the things in the heavens.

Ephesians 2:15
having abolished the law of commandments in decrees, so that He might create in Him one new human being out of the two, making peace,
  Colossians 2:14 — teaching parallel: Both describe the removal of a barrier expressed through decrees. Ephesians names abolishing the law of commandments in decrees to create one new human being; Colossians names a hostile handwritten record removed at the cross. The law and record descriptions are not simply equated.
    Colossians 2:14 having wiped out the handwritten record with its decrees that was against us, which was opposed to us, and He has taken it out of the way, having nailed it to the cross.

Ephesians 2:16
and might reconcile both in one body to God through the cross, having killed the hostility by it.
  Colossians 1:22 — teaching parallel: Both join reconciliation with Christ's bodily death. Ephesians says both are reconciled in one body to God through the cross; Colossians says in His fleshly body through death, with holy presentation as its aim. The body formulations are kept distinct.
    Colossians 1:22 yet now He has reconciled you in the body of His flesh through death, to present you holy and without blemish and beyond accusation before Him,

Ephesians 2:17
And He came and proclaimed peace to you who were far away, and peace to those who were near,
  Isaiah 57:19 — scriptural allusion: The peace-to-far-and-near wording corresponds to Isaiah's peace saying. Ephesians presents Christ proclaiming it to the readers and those near; Isaiah's actual saying has its own Lord/healing context. The source is a scriptural echo rather than a supplied identical narrative event.
    Isaiah 57:19 peace upon peace to those far away and those near. And the Lord said, ‘I will heal them.’

Ephesians 2:18
because through Him we both have access in one Spirit to the Father.
  Romans 5:1–5:2 — teaching parallel: Both describe access through Jesus Christ. Ephesians explicitly says both have access in one Spirit to the Father; Romans joins access by faith into grace with peace toward God through the Lord Jesus Christ. The Spirit and faith formulations are not substituted for each other.
    Romans 5:1 Therefore, having been justified from faith, we have peace with God through our Lord Jesus Christ,
    Romans 5:2 through whom we have also obtained access by faith into this grace in which we stand; and we boast in hope of God’s glory.

Ephesians 2:19
So then you are no longer strangers and resident foreigners, but you are fellow citizens with the saints and members of God’s household,
  Galatians 6:10 — teaching parallel: Both describe a community as a household. Ephesians contrasts foreigner status with being fellow citizens and God's household; Galatians gives the household of faith particular attention in doing good. The images and immediate purposes remain distinct.
    Galatians 6:10 So then, as we have opportunity, let us do good to everyone, especially to those of the household of faith.

Ephesians 2:20
having been built upon the foundation of the apostles and prophets, Christ Jesus Himself being the cornerstone,
  Isaiah 28:16 — scriptural allusion: The cornerstone image corresponds to Isaiah's chosen, precious cornerstone laid in Zion's foundations. Ephesians names Christ Jesus as cornerstone within its apostles-and-prophets foundation image; Isaiah's actual trust-in-it wording and setting are preserved rather than silently rewritten as the whole Ephesians sentence.
    Isaiah 28:16 therefore thus says the Lord: “Behold, I will place in Zion’s foundations a costly, chosen, precious cornerstone, in her foundations, and whoever trusts in it will certainly not be ashamed.

Ephesians 2:21
in whom the whole building, fitted together, grows into a holy sanctuary in the Lord,
  1 Peter 2:5 — teaching parallel: Both picture believers being built into a holy community. Ephesians describes the fitted building growing into a holy sanctuary; Peter's letter describes living stones and a spiritual house with priesthood and sacrifices. The added priestly details are not inserted into Ephesians.
    1 Peter 2:5 you yourselves, like living stones, are also being built into a spiritual house, for a holy priesthood, to offer spiritual sacrifices acceptable to God through Jesus Christ.

Ephesians 2:22
in whom you also are being built together into a dwelling place of God in the Spirit.
  1 Corinthians 3:16 — teaching parallel: Both describe the community as God's dwelling through the Spirit. Corinthians calls the readers God's temple with the Spirit dwelling among them; Ephesians describes their being built together into God's dwelling in the Spirit. The construction emphasis is specific here.
    1 Corinthians 3:16 Do you not know that you are God’s temple and that God’s Spirit dwells among you?

Ephesians 3:1
For this reason I, Paul, the prisoner of Christ Jesus for you Gentiles—
  Philemon 1:1 — personal designation parallel: Both explicitly call Paul a prisoner of Christ Jesus. Ephesians adds for you Gentiles; Philemon names Timothy and its recipient. The shared designation does not identify a prison location or date.
    Philemon 1:1 Paul, a prisoner of Christ Jesus, and Timothy the brother, to Philemon, our beloved fellow worker,

Ephesians 3:2
if indeed you have heard of the stewardship of God’s grace that was given to me for you,
  Colossians 1:25 — teaching parallel: Both describe a stewardship from God given to the speaker for the recipients. Ephesians names the stewardship of grace; Colossians names service and fulfilling God's word. The related commission language is not one identical description of every task.
    Colossians 1:25 of which I became a servant according to the stewardship from God given to me for you, to fulfill the word of God,

Ephesians 3:3
that the mystery was made known to me by revelation, as I wrote briefly before.
  Galatians 1:11–1:12 — teaching parallel: Both ground Paul's received message in revelation rather than merely human instruction. Ephesians names the mystery made known by revelation; Galatians names the gospel and revelation of Jesus Christ. No particular vision or prior writing is identified by this comparison.
    Galatians 1:11 For I make known to you, brothers, that the gospel proclaimed by me is not of human origin.
    Galatians 1:12 For I neither received it from a human being nor was taught it, but received it through a revelation of Jesus Christ.

Ephesians 3:4
By reading this you can perceive my understanding of the mystery of Christ,
  Ephesians 3:6 — internal explanation: The later that-clause gives the expressed mystery's content: Gentile fellow heirs, body members and promise sharers in Christ through the gospel. This supplies concrete internal content for what the readers can perceive by reading, without identifying an unpreserved earlier document.
    Ephesians 3:6 that the Gentiles are fellow heirs and fellow members of the body and fellow sharers of the promise in Christ Jesus through the gospel,

Ephesians 3:5
which in other generations was not made known to the sons of human beings as it has now been revealed to His holy apostles and prophets in the Spirit:
  Romans 16:25–16:26 — teaching parallel: Both contrast earlier concealment with present disclosure of a mystery. Romans names prophetic writings and all nations; Ephesians names holy apostles and prophets in the Spirit and says not made known as now. Their temporal qualifications and means of disclosure remain distinct.
    Romans 16:25 [Now to the One able to strengthen you according to my gospel and the proclamation of Jesus Christ, according to the revelation of a mystery kept silent through long ages,
    Romans 16:26 but now revealed, and through prophetic writings, according to the command of the eternal God, made known to all the nations for the obedience of faith,

Ephesians 3:6
that the Gentiles are fellow heirs and fellow members of the body and fellow sharers of the promise in Christ Jesus through the gospel,
  Galatians 3:28–3:29 — teaching parallel: Both connect unity in Christ across Jew/Greek distinctions with inheritance and promise. Galatians names Abraham's seed and heirs according to promise; Ephesians names Gentile fellow heirs, body membership and sharing through the gospel. The additional categories remain in their own passages.
    Galatians 3:28 There is neither Jew nor Greek; there is neither slave nor free; there is no male and female, for you are all one in Christ Jesus.
    Galatians 3:29 And if you belong to Christ, then you are Abraham’s seed, heirs according to promise.

Ephesians 3:7
of which I became a servant according to the gift of God’s grace given to me according to the working of His power.
  1 Corinthians 15:10 — teaching parallel: Both attribute Paul's ministry to God's given grace and working. Corinthians describes labor with God's grace rather than himself alone; Ephesians calls him a servant by grace's gift and divine power. The roles and personal expressions differ.
    1 Corinthians 15:10 But by God’s grace I am what I am, and His grace toward me was not empty. Instead, I labored more than all of them—yet not I, but God’s grace with me.

Ephesians 3:8
To me, the very least of all saints, this grace was given: to proclaim to the nations the untraceable riches of Christ,
  1 Corinthians 15:9 — teaching parallel: Both combine Paul's ministry with a least designation. Corinthians calls him least of the apostles and mentions persecuting God's Church; Ephesians calls him least of all saints and commissioned to proclaim Christ's riches to the nations. Neither comparison substitutes apostles for saints.
    1 Corinthians 15:9 For I am the least of the apostles, unworthy to be called an apostle, because I persecuted God’s Church.

Ephesians 3:9
and to enlighten everyone about the administration of the mystery hidden from the ages in God, who created all things,
  Colossians 1:26–1:27 — teaching parallel: Both speak of a mystery hidden through ages and now made known. Colossians locates its riches among the nations and identifies Christ in you as hope of glory; Ephesians names its administration and God as creator. Their descriptions are not merged.
    Colossians 1:26 the mystery hidden from the ages and from the generations, but now revealed to His saints,
    Colossians 1:27 to whom God willed to make known what are the riches of the glory of this mystery among the nations, which is Christ in you, the hope of glory.

Ephesians 3:10
so that now, through the Church, the richly varied wisdom of God might be made known to the rulers and authorities in the heavenly places,
  1 Peter 1:12 — thematic parallel: Both connect revealed salvation with heavenly observers. Peter says angels desire to look into the proclaimed things; Ephesians says God's varied wisdom is made known through the Church to heavenly rulers and authorities. Angels and those authorities are not assigned identical activities or identities.
    1 Peter 1:12 It was revealed to them that they were serving these things for you, and not for themselves—things now announced to you through those who proclaimed the good news to you by the Holy Spirit sent from heaven, things into which angels long to look.

Ephesians 3:11
according to the purpose of the ages which He accomplished in Christ Jesus our Lord,
  2 Timothy 1:9 — teaching parallel: Both locate divine purpose in Christ with an ages-related horizon. Ephesians says the purpose of the ages accomplished in Christ Jesus; Timothy's letter names purpose and grace given before the times of the ages. The temporal and ministry statements differ.
