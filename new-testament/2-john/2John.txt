2 John 1:1
The elder, to the chosen lady and her children, whom I love in truth—and not I alone, but also all who have come to know the truth—
  3 John 1:1 — thematic parallel: Both openings name the elder as sender and speak of loving the addressee in truth. Third John names Gaius; Second John names the chosen lady and her children and adds others knowing truth. The recipients are not equated.
    3 John 1:1 The elder, to the beloved Gaius, whom I love in truth.

2 John 1:2
because of the truth that abides in us and will be with us forever:
  John 8:31–8:32 — thematic parallel: Jesus connects remaining in His word with knowing the truth; the letter speaks of truth now abiding in us and being with us forever. Word, truth, the direction of abiding and the future with-us promise remain distinct within this enduring-truth comparison.
    John 8:31 So Jesus said to the Jews who had believed Him, “If you remain in My word, you are truly My disciples,
    John 8:32 and you will know the truth, and the truth will set you free.”

2 John 1:3
Grace, mercy, and peace will be with us from God the Father and from Jesus Christ, the Son of the Father, in truth and love.
  1 Timothy 1:2 — thematic parallel: Both openings join grace, mercy and peace with God the Father and Jesus Christ. Timothy names Christ Jesus our Lord; Second John says these will be with us, names Jesus Christ the Son of the Father and adds truth and love. The designations and recipient forms remain distinct.
    1 Timothy 1:2 to Timothy, a true child in faith: grace, mercy, peace from God the Father and Christ Jesus our Lord.

2 John 1:4
I rejoiced greatly that I have found some of your children walking in truth, just as we received a commandment from the Father.
  3 John 1:3–1:4 — thematic parallel: Both describe the elder’s joy over walking in truth and use children language. Second John says some of the lady’s children were found walking in truth; Third John speaks of brothers’ testimony and the writer’s children, without equating the groups.
    3 John 1:3 For I rejoiced greatly when brothers came and bore witness to your truthfulness, just as you walk in truth.
    3 John 1:4 I have no greater joy than this: to hear that my children are walking in the truth.

2 John 1:5
And now I ask you, lady—not as though writing you a new commandment, but the one we had from the beginning—that we love one another.
  1 John 3:11 — thematic parallel: Both describe loving one another as a message or command received from the beginning. Second John explicitly denies presenting it as new and addresses the lady; First John identifies the beginning-heard message as mutual love without that personal address.
    1 John 3:11 For this is the message you heard from the beginning: that we should love one another,

2 John 1:6
And this is love: that we walk according to His commandments. This is the commandment, just as you all heard from the beginning: that you walk in it.
  1 John 5:3 — thematic parallel: Both explain love through keeping or walking according to God’s commandments. First John explicitly calls this love of God and adds that His commandments are not burdensome; Second John repeats the beginning-heard command and walking in it.
    1 John 5:3 For this is the love of God: that we keep His commandments; and His commandments are not burdensome,

2 John 1:7
For many deceivers have gone out into the world, those who do not confess Jesus Christ coming in the flesh. Such a person is the deceiver and the antichrist.
  1 John 4:1–4:3 — thematic parallel: Both warn of many misleading agents gone out into the world and connect antichrist with failure to confess Jesus. Second John says Jesus Christ coming in flesh; First John says having come in flesh and its next unit says not confessing Jesus. The actual forms remain distinct.
    1 John 4:1 Beloved, do not believe every spirit, but test the spirits to see whether they are from God, because many false prophets have gone out into the world.
    1 John 4:2 By this you know the Spirit of God: every spirit that confesses Jesus Christ as having come in the flesh is from God,
    1 John 4:3 and every spirit that does not confess Jesus is not from God. And this is the spirit of the antichrist, which you heard was coming and is already in the world now.

2 John 1:8
Watch yourselves, so that you do not lose what we have worked for, but receive a full reward.
  1 Corinthians 3:14–3:15 — thematic parallel: Both contrast a work-related benefit with loss. Corinthians says surviving building-work receives wages and burned work brings loss, distinguishing personal salvation; Second John warns readers not to lose what we worked for and to receive full reward. Wages/reward and the building test retain their forms.
    1 Corinthians 3:14 If anyone’s work that he has built upon the foundation remains, he will receive wages.
    1 Corinthians 3:15 If anyone’s work is burned up, he will suffer loss, but he himself will be saved, though in this way: as through fire.

2 John 1:9
Everyone who goes ahead and does not abide in the teaching of Christ does not have God. Whoever abides in the teaching has both the Father and the Son.
  1 John 2:23–2:24 — thematic parallel: Both connect relation to the Son and Father with confession or abiding. First John joins confession of the Son with having the Father and abiding in the beginning-heard word; Second John specifies abiding in Christ’s teaching and having both Father and Son.
    1 John 2:23 Everyone who denies the Son does not have the Father either; the one who confesses the Son has the Father also.
    1 John 2:24 As for you, let what you heard from the beginning abide in you. If what you heard from the beginning abides in you, you too will abide in the Son and in the Father.

2 John 1:10
If anyone comes to you and does not bring this teaching, do not receive him into a house, and do not offer him a greeting,
  Romans 16:17 — thematic parallel: Both direct a boundary toward people whose teaching conflicts with received teaching. Romans calls for watching and turning away from divisive teachers; Second John specifies withholding house reception and greeting. Their concrete prohibitions and immediate circumstances remain distinct.
    Romans 16:17 And I urge you, brothers, to watch those who cause divisions and snares contrary to the teaching you learned, and turn away from them;

2 John 1:11
for whoever offers him a greeting shares in his evil works.
  1 Timothy 5:22 — thematic parallel: Both warn against sharing in another person’s wrongdoing. Timothy prohibits hasty laying on of hands and sharing others’ sins; Second John says whoever greets the person not bringing this teaching shares his evil works. The different outward actions and sins/works wording remain distinct.
    1 Timothy 5:22 Do not lay hands on anyone hastily, nor share in the sins of others. Keep yourself pure.

2 John 1:12
Although I have many things to write to you all, I did not wish to do so with paper and ink. Instead, I hope to come to you and speak face to face, so that our joy may be made full.
  3 John 1:13–1:14 — thematic parallel: Both closing statements prefer hoped-for face-to-face speech over writing with ink. Second John also names paper and the fullness of our joy; Third John names a reed pen and the hope of seeing the addressee soon. Their materials and added purposes remain distinct.
    3 John 1:13 I had many things to write to you, but I do not wish to write to you with ink and a reed pen.
    3 John 1:14 But I hope to see you soon, and we will speak face to face.

2 John 1:13
The children of your chosen sister greet you.
  2 John 1:1 — thematic parallel: The closing children of your chosen sister connect with the opening chosen lady and her children. The corresponding chosen and family descriptions frame sender and recipient relations without deciding whether the women are individuals or congregations, or naming an otherwise unnamed sister.
    2 John 1:1 The elder, to the chosen lady and her children, whom I love in truth—and not I alone, but also all who have come to know the truth—
