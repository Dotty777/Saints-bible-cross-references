1 Corinthians 1:1
Paul, called as an apostle of Christ Jesus through God’s will, and Sosthenes the brother,
  2 Corinthians 1:1 — specific literary or thematic correspondence: Both openings ground Paul's apostleship in God's will. Sosthenes is the brother in this address; Timothy is the brother in the other. The co-senders remain distinct.
    2 Corinthians 1:1 Paul, an apostle of Christ Jesus through the will of God, and Timothy the brother, to the Church of God in Corinth, together with all the saints in the whole of Achaia:

1 Corinthians 1:2
to the Church of God in Corinth, to those sanctified in Christ Jesus, called as saints, together with all who call upon the name of our Lord Jesus Christ in every place, theirs and ours:
  Romans 1:7 — specific literary or thematic correspondence: Both addresses describe their recipients as called saints. The Roman address names those in Rome; this address names God's Corinthian Church and extends to those invoking Jesus' name everywhere.
    Romans 1:7 to all who are in Rome, beloved of God, called as saints: grace to you and peace from God our Father and the Lord Jesus Christ.

1 Corinthians 1:3
Grace to you and peace from God our Father and the Lord Jesus Christ.
  2 Corinthians 1:2 — specific literary or thematic correspondence: The greetings invoke grace and peace from God our Father and the Lord Jesus Christ, giving a closely corresponding epistolary blessing.
    2 Corinthians 1:2 Grace to you and peace from God our Father and the Lord Jesus Christ.

1 Corinthians 1:4
I always thank my God for you because of the grace of God given to you in Christ Jesus,
  Romans 1:8 — specific literary or thematic correspondence: Paul thanks God for his recipients in both letters. Here the stated ground is grace given in Christ; Romans emphasizes their faith being proclaimed throughout the world.
    Romans 1:8 First, I thank my God through Jesus Christ for all of you, because your faith is proclaimed in the whole world.

1 Corinthians 1:5
because in Him you were enriched in everything, in all speech and all knowledge,
  2 Corinthians 8:7 — specific literary or thematic correspondence: Both passages name abundance in speech and knowledge. The later appeal adds faith, earnestness and love and urges generosity; this thanksgiving attributes enrichment to Christ.
    2 Corinthians 8:7 But just as you abound in everything—in faith and word and knowledge and all diligence and the love from us in you—see that you abound also in this grace.

1 Corinthians 1:6
just as the testimony of Christ was confirmed among you,
  2 Thessalonians 1:10 — specific literary or thematic correspondence: Both relate apostolic testimony to its reception among believers. Confirmation here and believing the testimony there remain different expressions; the latter also looks toward Christ's future glorification.
    2 Thessalonians 1:10 when He comes to be glorified in His saints and marveled at among all who have believed—because our testimony to you was believed—on that day.

1 Corinthians 1:7
so that you lack no gift as you eagerly await the revelation of our Lord Jesus Christ.
  Philippians 3:20 — specific literary or thematic correspondence: Both describe expectation directed toward the Lord Jesus Christ. Philippians names awaiting a Savior from heaven; Corinthians also speaks of gifts while awaiting His revelation.
    Philippians 3:20 For our citizenship is in the heavens, from which we also eagerly await a Savior, the Lord Jesus Christ,

1 Corinthians 1:8
He will also keep you firm until the end, free from accusation on the day of our Lord Jesus Christ.
  1 Thessalonians 5:23 — specific literary or thematic correspondence: Both connect preservation free from blame with the Lord Jesus Christ's coming or day. Thessalonians' prayer specifies spirit, soul and body; that list is not inserted into Corinthians.
    1 Thessalonians 5:23 Now may the God of peace Himself sanctify you completely, and may your spirit and soul and body be kept whole, blameless at the coming of our Lord Jesus Christ.

1 Corinthians 1:9
God is faithful, through whom you were called into the fellowship of His Son, Jesus Christ our Lord.
  1 Thessalonians 5:24 — specific literary or thematic correspondence: Both affirm the faithfulness of the God who calls. Corinthians specifies fellowship with His Son; Thessalonians assures that the One calling will act.
    1 Thessalonians 5:24 He who calls you is faithful; He will also do it.

1 Corinthians 1:10

  Philippians 2:2 — specific literary or thematic correspondence: Both appeals seek shared thought and unity. Philippians also stresses shared love and soul; Corinthians explicitly rejects divisions and calls for agreement in judgment.
    Philippians 2:2 complete my joy by thinking the same thing, having the same love, united in soul, thinking the one thing,

    1 Corinthians 3:3 for you are still fleshly. For where there are jealousy and quarrels among you, are you not fleshly and walking in a merely human way?

1 Corinthians 1:12
What I mean is this: each of you says, “I belong to Paul,” or “I to Apollos,” or “I to Cephas,” or “I to Christ.”
  1 Corinthians 3:4 — internal argument or explanation: The later argument repeats the Paul and Apollos party slogans. The present list also includes Cephas and Christ; those additional slogans are not silently added to the later verse.
    1 Corinthians 3:4 For whenever someone says, “I belong to Paul,” and another, “I to Apollos,” are you not merely human?

1 Corinthians 1:13

  Romans 6:3 — specific literary or thematic correspondence: Romans identifies baptism with Christ Jesus and His death, illuminating Paul's rhetorical rejection of baptism into Paul's name. It does not supply a baptismal transcript for each Corinthian.
    Romans 6:3 Or do you not know that all of us who were baptized into Christ Jesus were baptized into His death?

1 Corinthians 1:14
I give thanks to God that I baptized none of you except Crispus and Gaius,
  Acts 18:8 — specific literary or thematic correspondence: Acts names Crispus at Corinth and reports his household's belief alongside many Corinthians believing and being baptized. It does not say in this verse that Paul baptized every named person or identify this Gaius.
    Acts 18:8 Crispus, the synagogue ruler, believed in the Lord with his whole household. Many of the Corinthians who heard believed and were baptized.

1 Corinthians 1:15
so that no one may say that you were baptized into my own name.
  Romans 6:3 — specific literary or thematic correspondence: Baptism into Christ Jesus in Romans provides the positive reference point for Paul's rejection of the claim that recipients were baptized into his own name.
    Romans 6:3 Or do you not know that all of us who were baptized into Christ Jesus were baptized into His death?

1 Corinthians 1:16
I did also baptize the household of Stephanas; beyond that, I do not know whether I baptized anyone else.
  1 Corinthians 16:15 — internal argument or explanation: The later passage identifies Stephanas's household as Achaia's firstfruits devoted to service, giving a particular internal connection to the household Paul remembers baptizing.
    1 Corinthians 16:15 Now I appeal to you, brothers—you know the household of Stephanas, that it is the firstfruits of Achaia and that they devoted themselves to serving the saints—

1 Corinthians 1:17
For Christ did not send me to baptize but to proclaim the good news, without wisdom of speech, so that the cross of Christ might not be emptied of its power.
  1 Corinthians 2:4 — internal argument or explanation: The later description contrasts persuasive wisdom with Spirit and power, explaining the concern about wisdom of speech here. Paul's mission emphasis is not a denial that he performed baptisms.
    1 Corinthians 2:4 My speech and my proclamation were not in persuasive words of wisdom, but in a demonstration of Spirit and power,

1 Corinthians 1:18
For the word of the cross is foolishness to those who are perishing, but to us who are being saved it is God’s power.
  Romans 1:16 — specific literary or thematic correspondence: Both describe the saving proclamation as God's power. Corinthians contrasts responses to the word of the cross; Romans describes the good news as power for salvation to everyone believing.
    Romans 1:16 For I am not ashamed of the gospel, for it is God’s power for salvation to everyone who believes, to the Jew first and also to the Greek.

1 Corinthians 1:19
For it is written, “I will destroy the wisdom of the wise, and I will set aside the understanding of the discerning.”
  Isaiah 29:14 — specific literary or thematic correspondence: The citation corresponds to Isaiah's destruction of the wise people's wisdom and concealment of the discerning people's discernment. Actual supplied wording is retained rather than making both clauses mechanically identical.
    Isaiah 29:14 Therefore, behold, I will proceed to remove this people; I will remove them, destroy the wise people’s wisdom and hide the discerning people’s understanding.”

1 Corinthians 1:20
Where is the wise person? Where is the scribe? Where is the debater of this age? Has not God made the world’s wisdom foolish?
  1 Corinthians 3:19 — internal argument or explanation: The later assertion that this world's wisdom is foolishness with God directly develops the present question about God making worldly wisdom foolish.
    1 Corinthians 3:19 For the wisdom of this world is foolishness with God. For it is written, “He catches the wise in their craftiness,”

1 Corinthians 1:21
For since, in God’s wisdom, the world did not know God through wisdom, God was pleased through the foolishness of the proclamation to save those who believe.
  Matthew 11:25 — specific literary or thematic correspondence: Both reverse expectations about human wisdom and divine disclosure. Jesus thanks the Father for revealing to infants; Paul describes salvation through believing the proclamation, not an identical audience or scene.
    Matthew 11:25 At that time Jesus answered, “I acknowledge Thee, Father, Lord of heaven and earth, because Thou didst hide these things from the wise and understanding and reveal them to infants.

1 Corinthians 1:22
For Jews ask for signs and Greeks seek wisdom,
  Matthew 12:38 — specific literary or thematic correspondence: The scribes and Pharisees' request for a sign illustrates the sign-seeking side of Paul's contrast. It does not establish that every Jewish person made that request or address Greek wisdom-seeking.
    Matthew 12:38 Then some of the scribes and Pharisees answered Him, saying, “Teacher, we wish to see a sign from Thee.”

1 Corinthians 1:23
but we proclaim Christ crucified: a stumbling block to Jews and foolishness to Gentiles,
  Galatians 5:11 — specific literary or thematic correspondence: Both connect the cross with a stumbling block. Galatians discusses the offense in relation to circumcision preaching; Corinthians distinguishes Jewish and Gentile reactions to Christ crucified.
    Galatians 5:11 But I, brothers, if I still proclaim circumcision, why am I still persecuted? Then the stumbling block of the cross has been abolished.

1 Corinthians 1:24
but to those who are called, both Jews and Greeks, Christ is God’s power and God’s wisdom.
  Colossians 2:3 — specific literary or thematic correspondence: Both locate divine wisdom in Christ. Colossians speaks of wisdom and knowledge's treasures hidden in Him; Corinthians also calls Christ God's power and names Jews and Greeks among those called.
    Colossians 2:3 in whom all the treasures of wisdom and knowledge are hidden.

1 Corinthians 1:25
For God’s foolishness is wiser than human beings, and God’s weakness is stronger than human beings.
  2 Corinthians 13:4 — specific literary or thematic correspondence: The later comparison of Christ's crucifixion in weakness and life by God's power illuminates the weakness/power reversal. It does not call God actually deficient in wisdom or strength.
    2 Corinthians 13:4 For indeed He was crucified in weakness, but lives by the power of God. For we too are weak in Him, but will live with Him by the power of God toward you.

1 Corinthians 1:26
For consider your calling, brothers: not many were wise according to the flesh, not many powerful, not many of noble birth.
  James 2:5 — specific literary or thematic correspondence: God's choice of the world's poor to be rich in faith parallels the calling that includes few socially powerful or noble. Neither passage says every believer has the same economic position.
    James 2:5 Listen, my beloved brothers: has not God chosen those poor in the world to be rich in faith and heirs of the kingdom He promised to those who love Him?

1 Corinthians 1:27
But God chose the foolish things of the world to shame the wise, and God chose the weak things of the world to shame the strong;
  Psalms 8:3 — specific literary or thematic correspondence: The psalm's praise established through infants against adversaries offers a particular weakness-to-strength reversal. Corinthians applies God's choice more broadly; the groups and images remain distinct.
    Psalms 8:3 From the mouth of infants and nursing children Thou didst prepare praise, because of Thine enemies, to bring down the enemy and the avenger.

1 Corinthians 1:28
and God chose the lowborn things of the world and the despised things, the things that are not, to bring to nothing the things that are,
  Luke 1:52 — specific literary or thematic correspondence: The lowering of the powerful and raising of the lowly corresponds to God's choice of despised and lowborn things. Luke's throne image does not define Paul's things that are not as a creation formula.
    Luke 1:52 He brought rulers down from thrones and raised up the lowly.

1 Corinthians 1:29
so that no flesh may boast before God.
  Ephesians 2:8–2:9 — specific literary or thematic correspondence: Both exclude human boasting in relation to God's saving action. Ephesians speaks of grace, faith and gift; Corinthians reaches the no-boasting conclusion through God's unexpected choices.
    Ephesians 2:8 For by grace you have been saved through faith, and this is not from yourselves; it is the gift of God,
    Ephesians 2:9 not from works, so that no one may boast.

1 Corinthians 1:30
But it is from Him that you are in Christ Jesus, who became for us wisdom from God, and righteousness and sanctification and redemption,
  Colossians 2:3 — specific literary or thematic correspondence: Christ as wisdom from God corresponds to the treasures of wisdom and knowledge in Him. The additional righteousness, sanctification and redemption terms here are not attributed verbatim to Colossians.
    Colossians 2:3 in whom all the treasures of wisdom and knowledge are hidden.

1 Corinthians 1:31
so that, as it is written, “Let the one who boasts boast in the Lord.”
  Jeremiah 9:22–9:23 — specific literary or thematic correspondence: Jeremiah rejects boasting in wisdom, strength and wealth and directs boasting toward understanding and knowing the Lord. Paul condenses this into boasting in the Lord; Greek-based identities 9:22–23 are preserved.
    Jeremiah 9:22 Thus says the Lord: Let no wise man boast in his wisdom, let no strong man boast in his strength, and let no rich man boast in his wealth,
    Jeremiah 9:23 but let whoever boasts boast in this: understanding and knowing that I am the Lord, doing mercy and judgment and righteousness upon the earth, because in these is My will, says the Lord.

1 Corinthians 2:1

    2 Corinthians 11:6 Even if I am untrained in speech, I am not in knowledge; but in every way we have made it clear to you in all things.

1 Corinthians 2:2

    Galatians 6:14 But may I never boast except in the cross of our Lord Jesus Christ, through which the world has been crucified to me, and I to the world.

1 Corinthians 2:3
And I was with you in weakness and in fear and in much trembling.
  Acts 18:9–18:10 — specific literary or thematic correspondence: The Lord's encouragement not to fear during Paul's Corinthian ministry provides a specific narrative comparison for weakness, fear and trembling. No unreported psychological cause or exact occasion is supplied.
    Acts 18:9 The Lord said to Paul by a vision in the night, “Do not fear, but speak and do not be silent,
    Acts 18:10 because I am with you, and no one will attack you to harm you, for I have many people in this city.”

1 Corinthians 2:4

  1 Thessalonians 1:5 — specific literary or thematic correspondence: Both contrast proclamation merely in words with power and the Holy Spirit. The Spirit's demonstration here is not expanded into particular signs absent from the retained passages.
    1 Thessalonians 1:5 because our gospel did not come to you in word alone, but also in power and in the Holy Spirit and in great assurance, just as you know what kind of people we became among you for your sake.

1 Corinthians 2:5

  2 Corinthians 4:7 — specific literary or thematic correspondence: Both reserve the decisive power to God rather than human agents. The later passage uses treasure in earthen vessels; the present statement applies the contrast to the basis of faith.
    2 Corinthians 4:7 But we have this treasure in clay jars, so that the surpassing power may be God’s and not from us.

    Hebrews 5:14 But solid food belongs to the mature, whose faculties have been trained by practice to distinguish both good and evil.

1 Corinthians 2:8

    Acts 3:17 And now, brothers, I know that you acted in ignorance, as did your rulers.

1 Corinthians 2:9

  Isaiah 64:3 — specific literary or thematic correspondence: The unseen/unheard divine works correspond to Paul's citation. Isaiah names works for those awaiting mercy; Corinthians adds the human-heart clause and speaks of preparation for those loving God. These differences are retained.
    Isaiah 64:3 From of old we have not heard, nor have our eyes seen a god except Thee and Thy works, which Thou wilt do for those awaiting mercy.

1 Corinthians 2:10

  John 16:13 — specific literary or thematic correspondence: Both associate the Spirit with disclosure of divine truth. John's promise of guidance into truth and Corinthians' searching of God's depths are not a grant of unrestricted knowledge to every reader.
    John 16:13 But when He comes, the Spirit of truth, He will guide you in all the truth; for He will not speak from Himself, but will speak whatever He hears, and will announce to you the things to come.

1 Corinthians 2:11

  Romans 8:27 — specific literary or thematic correspondence: Romans joins the searching of hearts with knowledge of the Spirit's mind. Corinthians contrasts human self-knowledge and God's Spirit's knowledge, without excluding God's knowledge of human hearts.
    Romans 8:27 And the One who searches hearts knows the mind of the Spirit, because He intercedes for saints according to God.

1 Corinthians 2:12

  Romans 8:15 — specific literary or thematic correspondence: Both describe receiving a Spirit whose origin and character differ from an opposed condition. Romans contrasts slavery and adoption; Corinthians contrasts the world's spirit with the Spirit from God.
    Romans 8:15 For you did not receive a spirit of slavery leading again to fear, but you received the Spirit of adoption as sons, in whom we cry, “Abba, Father.”

1 Corinthians 2:13
We also speak these things, not in words taught by human wisdom but in those taught by the Spirit, explaining spiritual things to spiritual people.
  John 14:26 — specific literary or thematic correspondence: The Holy Spirit's teaching in John corresponds to words taught by the Spirit here. The supplied explaining spiritual things to spiritual people wording is retained without replacing it with another translation.
    John 14:26 But the Advocate, the Holy Spirit, whom the Father will send in My name, He will teach you all things and remind you of all that I said to you.

1 Corinthians 2:14

  John 14:17 — specific literary or thematic correspondence: The world's inability to receive or know the Spirit of truth parallels the natural person's nonreception of the Spirit's things. The descriptions are not used to declare every individual's condition irreversible.
    John 14:17 the Spirit of truth, whom the world cannot receive, because it neither beholds Him nor knows Him. You know Him, because He remains with you and will be in you.

1 Corinthians 2:15

    1 Corinthians 2:14 But a natural person does not receive the things of the Spirit of God, for they are foolishness to such a person, who cannot know them because they are discerned spiritually.

1 Corinthians 2:16

    John 15:5 I am the vine; you are the branches. The one who remains in Me, and I in him, bears much fruit, because apart from Me you can do nothing.

    1 Corinthians 15:58 Therefore, my beloved brothers, be steadfast, immovable, always abounding in the Lord’s work, knowing that your labor is not empty in the Lord.

    1 Corinthians 3:13 each person’s work will become clear, for the day will disclose it, because it is revealed in fire; and the fire itself will test what sort of work each has done.

1 Corinthians 3:13
