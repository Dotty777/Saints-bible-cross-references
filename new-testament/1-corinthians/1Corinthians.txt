  1 Corinthians 14:1 — internal thematic parallel: The introduction concerning spiritual things leads to the later instruction to pursue love and desire spiritual things, especially prophecy. This identifies the same subject across the discussion without treating the later target as newly reviewed source material.
    1 Corinthians 14:1 Pursue love, and earnestly desire spiritual things, especially that you may prophesy.

1 Corinthians 12:2
You know that when you were Gentiles, you were carried away to the mute idols, however you were led.
  Psalms 113:13 — scriptural thematic parallel: Paul recalls the recipients' former attachment to mute idols. The Psalm describes idols with mouths that do not speak. The shared point is the idols' inability to speak, not an identification of the Corinthians' particular former idols or circumstances.
    Psalms 113:13 They have a mouth and will not speak; they have eyes and will not see.

1 Corinthians 12:3
Therefore I make known to you that no one speaking in God’s Spirit says, “Jesus is accursed,” and no one can say, “Jesus is Lord,” except in the Holy Spirit.
  Romans 10:9 — teaching parallel: Both foreground the confession Jesus is Lord. Corinthians contrasts Spirit-enabled confession with calling Jesus accursed; Romans joins the mouth's confession with the heart's belief in God's raising Him from the dead. Romans does not repeat the explicit Spirit test.
    Romans 10:9 Because if you confess Jesus as Lord with your mouth, and believe in your heart that God raised Him from the dead, you will be saved;

1 Corinthians 12:4
There are varieties of gifts, but the same Spirit;
  Romans 12:6 — teaching parallel: Both describe gifts that differ among believers. Corinthians stresses the same Spirit amid varied gifts; Romans speaks of differing gifts according to the grace given and begins with prophecy. Their stated grounds and examples are preserved.
    Romans 12:6 And since we have gifts differing according to the grace given to us: if prophecy, let it be according to the proportion of faith;

1 Corinthians 12:5
and there are varieties of service, and the same Lord;
  Ephesians 4:11–4:12 — teaching parallel: Varieties of service under the same Lord correspond to the various people Christ gives for equipping the saints for ministry and building His body. Ephesians lists particular roles, while this Corinthians unit states the shared Lord behind varied service.
    Ephesians 4:11 And He Himself gave some as apostles, some as prophets, some as evangelists, and some as shepherds and teachers,
    Ephesians 4:12 for the equipping of the saints for the work of service, for building up the body of Christ,

1 Corinthians 12:6
and there are varieties of work, but the same God, who works all things in everyone.
  Philippians 2:13 — teaching parallel: Both identify God as the one working in believers. Corinthians says the same God works all things in everyone amid varied works; Philippians says God works willing and working in the recipients for His good pleasure. The scopes are stated without equating every work with a particular gift.
    Philippians 2:13 for God is the One working in you both to will and to work for His good pleasure.

1 Corinthians 12:7
To each is given the manifestation of the Spirit for the common benefit.
  1 Peter 4:10 — teaching parallel: The manifestation of the Spirit is given to each for common benefit; Peter likewise tells each recipient of a gift to serve others as a steward of God's varied grace. The shared emphasis is gifts directed toward others' benefit.
    1 Peter 4:10 As each has received a gift, use it in serving one another, as good stewards of God’s varied grace.

1 Corinthians 12:8
For to one a word of wisdom is given through the Spirit, and to another a word of knowledge according to the same Spirit;
  1 Corinthians 2:12–2:13 — internal thematic parallel: The earlier discussion connects the Spirit from God with knowing God's gifts and speech taught by the Spirit rather than human wisdom. Here words of wisdom and knowledge are distributed through the same Spirit. The earlier paragraph is not a complete definition of each named gift.
    1 Corinthians 2:12 Now we did not receive the spirit of the world but the Spirit who is from God, so that we may know the things freely given to us by God.
    1 Corinthians 2:13 We also speak these things, not in words taught by human wisdom but in those taught by the Spirit, explaining spiritual things to spiritual people.

1 Corinthians 12:9
to another faith in the same Spirit, and to another gifts of healings in the one Spirit;
  1 Corinthians 13:2 — internal thematic parallel: Faith appears among the Spirit-given gifts here; the later hypothetical names all faith sufficient to move mountains while insisting on love. This links the expressed faith theme without claiming that the later unit also lists gifts of healing.
    1 Corinthians 13:2 And if I have prophecy and know all mysteries and all knowledge, and if I have all faith so as to move mountains, but have no love, I am nothing.

1 Corinthians 12:10
to another acts of power, to another prophecy, to another discernments of spirits, to another kinds of tongues, and to another interpretation of tongues.
  1 Corinthians 14:27–14:29 — internal teaching parallel: The later assembly instructions address tongues with interpretation and prophets whose speech others discern. They develop several gifts named here without repeating every item in this list or equating discernment with an unrestricted approval of every message.
    1 Corinthians 14:27 If anyone speaks in a tongue, let it be by two or at most three, each in turn, and let one interpret.
    1 Corinthians 14:28 But if there is no interpreter, let him be silent in Church, and let him speak to himself and to God.
    1 Corinthians 14:29 Let two or three prophets speak, and let the others discern.

1 Corinthians 12:11
But the one and the same Spirit works all these things, distributing to each individually just as He wills.
  Hebrews 2:4 — teaching parallel: Both relate varied spiritual distributions to divine will. Corinthians explicitly makes the one Spirit the distributor as He wills; Hebrews describes God bearing witness through signs, acts of power and distributions of the Holy Spirit according to His will. The grammatical formulations are preserved.
    Hebrews 2:4 God bearing witness together with signs and wonders and various mighty deeds and distributions of the Holy Spirit according to His own will.

1 Corinthians 12:12
For just as the body is one and has many members, and all the body’s members, though many, are one body, so also is Christ.
  Romans 12:4–12:5 — teaching parallel: Both use one body with many members to describe believers' unity in Christ while retaining the members' diversity. Corinthians develops the physical-body analogy; Romans adds that the members do not all have the same function and belong individually to one another.
    Romans 12:4 For as we have many parts in one body, and the parts do not all have the same function,
    Romans 12:5 so we, the many, are one body in Christ, and each one is a member of the others.

1 Corinthians 12:13
For in one Spirit we were all baptized into one body, whether Jews or Greeks, whether slaves or free, and we were all given one Spirit to drink.
  Galatians 3:27–3:28 — teaching parallel: Both connect baptism or incorporation into Christ with unity across Jew/Greek and slave/free distinctions. Corinthians names baptism in one Spirit into one body and drinking one Spirit; Galatians names being clothed with Christ and also includes male/female. The formulations are not interchangeable.
    Galatians 3:27 For as many of you as were baptized into Christ have put on Christ.
    Galatians 3:28 There is neither Jew nor Greek; there is neither slave nor free; there is no male and female, for you are all one in Christ Jesus.

1 Corinthians 12:14
For the body is not one member but many.
  Romans 12:4 — teaching parallel: The many-member body assertion corresponds to Romans's one body with many members that do not all have the same function. Both preserve plurality rather than reducing the body to a single undifferentiated part.
    Romans 12:4 For as we have many parts in one body, and the parts do not all have the same function,

1 Corinthians 12:15
If the foot says, “Because I am not a hand, I am not part of the body,” that does not stop it from being part of the body, does it?
  1 Corinthians 12:18 — internal argument parallel: The foot's claim not to belong because it is not a hand is answered within the same argument by God's placing each member as He wished. The link connects the imagined self-exclusion with the stated basis of belonging and arrangement.
    1 Corinthians 12:18 But as it is, God placed the members, each one of them, in the body just as He wished.

1 Corinthians 12:16
And if the ear says, “Because I am not an eye, I am not part of the body,” that does not stop it from being part of the body, does it?
  1 Corinthians 12:17 — internal argument parallel: The ear's imagined exclusion because it is not an eye is followed by the whole-eye question about the loss of hearing. These are linked illustrations of why a different bodily function does not remove membership.
    1 Corinthians 12:17 If the whole body were an eye, where would hearing be? If the whole were hearing, where would the sense of smell be?

1 Corinthians 12:17
If the whole body were an eye, where would hearing be? If the whole were hearing, where would the sense of smell be?
  Romans 12:4 — teaching parallel: The imagined all-eye or all-hearing body demonstrates the need for different functions. Romans states the corresponding point directly: the many bodily members do not all have the same function.
    Romans 12:4 For as we have many parts in one body, and the parts do not all have the same function,

1 Corinthians 12:18
But as it is, God placed the members, each one of them, in the body just as He wished.
  1 Corinthians 12:28 — internal metaphor and application: God's placing the members in the body corresponds to God's appointing varied people and gifts in the Church later in the paragraph. This relates the explicit body arrangement to its community application without identifying an anatomical part with a particular office.
    1 Corinthians 12:28 God has appointed in the Church first apostles, second prophets, third teachers, then acts of power, then gifts of healings, helps, forms of guidance, and kinds of tongues.

1 Corinthians 12:19
If they were all one member, where would the body be?
  1 Corinthians 12:14 — internal argument parallel: The question where the body would be if all were one member recalls the earlier assertion that the body is not one member but many. The counterfactual and assertion make the same particular point about essential plurality.
    1 Corinthians 12:14 For the body is not one member but many.

1 Corinthians 12:20
But as it is, there are many members, yet one body.
  Romans 12:5 — teaching parallel: The many-members/one-body conclusion corresponds to the many being one body in Christ and individually members of one another. Both retain personal plurality within the shared body.
    Romans 12:5 so we, the many, are one body in Christ, and each one is a member of the others.

1 Corinthians 12:21
The eye cannot say to the hand, “I have no need of you,” or again the head to the feet, “I have no need of you.”
  Ephesians 4:16 — teaching parallel: The eye and head cannot dismiss the hand and feet as unnecessary; Ephesians describes the body's growth through supporting joints and each individual part's work. Both portray interdependence, without identifying the argumentative head in Corinthians with Ephesians's Christology by anatomy alone.
    Ephesians 4:16 from whom the whole body, fitted and held together through every supporting joint, as each individual part works in its proper measure, produces the growth of the body for building itself up in love.

1 Corinthians 12:22
On the contrary, the members of the body that seem weaker are much more necessary,
  1 Corinthians 1:27 — internal thematic parallel: The apparently weaker members being necessary corresponds to the earlier statement that God chose the world's weak things to shame the strong. The shared reversal challenges judgments based on apparent strength, while the earlier text is not an anatomical explanation.
    1 Corinthians 1:27 But God chose the foolish things of the world to shame the wise, and God chose the weak things of the world to shame the strong;

1 Corinthians 12:23
and those parts of the body we consider less honorable, we clothe with greater honor; and our unpresentable parts receive greater modesty,
  James 2:2–2:4 — thematic contrast: Corinthians describes giving greater honor and covering to bodily parts considered less honorable. James criticizes honoring the finely clothed while humiliating the poorly clothed. This compares responses to perceived honor and outward covering without identifying particular anatomical parts with economic groups.
    James 2:2 For if a man with gold rings and splendid clothing enters your gathering, and a poor person in filthy clothing also enters,
    James 2:3 and you pay attention to the one wearing splendid clothing and say, “You sit here in a good place,” but say to the poor person, “You stand, or sit there beneath my footstool,”
    James 2:4 have you not made distinctions among yourselves and become judges with evil thoughts?

1 Corinthians 12:24
while our presentable parts have no need of it. But God combined the body, giving greater honor to the part that lacked it,
  1 Corinthians 1:28–1:29 — internal thematic parallel: God's giving greater honor to a bodily part that lacked it corresponds to His choosing the lowly and despised rather than enabling human boasting. The comparison concerns God's reversal of perceived status in two different images.
    1 Corinthians 1:28 and God chose the lowborn things of the world and the despised things, the things that are not, to bring to nothing the things that are,
    1 Corinthians 1:29 so that no flesh may boast before God.

1 Corinthians 12:25
so that there may be no division in the body, but that the members may have the same care for one another.
  1 Corinthians 1:10 — internal teaching parallel: Both oppose division within the community. The opening appeal asks for no divisions and shared mind and judgment; the body argument seeks no division and the same care for one another. The later image develops the earlier communal aim.
    1 Corinthians 1:10 Now I appeal to you, brothers, through the name of our Lord Jesus Christ, that you all speak in agreement and that there be no divisions among you, but that you be restored to unity in the same mind and the same judgment.

1 Corinthians 12:26
If one member suffers, all the members suffer together; if one member is honored, all the members rejoice together.
  Romans 12:15 — teaching parallel: Shared suffering and rejoicing among bodily members corresponds to rejoicing with those who rejoice and weeping with those who weep. Romans gives the communal response as direct instruction; Corinthians expresses it through the body image.
    Romans 12:15 Rejoice with those who rejoice; weep with those who weep.

1 Corinthians 12:27
Now you are Christ’s body, and individually members of it.
  Ephesians 1:22–1:23 — teaching parallel: Corinthians explicitly calls the recipients Christ's body with individual membership; Ephesians names the Church as His body and Christ as head. This connects the expressed community-body descriptions without replacing their different emphases.
    Ephesians 1:22 And He subjected all things under His feet and gave Him as head over all things to the Church,
    Ephesians 1:23 which is His body, the fullness of the One who fills all things in all.

1 Corinthians 12:28
God has appointed in the Church first apostles, second prophets, third teachers, then acts of power, then gifts of healings, helps, forms of guidance, and kinds of tongues.
  Ephesians 4:11 — teaching parallel: Both list apostles, prophets and teachers among divine gifts or appointments for the community. Corinthians makes God the appointer and also lists acts of power, healings, helps, guidance and tongues; Ephesians speaks of Christ's giving and includes evangelists and shepherds. The lists and order are not made identical.
    Ephesians 4:11 And He Himself gave some as apostles, some as prophets, some as evangelists, and some as shepherds and teachers,

1 Corinthians 12:29
Are all apostles? Are all prophets? Are all teachers? Do all perform acts of power?
  1 Corinthians 12:28 — internal argument parallel: The questions whether all are apostles, prophets, teachers or performers of acts of power recall the immediately named varied appointments. Their place in the diversity argument is retained without turning every appointment into a universal role.
    1 Corinthians 12:28 God has appointed in the Church first apostles, second prophets, third teachers, then acts of power, then gifts of healings, helps, forms of guidance, and kinds of tongues.

1 Corinthians 12:30
Do all have gifts of healings? Do all speak in tongues? Do all interpret?
  1 Corinthians 12:9–12:10 — internal argument parallel: The questions about universal healing gifts, tongues and interpretation refer back to these separately distributed gifts in the earlier list. The comparison preserves the distinction of gifts rather than assuming every member has all of them.
    1 Corinthians 12:9 to another faith in the same Spirit, and to another gifts of healings in the one Spirit;
    1 Corinthians 12:10 to another acts of power, to another prophecy, to another discernments of spirits, to another kinds of tongues, and to another interpretation of tongues.

1 Corinthians 12:31
But earnestly desire the greater gifts. And I show you a way beyond all comparison.
  1 Corinthians 14:1 — internal teaching progression: The call to desire greater gifts and the promised incomparable way lead through the love discussion to pursuing love and desiring spiritual things, especially prophecy. This traces the letter's own progression without making the greater-gifts phrase an exhaustive ranking by itself.
    1 Corinthians 14:1 Pursue love, and earnestly desire spiritual things, especially that you may prophesy.

1 Corinthians 13:1
If I speak in the tongues of human beings and of angels but have no love, I have become resounding bronze or a clanging cymbal.
  1 Corinthians 14:7–14:9 — internal analogy parallel: Speech without love is compared with resounding metal; later, indistinct instrumental sounds and unintelligible speech illustrate failure to communicate. Both use unhelpful sound analogies, but lack of love and lack of intelligibility are different defects, and angelic speech is not independently established by the hypothetical.
    1 Corinthians 14:7 Even lifeless things that give a sound, whether a flute or a lyre—if they do not produce distinct notes, how will what is played on the flute or lyre be recognized?
    1 Corinthians 14:8 For if a trumpet gives an unclear sound, who will prepare for battle?
    1 Corinthians 14:9 So also with you: unless you speak an intelligible word with your tongue, how will what is spoken be known? For you will be speaking into the air.

1 Corinthians 13:2
And if I have prophecy and know all mysteries and all knowledge, and if I have all faith so as to move mountains, but have no love, I am nothing.
  Matthew 17:20 — teaching parallel: Both describe faith in terms of moving a mountain. Jesus connects mustard-seed faith with that possibility; Paul makes the all-faith mountain-moving claim hypothetical and says it leaves him nothing without love. The target does not itself state that qualification.
    Matthew 17:20 He said to them, “Because of your little faith. For truly I tell you, if you have faith like a mustard seed, you will say to this mountain, ‘Move from here to there,’ and it will move; and nothing will be impossible for you.”

1 Corinthians 13:3
And if I distribute all my possessions as food, and if I hand over my body so that I may boast, but have no love, I gain nothing.
  Matthew 6:1–6:2 — thematic parallel: Both deny that an impressive outward religious act automatically secures its desired benefit. Paul describes giving all possessions as food and surrendering his body so that he may boast, yet gaining nothing without love; Jesus warns against doing righteousness and alms for human recognition. Paul says boast, not burn.
    Matthew 6:1 Take care not to practice your righteousness before people in order to be seen by them; otherwise you have no reward with your Father in the heavens.
    Matthew 6:2 So when you give alms, do not sound a trumpet before you as the hypocrites do in the synagogues and streets, so that people may glorify them. Truly I tell you, they have received their reward in full.

1 Corinthians 13:4
Love is patient; love is kind; it does not envy; [love] does not boast, is not puffed up,
  Galatians 5:22 — teaching parallel: Corinthians describes love as patient and kind; Galatians includes love, patience and kindness in the Spirit's fruit. These shared qualities link the descriptions without making the whole lists identical or turning gifts and fruit into the same category.
    Galatians 5:22 But the fruit of the Spirit is love, joy, peace, patience, kindness, goodness, faithfulness,

1 Corinthians 13:5
does not behave shamefully, does not seek its own interests, is not provoked, does not keep account of wrong,
  1 Corinthians 10:24 — internal teaching parallel: Love's not seeking its own interests corresponds to the instruction that no one seek his own interest but the other's. The earlier instruction appears in the food-and-conscience discussion; the present love description also includes other qualities not restated there.
    1 Corinthians 10:24 Let no one seek his own good, but the other’s.

1 Corinthians 13:6
does not rejoice in unrighteousness, but rejoices with the truth.
  2 Thessalonians 2:12 — teaching contrast: Love rejoices with the truth rather than in unrighteousness. Thessalonians describes those who did not believe the truth but took pleasure in unrighteousness. The paired truth/unrighteousness contrast is specific, though the grammatical subjects and judgment setting differ.
    2 Thessalonians 2:12 in order that all who did not believe the truth but took pleasure in unrighteousness may be judged.

1 Corinthians 13:7
It bears all things, believes all things, hopes all things, endures all things.
  1 Corinthians 9:12 — internal thematic parallel: Love bears all things; Paul earlier says we bear everything rather than obstruct Christ's gospel by exercising a right. The concrete apostolic example shares the bearing language without exhausting love's additional believing, hoping and enduring descriptions.
    1 Corinthians 9:12 If others share in this right over you, do we not all the more? But we did not use this right; instead, we bear everything so that we may put no hindrance in the way of the good news of Christ.

1 Corinthians 13:8
Love never falls. But prophecies will be brought to an end; tongues will cease; knowledge will be brought to an end.
  1 Corinthians 13:13 — internal thematic parallel: Love's never falling and its contrast with temporary gifts correspond to the conclusion that faith, hope and love remain, with love greatest. The link follows the text's enduring-love emphasis without imposing an unprinted timetable for gifts.
    1 Corinthians 13:13 And now faith, hope, and love remain, these three; but the greatest of these is love.

1 Corinthians 13:9
For we know in part and prophesy in part,
  1 Corinthians 13:12 — internal thematic parallel: Knowing in part is repeated in the mirror/face-to-face comparison, which contrasts present partial knowledge with future full knowledge. The later unit elaborates the stated limit without defining the partial as a defective or false revelation.
    1 Corinthians 13:12 For now we see through a mirror, as in a riddle, but then face to face. Now I know in part, but then I will know fully, just as I also was fully known.

1 Corinthians 13:10
but when what is complete comes, what is partial will be brought to an end.
  1 Corinthians 13:12 — internal explanation: The coming of what is complete and ending of the partial correspond to the later now/then contrast in seeing and knowing. The later comparison helps describe the contrast; neither target nor source here identifies the complete with a particular canon-completion date.
    1 Corinthians 13:12 For now we see through a mirror, as in a riddle, but then face to face. Now I know in part, but then I will know fully, just as I also was fully known.

1 Corinthians 13:11
When I was a child, I spoke as a child, thought as a child, reasoned as a child. When I became a man, I brought the child’s ways to an end.
  1 Corinthians 14:20 — internal analogy parallel: The child-to-man illustration contrasts former ways of speaking, thinking and reasoning with maturity. The later exhortation tells the community to be mature in thinking while being infants in evil. Both use maturity language, with an autobiographical illustration in one and a command in the other.
    1 Corinthians 14:20 Brothers, do not become children in your thinking. Be infants in evil, but become mature in your thinking.

1 Corinthians 13:12
For now we see through a mirror, as in a riddle, but then face to face. Now I know in part, but then I will know fully, just as I also was fully known.
  Numbers 12:8 — scriptural thematic parallel: Paul contrasts seeing through a mirror as in a riddle with seeing face to face and contrasts partial with full knowing. Numbers describes God speaking mouth to mouth with Moses, in visible appearance rather than riddles, and Moses seeing the Lord's glory. This is a related contrast, not a verbatim quotation or an equation of their entire prophetic statuses.
    Numbers 12:8 I will speak unto him mouth to mouth, in visible appearance, and not through riddles; and he saw the glory of the Lord. And why were you not afraid to speak against My servant Moses?

1 Corinthians 13:13
And now faith, hope, and love remain, these three; but the greatest of these is love.
  1 Thessalonians 1:3 — teaching parallel: Both join faith, love and hope. Thessalonians recalls faith's work, love's labor and hope's endurance in the Lord Jesus Christ; Corinthians says the three remain and explicitly makes love greatest. The target does not supply that ranking.
    1 Thessalonians 1:3 remembering your work of faith and labor of love and endurance of hope in our Lord Jesus Christ before our God and Father,

1 Corinthians 14:1

  1 Corinthians 12:31–13:1 — teaching parallel: The preceding appeal to desire greater gifts introduces love's priority even over speaking in human and angelic tongues. The present instruction pursues love and spiritual things, especially prophecy, carrying that priority into the discussion of speech.
    1 Corinthians 12:31 But earnestly desire the greater gifts. And I show you a way beyond all comparison.
    1 Corinthians 13:1 If I speak in the tongues of human beings and of angels but have no love, I have become resounding bronze or a clanging cymbal.

1 Corinthians 14:2

  1 Corinthians 14:28 — teaching parallel: Both describe tongue-speech directed to God when the congregation lacks understanding or interpretation. The later rule permits speaking to oneself and God while requiring silence in Church without an interpreter; this develops the distinction between divine address and human reception.
    1 Corinthians 14:28 But if there is no interpreter, let him be silent in Church, and let him speak to himself and to God.

1 Corinthians 14:3

  Acts 15:32 — teaching parallel: The stated human benefit of prophecy—building, encouragement and consolation—has a concrete comparison in Judas and Silas, explicitly called prophets, encouraging and strengthening the brothers with many words.
    Acts 15:32 Judas and Silas, themselves also prophets, encouraged the brothers with many words and strengthened them.

1 Corinthians 14:4

  1 Corinthians 14:17 — teaching parallel: The contrast between building oneself and building the Church explains the later judgment that thanksgiving can be given well while another person is not built up. Both evaluate speech by its benefit beyond the speaker.
    1 Corinthians 14:17 For you give thanks well, but the other person is not built up.

1 Corinthians 14:5

  1 Corinthians 12:10 — teaching parallel: Both name prophecy, kinds of tongues and interpretation of tongues as distinct spiritual activities. The catalogue lists their distribution; this verse compares their congregational benefit and explicitly allows interpretation to affect that comparison.
    1 Corinthians 12:10 to another acts of power, to another prophecy, to another discernments of spirits, to another kinds of tongues, and to another interpretation of tongues.

1 Corinthians 14:6
But now, brothers, if I come to you speaking in tongues, how will I benefit you unless I speak to you in revelation or knowledge or prophecy or teaching?
  1 Corinthians 14:26 — teaching parallel: Both list forms of congregational speech and test them by benefit or building up. Revelation, teaching and tongue-speech recur; the later gathering description also includes psalm and interpretation rather than reproducing this list exactly.
    1 Corinthians 14:26 What, then, is to be done, brothers? When you gather, each has a psalm, has a teaching, has a revelation, has a tongue, has an interpretation. Let everything be done for building up.

1 Corinthians 14:7
Even lifeless things that give a sound, whether a flute or a lyre—if they do not produce distinct notes, how will what is played on the flute or lyre be recognized?
  1 Corinthians 14:9 — teaching parallel: The flute-and-lyre question about recognizable notes supplies the analogy applied by the following 'So also with you': an intelligible word is needed for spoken communication to be known. The instruments are the illustration, not identifications of particular gifts.
    1 Corinthians 14:9 So also with you: unless you speak an intelligible word with your tongue, how will what is spoken be known? For you will be speaking into the air.

1 Corinthians 14:8
For if a trumpet gives an unclear sound, who will prepare for battle?
  Numbers 10:9 — thematic parallel: Both connect a trumpet signal with war. Numbers gives an actual instruction for signaling against enemies; Paul uses the failure of an unclear trumpet to prepare people for battle as an illustration of intelligibility, without importing the whole military setting into worship.
    Numbers 10:9 But if you go out to war in your land against the adversaries who resist you, you shall give a signal with the trumpets; and you shall be remembered before the Lord, and shall be delivered from your enemies.

1 Corinthians 14:9
So also with you: unless you speak an intelligible word with your tongue, how will what is spoken be known? For you will be speaking into the air.
  1 Corinthians 14:19 — teaching parallel: The later preference for five words with the mind that instruct others provides a concrete application of the requirement for an intelligible word. It contrasts this teaching benefit with ten thousand words in a tongue.
    1 Corinthians 14:19 But in Church I would rather speak five words with my mind, so that I may instruct others too, than ten thousand words in a tongue.

1 Corinthians 14:10

    1 Corinthians 14:11 Therefore, if I do not know the meaning of the voice, I will be a foreigner to the speaker, and the speaker a foreigner to me.

1 Corinthians 14:11
Therefore, if I do not know the meaning of the voice, I will be a foreigner to the speaker, and the speaker a foreigner to me.
  1 Corinthians 14:9 — teaching parallel: The mutual foreignness caused by not knowing a voice's meaning develops the earlier warning that an unintelligible word is not known by the hearer. Foreigner here is the stated relation of incomprehension, not an identification of an ethnic group.
    1 Corinthians 14:9 So also with you: unless you speak an intelligible word with your tongue, how will what is spoken be known? For you will be speaking into the air.

1 Corinthians 14:12

  1 Corinthians 12:7 — teaching parallel: The earlier description gives each person's manifestation of the Spirit for the common benefit.     1 Corinthians 12:7 To each is given the manifestation of the Spirit for the common benefit.

1 Corinthians 14:13
