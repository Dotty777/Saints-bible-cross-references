Mark 1:1

  1. John 20:31 thematic parallel

    Mark introduces Jesus Christ as Son of God; John's written-purpose statement likewise joins Jesus, the Christ and the Son of God. John explains believing and life in His name, while Mark's verse introduces the gospel rather than stating that whole purpose.

Mark 1:2

  1. Malachi 3:1 quotation

    The announced messenger preparing the way corresponds to Malachi's messenger clause. Mark introduces the combined citation under Isaiah's name; the following wilderness-voice verse corresponds to Isaiah 40:3. Malachi's supplied wording has the way before My face, while Mark has Thy face and Thy way; the attribution and wording are preserved rather than silently corrected.

    Read matching passage

    Pinned target record

  2. Exodus 23:20 allusion

    The phrase about sending a messenger before Thy face also recalls Exodus' I send My angel before your face. Exodus concerns guidance into a prepared land; Mark uses the preparatory message in its opening about John. The shared sending formula does not identify John as the angel in Exodus.
