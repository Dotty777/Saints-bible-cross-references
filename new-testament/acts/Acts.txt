Acts 1:1
In my first account, Theophilus, I wrote about all that Jesus began both to do and to teach
  Luke 1:1–1:4 — thematic parallel: Both accounts address Theophilus and describe the writing of a narrative. Acts recalls its first account about Jesus' deeds and teaching; Luke explains its orderly account and purpose of assurance. The shared addressee is not given an additional unrecorded biography.
    Luke 1:1 Since many have undertaken to compile an account of the events fulfilled among us,
    Luke 1:2 just as those who from the beginning were eyewitnesses and servants of the word handed them down to us,
    Luke 1:3 it seemed good to me also, having followed everything carefully from the beginning, to write an orderly account for you, most excellent Theophilus,
    Luke 1:4 so that you may know the certainty of the matters in which you have been instructed.

  Luke 24:50–24:51 — narrative parallel: Both describe Jesus being taken into heaven after His ministry with the disciples. Acts adds Spirit-mediated instructions to chosen apostles; Luke describes the Bethany setting and blessing. Luke does not repeat every detail of the Spirit's role stated in Acts.
    Luke 24:50 And He led them out as far as toward Bethany, and lifted His hands and blessed them.
    Luke 24:51 And it happened that while He blessed them, He parted from them and was carried up into heaven.

    Luke 24:49 And behold, I am sending out the promise of My Father upon you; but remain in the city until you are clothed with power from on high.”

Acts' preceding cloud belongs to His departure; Matthew adds power, glory, and mourning. The comparison does not fix the date or collapse departure and return into one event.
    Matthew 24:30 Then the sign of the Son of Man will appear in heaven, and then all the tribes of the earth will mourn; they will see the Son of Man coming upon the clouds of heaven with power and great glory.

  Psalms 68:26 — quotation: Peter applies the deserted-dwelling Psalm language to Judas. The Psalm speaks of their homestead and dwellings; Acts gives the application in the singular. The original Psalm does not name Judas, and its plural wording is not silently rewritten.

Reviewed units still unfinished
Acts 1:23: Joseph Barsabbas/Justus and Matthias are named as the two candidates here; no other same-name person or the unnamed members of another disciple group is identified with either without evidence.
Acts 2:9: This particular list of peoples and regions is not equated with later church addressees or used to infer that named visitors founded churches in their homelands.
Acts 2:10: The continued regional and visitor list has no sufficiently close retained counterpart. Shared place-names alone are not used to identify later people or to assert an unrecorded founding of the Roman church.
Acts 3:3: The man's particular request for alms from Peter and John is not assigned to another beggar encounter or treated as a verbatim fulfillment of a general giving command.
Acts 3:5: The man's expectation of receiving something is retained as stated. It is not equated with the explicit faith-for-healing perception in the separate Lystra narrative.
Acts 5:24: The officials' particular perplexity about the empty prison and its consequences has no sufficiently useful retained comparison beyond the already linked release and report details.
Acts 5:36: Gamaliel's specific Theudas account is retained without identifying this man with an externally dated namesake or treating an unrelated group with a similar number as the same movement.
Acts 5:37: Judas the Galilean and this registration are not identified with another Judas or a separately narrated census without an explicit link in the saved text. No unrecorded chronology is imposed.
Acts 6:9: The named Freedmen's synagogue and regional membership are particular to this dispute notice. A generic dispute elsewhere does not identify this congregation or establish Saul's membership in it; no such association is inferred.
Acts 8:24: Simon's specific request that the others pray so the threatened things not happen is retained without forcing a different intercession request into his situation or deciding an unreported repentance, motive or outcome.
Acts 9:26: The Jerusalem disciples' initial fear and disbelief that Saul was a disciple are particular to this attempted joining. General apostolic contact or another person's objection does not closely reproduce this reaction; no extra encounter or emotional testimony is supplied.
Acts 12:21: The appointed day, royal clothing, tribunal and public address have no sufficiently specific retained counterpart. General scenes of rulers in ceremonial dress would give only a loose resemblance for this unit.
