Galatians 6:10
So then, as we have opportunity, let us do good to everyone, especially to those of the household of faith.
  Ephesians 2:19 — household community image: Both describe the believing community through a household image. Ephesians replaces stranger status with fellow citizenship and God's household; Galatians commands doing good to everyone with special attention to the household of faith. The comparison does not restrict doing good to believers only.
    Ephesians 2:19 So then you are no longer strangers and resident foreigners, but you are fellow citizens with the saints and members of God’s household,

Galatians 6:11
See with what large letters I have written to you in my own hand.
  2 Thessalonians 3:17 — explicit own-hand writing notice: Both call attention to Paul's own handwriting. Galatians names the large letters; Thessalonians identifies the own-hand greeting as a sign in each letter. Neither a medical cause for the large letters nor the extent of the Galatians autograph is supplied.
    2 Thessalonians 3:17 The greeting is in my own hand—Paul’s—which is a sign in every letter. This is how I write.

Galatians 6:12
All who want to make a good outward show in the flesh compel you to receive circumcision, only so that they may not be persecuted for the cross of Christ.
  Galatians 5:11 — internal circumcision and persecution argument: The earlier rhetorical question connects still proclaiming circumcision with whether persecution and the cross's stumbling block would remain. Here Paul attributes the pressure to receive circumcision to avoiding persecution for Christ's cross. The comparison connects the stated issue without naming the advocates or supplying additional motives.
    Galatians 5:11 But I, brothers, if I still proclaim circumcision, why am I still persecuted? Then the stumbling block of the cross has been abolished.

Galatians 6:13
For even those who receive circumcision do not themselves keep the law, but they want you to receive circumcision so that they may boast in your flesh.
  Galatians 5:3 — law obligation and charged failure contrast: The earlier testimony obligates the man receiving circumcision to do the whole law. Here Paul says the circumcision advocates do not themselves keep law while seeking to boast in the readers' flesh. The connection contrasts obligation and the stated failure without assigning that charge to every circumcised person indiscriminately.
    Galatians 5:3 And I testify again to every man who receives circumcision that he is obligated to do the whole law.

Galatians 6:14
But may I never boast except in the cross of our Lord Jesus Christ, through which the world has been crucified to me, and I to the world.
  1 Corinthians 1:30–1:31 — boasting centered on the Lord: Both direct boasting toward the Lord rather than human grounds. Corinthians places believers in Christ and cites boasting in the Lord; Galatians specifies the cross of the Lord Jesus Christ and adds the mutual world/crucifixion image. That additional world statement is not inserted into Corinthians.
    1 Corinthians 1:30 But it is from Him that you are in Christ Jesus, who became for us wisdom from God, and righteousness and sanctification and redemption,
    1 Corinthians 1:31 so that, as it is written, “Let the one who boasts boast in the Lord.”

Galatians 6:15
For neither circumcision nor uncircumcision counts for anything, but a new creation does.
  2 Corinthians 5:17 — new creation in Christ: Both expressly name a new creation in relation to Christ. Corinthians contrasts old things passing with the new; Galatians contrasts new creation with circumcision and uncircumcision counting for anything. The old/new and circumcision formulations remain distinct.
    2 Corinthians 5:17 Therefore, if anyone is in Christ, there is a new creation. The old things passed away; behold, new things have come to be.

Galatians 6:16
And as many as will keep in step with this rule, peace and mercy be upon them, and upon the Israel of God.
  Galatians 6:14–6:15 — immediate context for this rule: Boasting in the cross and the statement that new creation counts while circumcision and uncircumcision do not supply the immediate printed context before keeping in step with this rule. The link locates that rule within the preceding argument without claiming an independently repeated peace blessing or resolving the identity and scope of the Israel of God.
    Galatians 6:14 But may I never boast except in the cross of our Lord Jesus Christ, through which the world has been crucified to me, and I to the world.
    Galatians 6:15 For neither circumcision nor uncircumcision counts for anything, but a new creation does.

Galatians 6:17
From now on, let no one cause me trouble, for I carry the marks of Jesus in my body.
  2 Corinthians 4:10 — carrying identification with Jesus in the body: Both use carrying language in relation to Jesus and the speakers' bodily life. Corinthians says carrying Jesus' dying in the body so His life may be manifested there; Galatians says Paul carries Jesus' marks in his body. The link does not identify particular injuries as the marks or add a new account of their origin.
    2 Corinthians 4:10 always carrying in the body the dying of Jesus, so that the life of Jesus may also be revealed in our body.

Galatians 6:18
The grace of our Lord Jesus Christ be with your spirit, brothers. Amen.
  Philippians 4:23 — shared grace with your spirit closing: Both close with the grace of the Lord Jesus Christ directed to the recipients' spirit. Galatians says our Lord and also names brothers and prints Amen; Philippians says the Lord and has the shorter close. Each actual formula is preserved.
    Philippians 4:23 The grace of the Lord Jesus Christ be with your spirit.
