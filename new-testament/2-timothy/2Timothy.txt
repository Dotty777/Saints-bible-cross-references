2 Timothy 1:1
Paul, an apostle of Christ Jesus through the will of God, according to the promise of life in Christ Jesus,
  2 Corinthians 1:1 — shared apostolic opening: Both introduce Paul as an apostle of Christ Jesus through God's will. Corinthians also names Timothy and its Corinth/Achaia recipients; Timothy's letter adds the promise of life in Christ Jesus. The different opening contents are preserved.
    2 Corinthians 1:1 Paul, an apostle of Christ Jesus through the will of God, and Timothy the brother, to the Church of God in Corinth, together with all the saints in the whole of Achaia:

2 Timothy 1:2
to Timothy, a beloved child: grace, mercy, peace from God the Father and Christ Jesus our Lord.
  1 Timothy 1:2 — shared Timothy address and blessing: Both address Timothy with a child designation and grace, mercy and peace from God and Christ Jesus. The first letter calls him a true child in faith; the second calls him a beloved child. Neither designation supplies a biological parentage claim.
    1 Timothy 1:2 to Timothy, a true child in faith: grace, mercy, peace from God the Father and Christ Jesus our Lord.

2 Timothy 1:3
I thank God, whom I serve, as my forebears did, with a pure conscience, as I remember you unceasingly in my petitions night and day,
  Romans 1:9–1:10 — continuing prayer remembrance: Both describe Paul's service to God and continual remembrance of the recipients in prayer. Romans also asks for a possible journey by God's will; Timothy's letter names a pure conscience and service as his forebears did. The recipients and expressed details differ.
    Romans 1:9 For God, whom I serve in my spirit in the gospel of His Son, is my witness that I remember you without ceasing,
    Romans 1:10 always in my prayers asking whether somehow now at last I may be granted a way, by the will of God, to come to you.

2 Timothy 1:4
longing to see you, remembering your tears, so that I may be filled with joy,
  Philippians 1:8 — longing for the recipient: Both express Paul's longing for the addressed believers. Philippians names affection in Christ Jesus; Timothy's letter connects longing with remembered tears and hoped-for joy. No occasion for the tears or matching visit is supplied.
    Philippians 1:8 For God is my witness how I long for all of you with the tender affection of Christ Jesus.

2 Timothy 1:5
having been reminded of the sincere faith in you, which dwelt first in your grandmother Lois and your mother Eunice, and I am convinced that it dwells also in you.
  Acts 16:1–16:2 — Timothy maternal faith context: Acts names Timothy and describes his mother as a Jewish believer; the letter names the sincere faith in his grandmother Lois and mother Eunice before speaking of his own. Acts does not name either woman or supply the whole three-generation statement.
    Acts 16:1 He also came to Derbe and Lystra. There was a disciple there named Timothy, the son of a believing Jewish woman, but of a Greek father.
    Acts 16:2 He was well spoken of by the brothers in Lystra and Iconium.

2 Timothy 1:6
For this reason I remind you to rekindle God’s gift, which is in you through the laying on of my hands.
  1 Timothy 4:14 — gift and laying on of hands: Both connect Timothy's gift with a laying on of hands. The first letter names prophecy and the elders' hands; the second names Paul's hands and urges rekindling the gift. Those agents and formulations are not silently made identical.
    1 Timothy 4:14 Do not neglect the gift in you, which was given to you through prophecy with the laying on of the hands of the council of elders.

2 Timothy 1:7
For God did not give us a spirit of cowardice, but of power and love and self-discipline.
  Romans 8:15 — divine gift contrasted with fear: Both contrast a received spirit with fear. Romans distinguishes a spirit of slavery leading to fear from the Spirit of adoption; Timothy's letter contrasts cowardice with power, love and self-discipline. The printed capitalization and different lists remain distinct.
    Romans 8:15 For you did not receive a spirit of slavery leading again to fear, but you received the Spirit of adoption as sons, in whom we cry, “Abba, Father.”

2 Timothy 1:8
Therefore do not be ashamed of the testimony of our Lord or of me, His prisoner, but share suffering for the gospel according to the power of God,
  Romans 1:16 — not being ashamed of the gospel: Paul's statement that he is not ashamed of the gospel corresponds to the command not to be ashamed of the Lord's testimony or His prisoner. Both name God's power; Timothy's letter also asks shared suffering without identifying a prison location in this unit.
    Romans 1:16 For I am not ashamed of the gospel, for it is God’s power for salvation to everyone who believes, to the Jew first and also to the Greek.

2 Timothy 1:9
who saved us and called us with a holy calling, not according to our works but according to His own purpose and grace, which was given to us in Christ Jesus before the times of the ages,
  Titus 3:5 — salvation apart from meritorious works: Both attribute salvation to God's provision rather than the recipients' works. Titus names mercy, washing and Spirit renewal; Timothy's letter names a holy calling, purpose and grace in Christ before the ages. The different grounds and accompanying descriptions remain distinct.
    Titus 3:5 He saved us, not on the basis of works done by us in righteousness, but according to His mercy, through the washing of regeneration and renewal by the Holy Spirit,

2 Timothy 1:10
but has now been revealed through the appearing of our Savior Christ Jesus, who abolished death and brought life and incorruptibility to light through the gospel.
  1 Corinthians 15:54–15:57 — Christ and victory over death: Both relate Christ to death's defeat. Corinthians looks to mortality putting on immortality and victory through the Lord Jesus Christ; Timothy's letter names the Savior's appearing, abolishing death and revealing life and incorruptibility through the gospel. The temporal settings and images differ.
    1 Corinthians 15:54 But when this corruptible puts on incorruption and this mortal puts on immortality, then the written saying will come to pass: “Death has been swallowed up in victory.”
    1 Corinthians 15:55 “Where, death, is your victory? Where, death, is your sting?”
    1 Corinthians 15:56 The sting of death is sin, and the power of sin is the law.
    1 Corinthians 15:57 But thanks be to God, who gives us the victory through our Lord Jesus Christ.

2 Timothy 1:11
For this I was appointed a herald and an apostle and a teacher.
  1 Timothy 2:7 — shared herald apostle teacher appointment: Both name Paul's appointment as herald, apostle and teacher. The first letter explicitly names nations and faith and truth; the actual second-letter unit has the shorter appointment statement. The missing nations qualifier is not inserted into it.
    1 Timothy 2:7 For this I was appointed a herald and an apostle—I speak truth, I do not lie—a teacher of nations in faith and truth.

2 Timothy 1:12
For this reason I also suffer these things, but I am not ashamed, for I know whom I have believed, and I am persuaded that He is able to guard my deposit for that day.
  2 Timothy 1:14 — guarding and deposit language: The statement that He is able to guard my deposit for that day is set beside Timothy's command to guard the good deposit through the indwelling Holy Spirit. The repeated guarding/deposit language has different agents and possessives; it does not settle every proposed interpretation of the deposit.
    2 Timothy 1:14 Guard the good deposit through the Holy Spirit who dwells in us.

2 Timothy 1:13
Keep the pattern of sound words that you heard from me, in faith and love in Christ Jesus.
  1 Timothy 6:3 — sound words and faithful teaching: Both refer to sound words in a teaching context. The first letter names the Lord Jesus Christ's words and teaching according to godliness; the second directs Timothy to retain the pattern heard from Paul in faith and love. The speaker formulations remain distinct.
    1 Timothy 6:3 If anyone teaches differently and does not adhere to sound words, those of our Lord Jesus Christ, and the teaching according to godliness,

2 Timothy 1:14
Guard the good deposit through the Holy Spirit who dwells in us.
  1 Timothy 6:20 — guarding an entrusted deposit: Both command guarding what is entrusted. The first letter also rejects profane empty talk and falsely named knowledge; the second explicitly names the indwelling Holy Spirit as the means of guarding. The extra warning and agency are not exchanged as identical clauses.
    1 Timothy 6:20 O Timothy, guard what has been entrusted, turning away from profane empty talk and the opposing claims of falsely named knowledge.

2 Timothy 1:15
You know this, that all those in Asia turned away from me, among whom are Phygelus and Hermogenes.
  2 Timothy 4:16 — distinct reports of desertion: Both report people deserting Paul. Here those in Asia and two named men are specified; the later first-defense notice says no one stood with him and includes a prayer of non-imputation. The two groups and occasions are not asserted to be identical.
    2 Timothy 4:16 At my first defense no one came to my side, but all deserted me. May it not be counted against them.

2 Timothy 1:16
May the Lord give mercy to the household of Onesiphorus, because he often refreshed me and was not ashamed of my chain,
  2 Timothy 4:19 — named Onesiphorus household: The same named household of Onesiphorus receives a greeting later in the letter. Here the mercy request is connected with his refreshing service and lack of shame over Paul's chain. The household greeting does not establish that Onesiphorus had died.
    2 Timothy 4:19 Greet Prisca and Aquila and the household of Onesiphorus.

2 Timothy 1:17
but when he came to Rome, he earnestly searched for me and found me.
  2 Timothy 1:16 — explicit internal personal antecedent: The preceding named Onesiphorus supplies the antecedent of he in the account of coming to Rome, searching and finding Paul. His refreshing service and response to the chain form its immediate context; no unprinted journey date or death is inferred.
    2 Timothy 1:16 May the Lord give mercy to the household of Onesiphorus, because he often refreshed me and was not ashamed of my chain,

2 Timothy 1:18
May the Lord grant him to find mercy from the Lord on that day. And you know very well how much service he gave in Ephesus.
  2 Timothy 1:16 — repeated mercy request and service context: Both pray for the Lord's mercy in relation to Onesiphorus and recall his service. The earlier request names his household and refreshing help; this one names finding mercy on that day and service in Ephesus. The recipients and temporal wording remain as supplied.
    2 Timothy 1:16 May the Lord give mercy to the household of Onesiphorus, because he often refreshed me and was not ashamed of my chain,

2 Timothy 2:1
You, then, my child, be strengthened in the grace that is in Christ Jesus,
  Ephesians 6:10 — strength in Christ and the Lord: Both urge divine strengthening. Ephesians names being strengthened in the Lord and the might of His strength; Timothy's letter names grace in Christ Jesus and addresses a child. The corresponding exhortations do not identify every part of their surrounding instructions.
    Ephesians 6:10 Finally, be strengthened in the Lord and in the might of His strength.

2 Timothy 2:2
and the things you heard from me through many witnesses, entrust these to faithful people who will be able to teach others also.
  Titus 1:9 — faithful teaching transmission: Both require reliable teaching grounded in what has been received. Titus names holding the trustworthy word in accordance with teaching to encourage and refute; Timothy's letter names entrusting heard things to faithful people able to teach others. Their qualifications and teaching chains differ.
    Titus 1:9 holding firmly to the trustworthy word in accordance with the teaching, so that he may be able both to encourage through sound teaching and to refute those who contradict it.

2 Timothy 2:3
Share suffering as a good soldier of Christ Jesus.
  Philippians 1:29–1:30 — sharing suffering for Christ: Both speak of suffering in relation to Christ and shared conflict. Philippians names the granted suffering and same struggle seen in Paul; Timothy's letter uses the good-soldier image for sharing hardship. The military image is not inserted into Philippians.
    Philippians 1:29 because it has been graciously granted to you on behalf of Christ not only to believe in Him but also to suffer for Him,
    Philippians 1:30 having the same struggle which you saw in me and now hear that I have.

2 Timothy 2:4
No one serving as a soldier entangles himself in the affairs of ordinary life, so that he may please the one who enlisted him.
  1 Corinthians 9:7 — different uses of soldier service: Both use soldier service as an analogy. Corinthians asks who serves at his own expense in an argument about ministry support; Timothy's letter describes avoiding ordinary-life entanglement to please the enlisting person. The financial question and undivided-service point remain distinct.
    1 Corinthians 9:7 Who ever serves as a soldier at his own expense? Who plants a vineyard and does not eat its fruit? Who shepherds a flock and does not share in the flock’s milk?

2 Timothy 2:5
And if someone competes as an athlete, he is not crowned unless he competes according to the rules.
  1 Corinthians 9:24–9:25 — athletic competition and crown: Both relate athletic competition to a crown. Corinthians names runners and competitors exercising self-control for a perishable wreath, contrasted with an imperishable one; Timothy's letter emphasizes competing according to the rules. Their particular conditions are preserved.
    1 Corinthians 9:24 Do you not know that all the runners in a stadium run, but one receives the prize? Run in such a way that you may obtain it.
    1 Corinthians 9:25 Everyone who competes exercises self-control in all things. They do so to receive a perishable wreath, but we an imperishable one.

2 Timothy 2:6
The farmer who labors must be first to share in the fruits.
  1 Corinthians 9:10 — farming labor and sharing harvest: Both connect agricultural labor with a share of its yield. Corinthians names the plowman and thresher working in hope of sharing; Timothy's letter names the laboring farmer's first share in the fruits. Their precise workers and first-share wording differ.
    1 Corinthians 9:10 or does He surely say it for our sake? For it was written for our sake, because the plowman ought to plow in hope, and the thresher in hope of sharing.

2 Timothy 2:7
Consider what I say, for the Lord will give you understanding in all things.
  Luke 24:45 — understanding supplied by the Lord: The promise that the Lord will give understanding relates to Jesus opening their mind to understand the Scriptures. Timothy is asked to consider the preceding sayings; Luke records a specific encounter. The comparison does not make these the same event or explanatory method.
    Luke 24:45 Then He opened their mind to understand the Scriptures

2 Timothy 2:8
Remember Jesus Christ, raised from the dead, from David’s seed, according to my gospel,
  Romans 1:3–1:4 — Davidic descent and resurrection: Both join Jesus Christ's Davidic descent with resurrection from the dead in a gospel statement. Romans distinguishes fleshly descent and being designated as God's Son in power; Timothy's letter gives a brief remembrance of the risen Jesus from David's seed. The fuller predicates remain in Romans.
    Romans 1:3 concerning His Son, who came from David’s seed according to the flesh,
    Romans 1:4 who was designated Son of God in power according to the Spirit of holiness by resurrection from the dead, Jesus Christ our Lord,

2 Timothy 2:9
for which I suffer hardship even to chains as a criminal. But the word of God has not been chained.
  Philippians 1:12–1:14 — bonds and continuing gospel proclamation: Both set Paul's imprisonment alongside the gospel's advance. Philippians names bonds becoming known and others speaking boldly; Timothy's letter says he suffers chains as a criminal but God's word is not chained. The passages do not identify these chains as the same imprisonment, and their stated effects remain distinct.
    Philippians 1:12 Now I want you to know, brothers, that my circumstances have instead served to advance the gospel,
    Philippians 1:13 so that my chains have become known as being in Christ throughout the whole praetorium and to all the rest,
    Philippians 1:14 and most of the brothers, having gained confidence in the Lord because of my chains, dare much more to speak the word without fear.

2 Timothy 2:10
For this reason I endure all things for the chosen ones, so that they too may obtain the salvation in Christ Jesus with eternal glory.
  2 Corinthians 4:15–4:17 — enduring suffering for others and glory: Both place Paul's suffering and endurance in relation to the recipients' benefit and glory. Corinthians names all things for their sake, not losing heart and affliction producing glory; Timothy's letter names the chosen ones obtaining salvation in Christ with eternal glory. The stated aims remain distinct.
    2 Corinthians 4:15 For all things are for your sake, so that grace, having increased through the many, may cause thanksgiving to abound to the glory of God.
    2 Corinthians 4:16 Therefore we do not lose heart; but even if our outer person is wasting away, our inner person is being renewed day by day.
    2 Corinthians 4:17 For the momentary lightness of our affliction produces for us an eternal weight of glory beyond all measure,

2 Timothy 2:11
The saying is trustworthy: for if we died with Him, we will also live with Him;
  Romans 6:8 — dying and living with Christ: Both state that dying with Christ is followed by living with Him. Romans places the statement in its death-and-resurrection argument; Timothy's letter gives it within a trustworthy saying. The shorter conditional line is preserved rather than expanded with every Romans clause.
    Romans 6:8 And if we died with Christ, we believe that we will also live with Him,

2 Timothy 2:12
if we endure, we will also reign with Him; if we deny Him, He also will deny us;
  Luke 22:28–22:30 — remaining through trials and kingdom sharing: Jesus relates His companions' remaining through trials to appointing them a kingdom and sharing His table and thrones. This corresponds to enduring and reigning with Him in the saying. Luke's particular disciple promises are not assigned indiscriminately to everyone in Timothy's we.
    Luke 22:28 And you are those who have remained with Me in My trials.
    Luke 22:29 And I assign to you a kingdom, just as My Father assigned one to Me,
    Luke 22:30 so that you may eat and drink at My table in My kingdom, and you will sit on thrones judging the twelve tribes of Israel.
  Matthew 10:33 — denial and reciprocal denial: Both say that denying Christ is met by His denial. Matthew names denying before people and denial before the heavenly Father; Timothy's saying gives the shorter reciprocal conditional. Neither passage's additional setting is silently inserted into the other.
    Matthew 10:33 But whoever denies Me before people, I too will deny before My Father in the heavens.

2 Timothy 2:13
if we are unfaithful, He remains faithful, for He cannot deny Himself.
  Romans 3:3–3:4 — faithfulness despite human unfaithfulness: Both distinguish human unfaithfulness from continuing divine faithfulness. Romans asks whether unfaithfulness nullifies God's faithfulness and affirms God as true; Timothy's saying says He remains faithful and cannot deny Himself. Their pronouns and stated reasoning remain distinct.
    Romans 3:3 For what if some were unfaithful? Will their unfaithfulness nullify God’s faithfulness?
    Romans 3:4 May it never be! But let God be true, and every human being a liar, just as it is written, “So that Thou mayest be justified in Thy words, and wilt prevail when Thou art judged.”

2 Timothy 2:14
Remind them of these things, charging them before God not to quarrel over words, which does no good and leads to the ruin of those who hear.
  1 Timothy 6:4 — word disputes and harmful effects: Both reject quarrels over words and describe harmful effects. The first letter lists envy, strife, slanders and evil suspicions; the second says the quarrel profits nothing and ruins hearers. Their diagnostic descriptions and addressed situations differ.
    1 Timothy 6:4 he has become conceited, knowing nothing, but is sick with disputes and battles over words, from which come envy, strife, slanders, evil suspicions,

2 Timothy 2:15
Be diligent to present yourself approved to God, a worker with no cause for shame, cutting a straight course for the word of truth.
  2 Corinthians 4:2 — faithful handling of the word before God: Both connect ministry of God's word and truth with presentation before God. Corinthians refuses tampering with God's word and commends open truth to conscience; Timothy's letter names the approved unashamed worker cutting a straight course for the word of truth. The craft metaphor is not recast as a division scheme.
    2 Corinthians 4:2 But we have renounced the secret and shameful things, not walking in cunning or tampering with the word of God, but commending ourselves to every human conscience before God by making the truth known.

2 Timothy 2:16
But avoid profane empty talk, for they will advance into further ungodliness,
  1 Timothy 6:20 — profane empty talk warning: Both specifically warn against profane empty talk. The first letter links avoidance to guarding what is entrusted and falsely named knowledge; the second names further advance in ungodliness. The extra terms and consequence remain distinct.
    1 Timothy 6:20 O Timothy, guard what has been entrusted, turning away from profane empty talk and the opposing claims of falsely named knowledge.

2 Timothy 2:17
and their word will spread like gangrene. Among them are Hymenaeus and Philetus,
  1 Timothy 1:19–1:20 — Hymenaeus in faith-error contexts: Both name Hymenaeus in a context of faith going wrong. The first letter also names Alexander and its stated discipline; the second names Philetus and compares the spreading word with gangrene. The companions and descriptions are not conflated.
    1 Timothy 1:19 holding faith and a good conscience, which some have thrust aside and suffered shipwreck concerning the faith.
    1 Timothy 1:20 Among these are Hymenaeus and Alexander, whom I handed over to Satan so that they might be disciplined not to blaspheme.

2 Timothy 2:18
who missed the mark concerning truth, saying that the resurrection has already happened, and overturn the faith of some.
  1 Corinthians 15:12–15:14 — different resurrection errors and faith: Both challenge resurrection-related teaching that threatens faith. Corinthians addresses the claim that there is no resurrection of the dead and its implications for Christ and empty faith; Timothy's letter says these men claim it already happened. The denial and already-past claims remain different.
    1 Corinthians 15:12 Now if Christ is proclaimed as raised from the dead, how can some among you say that there is no resurrection of the dead?
    1 Corinthians 15:13 But if there is no resurrection of the dead, neither has Christ been raised.
    1 Corinthians 15:14 And if Christ has not been raised, then our proclamation is also empty, and your faith is empty too.

2 Timothy 2:19
Nevertheless, the firm foundation of God stands, having this seal: “The Lord knew those who are His,” and, “Let everyone who names the name of the Lord depart from unrighteousness.”
  Numbers 16:5 — scriptural knowledge-of-His-own formula: The Lord-knew-those-who-are-His inscription corresponds to Moses' saying that God has looked upon and known those who are His in the Korah dispute. The divine titles and wording remain distinct; the rest of the foundation seal and departure-from-unrighteousness instruction are not supplied as the Numbers wording.
    Numbers 16:5 And he said to Korah and all his congregation, God has looked upon and known those who are His and those who are holy, and has brought them near to Himself. Those He chose for Himself He brought near to Himself.

2 Timothy 2:20
Now in a great house there are vessels not only of gold and silver but also of wood and clay, some for honor and some for dishonor.
  Romans 9:21 — honor and dishonor vessel imagery: Both use vessels for honor and dishonor. Romans depicts the potter's authority over clay; Timothy's letter depicts varied materials in a great house. The different analogies do not assign particular metals or a complete predestination interpretation to the household image.
    Romans 9:21 Or does the potter not have authority over the clay, from the same lump to make one vessel for honor and another for dishonor?

2 Timothy 2:21
Therefore if someone cleanses himself from these things, he will be a vessel for honor, sanctified, useful to the Master, prepared for every good work.
  2 Timothy 3:16–3:17 — preparation for every good work: Both lead to preparation for every good work. The later statement connects this with Scripture's teaching and training; here cleansing leads to a sanctified vessel useful to the Master. The different forms of preparation remain as printed.
    2 Timothy 3:16 All Scripture is God-breathed and profitable for teaching, for reproof, for correction, for training in righteousness,
    2 Timothy 3:17 so that the person of God may be capable, fully equipped for every good work.

2 Timothy 2:22
But flee youthful desires, and pursue righteousness, faith, love, peace with those who call upon the Lord from a pure heart.
  1 Timothy 6:11 — fleeing and pursuing virtue: Both pair fleeing with pursuing righteousness, faith and love. The first letter also names godliness, endurance and gentleness; the second specifies youthful desires, peace and a company calling on the Lord from a pure heart. The distinct lists remain intact.
    1 Timothy 6:11 But you, O man of God, flee these things. Pursue righteousness, godliness, faith, love, endurance, gentleness.

2 Timothy 2:23
And reject foolish and ignorant disputes, knowing that they give birth to fights.
  Titus 3:9 — foolish disputes warning: Both reject foolish disputes and associated conflict. Titus also names genealogies and legal quarrels as unprofitable and futile; Timothy's letter names ignorant disputes as giving birth to fights. The instruction is not a condemnation of every biblical genealogy.
    Titus 3:9 But avoid foolish controversies, genealogies, disputes, and quarrels about the law, for they are unprofitable and futile.

2 Timothy 2:24
But a slave of the Lord must not fight, but must be gentle toward all, able to teach, patient with wrong,
  1 Timothy 3:2–3:3 — gentle teachable ministry conduct: Both name ability to teach and conduct opposed to fighting. The first letter lists an overseer's qualifications, including gentleness and being peaceable; the second describes the Lord's slave as gentle toward all and patient with wrong. The titles and complete lists differ.
    1 Timothy 3:2 Therefore the overseer must be above reproach, a husband of one wife, sober, self-controlled, orderly, hospitable, able to teach,
    1 Timothy 3:3 not given to wine, not a striker, but gentle, peaceable, not a lover of money,

2 Timothy 2:25
instructing opponents with gentleness, in case God may give them repentance toward the knowledge of truth,
  Galatians 6:1 — gentle restoration and correction: Both name gentleness in dealing with someone in error. Galatians calls spiritual people to restore the overtaken person while watching themselves; Timothy's letter names instruction of opponents in hope of God granting repentance toward truth. The roles and stated outcomes remain distinct.
    Galatians 6:1 Brothers, even if a person is overtaken in some transgression, you who are spiritual are to restore such a person in a spirit of gentleness, watching yourself, lest you too be tempted.

2 Timothy 2:26
and they may regain their sobriety and escape the devil’s snare, having been captured by him to do his will.
  1 Timothy 3:7 — devil snare image: Both use the devil's snare image. The first letter warns an overseer about falling into reproach and that snare; the second speaks of regaining sobriety and escaping it. The image does not by itself settle every pronoun referent in the final captivity-and-will clause.
    1 Timothy 3:7 And he must also have a good testimony from those outside, lest he fall into reproach and the snare of the devil.

2 Timothy 3:1
But know this: in the last days difficult times will come.
  1 Timothy 4:1 — future difficult-times warnings: Both warn of a coming period of religious danger. First Timothy says the Spirit speaks about later times and departure from faith; Second Timothy says last days and difficult times. The time expressions and particular warnings are not identified as a dated timetable.
    1 Timothy 4:1 But the Spirit says clearly that in later times some will depart from the faith, paying attention to deceiving spirits and teachings of demons,

2 Timothy 3:2
For people will be lovers of themselves, lovers of money, boastful, arrogant, blasphemous, disobedient to parents, ungrateful, unholy,
  Romans 1:29–1:30 — overlapping vice catalogues: Both lists include arrogance, boasting and disobedience to parents, alongside greed or love of money. Romans gives its wider account of wrongdoing; Timothy's letter introduces conduct in difficult times. Neither list is silently made identical to the other.
    Romans 1:29 filled with all unrighteousness, wickedness, greed, evil; full of envy, murder, strife, deceit, malice; whisperers,
    Romans 1:30 slanderers, haters of God, insolent, proud, boastful, inventors of evils, disobedient to parents,

2 Timothy 3:3
without natural affection, irreconcilable, slanderers, without self-control, savage, not loving the good,
  Romans 1:29–1:31 — slander and absent affection: Both vice catalogues name slander and the lack of natural affection. Each includes other faults and its own context. Timothy's irreconcilability, savagery and lack of self-control are not supplied as additional words in Romans.
    Romans 1:29 filled with all unrighteousness, wickedness, greed, evil; full of envy, murder, strife, deceit, malice; whisperers,
    Romans 1:30 slanderers, haters of God, insolent, proud, boastful, inventors of evils, disobedient to parents,

2 Timothy 3:4
traitors, reckless, conceited, lovers of pleasure rather than lovers of God,
  1 John 2:15–2:17 — misdirected love contrasted with God: Loving pleasure rather than God corresponds to John's contrast between loving the world and the love of the Father. John specifies worldly desires and their passing; Timothy's list also names betrayal, recklessness and conceit. Pleasure and world are not treated as identical printed categories.
    1 John 2:15 Do not love the world or the things in the world. If anyone loves the world, the love of the Father is not in him.
    1 John 2:16 For everything in the world—the desire of the flesh, the desire of the eyes, and the boastfulness of life—is not from the Father, but is from the world.
    1 John 2:17 And the world is passing away, along with its desire; but the one who does the will of God abides forever.

2 Timothy 3:5
having a form of godliness but denying its power. Turn away from these also.
  Titus 1:16 — profession and denial contrast: Both expose a gap between outward religious profession and denial. Titus says people profess knowing God but deny Him by works; Timothy's letter names a form of godliness with denial of its power. The different objects of denial are preserved.
    Titus 1:16 They profess to know God, but by their deeds they deny Him, being detestable and disobedient and unfit for every good work.

2 Timothy 3:6
For among these are those who slip into houses and take captive weak women, laden with sins, led by various desires,
  2 Peter 2:18–2:19 — desire enticement and captivity: Both place captivity or enticement alongside desires. Peter explicitly names enticing through fleshly desires and the teachers' own slavery to corruption; Timothy's letter names entering houses and taking captive particular weak women described as led by various desires. It does not independently state every method of gaining control, extend that description to all women or supply an unprinted sexual act.
    2 Peter 2:18 For, uttering inflated words of emptiness, they entice through fleshly desires and debaucheries those who are truly escaping from people who live in error,
    2 Peter 2:19 promising them freedom while they themselves are slaves of corruption; for by whatever someone has been overcome, to this he has also been enslaved.

2 Timothy 3:7
always learning and never able to come to the knowledge of truth.
  2 Timothy 2:25 — knowledge of truth contrast: Always learning without arriving at knowledge of truth contrasts with the hope that God may grant repentance toward knowledge of truth. The source's captive-learning context and the opponents addressed in the correction instruction are not declared the same people.
    2 Timothy 2:25 instructing opponents with gentleness, in case God may give them repentance toward the knowledge of truth,

2 Timothy 3:8
And just as Jannes and Jambres opposed Moses, so these too oppose the truth, people corrupted in mind, disqualified concerning the faith.
  Exodus 7:10–7:12 — Moses-opposition narrative comparison: Exodus records Pharaoh's experts countering Moses and Aaron through rival staff signs. This provides a Moses-opposition comparison for the letter's Jannes-and-Jambres analogy. Exodus does not name those two men or independently establish each man's identity.
    Exodus 7:10 Moses and Aaron went before Pharaoh and his attendants and did as the Lord commanded. Aaron threw his staff before Pharaoh and his attendants, and it became a dragon.
    Exodus 7:11 Pharaoh summoned the wise men of Egypt and the sorcerers. The Egyptian enchanters did the same by their sorceries.
    Exodus 7:12 Each threw down his staff, and they became dragons. But Aaron’s staff swallowed their staffs.

2 Timothy 3:9
But they will make no further progress, for their folly will be clear to all, as the folly of those men also became.
  Exodus 8:14–8:15 — limits exposed in Moses-opposition context: The enchanters cannot produce the gnats and acknowledge God's finger, giving a specific episode where opposing expertise meets a visible limit. Timothy's letter compares the exposure of its opponents' folly to those earlier men. Exodus does not supply the names Jannes and Jambres.
    Exodus 8:14 The enchanters tried by their sorceries to bring forth gnats, but they could not. The gnats were on people and four-footed animals.
    Exodus 8:15 The enchanters said to Pharaoh, “This is the finger of God.” But Pharaoh’s heart was hardened, and he did not listen to them, as the Lord had said.

2 Timothy 3:10
But you followed my teaching, conduct, purpose, faith, patience, love, endurance,
  1 Corinthians 4:16–4:17 — Paul teaching and Timothy example: Corinthians asks readers to imitate Paul and says Timothy will remind them of his ways and teaching in Christ. Timothy's letter says he followed Paul's teaching and conduct with named virtues. Their teaching/example relation does not identify every virtue or circumstance as the same episode.
    1 Corinthians 4:16 Therefore I appeal to you: imitate me.
    1 Corinthians 4:17 For this reason I sent Timothy to you, who is my beloved and faithful child in the Lord. He will remind you of my ways in Christ Jesus, just as I teach everywhere in every Church.

2 Timothy 3:11
persecutions, sufferings, such as happened to me in Antioch, in Iconium, in Lystra, what persecutions I endured; and out of all of them the Lord rescued me.
  Acts 14:19–14:22 — named places persecution and recovery: Acts narrates Paul's stoning, his rising, and the ministry route through Lystra, Iconium and Antioch. The letter recalls persecutions in those named places and the Lord's rescue. This connects the specific locations and suffering without calculating an unprinted date or making every incident identical.
    Acts 14:19 Jews came from Antioch and Iconium, persuaded the crowds, stoned Paul, and dragged him outside the city, supposing he was dead.
    Acts 14:20 But when the disciples surrounded him, he rose and entered the city. The next day he went with Barnabas to Derbe.
    Acts 14:21 After proclaiming the good news to that city and making many disciples, they returned to Lystra, Iconium, and Antioch,
    Acts 14:22 strengthening the souls of the disciples, encouraging them to remain in the faith, and saying, “Through many afflictions we must enter the kingdom of God.”

2 Timothy 3:12
And all who want to live godly lives in Christ Jesus will be persecuted.
  John 15:20 — persecution of Christ followers: Jesus says that if people persecuted Him they will persecute His servants; Timothy's letter says those seeking godly life in Christ will be persecuted. The slave/master explanation and the godly-life formulation remain distinct.
    John 15:20 Remember the word that I said to you: ‘A slave is not greater than his master.’ If they persecuted Me, they will also persecute you; if they kept My word, they will also keep yours.

2 Timothy 3:13
But evil people and impostors will go from bad to worse, deceiving and being deceived.
  2 Timothy 2:16–2:18 — internal worsening error pattern: Advancing ungodliness and spreading false teaching in the earlier warning form a specific internal counterpart to evil impostors going from bad to worse. The named resurrection-error teachers are not asserted to be every person covered by this later general warning.
    2 Timothy 2:16 But avoid profane empty talk, for they will advance into further ungodliness,
    2 Timothy 2:17 and their word will spread like gangrene. Among them are Hymenaeus and Philetus,
    2 Timothy 2:18 who missed the mark concerning truth, saying that the resurrection has already happened, and overturn the faith of some.

2 Timothy 3:14
But you, remain in the things you learned and became convinced of, knowing those from whom you learned,
  2 Timothy 1:13 — retaining learned instruction: Both tell Timothy to retain instruction received from known teachers. The earlier command explicitly names words heard from Paul in faith and love; this one refers plurally to those from whom he learned. The plural is not reduced to Paul alone.
    2 Timothy 1:13 Keep the pattern of sound words that you heard from me, in faith and love in Christ Jesus.

2 Timothy 3:15
and that from infancy you have known the sacred writings, which are able to make you wise for salvation through faith in Christ Jesus.
  John 5:39–5:40 — Scriptures salvation and Christ: Jesus connects the Scriptures' testimony to Him with coming to Him for life. Timothy's letter names sacred writings known from infancy that make wise for salvation through faith in Christ. Neither passage says bare literacy alone saves or defines the whole later canon's extent.
    John 5:39 You search the Scriptures because you think that in them you have eternal life; and it is they that bear witness concerning Me.
    John 5:40 Yet you are unwilling to come to Me so that you may have life.

2 Timothy 3:16
All Scripture is God-breathed and profitable for teaching, for reproof, for correction, for training in righteousness,
  2 Peter 1:20–1:21 — divine origin of scriptural witness: Both connect scriptural witness with God rather than merely human initiative. Peter speaks of prophecy and people carried by the Holy Spirit; Timothy's letter says all Scripture is God-breathed and names its uses. Prophecy and all Scripture are not silently made identical scopes.
    2 Peter 1:20 knowing this first: that no prophecy of Scripture arises from one’s own interpretation.
    2 Peter 1:21 For prophecy was never borne along by human will, but human beings spoke from God as they were borne along by the Holy Spirit.

2 Timothy 3:17
so that the person of God may be capable, fully equipped for every good work.
  Hebrews 13:21 — equipping for good work: Both name equipping people for good action. Hebrews prays for God to equip the readers to do His will; Timothy's letter gives Scripture's outcome as the person of God being equipped for every good work. Their agents and immediate explanations remain distinct.
    Hebrews 13:21 equip you with every good thing to do His will, working in us what is pleasing in His sight, through Jesus Christ, to whom be glory for the ages of ages. Amen.

2 Timothy 4:1
I charge you before God and Christ Jesus, who is to judge the living and the dead, and by His appearing and His kingdom:
  Acts 10:42 — judge of living and dead: Both explicitly name Christ's judgment of the living and dead. Acts places it in the commanded proclamation about God's appointed judge; Timothy's letter gives a charge before God and Christ and names His appearing and kingdom. The speech settings differ.
    Acts 10:42 He commanded us to proclaim to the people and bear solemn witness that He is the One appointed by God as judge of the living and the dead.

2 Timothy 4:2
proclaim the word; be ready in season and out of season; reprove, rebuke, exhort, with all patience and teaching.
  Titus 2:15 — speaking exhorting and rebuking: Both commission speaking, exhortation and rebuke. Titus names authority and not being disregarded; Timothy's letter names proclaiming the word, readiness in and out of season, patience and teaching. Each charge keeps its specific terms.
    Titus 2:15 Speak these things, encourage, and rebuke with all authority. Let no one disregard you.

2 Timothy 4:3
For a time will come when they will not endure sound teaching, but with itching ears they will heap up teachers for themselves according to their own desires,
  Isaiah 30:9–30:11 — rejecting unwanted instruction: Both portray hearers resisting truthful instruction and seeking a message suited to their own wishes. Isaiah records demands made to prophetic speakers; Timothy's letter describes accumulating teachers according to desires. The addressed people and historical setting are not made identical.
    Isaiah 30:9 For this is a disobedient people, lying sons who did not want to hear God’s law,
    Isaiah 30:10 who say to the prophets, “Do not announce to us,” and to those seeing visions, “Do not speak to us. Instead, speak to us and announce another deception,
    Isaiah 30:11 and turn us from this way, remove this path from us, and remove Israel’s Holy One from us.”

2 Timothy 4:4
and they will turn their hearing away from the truth and turn aside to myths.
  Titus 1:14 — myths and turning from truth: Both contrast truth with myths and turning away. Titus names Jewish myths and commands of people who turn from truth; Timothy's letter names hearing turning from truth toward myths. The particular qualifiers in Titus are not imposed on every hearer here.
    Titus 1:14 not paying attention to Jewish myths and commands of people who turn away from the truth.

2 Timothy 4:5
But you, be sober in all things, endure hardship, do the work of an evangelist, fulfill your service.
  Colossians 4:17 — fulfilling received service: The command to fulfill service corresponds to the instruction that Archippus attend to and fulfill the service received in the Lord. Timothy is also told to be sober, endure and do an evangelist's work; Colossians does not assign that same title to Archippus.
    Colossians 4:17 And say to Archippus, “Attend to the service which you received in the Lord, so that you may fulfill it.”

2 Timothy 4:6
For I am already being poured out, and the time of my departure has arrived.
  Philippians 2:17 — being poured out image: Both use being poured out in a ministry and offering context. Philippians states the image conditionally and relates it to the readers' faith; Timothy's letter says already and names departure's time. No death method or date is supplied by this comparison.
    Philippians 2:17 But even if I am being poured out upon the sacrifice and service of your faith, I rejoice and rejoice together with all of you.

2 Timothy 4:7
I have fought the good fight; I have finished the course; I have kept the faith.
  Acts 20:24 — finishing the ministry course: Paul in Acts speaks of finishing his course and service received from the Lord Jesus; the letter says the course has been finished and the faith kept. The intended goal and completed-course statement are preserved without fixing a chronology from the comparison alone.
    Acts 20:24 But I consider my life of no value to myself, so that I may complete my course and the ministry I received from the Lord Jesus: to bear solemn witness to the good news of God’s grace.

2 Timothy 4:8
From now on the crown of righteousness is laid up for me, which the Lord, the righteous Judge, will award me on that day, and not to me alone but also to all who have loved His appearing.
  James 1:12 — promised crown and persevering love: Both connect receiving a crown with fidelity and love. James names the tested person's crown of life that He promised to those who love Him; Timothy's letter names righteousness's crown, the Lord as righteous Judge, and love of His appearing. The distinct crown titles and actual pronouns remain as printed.
    James 1:12 Blessed is the man who endures a trial, because, having been approved, he will receive the crown of life that He promised to those who love Him.

2 Timothy 4:9
Make every effort to come to me soon,
  2 Timothy 4:21 — repeated personal visit request: The later command repeats the effort to come and specifies before winter, supplying a concrete counterpart to come to me soon. It concerns the same letter's addressed visitor without explaining every name in the following greetings.
    2 Timothy 4:21 Make every effort to come before winter. Eubulus greets you, and Pudens and Linus and Claudia and all the brothers.

2 Timothy 4:10
for Demas deserted me, having loved the present age, and went to Thessalonica; Crescens to Galatia; Titus to Dalmatia.
  Philemon 1:24 — Demas in Paul associate lists: Philemon names Demas among Paul's fellow workers. Timothy's letter reports his desertion and love for the present age and names Thessalonica. The descriptions differ; the comparison does not date the letters or fill in Crescens's and Titus's journeys.
    Philemon 1:24 as do Mark, Aristarchus, Demas, and Luke, my fellow workers.

2 Timothy 4:11
Luke alone is with me. Take Mark and bring him with you, for he is useful to me for service.
  Philemon 1:24 — named Mark and Luke service context: Both explicitly name Luke and Mark among Paul's associates. Philemon calls its group fellow workers; Timothy's letter names Luke's presence and asks for Mark as useful for service. The personal circumstances and tasks remain distinct.
    Philemon 1:24 as do Mark, Aristarchus, Demas, and Luke, my fellow workers.

2 Timothy 4:12
But I sent Tychicus to Ephesus.
  Ephesians 6:21–6:22 — Tychicus sending and messenger role: Both name Tychicus in a sending and ministry context. Ephesians describes his report and encouragement mission; Timothy's letter states sending him to Ephesus. No identical delivery date or additional itinerary is established.
    Ephesians 6:21 Now so that you also may know my circumstances, what I am doing, Tychicus, the beloved brother and faithful servant in the Lord, will make everything known to you.
    Ephesians 6:22 I sent him to you for this very purpose, so that you may know about us and he may encourage your hearts.

  2 Timothy 4:9 — visit request and items to bring: The earlier request that Timothy come soon supplies the concrete visit context for when you come and the cloak, books and parchments request. The link does not infer an illness, a cause for needing the cloak or the contents of the books.

Alexander the coppersmith did me much harm; the Lord will repay him according to his works.
  Romans 2:6 — recompense according to works: The Lord's repayment of Alexander according to his works corresponds to the statement that God repays each according to works. The named coppersmith is not identified as another Alexander from a name alone, nor is a precise penalty supplied.
    Romans 2:6 who will repay each according to his works:

2 Timothy 4:15
You too must guard yourself against him, for he strongly opposed our words.
  2 Timothy 4:14 — explicit named antecedent: Alexander the coppersmith in the preceding notice supplies the referent of him against whom Timothy must guard himself. The instruction adds his opposition to their words, without identifying him with every other named Alexander.
    2 Timothy 4:14 Alexander the coppersmith did me much harm; the Lord will repay him according to his works.

2 Timothy 4:16
At my first defense no one came to my side, but all deserted me. May it not be counted against them.
  Acts 7:60 — prayer for non-imputation: Both ask that wrongdoing not be counted against the people responsible. Stephen prays over his attackers while dying; Paul speaks of those who deserted him at his first defense. The actions and legal circumstances are not treated as identical.
    Acts 7:60 Kneeling down, he cried with a loud voice, “Lord, do not hold this sin against them.” After saying this, he fell asleep.

2 Timothy 4:17
But the Lord stood beside me and strengthened me, so that through me the proclamation might be fulfilled and all the nations might hear, and I was rescued from a lion’s mouth.
  Acts 23:11 — Lord standing by and testimony: Acts says the Lord stood by Paul and connected courage with testimony in Jerusalem and Rome. Timothy's letter says the Lord stood beside and strengthened him for proclamation to the nations. The similar support and witness language does not identify the two occasions.
    Acts 23:11 The following night the Lord stood beside him and said, “Take courage, for as you have borne solemn witness about Me in Jerusalem, so you must also testify in Rome.”
  Psalms 21:22 — lion mouth rescue language: The Psalm's request for rescue from a lion's mouth corresponds to Paul's statement of being rescued from a lion's mouth. The prayer and reported rescue differ; the phrase does not establish that Paul faced a literal lion or identify the two speakers' circumstances.
    Psalms 21:22 Save me from a lion’s mouth and my lowliness from the horns of one-horned animals.

2 Timothy 4:18
The Lord will rescue me from every evil work and save me into His heavenly kingdom. To Him be glory for the ages of ages. Amen.
  Matthew 6:13 — divine deliverance from evil: The prayer to be delivered from evil relates to confidence that the Lord will rescue Paul from every evil work and save him into the heavenly kingdom. The communal prayer and personal assurance have different objects; the actual Matthew unit says evil, without an added one.
    Matthew 6:13 and bring us not into temptation, but deliver us from evil.’

2 Timothy 4:19
Greet Prisca and Aquila and the household of Onesiphorus.
  Romans 16:3–16:5 — named Prisca and Aquila greeting: Both greet the named Prisca and Aquila. Romans also names their work, risk and the Church in their house; Timothy's letter separately greets Onesiphorus's household. The two households are not merged.
    Romans 16:3 Greet Prisca and Aquila, my fellow workers in Christ Jesus,
    Romans 16:4 who risked their own neck for my life, to whom not only I give thanks, but also all the Churches of the Gentiles;
    Romans 16:5 and greet the Church in their house. Greet Epaenetus my beloved, who is the firstfruits of Asia for Christ.

2 Timothy 4:20
Erastus remained in Corinth, but I left Trophimus sick in Miletus.
  Acts 21:29 — Trophimus associated with Paul: Both name Trophimus in association with Paul. Acts explicitly calls him the Ephesian and describes seeing him with Paul; Timothy's letter names leaving him sick in Miletus. It does not date the sickness or make it the cause of the temple accusation, and supplies no new Erastus identity.
    Acts 21:29 For they had previously seen Trophimus the Ephesian with him in the city and supposed that Paul had brought him into the temple.

    2 Timothy 4:9 Make every effort to come to me soon,

2 Timothy 4:22
The Lord be with your spirit. Grace be with you all.
  Galatians 6:18 — Lord grace and spirit closing: Both close with the Lord, grace and the recipients' spirit in view. Galatians explicitly names our Lord Jesus Christ, brothers and Amen; Timothy's letter has the separate Lord-with-your-spirit and grace-with-you-all statements. Its shorter actual wording is not expanded.
    Galatians 6:18 The grace of our Lord Jesus Christ be with your spirit, brothers. Amen.
