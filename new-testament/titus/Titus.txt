Titus 1:1
Paul, a slave of God and an apostle of Jesus Christ, for the faith of God’s chosen ones and the knowledge of the truth that accords with godliness,
  1 Timothy 2:7 — thematic parallel: Both identify Paul's apostolic ministry in relation to faith and truth. Timothy's letter adds herald and teacher of nations, with an assurance of truthfulness; Titus names God's slave, God's chosen ones and truth according with godliness. The distinct ministry descriptions remain intact.
    1 Timothy 2:7 For this I was appointed a herald and an apostle—I speak truth, I do not lie—a teacher of nations in faith and truth.

Titus 1:2
in hope of eternal life, which God, who does not lie, promised before the ages,
  Hebrews 6:18 — thematic parallel: Both connect hope with God's inability to lie and His promise. Hebrews names encouragement through two unchangeable things in a promise-and-oath context; Titus names eternal life promised before the ages. Their promise descriptions and temporal details are not made identical.
    Hebrews 6:18 so that through two unchangeable things, in which it is impossible for God to lie, we who have fled for refuge might have strong encouragement to hold fast the hope set before us.

Titus 1:3
and in His own appointed times He revealed His word through proclamation, with which I was entrusted by the command of God our Savior,
  1 Timothy 1:11 — thematic parallel: Both describe Paul as entrusted with a gospel-related proclamation. Timothy's letter calls it the gospel of the blessed God's glory; Titus describes God's word revealed through proclamation at His appointed times by God our Savior's command. The timing and command clauses remain particular to Titus.
    1 Timothy 1:11 according to the gospel of the glory of the blessed God, with which I was entrusted.

Titus 1:4
to Titus, a genuine child in a shared faith: grace and peace from God the Father and Christ Jesus our Savior.
  1 Timothy 1:2 — thematic parallel: Both address a named colleague as a child in faith and give a blessing from God the Father and Christ Jesus. Titus is a genuine child in shared faith and Christ is called our Savior; Timothy is a true child in faith, Christ is our Lord, and mercy is included. The people and formulas are not merged.
    1 Timothy 1:2 to Timothy, a true child in faith: grace, mercy, peace from God the Father and Christ Jesus our Lord.

Titus 1:5

  Acts 14:23 — thematic parallel: Both describe appointing elders for local believing communities. Acts names each Church and adds prayer, fasting and entrusting to the Lord; Titus directs the left-behind worker in Crete to complete unfinished matters and appoint elders city by city. This does not identify the trips or make city and Church identical territorial definitions.
    Acts 14:23 They appointed elders for them in each Church and, after praying with fasting, entrusted them to the Lord in whom they had believed.

The differing child qualifications remain as printed.

    1 Timothy 3:5 But if someone does not know how to lead his own household, how will he care for the Church of God?

Titus 1:9
holding firmly to the trustworthy word in accordance with the teaching, so that he may be able both to encourage through sound teaching and to refute those who contradict it.
  2 Timothy 4:2 — thematic parallel: Both connect fidelity to the word with teaching that encourages and corrects. Titus describes holding the trustworthy word to encourage through sound teaching and refute contradiction; Timothy is charged to proclaim, reprove, rebuke and exhort with patience and teaching. The holding/proclaiming roles and stated methods differ.
    2 Timothy 4:2 proclaim the word; be ready in season and out of season; reprove, rebuke, exhort, with all patience and teaching.

  Romans 16:17–16:18 — thematic parallel: Both warn about deceptive speech opposed to received teaching. The Roman people are not assigned the latter identification or equated with every circumcised person.

    Romans 16:18 for such people do not serve our Lord Christ, but their own belly, and through smooth speech and flattery they deceive the hearts of the unsuspecting.

Titus 1:11
whose mouths must be stopped. They overturn whole households, teaching what they ought not to teach for the sake of shameful gain.
  1 Timothy 6:5 — thematic parallel: Both link corrupt religious instruction or contention with gain. Timothy's letter describes people supposing godliness to be a means of gain; Titus specifies teaching what should not be taught for shameful gain and overturning households. The household effect and mouths-to-be-stopped wording are not supplied as Timothy's exact description.
    1 Timothy 6:5 constant friction among people corrupted in mind and deprived of the truth, supposing godliness to be a means of gain.

Titus 1:15
To the pure, everything is pure; but to those who are defiled and unbelieving, nothing is pure. Instead, both their mind and their conscience are defiled.
  Romans 14:14–14:15 — thematic parallel: Both relate clean or unclean judgments to a person's inward condition rather than an object alone. Romans explicitly discusses food and the one reckoning it unclean; Titus speaks of pure people versus defiled unbelievers whose mind and conscience are defiled. The food-related brother is not thereby labelled an unbeliever, and the statements are not turned into permission for every deed.
    Romans 14:14 I know and am convinced in the Lord Jesus that nothing is unclean in itself; except that to the one who reckons something unclean, to that person it is unclean.
    Romans 14:15 For if your brother is grieved because of food, you no longer walk according to love. Do not destroy with your food the one for whom Christ died.

Titus 1:16

  1 John 2:4 — thematic parallel: Titus contrasts professing to know God with denying Him by deeds. John contrasts saying I have come to know Him with not keeping His commandments and calls that profession a lie. Both test verbal claims through conduct, with the respective pronouns, disobedience and good-work statements retained.
    1 John 2:4 The one who says, “I have come to know Him,” and does not keep His commandments is a liar, and the truth is not in that person.
