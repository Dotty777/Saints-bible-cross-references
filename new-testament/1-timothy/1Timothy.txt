    Titus 1:3 and in His own appointed times He revealed His word through proclamation, with which I was entrusted by the command of God our Savior,

  2 Timothy 1:2 — thematic parallel: Both letters address Timothy as a child and give grace, mercy and peace from God the Father and Christ Jesus our Lord. This verse calls him a true child in faith; the other calls him beloved, preserving the stated relational description.
    2 Timothy 1:2 to Timothy, a beloved child: grace, mercy, peace from God the Father and Christ Jesus our Lord.

1 Timothy 1:4
nor to pay attention to myths and endless genealogies, which produce speculations rather than God’s stewardship in faith—
  Titus 3:9 — thematic parallel: Both warn against genealogies within disputes that divert from the intended religious task. Timothy names myths, endless genealogies and speculation instead of stewardship in faith; Titus names unprofitable controversies and law quarrels. The targets of criticism are not equated with every scriptural genealogy.
    Titus 3:9 But avoid foolish controversies, genealogies, disputes, and quarrels about the law, for they are unprofitable and futile.

1 Timothy 1:7

  1 Corinthians 8:2 — thematic parallel: The uncomprehending but confident would-be law teachers illustrate a gap between claimed knowledge and understanding. Corinthians also warns that thinking one knows does not mean knowing as one ought, in its food-and-knowledge discussion; it does not identify those readers as these law teachers.
    1 Corinthians 8:2 If anyone thinks he has come to know something, he does not yet know as he ought to know.

1 Timothy 1:8

  Romans 7:12 — thematic parallel: Both affirm the law's goodness. Timothy qualifies its use as lawful, while Romans explicitly calls the law holy and the commandment holy, righteous and good. The affirmation is not converted into identical claims about every use of law.
    Romans 7:12 So the law is holy, and the commandment holy and righteous and good.

Exodus states a penalty; Timothy's list does not state that penalty here or replace striking with an assertion of completed parental murder.
    Exodus 21:15 He who strikes his father or his mother shall surely be put to death.

    Exodus 21:17 And if a man steals one of the sons of Israel, and overpowers him and sells him, and he is found with him, he shall surely die.
