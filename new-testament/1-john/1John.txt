1 John 1:1
What was from the beginning, what we have heard, what we have seen with our eyes, what we looked upon and our hands touched, concerning the word of life—
  Luke 24:39 — Specific passage comparison: The letter names hearing, seeing and hands touching the word of life; Jesus invites seeing His hands and feet and touching Him in Luke. Both give concrete sensory testimony, without identifying the letter’s occasion or witnesses with that scene.
    Luke 24:39 See My hands and My feet, that it is I Myself. Feel Me and see, because a spirit does not have flesh and bones as you see that I have.”

1 John 1:2
and the life was manifested, and we have seen and bear witness and announce to you the eternal life that was with the Father and was manifested to us—
  John 1:1–1:4 — Specific passage comparison: John places the Word with God in the beginning and says life was in Him; the letter announces manifested eternal life that was with the Father. The life and prior-with-God correspondence preserves the distinct Word, God and Father formulations.
    John 1:1 In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God.
    John 1:2 He was in the beginning with God.
    John 1:3 All things came into being through Him, and without Him not one thing came into being. What has come into being
    John 1:4 in Him was life, and the life was the light of humankind.

1 John 1:3
what we have seen and heard we announce also to you, so that you too may have fellowship with us. And our fellowship is with the Father and with His Son Jesus Christ.
  1 Corinthians 1:9 — Specific passage comparison: Paul names fellowship with God’s Son Jesus Christ as the calling of the faithful God. The letter names fellowship with the Father and His Son Jesus Christ and with the announcers; the participants and announced testimony remain distinct.
    1 Corinthians 1:9 God is faithful, through whom you were called into the fellowship of His Son, Jesus Christ our Lord.

1 John 1:4
And we write these things so that our joy may be made full.
  John 15:11 — Specific passage comparison: Jesus gives His spoken words the purpose of His joy being in the disciples and their joy being full. The letter gives its writing the purpose of our joy being made full; the possessives and spoken/written settings remain distinct.
    John 15:11 These things I have spoken to you so that My joy may be in you and your joy may be made full.

1 John 1:5
And this is the message we have heard from Him and announce to you: God is light, and there is no darkness at all in Him.
  James 1:17 — Specific passage comparison: James associates the Father of lights with the absence of variation or shifting shadow; the letter declares God light with no darkness at all. The light and excluded shadow/darkness imagery corresponds without making the two descriptions identical.
    James 1:17 Every good act of giving and every complete gift is from above, coming down from the Father of lights, with whom there is no variation or shadow from turning.

1 John 1:6
If we say that we have fellowship with Him while walking in darkness, we lie and do not practice the truth.
  1 John 2:4 — Specific passage comparison: Both passages test a claim about relationship with Him against conduct and call the contradiction a lie. Here it is fellowship while walking in darkness; there it is knowing Him without keeping His commandments. The different claims and conduct remain explicit.
    1 John 2:4 The one who says, “I have come to know Him,” and does not keep His commandments is a liar, and the truth is not in that person.

1 John 1:7
But if we walk in the light, as He is in the light, we have fellowship with one another, and the blood of Jesus His Son cleanses us from every sin.
  Hebrews 9:14 — Specific passage comparison: Both passages attribute cleansing to Christ’s blood. Hebrews specifies cleansing the conscience from dead works to serve the living God; the letter names cleansing us from every sin while walking in light, alongside fellowship. The objects and conditions remain distinct.
    Hebrews 9:14 how much more will the blood of Christ, who through an eternal Spirit offered Himself unblemished to God, cleanse our conscience from dead works to serve the living God!

1 John 1:8
If we say that we have no sin, we deceive ourselves, and the truth is not in us.
  Romans 3:23 — Specific passage comparison: Paul’s statement that all sinned supplies a concrete counterpart to the letter’s rejection of claiming to have no sin. The past sinned formulation and present have-no-sin claim are preserved, without treating them as identical grammatical statements.
    Romans 3:23 for all sinned and fall short of God’s glory,

1 John 1:9
If we confess our sins, He is faithful and just to forgive us our sins and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness.
  Psalms 31:5 — Specific passage comparison: The Psalm joins making sin known and confession to the Lord with divine forgiveness. The letter likewise joins confessing sins with forgiveness and cleansing, adding His faithfulness and justice. The Psalm is cited in its native Greek-based Psalm31 numbering.
    Psalms 31:5 I made my sin known, and I did not cover my lawlessness. I said, “I will confess my lawlessness against myself to the Lord,” and Thou forgavest the impiety of my sin. Diapsalma.

1 John 1:10
If we say that we have not sinned, we make Him a liar, and His word is not in us.
  1 John 5:10 — Specific passage comparison: Both passages use making Him a liar for rejecting God’s testimony. Here the rejected truth concerns having sinned; there unbelief rejects God’s testimony about His Son. The two specific grounds are retained without merging the cases.
    1 John 5:10 The one who believes in the Son of God has the testimony within himself. The one who does not believe God has made Him a liar, because he has not believed in the testimony God has borne concerning His Son.

1 John 2:1
My little children, I write these things to you so that you may not sin. And if anyone sins, we have an Advocate with the Father, Jesus Christ the righteous,
  Romans 8:34 — Specific passage comparison: Romans names Christ Jesus as the one at God’s right hand who intercedes for us; the letter names Jesus Christ the righteous as Advocate with the Father when anyone sins. Intercession and advocacy correspond without claiming the role terms are identical.
    Romans 8:34 Who is the one who condemns? Christ Jesus is the One who died—more than that, was raised—who is also at God’s right hand, who also intercedes for us.

1 John 2:2
and He Himself is the propitiation for our sins, and not for ours only, but also for the whole world.
  John 1:29 — Specific passage comparison: John identifies Jesus as the Lamb of God taking away the sin of the world; the letter calls Him propitiation for our sins and also for the whole world. The shared world scope preserves the distinct Lamb and propitiation formulations.
    John 1:29 The next day he saw Jesus coming toward him and said, “Behold, the Lamb of God, who takes away the sin of the world!

1 John 2:3
And by this we know that we have come to know Him: if we keep His commandments.
  John 14:21 — Specific passage comparison: Jesus identifies having and keeping His commandments with loving Him; the letter makes keeping His commandments the test of having come to know Him. The same concrete obedience is connected with different stated predicates, love and knowledge.
    John 14:21 The one who has My commandments and keeps them is the one who loves Me. And the one who loves Me will be loved by My Father, and I will love him and reveal Myself to him.”

1 John 2:4
The one who says, “I have come to know Him,” and does not keep His commandments is a liar, and the truth is not in that person.
  Titus 1:16 — Specific passage comparison: Titus contrasts professing to know God with denying Him by works; the letter contrasts claiming to know Him with failing to keep His commandments and calls the claimant a liar. The conduct/profession contradiction corresponds without identifying the groups.
    Titus 1:16 They profess to know God, but by their deeds they deny Him, being detestable and disobedient and unfit for every good work.

1 John 2:5
But whoever keeps His word, in that person the love of God has truly been brought to completion. By this we know that we are in Him.
  John 14:23 — Specific passage comparison: Jesus connects loving Him and keeping His word with the Father and Son making their dwelling with that person. The letter connects keeping His word, love of God brought to completion, and being in Him; the possessive love of God is not resolved beyond the text.
    John 14:23 Jesus answered and said to him, “If anyone loves Me, he will keep My word, and My Father will love him, and We will come to him and make Our dwelling with him.

1 John 2:6
The one who says that he abides in Him ought himself also to walk just as He walked.
  1 Peter 2:21 — Specific passage comparison: Peter presents Christ’s suffering as an example to follow in His steps. The letter makes abiding in Him entail walking as He walked. The pattern of conduct corresponds, while Peter’s specific suffering context is not made the whole meaning of walking here.
    1 Peter 2:21 For to this you were called, because Christ also suffered for you, leaving you an example, so that you might follow in His footsteps.

1 John 2:7
Beloved, I am not writing a new commandment to you, but an old commandment that you had from the beginning. The old commandment is the word you heard.
  1 John 3:11 — Specific passage comparison: The later passage identifies the message heard from the beginning as loving one another. This verse calls the old commandment the word heard from the beginning. The repeated heard-from-the-beginning description connects the passages without inventing a dated beginning or verbatim quotation.
    1 John 3:11 For this is the message you heard from the beginning: that we should love one another,

1 John 2:8
Again, I am writing a new commandment to you, which is true in Him and in you, because the darkness is passing away and the true light is already shining.
  John 13:34 — Specific passage comparison: Jesus gives a new commandment to love one another as He loved them. The letter again calls its commandment new and places it amid light and the following brother-love teaching. The Gospel does not itself print the letter’s passing-darkness explanation.
    John 13:34 I give you a new commandment: that you love one another. Just as I have loved you, you also are to love one another.

1 John 2:9
The one who says that he is in the light and hates his brother is still in the darkness.
  1 John 4:20 — Specific passage comparison: The later passage rejects claiming love for God while hating one’s brother as a lie. Here claiming to be in light while hating one’s brother leaves one in darkness. The same brother-hatred contradicts differently stated claims.
    1 John 4:20 If anyone says, “I love God,” and hates his brother, he is a liar. For the one who does not love his brother, whom he has seen, cannot love God, whom he has not seen.

1 John 2:10
The one who loves his brother abides in the light, and there is no cause of stumbling in him.
  John 11:9–11:10 — Specific passage comparison: Jesus contrasts walking in the day without stumbling through seeing light with walking at night and stumbling because light is not in that person. The letter applies light and absence of stumbling to brother-love; the journey image and ethical application remain distinct.
    John 11:9 Jesus answered, “Are there not twelve hours of the day? If anyone walks in the day, he does not stumble, because he sees the light of this world.
    John 11:10 But if anyone walks in the night, he stumbles, because the light is not in him.”

1 John 2:11
But the one who hates his brother is in the darkness and walks in the darkness, and he does not know where he is going, because the darkness has blinded his eyes.
  John 12:35 — Specific passage comparison: John says the person walking in darkness does not know where he is going. The letter repeats that concrete darkness-and-direction description and applies it to brother-hatred, adding blinded eyes. The Gospel’s immediate invitation to walk while light remains is preserved.
    John 12:35 Then Jesus said to them, “For a little time longer the Light is among you. Walk while you have the Light, so that darkness may not overtake you. The one walking in the darkness does not know where he is going.

1 John 2:12
I write to you, little children, because your sins have been forgiven you for His name’s sake.
  Acts 10:43 — Specific passage comparison: Peter describes believers receiving forgiveness of sins through His name; the letter tells its children their sins have been forgiven for His name’s sake. The name-related forgiveness correspondence preserves through versus for-the-sake-of wording and the distinct audiences.
    Acts 10:43 All the prophets bear witness to Him: everyone who believes in Him receives forgiveness of sins through His name.”

1 John 2:13
I write to you, fathers, because you have come to know Him who is from the beginning. I write to you, young men, because you have overcome the evil one.
  1 John 2:14 — Specific passage comparison: Both verses address fathers as knowing Him from the beginning and young men as overcoming the evil one. The repeated statements have I write versus I have written forms; the latter adds strength, the abiding word and children knowing the Father.
    1 John 2:14 I have written to you, children, because you have come to know the Father. I have written to you, fathers, because you have come to know Him who is from the beginning. I have written to you, young men, because you are strong, and the word of God abides in you, and you have overcome the evil one.

1 John 2:14
I have written to you, children, because you have come to know the Father. I have written to you, fathers, because you have come to know Him who is from the beginning. I have written to you, young men, because you are strong, and the word of God abides in you, and you have overcome the evil one.
  1 John 2:12–2:13 — Specific passage comparison: The preceding I write addresses supply the repeated children, fathers and young-men pattern. This verse uses I have written, changes the children’s stated ground from forgiven sins to knowing the Father, and expands the young men’s description. No life-stage scheme is imposed.
    1 John 2:12 I write to you, little children, because your sins have been forgiven you for His name’s sake.
    1 John 2:13 I write to you, fathers, because you have come to know Him who is from the beginning. I write to you, young men, because you have overcome the evil one.

1 John 2:15
Do not love the world or the things in the world. If anyone loves the world, the love of the Father is not in him.
  James 4:4 — Specific passage comparison: James calls friendship with the world enmity with God; the letter prohibits love of the world and says the love of the Father is absent in the world-lover. Friendship/love and enmity/absence remain distinct, and the possessive love of the Father is not forced into one direction.
    James 4:4 Adulteresses! Do you not know that friendship with the world is hostility toward God? Whoever therefore wishes to be a friend of the world makes himself an enemy of God.

1 John 2:16
For everything in the world—the desire of the flesh, the desire of the eyes, and the boastfulness of life—is not from the Father, but is from the world.
  Romans 13:14 — Specific passage comparison: Romans forbids provision for the flesh toward its desires; the letter names desire of the flesh among what comes from the world rather than the Father. The reference supports that specific flesh-and-desire clause, without claiming Romans also prints the eyes and boastfulness clauses.
    Romans 13:14 but put on the Lord Jesus Christ, and do not make provision for the flesh to satisfy its desires.

1 John 2:17
And the world is passing away, along with its desire; but the one who does the will of God abides forever.
  1 Peter 1:24–1:25 — Specific passage comparison: Peter contrasts grass and flower that wither and fall with the Lord’s word remaining forever. The letter contrasts the passing world and its desire with the person doing God’s will abiding forever. The fading/enduring contrast preserves the different subjects.
    1 Peter 1:24 For “All flesh is like grass, and all its glory like a flower of grass. The grass withered, and the flower fell,
    1 Peter 1:25 but the word of the Lord remains forever.” And this is the word that was proclaimed to you as good news.

1 John 2:18
Children, it is the last hour. And just as you heard that antichrist is coming, even now many antichrists have arisen; from this we know that it is the last hour.
  1 John 4:3 — Specific passage comparison: Both passages recall hearing that antichrist is coming and state a present reality. Here many antichrists have arisen and mark the last hour; there the antichrist spirit is already in the world. The plurality, spirit wording and last-hour conclusion remain distinct.
    1 John 4:3 and every spirit that does not confess Jesus is not from God. And this is the spirit of the antichrist, which you heard was coming and is already in the world now.

1 John 2:19
They went out from us, but they were not of us; for if they had been of us, they would have remained with us. But this happened so that it might become clear that they are not all of us.
  Acts 20:30 — Specific passage comparison: Paul warns that men will arise from among the hearers and draw disciples away by distorted speech; the letter describes people who went out from us and did not remain. This is a bounded comparison of departure arising within a community, without identifying the persons or occasions.
    Acts 20:30 Even from among you yourselves men will arise, speaking distorted things to draw the disciples away after themselves.

1 John 2:20
And you have an anointing from the Holy One, and all of you know.
  1 John 2:27 — Specific passage comparison: The later verse develops the received, abiding anointing and its truthful teaching. Here the anointing is from the Holy One and all of you know. The wording all of you know is preserved rather than changed to a claim of knowing all things.
    1 John 2:27 And as for you, the anointing that you received from Him abides in you, and you have no need for anyone to teach you. But as His anointing teaches you about all things—and is true and is not a lie—so, just as it taught you, abide in Him.

1 John 2:21
I have not written to you because you do not know the truth, but because you know it, and because no lie is from the truth.
  1 John 1:6 — Specific passage comparison: The opening test says a fellowship claim combined with walking in darkness lies and does not practice the truth. This verse says the readers know the truth and no lie is from it. The truth/lie opposition corresponds without identifying every knowing claim as the earlier false claim.
    1 John 1:6 If we say that we have fellowship with Him while walking in darkness, we lie and do not practice the truth.

1 John 2:22
Who is the liar except the one who denies that Jesus is the Christ? This is the antichrist: the one who denies the Father and the Son.
  1 John 4:2–4:3 — Specific passage comparison: The later test concerns confessing Jesus Christ come in flesh and a spirit that does not confess Jesus, called antichrist. This verse defines the liar by denying Jesus is the Christ and denying Father and Son. The distinct confession formulations are retained.
    1 John 4:2 By this you know the Spirit of God: every spirit that confesses Jesus Christ as having come in the flesh is from God,
    1 John 4:3 and every spirit that does not confess Jesus is not from God. And this is the spirit of the antichrist, which you heard was coming and is already in the world now.

1 John 2:23
Everyone who denies the Son does not have the Father either; the one who confesses the Son has the Father also.
  John 5:23 — Specific passage comparison: Jesus connects honoring the Son with honoring the Father and says failure to honor the Son fails to honor the sending Father. The letter connects denying or confessing the Son with not having or having the Father. Honor and confession are related but not substituted as identical words.
    John 5:23 so that all may honor the Son just as they honor the Father. The one who does not honor the Son does not honor the Father who sent Him.

1 John 2:24
As for you, let what you heard from the beginning abide in you. If what you heard from the beginning abides in you, you too will abide in the Son and in the Father.
  2 John 1:9 — Specific passage comparison: Second John connects abiding in Christ’s teaching with having both Father and Son. Here the word heard from the beginning abiding in the readers leads to their abiding in the Son and Father. The teaching/word and having/abiding formulations remain distinct.
    2 John 1:9 Everyone who goes ahead and does not abide in the teaching of Christ does not have God. Whoever abides in the teaching has both the Father and the Son.

1 John 2:25
And this is the promise He Himself promised us: eternal life.
  Titus 1:2 — Specific passage comparison: Titus places hope in eternal life promised by God who does not lie before the ages; this verse identifies the promise He Himself promised us as eternal life. The promised-life correspondence retains the stated time and divine description supplied only by Titus.
    Titus 1:2 in hope of eternal life, which God, who does not lie, promised before the ages,

1 John 2:26
I have written these things to you about those who are leading you astray.
  1 John 2:22–2:23 — Specific passage comparison: The nearby denial of Jesus as Christ and of Father and Son, followed by the denial/confession distinction, gives specific doctrinal content alongside this warning about people leading the readers astray. The warning does not supply names or authorize identifying contemporary individuals.
    1 John 2:22 Who is the liar except the one who denies that Jesus is the Christ? This is the antichrist: the one who denies the Father and the Son.
    1 John 2:23 Everyone who denies the Son does not have the Father either; the one who confesses the Son has the Father also.

1 John 2:27
And as for you, the anointing that you received from Him abides in you, and you have no need for anyone to teach you. But as His anointing teaches you about all things—and is true and is not a lie—so, just as it taught you, abide in Him.
  John 14:26 — Specific passage comparison: Jesus says the Holy Spirit will teach the disciples all things and remind them of what He said. The letter describes the received anointing as teaching about all things truthfully and calls readers to abide. The anointing is not silently replaced with the Gospel’s explicit Spirit noun, nor read as universal personal omniscience.
    John 14:26 But the Advocate, the Holy Spirit, whom the Father will send in My name, He will teach you all things and remind you of all that I said to you.

1 John 2:28
And now, little children, abide in Him, so that when He is manifested we may have confidence and not shrink from Him in shame at His coming.
  Colossians 3:4 — Specific passage comparison: Colossians says that when Christ, your life, is revealed, believers will also be revealed with Him in glory; the letter joins His being manifested and His coming with confidence rather than shrinking in shame. The related revealed/manifested wording preserves the distinct glory and confidence consequences and supplies no date.
    Colossians 3:4 When Christ, your life, is revealed, then you also will be revealed with Him in glory.

1 John 2:29
If you know that He is righteous, you know that everyone who practices righteousness has also been born of Him.
  1 John 3:7 — Specific passage comparison: The later verse says the person practicing righteousness is righteous just as He is righteous. This verse connects knowing His righteousness with the righteous practitioner’s having been born of Him. The shared righteousness/practice comparison preserves the additional birth claim and does not resolve an unprinted pronoun identity.
    1 John 3:7 Children, let no one lead you astray. The one who practices righteousness is righteous, just as He is righteous.
