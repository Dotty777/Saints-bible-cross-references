Jude 1:1
Jude, a servant of Jesus Christ and brother of James, to those who are called, beloved in God the Father and kept for Jesus Christ:
  James 1:1 — epistolary self-description: Both letter openings identify the named writer as a servant in relation to Jesus Christ and address recipients. Jude additionally identifies himself as James’s brother; the comparison does not establish which other person named James is intended from the greeting alone.
    James 1:1 James, a servant of God and of the Lord Jesus Christ, to the twelve tribes in the dispersion: greetings.

Jude 1:2
May mercy and peace and love be multiplied to you.
  2 Timothy 1:2 — greeting blessing: Both greetings address recipients with mercy and peace. Jude asks that mercy, peace and love be multiplied; Timothy’s greeting names grace, mercy and peace from God the Father and Christ Jesus our Lord. The lists and grammatical forms remain distinct.
    2 Timothy 1:2 to Timothy, a beloved child: grace, mercy, peace from God the Father and Christ Jesus our Lord.

Jude 1:3
Beloved, while making every effort to write to you about the salvation we share, I found it necessary to write and urge you to contend for the faith delivered once for all to the saints.
  Philippians 1:27 — contending for the faith: Jude urges contending for the faith delivered once for all to the saints. Philippians urges standing together and striving for the faith of the gospel. The common faith-directed struggle is explicit, while Jude’s delivered-once-for-all qualification remains his own.
    Philippians 1:27 Only conduct your common life worthily of the gospel of Christ, so that whether I come and see you or remain absent, I may hear about you that you stand firm in one spirit, with one soul striving together for the faith of the gospel,

Jude 1:4
For certain people have slipped in unnoticed, those written about long ago for this judgment: ungodly people who turn the grace of our God into shameless indulgence and deny our only Master and Lord, Jesus Christ.
  2 Peter 2:1 — destructive teachers denying the Master: Jude describes people slipping in unnoticed and denying the only Master and Lord Jesus Christ. Peter warns of secretly introduced destructive factions and denial of the Master who bought them. The target does not supply every detail of Jude’s grace-perversion charge.
    2 Peter 2:1 But false prophets also arose among the people, just as there will also be false teachers among you. They will secretly bring in destructive factions, even denying the Master who bought them, bringing swift destruction upon themselves.

Jude 1:5
Now I wish to remind you, although you have come to know all this once for all, that Jesus, after saving a people from the land of Egypt, afterward destroyed those who did not believe.
  1 Corinthians 10:1–10:5 — deliverance followed by judgment: Paul recalls the ancestors’ passage through the sea, identifies the spiritual rock as Christ, and says God was not pleased with most, who were strewn over the wilderness. Jude explicitly names Jesus saving from Egypt before destroying unbelievers; that named agency is retained.
    1 Corinthians 10:1 For I do not want you to be unaware, brothers, that our fathers were all under the cloud and all passed through the sea,
    1 Corinthians 10:2 and all were baptized into Moses in the cloud and in the sea,
    1 Corinthians 10:3 and all ate the same spiritual food,
    1 Corinthians 10:4 and all drank the same spiritual drink, for they drank from a spiritual rock that followed them, and the rock was Christ.
    1 Corinthians 10:5 But God was not pleased with most of them, for they were strewn over the wilderness.

Jude 1:6
And He has kept the angels who did not keep their own domain, but abandoned their proper dwelling, in everlasting chains under gloom for the judgment of the great day.
  2 Peter 2:4 — angels kept for judgment: Both describe sinning or disobedient angels kept in chains of gloom for judgment. Jude specifies abandoning their proper dwelling and everlasting chains; Peter names Tartarus and chains of gloom. Neither set of additional details is inserted into the other account.
    2 Peter 2:4 For if God did not spare angels when they sinned, but cast them into Tartarus and handed them over to chains of gloom, kept for judgment,

Jude 1:7
In the same way, Sodom and Gomorrah and the cities around them, which practiced sexual immorality and went after other flesh in a manner like these, are set before us as an example, undergoing the penalty of eternal fire.
  Genesis 19:24–19:25 — Sodom and Gomorrah judged: Genesis narrates the Lord raining sulphur and fire upon Sodom and Gomorrah and overthrowing their cities and surroundings. Jude recalls these cities as an example of punishment, adding sexual conduct and eternal-fire language not all stated in the retained narrative.
    Genesis 19:24 And the Lord rained upon Sodom and Gomorrah sulphur and fire from the Lord out of the heaven;
    Genesis 19:25 and He overthrew those cities, and all the surrounding country, and all that lived in the cities, and all that sprang from the earth.

Jude 1:8
Yet in the same way these people too, in their dreaming, defile flesh, reject dominion, and slander glorious ones.
  2 Peter 2:10 — defilement and slander: Both accusations join defiled flesh or defiling desire, rejection of dominion or despised lordship, and slander of glorious ones or beings. Jude adds dreaming; Peter adds recklessness and self-will. The three corresponding charges preserve the distinct wording and unnamed referents.
    2 Peter 2:10 especially those who go after flesh in defiling desire and despise lordship. Reckless and self-willed, they do not tremble as they slander glorious beings,

Jude 1:9
But Michael the archangel, when he was contending with the devil and disputing about the body of Moses, did not dare to bring a slanderous judgment against him, but said, “May the Lord rebuke you.”
  Zechariah 3:2 — the Lord’s rebuke of the devil: Zechariah’s separate scene contains the Lord’s rebuke addressed to the slanderer. Jude places the same appeal to the Lord’s rebuke in Michael’s dispute with the devil over Moses’s body. Slanderer/devil wording and the different actors and disputed matter remain distinct.
    Zechariah 3:2 The Lord said to the slanderer, “May the Lord rebuke you, slanderer! May the Lord, who chose Jerusalem, rebuke you! Look, is this not like a brand pulled from fire?”

Jude 1:10
But these people slander whatever they do not know; and what they understand by instinct, like unreasoning animals, becomes the very thing by which they are corrupted.
  2 Peter 2:12 — uncomprehending animal comparison: Both compare the opponents with unreasoning or irrational animals and connect slandering what they do not understand with corruption or destruction. Peter adds capture and repeated destruction language; Jude distinguishes what they slander from what they understand instinctively.
    2 Peter 2:12 But these people, like irrational animals born as creatures of instinct for capture and destruction, slandering things they do not understand, will also be destroyed in their destruction,

Jude 1:11
Woe to them! For they have gone in the way of Cain, have poured themselves into Balaam’s error for pay, and have perished in Korah’s rebellion.
  Genesis 4:8 — Cain’s way: Genesis narrates Cain rising against Abel his brother and killing him. This supplies the fratricidal episode behind Jude’s named example of Cain’s way. Jude’s accusation against the present intruders is not a claim that they literally repeated that same killing.
    Genesis 4:8 And Cain said to Abel his brother, “Let us go out into the field.” And while they were in the field, Cain rose against Abel his brother and slew him.
  2 Peter 2:15 — Balaam and wrongful pay: Peter names Balaam’s love of wrongdoing’s wages, directly corresponding to Jude’s Balaam-error-for-pay accusation. Peter adds the patronymic Bosor and leaving the straight way. Those details are not made part of Jude’s shorter wording or silently normalized.
    2 Peter 2:15 Leaving the straight way, they wandered off, following the way of Balaam son of Bosor, who loved the wages of wrongdoing,
  Numbers 16:31–16:35 — Korah’s rebellion: Numbers narrates the ground opening, the rebels descending alive and fire consuming the incense-offering men. These judgments supply the narrative background to Jude’s named Korah rebellion, without collapsing the different participants or modes of death into one action.
    Numbers 16:31 As he finished speaking all these words, the ground beneath them split apart.
    Numbers 16:32 And the earth opened and swallowed them, their households, all the people who were with Korah, and their livestock.
    Numbers 16:33 And they and all that belonged to them went down alive to Hades; the earth covered them, and they perished from the midst of the congregation.
    Numbers 16:34 And all Israel around them fled at their cry, saying: Lest the earth swallow us!
    Numbers 16:35 And fire went out from the Lord and consumed the two hundred fifty men presenting the incense.

Jude 1:12
These are reefs at your love feasts, feasting together with you without fear, shepherding themselves; waterless clouds carried along by winds; late-autumn trees without fruit, twice dead, uprooted;
  2 Peter 2:13 — corrupt participants at shared feasts: Peter calls people feasting with the readers spots and blemishes while describing their deceptions and indulgence. Jude calls them reefs at love feasts and adds self-shepherding, waterless clouds and fruitless trees. Reefs is retained rather than substituted with Peter’s blemishes.
    2 Peter 2:13 suffering harm as the wages of wrongdoing. They consider indulgence in the daytime a pleasure. They are spots and blemishes, reveling in their deceptions while feasting with you,

Jude 1:13
wild waves of the sea foaming up their own shames; wandering stars for whom the gloom of darkness has been kept forever.
  2 Peter 2:17 — reserved gloom and empty images: Peter describes waterless springs and storm-driven mists for whom darkness’s gloom is kept. Jude describes shame-foaming waves and wandering stars with gloom kept forever. The reserved-gloom correspondence preserves the different images and Jude’s explicit duration.
    2 Peter 2:17 These people are waterless springs and mists driven by a storm, for whom the gloom of darkness has been kept.

Jude 1:14
Enoch also, the seventh from Adam, prophesied about these people, saying, “Look, the Lord came with His holy myriads,
  1 Thessalonians 3:13 — the Lord with His holy company: Jude’s quoted prophecy says the Lord came with His holy myriads; Thessalonians speaks of the Lord Jesus’s coming with all His saints. The past-form came, the coming formulation and myriads/saints wording remain distinct. This does not identify Thessalonians as the source of Enoch’s words.
    1 Thessalonians 3:13 so as to establish your hearts blameless in holiness before our God and Father at the coming of our Lord Jesus with all His saints. Amen.

Jude 1:15
to execute judgment against all and to convict every soul concerning all their ungodly works that they committed in ungodliness, and concerning all the harsh things that ungodly sinners spoke against Him.”
  Matthew 12:36–12:37 — judgment concerning spoken words: Jude’s judgment oracle includes conviction for harsh things ungodly sinners spoke against Him. Jesus says people must account for idle words on judgment day and speaks of condemnation by words. The target supports speech’s accountability without reproducing Jude’s entire oracle or naming Enoch.
    Matthew 12:36 But I tell you that people will give account on the day of judgment for every idle word they speak.
    Matthew 12:37 For by your words you will be justified, and by your words you will be condemned.”

Jude 1:16
These are grumblers, faultfinders, walking according to their own desires; their mouth speaks boastful things, and they flatter people for the sake of advantage.
  2 Peter 2:18 — desire and inflated speech: Peter joins inflated words of emptiness with desire-driven enticement. Jude joins walking according to desires with boastful speech and flattery for advantage. The related combination of desire and manipulative speech retains the distinct adjectives and descriptions of those affected.
    2 Peter 2:18 For, uttering inflated words of emptiness, they entice through fleshly desires and debaucheries those who are truly escaping from people who live in error,

Jude 1:17
But you, beloved, remember the words spoken beforehand by the apostles of our Lord Jesus Christ,
  2 Peter 3:2 — remembering apostolic words: Both urge remembrance of words spoken beforehand and connect the instruction with the apostles of Jesus Christ. Peter also explicitly names the holy prophets and the Lord’s commandment; that wider list is not silently inserted into Jude’s sentence.
    2 Peter 3:2 so that you may remember the words spoken beforehand by the holy prophets and the commandment of the Lord and Savior through your apostles,

Jude 1:18
for they used to tell you, “In the last time there will be mockers, walking according to their own ungodly desires.”
  2 Peter 3:3 — last-time mockers: Jude recalls mockers in the last time walking according to ungodly desires. Peter predicts scoffers with scoffing in the last days following their own desires. The close pattern preserves mockers/scoffers, the time expressions and Jude’s additional ungodly qualification.
    2 Peter 3:3 knowing this first: in the last days scoffers will come with scoffing, walking according to their own desires,

Jude 1:19
These are the ones who create divisions, unspiritual people who do not have the Spirit.
  1 Corinthians 2:14 — the merely natural and the Spirit: Paul says the natural person does not receive the things of God’s Spirit. Jude labels the divisive people unspiritual and without the Spirit. The contrast concerns their relation to the Spirit, not proof that the two passages identify the same people or use identical predicates.
    1 Corinthians 2:14 But a natural person does not receive the things of the Spirit of God, for they are foolishness to such a person, who cannot know them because they are discerned spiritually.

Jude 1:20
But you, beloved, building yourselves up upon your most holy faith and praying in the Holy Spirit,
  Ephesians 6:18 — prayer in the Spirit: Jude directs building on most holy faith and praying in the Holy Spirit. Ephesians directs prayer and petition at every time in the Spirit with perseverance for the saints. The prayer instruction corresponds; each passage’s surrounding commands retain their own scope.
    Ephesians 6:18 with every prayer and petition, praying at every time in the Spirit, and to this end keeping watch with all perseverance and petition for all the saints,

Jude 1:21
keep yourselves in the love of God, awaiting the mercy of our Lord Jesus Christ that leads to eternal life.
  Titus 3:5–3:7 — mercy and the hope of eternal life: Titus connects saving mercy, renewal and the Spirit poured out through Jesus Christ with heirs according to hope of eternal life. Jude directs awaiting Jesus Christ’s mercy leading to eternal life while keeping in God’s love. Their agency, tense and surrounding instructions remain distinct.
    Titus 3:5 He saved us, not on the basis of works done by us in righteousness, but according to His mercy, through the washing of regeneration and renewal by the Holy Spirit,
    Titus 3:6 whom He poured out richly upon us through Jesus Christ our Savior,
    Titus 3:7 so that, justified by His grace, we might become heirs in accordance with the hope of eternal life.

Jude 1:22
And have mercy on some who are doubting;
  Matthew 14:30–14:31 — help amid doubt: Peter cries for the Lord to save him, and Jesus immediately grasps him before asking why he doubted. This concrete help amid doubt can be read beside Jude’s command of mercy for doubting people, without claiming the command narrates Peter’s water-walking event.
    Matthew 14:30 But seeing the strong wind, he became afraid; beginning to sink, he cried out, “Lord, save me!”
    Matthew 14:31 Immediately Jesus stretched out His hand, grasped him, and said to him, “You of little faith, why did you doubt?”

Jude 1:23
save others, snatching them out of the fire; and have mercy on others with fear, hating even the tunic stained by the flesh.
  Zechariah 3:2–3:4 — fire rescue and stained clothing: Zechariah combines a brand rescued from fire with filthy clothing removed from the high priest. Jude combines snatching others from fire with hatred of a tunic stained by flesh. The paired images correspond without equating the persons, clothing or narrated and exhortatory settings.
    Zechariah 3:2 The Lord said to the slanderer, “May the Lord rebuke you, slanderer! May the Lord, who chose Jerusalem, rebuke you! Look, is this not like a brand pulled from fire?”
    Zechariah 3:3 Joshua was clothed in filthy garments and standing before the angel.
    Zechariah 3:4 He answered and said to those standing before him, “Remove the filthy garments from him.” He said to him, “Look, I have taken away your lawless deeds; clothe him in a robe reaching to the feet,

Jude 1:24
Now to Him who is able to guard you from stumbling and to make you stand blameless before His glory with exultation,
  1 Thessalonians 5:23 — divine preservation and blamelessness: Paul prays that the God of peace sanctify the readers and keep them whole and blameless at Jesus Christ’s coming. Jude praises the One able to guard from stumbling and present them blameless before His glory. The prayer and praise retain their different formulations.
    1 Thessalonians 5:23 Now may the God of peace Himself sanctify you completely, and may your spirit and soul and body be kept whole, blameless at the coming of our Lord Jesus Christ.

Jude 1:25
to the only God our Savior, through Jesus Christ our Lord, be glory, majesty, might, and authority before all time, and now, and into all the ages. Amen.
  Romans 16:27 — glory to God through Jesus Christ: Both ascribe glory to God through Jesus Christ and extend the praise across ages. Jude says the only God our Savior and adds majesty, might, authority and before-all-time wording; Romans says the only wise God. The additional titles and time expressions remain distinct.
    Romans 16:27 to the only wise God, through Jesus Christ, to whom be the glory throughout the ages. Amen.]
