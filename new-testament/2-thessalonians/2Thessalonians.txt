2 Thessalonians 1:1
Paul and Silvanus and Timothy, to the Church of the Thessalonians in God our Father and the Lord Jesus Christ:
  1 Thessalonians 1:1 — thematic parallel: Both openings name Paul, Silvanus and Timothy and address the Thessalonian Church in God the Father and the Lord Jesus Christ. The second letter says our Father; the first says the Father and includes grace and peace in that unit. The repeated sender and addressee formula does not merge the letters or their occasions.
    1 Thessalonians 1:1 Paul and Silvanus and Timothy, to the Church of the Thessalonians in God the Father and the Lord Jesus Christ: grace to you and peace.

2 Thessalonians 1:2
grace to you and peace from God our Father and the Lord Jesus Christ.
  1 Corinthians 1:3 — thematic parallel: Both greet the recipients with grace and peace from God our Father and the Lord Jesus Christ. The same named greeting sources occur in letters to different receiving communities; the repeated formula is a verbal parallel rather than evidence of one delivery occasion.
    1 Corinthians 1:3 Grace to you and peace from God our Father and the Lord Jesus Christ.

2 Thessalonians 1:3
We ought always to give thanks to God for you, brothers, as is fitting, because your faith is growing abundantly and the love of each and every one of you for one another is increasing,
  1 Thessalonians 3:12 — thematic parallel: The reported increase of love for one another corresponds to the earlier letter's petition that the Lord make such love increase and overflow. The thanksgiving also names growing faith; the petition extends love to all and uses a wish rather than a report. Those additional scope and grammatical differences remain distinct.
    1 Thessalonians 3:12 And may the Lord make you increase and overflow in love for one another and for all, just as we also do for you,

2 Thessalonians 1:4
so that we ourselves boast of you among the Churches of God for your endurance and faith in all your persecutions and the afflictions that you bear.
  Romans 5:3–5:4 — thematic parallel: Both associate boasting, affliction and endurance or perseverance. Thessalonians boasts of the recipients among God's Churches for the faith and endurance with which they bear persecution; Romans says we boast in afflictions because they produce perseverance and proven character. The objects of boasting and the explanatory chain differ.
    Romans 5:3 And not only this, but we also boast in afflictions, knowing that affliction produces perseverance,
    Romans 5:4 and perseverance, proven character, and proven character, hope.

2 Thessalonians 1:5
This is evidence of God’s righteous judgment, that you may be counted worthy of the kingdom of God, for which you also suffer,
  Acts 14:22 — thematic parallel: Both connect affliction or suffering with God's kingdom. Acts reports encouragement to continue in faith because entry into the kingdom involves many afflictions; Thessalonians describes suffering for that kingdom and being counted worthy in God's righteous judgment. The encouragement and judgment descriptions remain distinct.
    Acts 14:22 strengthening the souls of the disciples, encouraging them to remain in the faith, and saying, “Through many afflictions we must enter the kingdom of God.”

2 Thessalonians 1:6
since it is indeed just with God to repay affliction to those who afflict you,
  Romans 12:19 — thematic parallel: God's just repayment of affliction to those afflicting the readers corresponds to Romans reserving vengeance and repayment to the Lord. Romans commands readers not to avenge themselves; Thessalonians describes God's repayment. The actors and statement-versus-command roles remain separate.
    Romans 12:19 Do not avenge yourselves, beloved, but make room for wrath; for it is written, “Vengeance is Mine; I will repay, says the Lord.”

2 Thessalonians 1:7
and to you who are afflicted, relief with us, at the revelation of the Lord Jesus from heaven with the angels of His power,
  Matthew 25:31 — thematic parallel: Both portray revelation or coming with angels. Thessalonians names the Lord Jesus revealed from heaven with the angels of His power and relief for afflicted people; Matthew names the Son of Man coming with all angels and sitting on His glorious throne. Neither passage's added location, relief or throne detail is inserted into the other.
    Matthew 25:31 When the Son of Man comes in His glory, and all the angels with Him, then He will sit upon the throne of His glory.

2 Thessalonians 1:8
in a flame of fire, bringing vengeance on those who do not know God and on those who do not obey the gospel of our Lord Jesus.
  Isaiah 66:15 — allusion / thematic parallel: The flame-of-fire and vengeance language has a specific counterpart in Isaiah's description of the Lord coming like fire. Isaiah also names chariots like a storm, wrath and rejection; Thessalonians specifies those not knowing God or obeying the Lord Jesus' gospel. This is a scriptural image comparison rather than an identical account of every agent and circumstance.
    Isaiah 66:15 For behold, the Lord will come like fire, and His chariots like a storm, to render vengeance in wrath and rejection in a flame of fire.

2 Thessalonians 1:9
They will pay the penalty of eternal destruction from the presence of the Lord and from the glory of His strength,
  Isaiah 2:19 — allusion / thematic parallel: The Lord-and-glory-of-His-strength formula closely corresponds to Isaiah's judgment scene. Isaiah describes handmade things carried into caves, rock clefts and earth holes from the Lord's terror and the glory of His strength; Thessalonians names eternal destruction from the Lord's presence and the glory of His strength. The different objects and predicates, and the actual from wording, remain without deciding its spatial or causal interpretation.
    Isaiah 2:19 carrying them into caves, into clefts in the rocks and into holes in the earth, from the Lord’s terror and the glory of His strength, whenever He rises to shatter the earth.

2 Thessalonians 1:10
when He comes to be glorified in His saints and marveled at among all who have believed—because our testimony to you was believed—on that day.
  Psalms 88:8 — allusion / thematic parallel: Both associate being glorified with holy ones or saints. The Psalm says God is glorified in the council of holy ones; Thessalonians describes the Lord's future coming to be glorified in His saints and marveled at among believers. The council and the believing recipients are not simply identified as one group, and the Psalm does not reproduce the coming-day statement.
    Psalms 88:8 God is glorified in the council of holy ones, great and fearsome above all around Him.

2 Thessalonians 1:11
To this end we also always pray for you, that our God may count you worthy of the calling and fulfill every good resolve and work of faith with power,
  Colossians 1:9–1:11 — thematic parallel: Both prayers combine worthy language, good works and divine power. Thessalonians asks God to count the recipients worthy of calling and fulfill good resolve and faith's work with power; Colossians asks knowledge of His will toward a worthy walk, fruitful good work and empowerment for endurance and patience with joy. The petitions and stated effects remain distinct.
    Colossians 1:9 For this reason we too, since the day we heard, have not ceased praying for you and asking that you may be filled with the knowledge of His will in all spiritual wisdom and understanding,
    Colossians 1:10 to walk worthily of the Lord, pleasing Him in every way, bearing fruit in every good work and growing in the knowledge of God,
    Colossians 1:11 being empowered with every power according to the might of His glory for all endurance and patience with joy,

2 Thessalonians 1:12
so that the name of our Lord Jesus may be glorified in you, and you in Him, according to the grace of our God and Lord Jesus Christ.
  John 17:10 — thematic parallel: Jesus' statement in John that He has been glorified in those given to Him is a specific counterpart to the prayer that His name may be glorified in the readers. Thessalonians also names the readers in Him and grace; John states His own glorification in them. The perfect statement and the prayer's purpose, name wording and reciprocal addition are preserved.
    John 17:10 and all that is Mine is Thine, and what is Thine is Mine, and I have been glorified in them.

2 Thessalonians 2:1
Now we ask you, brothers, concerning the coming of our Lord Jesus Christ and our gathering together to Him,
  1 Thessalonians 4:16–4:17 — thematic parallel: Both look toward the community being with the Lord at His coming. First Thessalonians describes descent from heaven, the dead in Christ rising and the living caught up with them to meet the Lord; this unit introduces the coming and our gathering to Him. Those additional participants, movements and sequence are retained without constructing a further timetable.
    1 Thessalonians 4:16 For the Lord Himself will descend from heaven with a commanding cry, with an archangel’s voice and with God’s trumpet, and the dead in Christ will rise first.
    1 Thessalonians 4:17 Then we who are alive, who remain, will be caught up together with them in clouds to meet the Lord in the air; and so we will always be with the Lord.

2 Thessalonians 2:2
not to be quickly shaken from your understanding or alarmed, either by a spirit or by a word or by a letter as though from us, as if the day of the Lord is already present.
  1 Thessalonians 5:1–5:2 — thematic parallel: Both discuss the day of the Lord in teaching the Thessalonians. The first letter says it comes like a thief in the night; this unit warns against being shaken as if that day were already present, including by a letter as though from the writers. It neither identifies such a letter nor repeats every first-letter image.
    1 Thessalonians 5:1 Now concerning the times and the seasons, brothers, you have no need for anything to be written to you.
    1 Thessalonians 5:2 For you yourselves know exactly that the day of the Lord comes in this way, like a thief in the night.

2 Thessalonians 2:3
Let no one deceive you in any way. For that day will not come unless the rebellion comes first and the man of lawlessness is revealed, the son of destruction,
  2 Thessalonians 2:8 — internal thematic parallel: The later lawless-one revelation resumes the man-of-lawlessness revelation introduced here. The introduction also names rebellion and the son-of-destruction designation; the later unit names the Lord Jesus' destruction of the figure. This is internal narrative continuity, without supplying a personal identity or date.
    2 Thessalonians 2:8 And then the lawless one will be revealed, whom the Lord Jesus will kill with the breath of His mouth and bring to nothing by the manifestation of His coming.

2 Thessalonians 2:4
who opposes and exalts himself above everything called god or an object of worship, so that he takes his seat in God’s sanctuary, displaying himself as being God.
  Daniel 11:36 — allusion / thematic parallel: Daniel's king is exalted above every god and speaks against the God of gods. Thessalonians describes a figure who exalts himself above everything called god or worshiped and displays himself as God in God's sanctuary. The above-gods opposition is a specific scriptural counterpart, without identifying the king and lawless figure as one person or adding the sanctuary clause to Daniel.
    Daniel 11:36 The king will act according to his will, become enraged, and be exalted above every god. He will speak extraordinary things against the God of gods and prosper until the wrath is completed, for completion is coming upon him.

2 Thessalonians 2:5
Do you not remember that when I was still with you I used to tell you these things?
  1 Thessalonians 3:4 — thematic parallel: Both remind readers of instruction given while the writer or writers were with them. First Thessalonians explicitly recalls foretelling affliction and says it happened; this unit recalls these things within the lawless-one discussion. The pronouns and contents differ, and an unrecorded earlier lecture is not reconstructed.
    1 Thessalonians 3:4 For even when we were with you, we told you beforehand that we were going to suffer affliction, just as it also happened, as you know.

2 Thessalonians 2:6
And now you know what restrains, so that he may be revealed in his own time.
  2 Thessalonians 2:7 — internal thematic parallel: The adjacent account again describes present restraint, linking it with a later change. This unit says what restrains and his own revelation time; the next says the one who now restrains until he is out of the way. The what/one forms and timing are retained without naming the restrainer or explaining its removal.
    2 Thessalonians 2:7 For the mystery of lawlessness is already at work. Only, the one who now restrains does so until he is out of the way.

2 Thessalonians 2:7
For the mystery of lawlessness is already at work. Only, the one who now restrains does so until he is out of the way.
  2 Thessalonians 2:6 — internal thematic parallel: The preceding restraint statement supplies the immediate context for the one now restraining and the later change. It speaks of what restrains toward revelation in his own time; this unit adds lawlessness already working. This is internal continuity, not a supplied identity for the restrainer or an equation of present working with completed revelation.
    2 Thessalonians 2:6 And now you know what restrains, so that he may be revealed in his own time.

2 Thessalonians 2:8
And then the lawless one will be revealed, whom the Lord Jesus will kill with the breath of His mouth and bring to nothing by the manifestation of His coming.
  Isaiah 11:4 — allusion / thematic parallel: The mouth-and-breath judgment image has a specific counterpart in Isaiah. Isaiah distinguishes the mouth's word striking the earth and breath through the lips slaying the ungodly; Thessalonians names the Lord Jesus killing the lawless one with His mouth's breath. The objects and actual word/lip/mouth formulations remain different.
    Isaiah 11:4 but he will judge the cause of the lowly, and reprove the lowly of the earth; and he will strike the earth with the word of his mouth, and with breath through his lips he will slay the ungodly.

2 Thessalonians 2:9
His coming is according to the working of Satan, with all power and signs and wonders of falsehood,
  Matthew 24:24 — thematic parallel: The lawless-one sequence describes signs and wonders of falsehood with a coming according to Satan's working. Matthew describes false christs and false prophets giving great signs and wonders to lead astray. The deceptive-wonder language corresponds without identifying all the figures, signs or comings as one event.
    Matthew 24:24 For false christs and false prophets will arise and give great signs and wonders, so as to lead astray, if possible, even the chosen.

2 Thessalonians 2:10
and with every deception of unrighteousness for those who are perishing, because they did not receive the love of the truth so that they might be saved.
  2 Thessalonians 2:12 — internal thematic parallel: The later purpose clause again joins failure to believe truth with unrighteousness and judgment. This unit names not receiving love of truth, deception and perishing; the later one names pleasure in unrighteousness. Their linked argument preserves receiving/loving versus believing and the different causal and purpose clauses.
    2 Thessalonians 2:12 in order that all who did not believe the truth but took pleasure in unrighteousness may be judged.

2 Thessalonians 2:11
And for this reason God sends them a working of delusion, so that they believe the falsehood,
  1 Kings 22:23 — thematic parallel: Kings explicitly says the Lord put a lying spirit in the prophets' mouths and announced calamities. That is a specific judgment-through-deception counterpart to God sending a working of delusion so people believe falsehood. The spirit-in-prophets mechanism and the delusion statement are not made identical or assigned to one occasion.
    1 Kings 22:23 Now behold, the Lord has put a lying spirit in the mouth of all these prophets of yours, and the Lord has spoken calamities against you.”

2 Thessalonians 2:12
in order that all who did not believe the truth but took pleasure in unrighteousness may be judged.
  Romans 2:8 — thematic parallel: Both connect rejection of truth and attachment to unrighteousness with judgment. Romans says disobeying truth but obeying unrighteousness leads to wrath and fury; Thessalonians says those not believing truth but taking pleasure in unrighteousness may be judged. Belief/obedience and pleasure/obeying retain their different actual forms.
    Romans 2:8 but to those acting from selfish ambition, who disobey the truth but obey unrighteousness, wrath and fury,

2 Thessalonians 2:13
But we ought always to thank God for you, brothers beloved by the Lord, because God chose you as firstfruits for salvation in sanctification by the Spirit and faith in the truth.
  1 Peter 1:2 — thematic parallel: Both place sanctification by the Spirit within God's saving relation to a chosen community. Thessalonians names firstfruits for salvation and faith in truth; Peter names the Father's foreknowledge, obedience and sprinkling with Jesus Christ's blood. The shared sanctification phrase does not make those additional terms identical.
    1 Peter 1:2 according to the foreknowledge of God the Father, in sanctification by the Spirit, for obedience and sprinkling with the blood of Jesus Christ: May grace and peace be multiplied to you.

2 Thessalonians 2:14
To this He also called you through our gospel, for obtaining the glory of our Lord Jesus Christ.
  1 Thessalonians 2:12 — thematic parallel: Both connect God's calling of the recipients with glory. The first letter names His own kingdom and glory; this unit names calling through our gospel for obtaining the Lord Jesus Christ's glory. The named means and divine possessive formulations are kept distinct.
    1 Thessalonians 2:12 encouraging you and comforting you and solemnly urging you to walk worthily of God, who calls you into His own kingdom and glory.

2 Thessalonians 2:15
So then, brothers, stand firm and hold fast to the traditions you were taught, whether by word or by a letter from us.
  1 Corinthians 11:2 — thematic parallel: Both explicitly speak of holding fast transmitted traditions. Corinthians praises remembering the speaker and retaining traditions as handed over; Thessalonians commands standing firm and names teaching by word or a letter from the writers. The oral/written channels and plural/singular speaker forms are not silently transferred.
    1 Corinthians 11:2 Now I praise you because you remember me in all things and hold fast to the traditions just as I handed them over to you.

2 Thessalonians 2:16
Now may our Lord Jesus Christ Himself, and God our Father, who loved us and gave us eternal encouragement and good hope in grace,
  1 Thessalonians 3:11 — thematic parallel: Both prayers name God our Father and the Lord Jesus, with their order reversed. The first letter asks that their way to the readers be directed; this prayer, continued in2:17, asks encouragement and establishment of hearts and also names love, eternal encouragement and good hope in grace. The different petitions and the printed coactors remain distinct.
    1 Thessalonians 3:11 Now may our God and Father Himself, and our Lord Jesus, direct our way to you.

2 Thessalonians 2:17
encourage your hearts and establish them in every good work and word.
  1 Thessalonians 3:13 — thematic parallel: Both prayers ask for the readers' hearts to be established. First Thessalonians specifies blameless holiness before God at the Lord Jesus' coming; this unit adds encouragement and establishment in every good work and word. The stated aims and the first letter's coming clause remain in their own passages.
    1 Thessalonians 3:13 so as to establish your hearts blameless in holiness before our God and Father at the coming of our Lord Jesus with all His saints. Amen.

2 Thessalonians 3:1
Finally, brothers, pray for us, that the word of the Lord may run and be glorified, just as it also is among you,
  Acts 13:48–13:49 — thematic parallel: Acts describes Gentile hearers glorifying the word of the Lord and the word spreading throughout the region. That supplies a concrete word-glorified/spreading counterpart to the prayer that the Lord's word may run and be glorified. Running is not made a literal journey or the Acts scene identified as the Thessalonian reception.
    Acts 13:48 When the Gentiles heard this, they rejoiced and glorified the word of the Lord, and as many as had been appointed to eternal life believed.
    Acts 13:49 The word of the Lord spread through the whole region.

2 Thessalonians 3:2
and that we may be rescued from perverse and evil people, for not all have faith.
  Romans 15:31 — thematic parallel: Both request rescue from adverse people within a prayer context. Romans names those who disobey in Judea and also asks acceptance of the Jerusalem service; Thessalonians names perverse and evil people and says not all have faith. The disobey/faith descriptions and locations are not combined into one identified group.
    Romans 15:31 so that I may be rescued from those who disobey in Judea, and my service for Jerusalem may become acceptable to the saints,

2 Thessalonians 3:3
But the Lord is faithful; He will establish you and guard you from the evil one.
  John 17:15 — thematic parallel: Both speak of guarding people from the evil one. John records Jesus asking the Father to keep them without taking them out of the world; Thessalonians declares the faithful Lord will establish and guard the readers. The prayer, assurance and named divine roles are retained without replacing one title with another.
    John 17:15 I do not ask that Thou take them out of the world, but that Thou keep them from the evil one.

2 Thessalonians 3:4
And we are confident in the Lord about you, that you both do and will do what we command.
  2 Corinthians 7:16 — thematic parallel: Both express confidence in the readers. Corinthians rejoices in confidence in them in everything, following a report of obedience; Thessalonians locates confidence in the Lord and specifies present and future doing of the commands. Their stated grounds and scopes remain distinct.
    2 Corinthians 7:16 I rejoice that in everything I have confidence in you.

2 Thessalonians 3:5
And may the Lord direct your hearts into the love of God and into the endurance of Christ.
  1 Thessalonians 3:12–3:13 — thematic parallel: Both are prayers linking the Lord, the community's hearts and love. First Thessalonians asks increasing love and established hearts in holiness; this unit asks hearts directed into the love of God and the endurance of Christ. The different verbs and the actual love/endurance formulations remain without a supplied interpretation of the genitives.
    1 Thessalonians 3:12 And may the Lord make you increase and overflow in love for one another and for all, just as we also do for you,
    1 Thessalonians 3:13 so as to establish your hearts blameless in holiness before our God and Father at the coming of our Lord Jesus with all His saints. Amen.

2 Thessalonians 3:6
Now we command you, brothers, in the name of [our] Lord Jesus Christ, to keep away from every brother who walks in a disorderly way and not according to the tradition which they received from us.
  2 Thessalonians 3:14–3:15 — internal thematic parallel: The later letter-disobedience instruction develops the keep-away directive within the same discussion. It names non-association and then admonition as a brother rather than an enemy; this unit names a disorderly brother and received tradition. Those internal instructions are linked without losing the later qualification or changing the printed they-received wording.
    2 Thessalonians 3:14 And if anyone does not obey our word through this letter, take note of that person, and do not associate with him, so that he may be ashamed.
    2 Thessalonians 3:15 Yet do not regard him as an enemy, but admonish him as a brother.

2 Thessalonians 3:7
For you yourselves know how you ought to imitate us, because we did not behave in a disorderly way among you,
  1 Corinthians 11:1 — thematic parallel: Both direct imitation of the speaker or speakers. Corinthians says imitate me as I imitate Christ; Thessalonians says imitate us because they did not behave disorderly among the readers. The singular/plural and the explicit Christ-pattern versus conduct-example details remain distinct.
    1 Corinthians 11:1 Imitate me, just as I also imitate Christ.

2 Thessalonians 3:8
nor did we eat anyone’s bread without payment, but with labor and toil we worked night and day so as not to burden any of you.
  1 Thessalonians 2:9 — thematic parallel: Both explicitly recall labor and toil, working night and day so as not to burden the readers. First Thessalonians adds proclaiming God's gospel; this unit adds not eating anyone's bread without payment. The common labor formula retains those different additions.
    1 Thessalonians 2:9 For you remember, brothers, our labor and toil: working night and day so as not to burden any of you, we proclaimed to you the gospel of God.

2 Thessalonians 3:9
This was not because we lack the right, but so that we might give ourselves to you as an example for you to imitate.
  1 Corinthians 9:12 — thematic parallel: Both explain not using a ministry-support right. Corinthians says they bore everything rather than hinder Christ's good news; Thessalonians says working without burden did not mean lacking the right but supplied an example to imitate. The stated reasons for foregoing support are different.
    1 Corinthians 9:12 If others share in this right over you, do we not all the more? But we did not use this right; instead, we bear everything so that we may put no hindrance in the way of the good news of Christ.

2 Thessalonians 3:10
For even when we were with you, we gave you this command: if anyone is not willing to work, let him not eat either.
  2 Thessalonians 3:12 — internal thematic parallel: The later positive instruction to work quietly and eat one's own bread gives the internal work-and-food counterpart to this earlier command. This unit explicitly conditions non-eating on not being willing to work; it does not say inability to work. The conditional prohibition and the positive corrective command retain their different forms.
    2 Thessalonians 3:12 Now such people we command and urge in the Lord Jesus Christ to work quietly and eat their own bread.

2 Thessalonians 3:11
For we hear that some among you are walking in a disorderly way, not working at all but working at everyone else’s business.
  1 Timothy 5:13 — thematic parallel: Both associate idleness or not working with interference in others' affairs. Timothy's passage, in its younger-widow discussion, adds going around houses, gossip and things that ought not be said; Thessalonians describes some readers working at everyone else's business rather than working. The communities and described groups are not identified as one.
    1 Timothy 5:13 And at the same time they learn to be idle, going around the houses, and not only idle but also gossips and busybodies, saying things they ought not to say.

2 Thessalonians 3:12
Now such people we command and urge in the Lord Jesus Christ to work quietly and eat their own bread.
  1 Thessalonians 4:11–4:12 — thematic parallel: Both connect quiet living with one's own work. First Thessalonians urges quietness, attention to one's own affairs and work with one's hands toward proper outside conduct and need of no one; this unit commands working quietly and eating one's own bread. The different grounds and stated outcomes remain distinct.
    1 Thessalonians 4:11 and to make it your ambition to be quiet, to attend to your own affairs, and to work with your own hands, just as we instructed you,
    1 Thessalonians 4:12 so that you may walk properly toward those outside and have need of no one.

2 Thessalonians 3:13
But you, brothers, do not lose heart in doing good.
  Galatians 6:9 — thematic parallel: Both urge perseverance in doing good. Galatians says not to grow weary and adds reaping in the proper season if not giving up; Thessalonians says not to lose heart in doing good. The shared exhortation retains those different words and the additional harvest image.
    Galatians 6:9 And let us not grow weary in doing good, for in the proper season we will reap, if we do not give up.

2 Thessalonians 3:14
And if anyone does not obey our word through this letter, take note of that person, and do not associate with him, so that he may be ashamed.
  1 Corinthians 5:11 — thematic parallel: Both direct non-association in specified cases within the community. Corinthians lists conduct by someone called a brother and adds not even eating with such a person; Thessalonians names not obeying this letter and the purpose of shame. The conditions and added meal instruction are not transferred as identical rules, and3:15's brother-not-enemy qualification remains in context.
    1 Corinthians 5:11 But now I have written to you not to associate with anyone who is called a brother if he is sexually immoral or greedy or an idolater or abusive or a drunkard or grasping—not even to eat with such a person.

2 Thessalonians 3:15
Yet do not regard him as an enemy, but admonish him as a brother.
  Leviticus 19:17 — thematic parallel: Both pair correction with a rejection of hostility toward a brother. Leviticus prohibits hating a brother in one's mind while requiring reproof of the neighbor; Thessalonians says not to regard him as an enemy but to admonish as a brother. The mind, reproof and non-enemy formulations remain distinct rather than one identical legal instruction.
    Leviticus 19:17 You shall not hate your brother in your mind; you shall surely reprove your neighbour, and you shall not bear sin because of him.

2 Thessalonians 3:16
Now may the Lord of peace Himself give you peace always in every way. The Lord be with all of you.
  Romans 15:33 — thematic parallel: Both close with a peace-and-presence blessing for all recipients. Romans says may the God of peace be with you all; Thessalonians names the Lord of peace giving peace always in every way and adds the Lord be with all. The God/Lord titles and longer petition are retained separately.
    Romans 15:33 And may the God of peace be with you all. Amen.

2 Thessalonians 3:17
The greeting is in my own hand—Paul’s—which is a sign in every letter. This is how I write.
  1 Corinthians 16:21 — thematic parallel: Both present the greeting as in Paul's own hand. Thessalonians adds that it is a sign in every letter and this is how he writes; Corinthians gives the shorter signature formula. Its unit is not expanded with the additional sign-every-letter statement.
    1 Corinthians 16:21 The greeting is in my own hand—Paul’s.

2 Thessalonians 3:18
The grace of our Lord Jesus Christ be with all of you.
  1 Thessalonians 5:28 — thematic parallel: Both conclude with the grace of our Lord Jesus Christ being with the recipients. Thessalonians' second letter explicitly says all of you, while the first says you. The shared grace formula preserves the shorter target wording without adding an unprinted Amen.
    1 Thessalonians 5:28 The grace of our Lord Jesus Christ be with you.
