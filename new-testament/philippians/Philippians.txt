Philippians 1:1
Paul and Timothy, slaves of Christ Jesus, to all the saints in Christ Jesus who are in Philippi, together with overseers and deacons:
  1 Timothy 3:2 — thematic parallel: The address names overseers among the Philippian saints. The qualification passage describes the same office and specifies an overseer's character and ability to teach; it does not identify the Philippian officeholders with the people addressed in 1 Timothy.
    1 Timothy 3:2 Therefore the overseer must be above reproach, a husband of one wife, sober, self-controlled, orderly, hospitable, able to teach,
  1 Timothy 3:8 — thematic parallel: The Philippian deacons are named by office, while the target sets out deacons' dignity, truthful speech and restraint concerning wine and gain. This supplies a substantive office-and-qualification link without inventing the local deacons' names or a further hierarchy.
    1 Timothy 3:8 Deacons likewise must be dignified, not double-tongued, not given to much wine, not seeking shameful gain,

Philippians 1:2
grace to you and peace from God our Father and the Lord Jesus Christ.
  2 Corinthians 1:2 — thematic parallel: Both letters bless their recipients with grace and peace from God our Father and the Lord Jesus Christ. This is the shared epistolary blessing, addressed to different communities.
    2 Corinthians 1:2 Grace to you and peace from God our Father and the Lord Jesus Christ.

Philippians 1:3
I thank my God every time I remember you,
  Romans 1:8–1:9 — thematic parallel: Both passages give thanks to God for the recipients and speak of continuing remembrance. Romans explicitly mentions their widely proclaimed faith; that particular reason is not stated in this Philippians verse.
    Romans 1:8 First, I thank my God through Jesus Christ for all of you, because your faith is proclaimed in the whole world.
    Romans 1:9 For God, whom I serve in my spirit in the gospel of His Son, is my witness that I remember you without ceasing,

Philippians 1:4
always in every petition of mine for all of you, making my petition with joy,
  1 Thessalonians 1:2–1:3 — thematic parallel: The joyful petitions for all the Philippians parallel the Thessalonian letter's continual thanksgiving, prayer and remembrance of its recipients. The latter names faith, love and hope; it does not reproduce Philippians's express joy wording.
    1 Thessalonians 1:2 We always give thanks to God for all of you, mentioning you in our prayers, unceasingly
    1 Thessalonians 1:3 remembering your work of faith and labor of love and endurance of hope in our Lord Jesus Christ before our God and Father,

Philippians 1:5
because of your fellowship in the gospel from the first day until now,
  Philippians 4:15 — internal general statement and example: The later giving-and-receiving partnership supplies a concrete instance of the Philippians' fellowship in the gospel from its beginning. The comparison does not reduce every aspect of gospel fellowship to financial support.
    Philippians 4:15 And you Philippians also know that at the beginning of the gospel, when I departed from Macedonia, no Church shared with me in the matter of giving and receiving except you alone,

Philippians 1:6
being confident of this very thing, that the One who began a good work in you will carry it on to completion until the day of Christ Jesus.
  1 Thessalonians 5:23–5:24 — thematic parallel: Both passages place the hoped-for completion with the divine agent: Philippians speaks of finishing the begun good work until Christ's day, while Thessalonians prays for complete sanctification and blamelessness at His coming and affirms the caller's faithfulness.
    1 Thessalonians 5:23 Now may the God of peace Himself sanctify you completely, and may your spirit and soul and body be kept whole, blameless at the coming of our Lord Jesus Christ.
    1 Thessalonians 5:24 He who calls you is faithful; He will also do it.

Philippians 1:7
It is right for me to think this about all of you, because I hold you in my heart, since both in my chains and in the defense and confirmation of the gospel you all share grace with me.
  Philippians 4:14 — internal partnership comparison: The later acknowledgment that the Philippians shared Paul's affliction gives a concrete counterpart to their partnership with him amid chains and gospel defense. Their participation in grace is broader than a claim that everyone was personally imprisoned.
    Philippians 4:14 Nevertheless, you did well to share in my affliction.

Philippians 1:9
And this I pray: that your love may abound still more and more in knowledge and every kind of discernment,
  Colossians 1:9–1:10 — thematic parallel: Both are intercessions seeking informed spiritual growth: Philippians asks for increasing love in knowledge and discernment, and Colossians for knowledge, wisdom and understanding expressed in a worthy, fruitful walk. Their lists of requested qualities are related rather than identical.
    Colossians 1:9 For this reason we too, since the day we heard, have not ceased praying for you and asking that you may be filled with the knowledge of His will in all spiritual wisdom and understanding,
    Colossians 1:10 to walk worthily of the Lord, pleasing Him in every way, bearing fruit in every good work and growing in the knowledge of God,

Philippians 1:10
so that you may approve the things that matter, so that you may be pure and without offense for the day of Christ,
  1 Corinthians 1:8 — thematic parallel: Both connect the community's desired condition with the day of Jesus Christ. Philippians asks that they approve what matters and be pure and without offense; Corinthians speaks of being kept firm and free from accusation until that day.
    1 Corinthians 1:8 He will also keep you firm until the end, free from accusation on the day of our Lord Jesus Christ.

Philippians 1:11
filled with the fruit of righteousness that comes through Jesus Christ, to the glory and praise of God.
  John 15:5–15:8 — thematic parallel: John's vine teaching links fruitfulness to remaining in Jesus and describes the Father's glorification through much fruit. Philippians similarly describes righteousness's fruit as coming through Jesus Christ to God's glory, without equating all details of the vine image with the prayer.
    John 15:5 I am the vine; you are the branches. The one who remains in Me, and I in him, bears much fruit, because apart from Me you can do nothing.
    John 15:6 If anyone does not remain in Me, he is thrown outside like the branch and withers; and they gather them and throw them into the fire, and they are burned.
    John 15:7 If you remain in Me and My words remain in you, ask whatever you wish, and it will be done for you.
    John 15:8 In this My Father has been glorified: that you bear much fruit and become My disciples.

Philippians 1:12

  2 Timothy 2:9 — thematic parallel: Both accounts distinguish Paul's confinement from the gospel's effectiveness: his circumstances advance the gospel in Philippians, while 2 Timothy says his chains do not chain God's word. This does not identify the imprisonments' locations or dates.
    2 Timothy 2:9 for which I suffer hardship even to chains as a criminal. But the word of God has not been chained.

    Philippians 1:12 Now I want you to know, brothers, that my circumstances have instead served to advance the gospel,

Philippians 1:14
and most of the brothers, having gained confidence in the Lord because of my chains, dare much more to speak the word without fear.
  1 Thessalonians 2:2 — thematic parallel: Both link hardship with continuing bold gospel speech. Philippians describes brothers encouraged by Paul's chains; Thessalonians describes the missionaries' own boldness after mistreatment in Philippi. The speakers, places and occasions are not merged.
    1 Thessalonians 2:2 But after we had already suffered and been shamefully treated in Philippi, as you know, we were bold in our God to speak to you the gospel of God amid much struggle.

  Philippians 1:16–1:17 — internal contrast and explanation: The following two notices explain the contrasted motives already named here: goodwill is developed as love and concern for gospel defense, while envy and strife are developed as selfish ambition seeking to add affliction to Paul's chains.
    Philippians 1:16 The latter do so from love, knowing that I am appointed for the defense of the gospel;
    Philippians 1:17 but the former announce Christ from selfish ambition, not purely, supposing that they will stir up affliction in my chains.

    Philippians 1:15 Some indeed proclaim Christ because of envy and strife, but some also because of goodwill.

Philippians 1:19
for I know that this will turn out for my salvation through your petition and the supply of the Spirit of Jesus Christ,
  Job 13:16 — allusion: The 'turn out' wording concerning Paul's salvation resembles Job's statement that this will turn out to be deliverance for him. Job continues with deceit's exclusion, whereas Philippians adds petition and the Spirit's supply; this is a qualified verbal scriptural echo, not an identical circumstance.
    Job 13:16 This will turn out to be deliverance for me, for deceit will not enter before Him.
  2 Corinthians 1:11 — thematic parallel: Both passages describe the recipients' prayer as helping Paul amid danger or affliction. Corinthians places that help beside God's deliverance and consequent thanksgiving; Philippians also expressly names the supply of the Spirit of Jesus Christ.
    2 Corinthians 1:11 as you too help us by prayer, so that many people may give thanks on our behalf for the gift granted to us through many.

Philippians 1:20
according to my eager expectation and hope that I will not be put to shame in anything, but with all boldness, as always, so also now, Christ will be magnified in my body, whether through life or through death.
  Romans 14:8 — thematic parallel: Both place life and death in relation to the Lord rather than self alone. Philippians desires Christ to be magnified in Paul's body in either outcome; Romans states that those living or dying for the Lord belong to Him.
    Romans 14:8 for if we live, we live for the Lord, and if we die, we die for the Lord. So whether we live or die, we are the Lord’s.

    Acts 5:41 So they left the council rejoicing that they had been counted worthy to suffer dishonor for the Name.

    Ephesians 1:21 far above every rule and authority and power and dominion and every name that is named, not only in this age but also in the one to come.
