3 John 1:1
The elder, to the beloved Gaius, whom I love in truth.
  2 John 1:1 — Opening address and love in truth: Both letters open with the elder addressing people loved in truth. This letter names beloved Gaius; the other names a chosen lady and her children and adds others knowing the truth. The matching address formula does not identify Gaius with another similarly named person.
    2 John 1:1 The elder, to the chosen lady and her children, whom I love in truth—and not I alone, but also all who have come to know the truth—

3 John 1:2
Beloved, I pray that all may go well with you and that you may be in good health, just as all goes well with your soul.
  1 Thessalonians 5:23 — Prayer for the recipient’s whole well-being: The personal prayer for health and all going well, alongside the soul's well-being, can be compared with Paul's prayer for spirit, soul and body to be kept whole. Paul's sanctification and coming context differs; neither prayer is converted into a guarantee of material prosperity.
    1 Thessalonians 5:23 Now may the God of peace Himself sanctify you completely, and may your spirit and soul and body be kept whole, blameless at the coming of our Lord Jesus Christ.

3 John 1:3
For I rejoiced greatly when brothers came and bore witness to your truthfulness, just as you walk in truth.
  2 John 1:4 — Joy at walking in truth: Both report great joy over walking in truth. Here visiting brothers bear witness to the recipient's truthfulness; the other letter reports finding some of the lady's children walking in truth according to the Father's commandment. Their people and reports remain distinct.
    2 John 1:4 I rejoiced greatly that I have found some of your children walking in truth, just as we received a commandment from the Father.

3 John 1:4
I have no greater joy than this: to hear that my children are walking in the truth.
  1 Thessalonians 2:19–2:20 — Recipients as the writer’s joy: Paul calls the recipients his glory and joy in an expectation of Jesus' coming; the elder says his greatest joy is hearing his children walk in truth. These are specific expressions of joy in the recipients, without identifying the groups or turning children into an asserted biological relationship.
    1 Thessalonians 2:19 For what is our hope or joy or crown of boasting before our Lord Jesus at His coming? Is it not also you?
    1 Thessalonians 2:20 For you are our glory and joy.

3 John 1:5
Beloved, you act faithfully in whatever you do for the brothers, even though they are strangers,
  Hebrews 13:2 — Welcoming strangers: The faithful action toward brothers who are strangers provides a concrete counterpart to Hebrews' command not to forget hospitality. Hebrews adds unknowingly entertaining angels; this letter calls the welcomed people brothers and does not identify them as angels.
    Hebrews 13:2 Do not forget hospitality, for through it some unknowingly entertained angels.

3 John 1:6
who have borne witness to your love before the Church. You will do well to send them onward in a manner worthy of God,
  Titus 3:13 — Provision for onward travel: The request to send the brothers onward worthily corresponds to the instruction to send Zenas and Apollos onward diligently so they lack nothing. The named travelers and practical supply instruction in Titus are a comparison, not an identification of the unnamed brothers here.
    Titus 3:13 Make every effort to send Zenas the legal expert and Apollos on their way, so that they lack nothing.

3 John 1:7
for they went out for the sake of the Name, receiving nothing from the Gentiles.
  1 Corinthians 9:18 — Mission and refusing payment: Paul describes proclaiming the good news without charge so as not to make full use of his right in it. This gives a concrete mission-and-payment comparison with travelers receiving nothing from the Gentiles for the Name's sake; the different recipients and scope of payment restraint remain distinct.
    1 Corinthians 9:18 What, then, are my wages? That when proclaiming the good news I may offer it without charge, so as not to make full use of my right in the good news.

3 John 1:8
We therefore ought to support such people, so that we may become fellow workers with the truth.
  Philippians 4:14–4:16 — Supporting the work through sharing: The Philippians' sharing in affliction and sending supplies for Paul's need gives a concrete example of support for gospel work. Here the obligation to support such travelers makes supporters fellow workers with the truth; this letter does not name the Philippians or specify the same gifts.
    Philippians 4:14 Nevertheless, you did well to share in my affliction.
    Philippians 4:15 And you Philippians also know that at the beginning of the gospel, when I departed from Macedonia, no Church shared with me in the matter of giving and receiving except you alone,
    Philippians 4:16 because even in Thessalonica you sent once and twice for my need.

3 John 1:9
I wrote something to the Church, but Diotrephes, who loves to be first among them, does not welcome us.
  Mark 10:42–10:44 — Loving first place and servant leadership: Diotrephes' love of being first and refusal to welcome the writer can be set beside Jesus' contrast between domineering rulers and becoming first through service as slave of all. This is a specific leadership contrast, not an assertion that Diotrephes was among the Gospel's hearers.
    Mark 10:42 Jesus called them to Him and said to them, “You know that those regarded as rulers of the nations lord it over them, and their great ones exercise authority over them.
    Mark 10:43 But it is not so among you. Whoever wants to become great among you shall be your servant,
    Mark 10:44 and whoever wants to be first among you shall be slave of all.

3 John 1:10
Therefore, if I come, I will call attention to the works he is doing: he is talking malicious nonsense about us. Not content with these things, he neither welcomes the brothers himself nor allows those who want to do so, and he puts them out of the Church.
  3 John 1:5–1:8 — Refusal contrasted with support: The earlier commendation of faithful welcome and instruction to support traveling brothers directly contrasts with refusing brothers, obstructing willing hosts and expelling them here. The letter itself supplies the opposed conduct; no unprinted details about the expulsion's procedure are added.
    3 John 1:5 Beloved, you act faithfully in whatever you do for the brothers, even though they are strangers,
    3 John 1:6 who have borne witness to your love before the Church. You will do well to send them onward in a manner worthy of God,
    3 John 1:7 for they went out for the sake of the Name, receiving nothing from the Gentiles.
    3 John 1:8 We therefore ought to support such people, so that we may become fellow workers with the truth.

3 John 1:11
Beloved, do not imitate what is evil, but what is good. The one who does good is from God; the one who does evil has not seen God.
  1 John 3:10 — Conduct disclosing relation to God: Both statements distinguish a relation to God by conduct. First John names not practicing righteousness and not loving a brother; this letter contrasts doing good as from God with doing evil and not seeing God. The predicates are related without being exchanged as identical clauses.
    1 John 3:10 By this the children of God and the children of the devil are evident: everyone who does not practice righteousness is not from God, nor is the one who does not love his brother.

3 John 1:12
Demetrius has received testimony from everyone and from the truth itself. We also bear witness, and you know that our testimony is true.
  John 21:24 — Assurance of true testimony: The Gospel's statement that we know the disciple's testimony is true provides a specific testimony-assurance counterpart to we also bear witness and you know our testimony is true. The different witnesses and Demetrius' commendation remain distinct; the link does not identify him as that disciple.
    John 21:24 This is the disciple who bears witness about these things and who wrote these things; and we know that his testimony is true.

3 John 1:13
I had many things to write to you, but I do not wish to write to you with ink and a reed pen.
  2 John 1:12 — Writing materials and a desired visit: Both closings set many things to write beside a preference for personal communication. Second John names paper and ink; this verse names ink and a reed pen, with the face-to-face hope following separately in native verse14. Their native divisions and material words remain intact.
    2 John 1:12 Although I have many things to write to you all, I did not wish to do so with paper and ink. Instead, I hope to come to you and speak face to face, so that our joy may be made full.

3 John 1:14
But I hope to see you soon, and we will speak face to face.
  2 John 1:12 — Hope of face-to-face conversation: Both letters hope for personal presence and speaking face to face. Here the writer hopes to see the recipient soon; Second John hopes to come and adds the purpose of completed joy and a writing-material contrast. The different verbs and clauses do not establish the same planned journey.
    2 John 1:12 Although I have many things to write to you all, I did not wish to do so with paper and ink. Instead, I hope to come to you and speak face to face, so that our joy may be made full.

3 John 1:15
Peace to you. The friends greet you. Greet the friends by name.
  1 Peter 5:14 — Greeting and a wish of peace: Both closings join mutual greeting with a wish of peace. Peter names a kiss of love and all who are in Christ; this letter exchanges friends' greetings and asks greeting by name. Neither closing supplies the other's addressees or manner as unprinted words.
    1 Peter 5:14 Greet one another with a kiss of love. Peace to all of you who are in Christ.
