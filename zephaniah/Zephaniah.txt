Zephaniah 1:1
The word of the Lord that came to Zephaniah, son of Cushi, son of Gedaliah, son of Amariah, son of Hezekiah, in the days of Josiah son of Amon, king of Judah.
  2 Kings 21:24-26 — named royal setting: Zephaniah dates the word to Josiah son of Amon; Kings recounts Amon's death and the people's installation of Josiah his son, establishing the named royal succession.

Zephaniah 1:2
Let everything utterly disappear from the face of the earth, says the Lord,
  Genesis 6:7 — divine announcement of sweeping earthly destruction: Zephaniah announces the disappearance of everything from the earth; Genesis announces the removal of humanity together with animals and birds. The shared scope is a judgment pattern, without identifying the two events.

Zephaniah 1:3
let humanity and livestock disappear, let birds of the sky and fish of the sea disappear, and I will remove people from the face of the earth, says the Lord.
  Hosea 4:3 — judgment affecting land creatures, birds and fish: Zephaniah includes humanity, livestock, birds and fish in the announced disappearance; Hosea describes the land mourning with beasts, birds and even sea fish failing. Both extend judgment beyond human society.

Zephaniah 1:4
I will stretch My hand against Judah and all Jerusalem’s inhabitants, and remove from this place the names of Baal and the names of the priests,
  2 Kings 23:4-5 — removal of Baal worship and its priesthood: Zephaniah announces removal of Baal's names and the priests' names from Judah and Jerusalem; Kings describes Josiah removing Baal's objects and abolishing the priests of illicit worship. The correspondence concerns the named cult and priesthood.

Zephaniah 1:5
those worshiping the army of heaven on rooftops, those swearing by the Lord and those swearing by their king,
  Jeremiah 19:13 — rooftop worship of the heavenly host: Zephaniah identifies those worshiping heaven's army on rooftops; Jeremiah identifies roofs where offerings were made to heaven's army. The link concerns rooftop worship, not an identification of Zephaniah's separate oath by their king.

Zephaniah 1:6
those turning away from the Lord, those who did not seek the Lord and those who did not hold fast to the Lord.
  Hosea 7:10 — failure to return to or seek the Lord: Zephaniah condemns turning away and failing to seek or hold fast to the Lord; Hosea says the people did not return to the Lord or seek Him while announcing the humbling of their arrogance.

Zephaniah 1:7
Revere the Lord God, for the Lord’s day is near. The Lord has prepared His sacrifice and consecrated His invited guests.
  Isaiah 34:6-8 — divine judgment portrayed as the Lord's sacrifice: Zephaniah announces the near day and a sacrifice prepared by the Lord; Isaiah presents the Lord's sacrifice in Bosor in the setting of His judgment day. The sacrificial imagery belongs to judgment in both.

Zephaniah 1:8
On the day of the Lord’s sacrifice I will exact judgment upon the rulers, the king’s house and all wearing foreign clothes.
  Jeremiah 22:1-5 — judgment addressing the royal household: Zephaniah directs judgment against rulers and the king's house; Jeremiah addresses Judah's king and household and warns that the house will become desolate if justice is refused. The link does not interpret the foreign clothes in Zephaniah.

Zephaniah 1:9
On that day I will openly exact judgment upon everyone at the gateways, those filling the house of the Lord their God with impiety and deceit.
  Jeremiah 7:9-11 — impiety associated with the Lord's house: Zephaniah condemns those filling the Lord their God's house with impiety and deceit; Jeremiah condemns theft and falsehood followed by standing in God's house, which they treat as a robbers' cave.

Zephaniah 1:10
On that day, says the Lord, there will be a cry from the gate of those who pierce, a wail from the second quarter and a great crashing from the hills.
  Amos 5:16-17 — public cries and mourning during divine judgment: Zephaniah locates cries, wailing and crashing in the city's quarters and hills; Amos announces lamentation in public ways and streets when the Lord passes through. Both make judgment audible in communal spaces.

Zephaniah 1:11
Lament, inhabitants of the battered place, for all the people of Canaan have been made alike; all exalted by silver have been destroyed.
  James 5:1-3 — lament over wealth that cannot preserve its owners: Zephaniah calls for lament because those exalted by silver are destroyed; James tells the rich to weep over coming miseries and describes gold and silver becoming testimony against them.

Zephaniah 1:12
On that day I will search Jerusalem with a lamp and exact judgment upon men contemptuous about their safeguards, who say in their hearts, “The Lord will certainly do neither good nor harm.”
  Psalms 9:32-34 — denial of divine concern or accountability challenged: Zephaniah exposes hearts saying the Lord will do neither good nor harm before announcing His searching judgment; the psalm challenges the wicked person's belief that God forgets and will not call to account and calls on God to remember the needy.

Zephaniah 1:13
Their strength will become plunder and their houses destruction. They will build houses but certainly not inhabit them, plant vineyards but certainly not drink their wine.
  Deuteronomy 28:30 — covenant curse of building without inhabiting and planting without enjoyment: Zephaniah says houses will be built without being inhabited and vineyards planted without their wine being drunk; Deuteronomy's curse likewise denies enjoyment of a built house and planted vineyard.
  Amos 5:11 — prophetic repetition of uninhabited houses and undrunk vineyard wine: Zephaniah's denial of dwelling in newly built houses and drinking planted vineyards' wine closely matches Amos's judgment against oppressors who built houses and planted desirable vineyards.

Zephaniah 1:14
For the Lord’s great day is near, near and very swift. The sound of the Lord’s day is bitter and harsh; it is appointed mighty.
  Joel 1:15 — nearness of the Lord's day: Zephaniah declares the Lord's great day near and very swift; Joel laments that the Lord's day is near and comes with affliction. The correspondence concerns imminence and distress.

Zephaniah 1:15
That day is a day of wrath, a day of affliction and constraint, a day of untimely ruin and destruction, a day of darkness and gloom, a day of cloud and mist,
  Joel 2:2 — the Lord's day described by darkness, gloom, cloud and mist: Zephaniah calls the day one of darkness and gloom, cloud and mist; Joel uses the same cluster of dark-weather descriptions for the approaching day.

Zephaniah 1:16
a day of trumpet and shouting against fortified cities and high corners.
  Joel 2:1 — trumpet alarm associated with the near day: Zephaniah describes a day of trumpet and shouting against fortifications; Joel orders a trumpet in Zion and a proclamation on the holy mountain because the Lord's day approaches. Both connect alarm with the day, without equating their city details.

Zephaniah 1:17
I will press people hard, and they will walk like the blind because they sinned against the Lord. He will pour out their blood like dust and their flesh like dung.
  Deuteronomy 28:28-29 — punitive blindness and helpless movement: Zephaniah says sinners will walk like the blind; Deuteronomy's curse includes blindness and groping as a blind person does. Both portray impaired movement as judgment.

Zephaniah 1:18
Their silver and gold will certainly not rescue them on the day of the Lord’s wrath. In the fire of His jealousy all the earth will be consumed, for He will bring completion and haste upon all the earth’s inhabitants.
  Ezekiel 7:19 — silver and gold rendered useless in judgment: Zephaniah says silver and gold cannot rescue on the wrath day; Ezekiel describes silver thrown into streets and gold disregarded, unable to satisfy life or fill bellies. Both expose precious metals' failure under judgment, though Ezekiel's stated failure concerns sustenance.
  Zephaniah 3:8 — same-book repetition of universal judgment by jealous fire: The opening oracle says the earth will be consumed in the fire of God's jealousy; the later oracle repeats that all the earth will be consumed by His jealous fire when nations are assembled for wrath.

Zephaniah 2:1
Gather and bind yourselves together, O undisciplined nation,
  Joel 2:15-17 — summoning the people to gather before the Lord: Zephaniah orders the undisciplined nation to gather and bind together before wrath arrives; Joel calls for a gathered, consecrated congregation and intercession that the Lord spare His people.

Zephaniah 2:2
before you become like a passing flower, before the Lord’s wrath comes upon you, before the day of the Lord’s fury comes upon you.
  Isaiah 40:6-8 — the passing flower as an image of transience: Zephaniah urges action before becoming like a passing flower and before wrath; Isaiah likens human glory to grass's flower that falls while God's word remains. The shared flower image emphasizes the fragility underlying Zephaniah's urgency.

Zephaniah 2:3
Seek the Lord, all the humble of the earth; practice judgment, seek righteousness and answer these things, so that you may be sheltered on the day of the Lord’s wrath.
  Amos 5:14-15 — seeking good and practicing judgment in hope of mercy: Zephaniah calls the humble to seek the Lord and righteousness and practice judgment in hope of shelter; Amos urges seeking good and restoring judgment in hope of the Lord's mercy upon the remnant.

Zephaniah 2:4
For Gaza will be plundered and Ashkelon will disappear; Ashdod will be cast out at midday and Ekron uprooted.
  Amos 1:6-8 — parallel judgment against the named coastal cities: Zephaniah names Gaza, Ashkelon, Ashdod and Ekron in judgment; Amos's oracle names Gaza, Ascalon, Azotus and Accaron. The overlapping set of coastal cities is preserved with each passage's different native spellings and punishments.

Zephaniah 2:5
Woe to the seacoast’s inhabitants, sojourners of Cretans! The Lord’s word is against you, Canaan, land of foreigners: I will destroy you from your dwelling.
  Ezekiel 25:15-17 — judgment against Cretans and coastal inhabitants: Zephaniah addresses the coastal Cretans and announces destruction of their dwelling; Ezekiel announces the destruction of Cretans and the coast's remaining inhabitants. Both locate the threatened population by the coast.

Zephaniah 2:6
Crete will become pasture for flocks and an enclosure for sheep,
  Isaiah 17:1-2 — ruined territory becoming pasture for flocks: Zephaniah says Crete will become pasture and a sheep enclosure; Isaiah describes ruined Damascus as an abandoned resting place for flocks. The connection is the reversal from inhabited strength to grazing land, not identity of the places.

Zephaniah 2:7
and the seacoast will belong to the remnant of Judah’s house. They will graze upon them in Ashkelon’s houses; at evening they will lodge away from the sons of Judah, for the Lord their God has visited them and turned their captivity.
  Amos 9:14 — reversal of captivity associated with restored habitation: Zephaniah assigns the coast and houses of Ashkelon to the remnant and says the Lord visits and turns captivity; Amos promises to reverse Israel's captivity so they inhabit rebuilt cities. The shared restoration does not erase Zephaniah's unusual lodging clause.

Zephaniah 2:8
I heard Moab’s reproaches and the Ammonites’ blows, with which they reproached My people and exalted themselves against My borders.
  Ezekiel 25:3-11 — Moab and Ammon condemned for hostility toward God's people: Zephaniah cites Moab's reproaches and Ammon's blows and boasts against the people's borders; Ezekiel condemns Ammon's rejoicing over Israel's devastation and Moab's disparaging comparison of Israel and Judah with other nations.

Zephaniah 2:9
Therefore, as I live, says the Lord of powers, the God of Israel, Moab will be like Sodom and the Ammonites like Gomorrah, and Damascus abandoned like a heap on a threshing floor and vanished forever. My people’s remnant will plunder them; My nation’s remnant will inherit them.
  Genesis 19:24-25 — Sodom and Gomorrah as a named destruction comparison: Zephaniah says Moab will become like Sodom and Ammon like Gomorrah; Genesis records the Lord overthrowing those two cities and their surrounding country. This explains the named comparison without assigning every additional Zephaniah clause to Genesis.
  Isaiah 17:1-2 — parallel announcement of Damascus becoming an abandoned ruin: The native Zephaniah unit explicitly includes Damascus becoming abandoned and vanishing; Isaiah announces Damascus's removal from cities into an enduring abandoned ruin. The Damascus clause is retained rather than omitted to match another edition.

Zephaniah 2:10
This will be theirs for their arrogance, because they reproached and exalted themselves against the Lord Almighty.
  Isaiah 16:6-7 — Moab's arrogance followed by judgment and lament: Zephaniah attributes the preceding punishment to arrogance and reproach against the Lord; Isaiah likewise names Moab's excessive insolence and arrogance before announcing Moab's lamentation.

Zephaniah 2:11
The Lord will appear against them and destroy all the gods of the nations of the earth. All the islands of the nations will worship Him, each from his place.
  Malachi 1:11 — worship of the Lord among nations in their own places: Zephaniah says the nations' islands will worship the Lord, each from its place; Malachi says His name is glorified among the nations and offerings are brought in every place. Both extend worship beyond one local population.

Zephaniah 2:12
You too, Ethiopians, are slain by My sword.
  Ezekiel 30:3-6 — Ethiopia included in the sphere of divine military judgment: Zephaniah addresses Ethiopians as slain by God's sword; Ezekiel places turmoil in Ethiopia alongside the sword against Egypt and falling allies. The parallel is regional judgment involving Ethiopia, while Ezekiel explicitly places its wounded men in Egypt.

Zephaniah 2:13
He will stretch His hand against the north and destroy the Assyrian, making Nineveh a waterless wasteland like a wilderness.
  Nahum 3:7 — Nineveh named as the object of devastating judgment: Zephaniah announces Assyria's destruction and Nineveh's waterless desolation; Nahum depicts observers recoiling from miserable Nineveh and finding no consolation for her. Both directly name Nineveh's collapse.

Zephaniah 2:14
Flocks and all the wild animals of the earth will graze within her; chameleons and hedgehogs will lie in her recessed panels. Wild animals will cry in her openings, and ravens in her gateways, for cedar is her structure.
  Isaiah 34:10-15 — deserted built places occupied by wild creatures: Zephaniah describes grazing animals, hedgehogs and ravens among architectural remains; Isaiah describes deserted territory and fortifications becoming dwellings for hedgehogs, ravens and other creatures. The shared ruin scene does not identify the locations or every creature.

Zephaniah 2:15
This is the contemptuous city dwelling in confidence, saying in her heart, “I am, and there is no one after me.” How she became a wasteland, pasture for wild animals! Everyone passing through her will hiss and wave his hands.
  Isaiah 47:8-11 — self-sufficient city boast overturned by sudden ruin: Zephaniah's confident city says 'I am, and there is no one after me' before becoming a wasteland; Isaiah's confident city says 'I am, and there is no other' before unexpected destruction. Both expose the same kind of exclusive boast.

Zephaniah 3:2
She did not heed a voice or accept discipline; she did not trust the Lord or approach her God.
  Deuteronomy 9:23-24 — refusal to hear and trust the Lord: Zephaniah says the city did not heed the voice or trust the Lord; Deuteronomy recalls disobeying God's word, not believing Him and not hearkening to His voice. The corresponding failures are hearing and trust.
  Jeremiah 7:27–28 — refusal to hear the Lord and accept instruction: Zephaniah says the city did not heed a voice or accept discipline; Jeremiah's native composite unit names a nation that did not hear the Lord's voice or accept instruction. The matched refusal of hearing and correction is explicit in both; the composite target is preserved as one source-edition unit.

Zephaniah 3:3
Her rulers within her are like roaring lions; her judges are like Arabian wolves, leaving nothing for morning.
  Ezekiel 22:25-27 — predatory lions and wolves used for civic leaders: Zephaniah compares rulers to roaring lions and judges to Arabian wolves; Ezekiel compares leaders to roaring lions and rulers to prey-seizing wolves. Both expose leadership as predatory while retaining Zephaniah's Arabian designation.

Zephaniah 3:4
Her prophets are bearers of a spirit, contemptuous men; her priests profane holy things and act impiously toward law.
  Ezekiel 22:26-28 — priests profaning holy things and prophets failing their office: Zephaniah condemns priests who profane holy things and act impiously toward the law, alongside contemptuous prophets; Ezekiel condemns priests setting aside the law and profaning holy things, and prophets speaking without the Lord's word.

Zephaniah 3:5
But the Lord is righteous within her and will certainly do no injustice. Morning by morning He will bring His judgment to light; He was not hidden and knew no injustice in exaction, nor injustice in strife.
  Deuteronomy 32:4 — God's righteousness excludes injustice: Zephaniah says the righteous Lord does no injustice and brings His judgment to light; Deuteronomy calls His ways judgments and denies unrighteousness in Him. Both ground judgment in God's own righteousness.

Zephaniah 3:6
In destruction I tore down the proud; their corners vanished. I will make their roads utterly desolate, with no passing through; their cities failed because no one existed or dwelt there.
  Leviticus 26:31-33 — divine devastation of cities and land: Zephaniah describes roads desolate and cities without inhabitants through God's action; Leviticus warns that God will desolate cities and land and scatter their people among nations.

Zephaniah 3:7
I said, “Only fear Me and accept discipline, and you will certainly not be destroyed from her eyes,” with all I exacted upon her. Prepare, rise early! All their gleaning has been ruined.
  Proverbs 3:11-12 — call to accept the Lord's discipline: Zephaniah presents God's appeal to fear Him and accept discipline so destruction may be averted; Proverbs exhorts the son not to despise the Lord's discipline because He disciplines those He loves.

Zephaniah 3:8
Therefore wait for Me, says the Lord, for the day of My rising for testimony. My judgment is for assemblies of nations, to receive kings and pour all My furious wrath upon them; for all the earth will be consumed in My jealous fire.
  Joel 4:2 — assembly of nations for divine judgment: Zephaniah describes God's judgment for assemblies of nations, to receive kings and pour out wrath; Joel explicitly says God gathers all nations into the valley to enter judgment with them. Both place the international assembly before the judging Lord.

Zephaniah 3:9
For then I will change a tongue for peoples, for its generation, so that all call upon the Lord’s name and serve Him under one yoke.
  Genesis 11:6-9 — divine alteration of human speech with contrasting communal outcomes: Zephaniah promises a changed tongue so all call on the Lord and serve under one yoke; Genesis describes God confounding shared language and scattering people. The contrast concerns divine action on speech and communal direction, without claiming an explicit quotation or identifying the precise change of language.

Zephaniah 3:10
From the far reaches of Ethiopia’s rivers they will bring sacrifices to Me.
  Isaiah 18:1-7 — offerings to the Lord in a setting beyond Ethiopia's rivers: Zephaniah says sacrifices will be brought from the far reaches of Ethiopia's rivers; Isaiah opens with a land beyond those rivers and concludes with gifts brought to the Lord's named place on Zion. The common geography and offering motif support the link.

Zephaniah 3:11
On that day you will certainly not be ashamed of all the practices by which you acted impiously toward Me; then I will remove from you the contemptuous acts of your arrogance, and you will no longer boast upon My holy mountain.
  Isaiah 2:11-12 — removal of human arrogance before the Lord: Zephaniah promises to remove arrogant contemptuous behavior so there is no boast on His holy mountain; Isaiah announces a day humbling the insolent and elevated, leaving the Lord alone exalted.

Zephaniah 3:12
I will leave within you a meek and humble people, and they will revere the Lord’s name,
  Isaiah 14:32 — humble people preserved by the Lord in Zion's setting: Zephaniah promises a meek and humble people remaining and revering the Lord's name; Isaiah says the Lord founded Zion and that through Him the humble among the people will be saved.

Zephaniah 3:13
the remnant of Israel; they will do no injustice or speak vain things, and no deceitful tongue will be found in their mouth. For they will graze and lie down, and no one will frighten them.
  Micah 4:4 — rest without anyone causing fear: Zephaniah's remnant will graze and lie down with no one frightening them; Micah promises rest under vine and fig tree with no one causing fear. Both give concrete peaceful rest as a feature of restoration.

Zephaniah 3:14
Rejoice greatly, daughter Zion! Proclaim, daughter Jerusalem! Be glad and delight with all your heart, daughter Jerusalem!
  Zechariah 9:9 — daughter Zion and Jerusalem summoned to rejoice: Zephaniah calls daughter Zion and Jerusalem to rejoice and proclaim wholeheartedly; Zechariah similarly addresses daughter Zion and Jerusalem with rejoicing and proclamation as their king comes.

Zephaniah 3:15
The Lord removed your wrongdoings and has redeemed you from your enemies’ hand. The Lord, King of Israel, is within you; you will see evils no more.
  Zechariah 2:14 — the Lord dwelling within rejoicing Zion: Zephaniah grounds Zion's security in the Lord, Israel's King, being within her; Zechariah tells daughter Zion to rejoice because the Lord is coming to dwell within her.

Zephaniah 3:16
At that time the Lord will say to Jerusalem, “Take courage, Zion; do not let your hands hang loose.
  Isaiah 35:3-4 — encouragement and strengthened hands in anticipation of salvation: Zephaniah tells Zion to take courage and not let her hands loosen; Isaiah strengthens limp hands and urges courage because God comes to save. Both oppose physical slackness and fear with divine rescue.

Zephaniah 3:17
The Lord your God is within you; mighty, He shall save you. He shall bring gladness upon you, and renew you in His love; and He shall rejoice over you with delight, as on a day of feasting.”
  Isaiah 62:5 — the Lord's delight over His restored people: Zephaniah says the Lord will bring gladness, renew in love and rejoice with delight; Isaiah compares the Lord's rejoicing over His people to a bridegroom's joy over a bride. The connection concerns divine delight, without importing quietness or singing into Zephaniah.

Zephaniah 3:18
I will gather the crushed. Alas! Who took up reproach against her?
  Micah 4:6-7 — gathering the crushed into a restored remnant: Zephaniah promises to gather the crushed; Micah explicitly promises to gather the crushed one and make that one a remnant. The shared gathering addresses people in a condition of affliction.

Zephaniah 3:19
Look, I am acting within you for your sake at that time, says the Lord. I will save the oppressed and the rejected; I will receive them and make them a boast and renowned throughout the earth.
  Micah 4:6-7 — reception of rejected and afflicted people: Zephaniah promises to save the oppressed and receive the rejected; Micah promises to receive the expelled and rejected and make them a strong nation. Both reverse exclusion through God's reception.

Zephaniah 3:20
They will be ashamed at that time, when I do good to you and when I receive you. I will make you renowned and a boast among all the earth’s peoples, when I turn your captivity before you, says the Lord.
  Jeremiah 40:7-9 — restoration bringing public praise among the earth's peoples: Zephaniah promises returned captivity and renown among all peoples; Jeremiah promises to bring back Judah's and Israel's exiles and make the restoration a cause of praise and greatness before the earth's people.

Closed with no supportable cross-reference
Zephaniah 3:1: The verse addresses a "conspicuous and redeemed city, the dove-city"; the other passages denounce oppressive or false cities, or use "conspicuous" of fearsome people, feast days or public places, and none shares this address or the dove image.
