Ezra 1:1
In the first year of Cyrus king of the Persians, so that the Lord’s word through Jeremiah’s mouth might be fulfilled, the Lord stirred the spirit of Cyrus king of the Persians. He ordered a proclamation throughout his kingdom, also in writing, saying:
  2 Chronicles 36:22 — Parallel opening proclamation: Both passages date Cyrus's written proclamation to his first year and expressly connect the stirring of his spirit with fulfillment of the Lord's word through Jeremiah.
  Jeremiah 36:10 — Jeremiah's return promise invoked: Ezra explicitly invokes Jeremiah's fulfilled word; the prophet promises a return from Babylon when seventy years are fulfilled. The actual Greek reference is Jeremiah 36:10.

Ezra 1:2
“Thus says Cyrus king of the Persians: The Lord, God of heaven, has given me all the kingdoms of the earth, and He has charged me to build Him a house in Jerusalem in Judea.
  2 Chronicles 36:23 — Parallel royal commission: Cyrus's declaration that the God of heaven gave him the kingdoms and commanded a house in Jerusalem is also recorded in Chronicles' closing proclamation.

Ezra 1:3
Who among you belongs to any of His people? His God will be with him, and he will go up to Jerusalem in Judea. Let him build the house of the God of Israel—He is the God in Jerusalem.
  2 Chronicles 36:23 — Return invitation repeated: Both proclamations invite God's people to go up with God accompanying them; Ezra expressly continues with the rebuilding of the Jerusalem house.
  Isaiah 44:28 — Cyrus and rebuilding promised: The command to rebuild Jerusalem's temple under Cyrus corresponds to Isaiah's explicit naming of Cyrus alongside the promise that Jerusalem and the holy house will be rebuilt.

Ezra 1:4
As for everyone left behind, from whatever place he resides in, the men of his place will take him up with silver, gold, belongings and livestock, together with the freewill offering for the house of God in Jerusalem.”
  Ezra 1:6 — Support commanded and supplied; same-chapter: The instruction that neighbours support the returnees with precious metals, belongings and livestock is followed by those neighbours supplying those named categories and freewill gifts.

Ezra 1:5
The leaders of the families of Judah and Benjamin, the priests and the Levites arose, of all those whose spirit God had stirred to go up and build the Lord’s house in Jerusalem.
  Haggai 1:14 — Spirit stirred for temple work: The returnees arise because God stirs their spirits to build, resembling Haggai's later description of the spirits of Zerubbabel, Joshua and the people being stirred to resume work; the stages of the project differ.

Ezra 1:6
All those around them strengthened their hands with silver vessels, gold, belongings, livestock and gifts of hospitality, apart from the freewill offerings.
  Ezra 1:4 — Royal support instruction carried out; same-chapter: The neighbours' provision of silver, gold, belongings and livestock carries out the concrete support requested in Cyrus's proclamation.

Ezra 1:7
King Cyrus brought out the vessels of the Lord’s house that Nebuchadnezzar had taken from Jerusalem and placed in the house of his god.
  2 Kings 25:13-15 — Temple plunder reversed: The restoration of vessels removed by Nebuchadnezzar reverses the Babylonian removal of the sanctuary's utensils, including gold and silver bowls; the individual inventories are not made identical.
  Ezra 6:5 — Vessel restoration in the memorandum: The recovered royal memorandum expressly orders the return to Jerusalem of gold and silver vessels carried to Babylon, the reversal narrated under Cyrus here.

Ezra 1:8
Cyrus king of the Persians brought them out through Mithradates the gasbaren, who counted them out to Sasabasar, the ruler of Judah.
  Ezra 5:14 — Sasabasar receives the vessels: Both passages name Sasabasar as the recipient of vessels restored by Cyrus; the titles differ, ruler of Judah here and treasury keeper there, and neither is silently substituted for Mithradates.

Ezra 1:11
All the gold and silver vessels numbered five thousand four hundred; all went up with Sasabasar from the deportation, out of Babylon to Jerusalem.
  Ezra 5:14-16 — Returned vessels and rebuilding reported: The vessels' journey with Sasabasar from Babylon to Jerusalem is recalled in the elders' report of Cyrus entrusting him with the vessels and directing their return; the target does not repeat the 5400 total.

Ezra 3:2

  Exodus 29:38-42 — Mosaic altar offerings restored: Ezra expressly grounds the rebuilt altar's whole burnt offerings in Moses' law; Exodus prescribes continual altar offerings with a lamb morning and evening.

Ezra 3:3

  Exodus 29:38-39 — Morning and evening offerings: The restored morning and evening whole burnt offerings follow the specific daily schedule commanded for the altar in Exodus.

Ezra 3:4

  Leviticus 23:34-36 — Written Booths ordinance observed: The returned people keep Booths according to what is written; Leviticus specifies the seventh-month Feast of Tents, its seven days, offerings and eighth-day assembly.
  Numbers 29:12-19 — Daily festival offering numbers: Numbers gives the first and second days' prescribed numbers for the seventh-month festival, illustrating Ezra's statement that each day's offerings followed the required count.

Ezra 3:5

  Numbers 28:3-15 — Continual and new-moon offerings: Ezra's regular and new-moon offerings correspond to the law's continual daily sacrifices and its separately prescribed monthly offerings; the target does not exhaust every festival and freewill category named in Ezra.

Ezra 3:7

Ezra 3:8

  1 Chronicles 23:24-27 — Levites serving from twenty: Appointing Levites twenty years old and upward to temple work corresponds to the age threshold recorded in David's final arrangements for Levitical service.

Ezra 3:10

  2 Chronicles 5:12-13 — Temple musical ceremony compared: Priests with trumpets and Asaph-associated Levites with cymbals at the foundation resemble the musical combination at Solomon's temple ceremony, without equating foundation and dedication.

Ezra 3:11
