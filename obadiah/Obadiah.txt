Obadiah 1:1
The vision of Obadiah. Thus says the Lord God to Idumea: I heard a report from the Lord, and He sent a dispatch to the nations: “Rise up! Let us rise against her for battle!”
  Jeremiah 30:8 — parallel oracle against Idumea: Both oracles report a message from the Lord sent among the nations, calling them to rise for war against Idumea. The shared summons and named nation connect the two announcements.

Obadiah 1:2
Look, I have made you insignificant among the nations; you are greatly dishonored.
  Jeremiah 30:9 — parallel humiliation oracle: Obadiah announces that Idumea has been made insignificant and dishonored among the nations. Jeremiah likewise describes her as made small among nations and despised among people.

Obadiah 1:3
The pride of your heart has lifted you up, you who camp in holes in the rocks, raising his dwelling high and saying in his heart, “Who will bring me down to the ground?”
  Jeremiah 30:10 — parallel pride and height imagery: Both Idumea oracles connect the heart's pride or insolence with holes in rocks and an elevated position. Obadiah voices the inhabitant's boast as a question about who can bring him down. Jeremiah announces that the Lord will bring the addressee down despite the elevated eagle's nest.

Obadiah 1:4
If you soar like an eagle, and if you place your nest among the stars, from there I will bring you down, says the Lord.
  Jeremiah 30:10 — parallel eagle-nest oracle: Obadiah describes soaring like an eagle and placing a nest among the stars, then announces that the Lord will bring the addressee down from there. Jeremiah likewise joins the elevated eagle's nest with the Lord bringing the addressee down.

Obadiah 1:5
If thieves came in to you, or robbers by night, where would you have been cast away? Would they not have stolen enough for themselves? And if grape gatherers came in to you, would they not have left a gleaning?
  Jeremiah 30:3 — parallel plundering imagery: Both passages compare Idumea's loss with grape gatherers and thieves at night. Obadiah asks whether ordinary gatherers would leave a gleaning; Jeremiah announces gatherers who leave no remnants, sharpening the devastation described by the shared imagery.

Obadiah 1:6
How Esau has been searched out, and his hidden things seized!
  Jeremiah 30:4 — parallel exposure of Esau: Obadiah describes Esau searched out and his hidden things seized. Jeremiah likewise names Esau, then says their secrets are uncovered and they cannot hide, preserving that passage's change to plural pronouns.

Obadiah 1:7
All the men of your covenant sent you out as far as your borders; the men at peace with you opposed you and prevailed over you. They set ambushes beneath you. There is no understanding in them.
  Psalms 40:10 — betrayal by trusted allies: Obadiah's covenant partners and men at peace become opponents and set ambushes. The psalm similarly laments treachery from a trusted man of peace, presenting the reversal of an expected peaceful relationship.

Obadiah 1:8
On that day, says the Lord, I will destroy the wise from Idumea and understanding from Mount Esau.
  Jeremiah 30:1 — parallel loss of the wise and their counsel: Obadiah announces destruction of the wise out of Idumea and understanding out of Mount Esau. Jeremiah's Idumea oracle addresses the loss of wisdom in Teman and counsel among the discerning.

Obadiah 1:9
Your warriors from Teman will be terrified, so that a person may be removed from Mount Esau,
  Jeremiah 30:14-16 — parallel terror of Idumea's warriors: Obadiah describes Teman's warriors terrified so that a person may be removed from Mount Esau. Jeremiah joins judgment concerning Teman with Idumea's strong men's hearts becoming like the heart of a woman in labor.

Obadiah 1:10
because of the slaughter and the impiety toward your brother Jacob; and shame will cover you, and you will be removed forever.
  Deuteronomy 23:8 — kinship obligation violated: Obadiah condemns violence and impiety toward the brother Jacob. Deuteronomy explicitly forbids abhorring an Edomite because he is a brother, making the kinship obligation relevant to the violence condemned here.
  Ezekiel 25:12-14 — parallel judgment for violence against Judah: Obadiah gives slaughter and impiety toward Jacob as the reason for Esau's shame and removal. Ezekiel similarly grounds judgment on Idumea in its vengeance and grudge against Judah's house.
  Joel 4:19 — parallel judgment for bloodshed: Obadiah condemns the slaughter of Jacob by his brother. Joel specifically announces Idumea's desolation because of injustice toward Judah's sons and the shedding of righteous blood.

Obadiah 1:11
From the day you stood opposed, on the day foreigners took his strength captive and strangers entered his gates and cast lots over Jerusalem, you too were like one of them.
  Psalms 136:7 — Edom's participation in Jerusalem's calamity: Obadiah places Esau alongside foreigners who enter Jerusalem's gates and take its strength. The psalm remembers Edom's sons urging Jerusalem's destruction down to its foundations, likewise treating them as participants in her calamity.
  Joel 4:2-3 — parallel accusation of dispossession and lots: Obadiah describes foreigners taking strength captive and casting lots over Jerusalem. Joel presents the Lord judging the nations over scattered Israel, the division of His land and lots cast over His people.

Obadiah 1:12
Do not look on your brother’s day, on the day of strangers; do not rejoice over the sons of Judah on the day of their destruction; do not speak boastfully on the day of affliction.
  Proverbs 24:17-18 — shared prohibition of rejoicing at another's fall: Obadiah forbids rejoicing and boasting over Judah's destruction and affliction. Proverbs likewise forbids rejoicing when even an enemy falls, explaining that such delight displeases the Lord.

Obadiah 1:13
Do not enter the gates of peoples on the day of their suffering. You, too, must not look on their assembly on the day of their destruction, and do not join in attacking their strength on the day of their destruction.
  Ezekiel 35:10-13 — parallel condemnation of exploiting Israel's ruin: Obadiah forbids entering the afflicted people's gates and joining attacks on their strength. Ezekiel condemns Mount Seir for claiming the two countries and treating Israel's desolate mountains as food, describing another form of exploitation of Israel's calamity.

Obadiah 1:14
Do not stand at their escape routes to destroy those of them who are being saved, and do not shut in those fleeing from them on the day of affliction.
  Ezekiel 35:5 — parallel accusation of ambush: Obadiah forbids blocking escape routes to destroy or confine fugitives. Ezekiel accuses Mount Seir of treacherously ambushing Israel's house with the sword; both condemn violence directed at Israel in its distress.

Obadiah 1:15
For the day of the Lord is near against all the nations. Just as you acted, so it will be for you; your recompense will be repaid upon your own head.
  Joel 4:4-7 — parallel repayment upon the aggressor's head: Obadiah announces that a nation's own actions will be repaid upon its head. Joel uses that same repayment language when condemning the seizure of treasures and the sale of Judah's people.

Obadiah 1:16
For just as you drank on My holy mountain, all the nations will drink wine; they will drink and go down, and they will be as though they did not exist.
  Jeremiah 32:15-18 — shared wine-drinking imagery of judgment: Obadiah moves from drinking on the holy mountain to the nations drinking and going down. In the Jeremiah account, the nations receive the Lord's wine cup of judgment, with Jerusalem and Judah heading the listed recipients. Both connect the city's judgment with that of the nations; Jeremiah explicitly names the cup.

Obadiah 1:17
But on Mount Zion there will be salvation, and it will be holy; the house of Jacob will possess those who possessed them.
  Joel 3:5 — parallel salvation on Mount Zion: Obadiah locates salvation on Mount Zion. Joel likewise locates escape on Mount Zion and in Jerusalem within the promise that those calling on the Lord's name will be saved.
  Joel 4:17 — parallel holiness of restored Zion: Obadiah says Mount Zion will be holy as Jacob receives possession. Joel presents the Lord dwelling on His holy mountain and Jerusalem becoming a holy city, sharing the restoration's holiness setting.

Obadiah 1:18
The house of Jacob will be a fire, the house of Joseph a flame, and the house of Esau stubble. They will blaze against them and consume them, and there will be no bearer of fire in the house of Esau, because the Lord has spoken.
  Ezekiel 25:14 — Israel as the agent of judgment on Idumea: Obadiah pictures Jacob and Joseph as fire consuming Esau's stubble. Ezekiel states that the Lord will place His vengeance on Idumea by the hand of His people Israel; both place Israel in the role of executing that judgment.

Obadiah 1:19
Those in the Negeb will possess the mountain of Esau, and those in the Shephelah will possess the foreigners. They will possess the mountain of Ephraim, the plain of Samaria, Benjamin and the land of Gilead.
  Numbers 24:18-19 — parallel inheritance of Edom by Jacob: Obadiah includes possession of Esau's mountain in Israel's future holdings. Balaam's oracle likewise calls Edom and Esau an inheritance and speaks of one arising from Jacob.
  Ezekiel 35:10-12 — reversal of territorial claims: Obadiah promises possession of Esau's mountain and further territories. Ezekiel records Mount Seir's claim to possess the two countries and Israel's desolate mountains; the passages present opposing claims and a reversal of who will possess the land.

Obadiah 1:20
And this is the beginning of the deportation: for the sons of Israel, the land of the Canaanites as far as Zarephath; and the deportation of Jerusalem as far as Ephrathah. They will inherit the cities of the Negeb.
  Jeremiah 39:43-44 — parallel land restoration after deportation: Obadiah links Jerusalem's deportation with future inheritance of cities in the Negeb. Jeremiah promises renewed acquisition of fields, explicitly including the Negeb's cities, because the Lord will bring back the exiles. The connection concerns restored possession rather than identifying Obadiah's Ephrathah with another place.

Obadiah 1:21
And men who have been saved shall ascend from Mount Zion to execute judgment upon the mountain of Esau; and the kingdom shall be the Lord’s.
  Ezekiel 25:14 — parallel judgment on Esau through God's people: Obadiah describes saved men ascending from Zion to execute judgment on Esau's mountain. Ezekiel similarly says the Lord's vengeance on Idumea will be carried out by His people Israel.
  Psalms 21:28-29 — shared kingship of the Lord over the nations: Obadiah concludes that the kingdom will belong to the Lord after judgment on Esau. The psalm declares that kingship belongs to the Lord and that He rules the nations, setting the individual oracle within that wider sovereignty.
