2 Maccabees 1:2
May God do good to you and remember His covenant with Abraham, Isaac and Jacob, His faithful servants.
  Exodus 2:24 — Covenant recollection: The prayer that God remember His covenant with Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob repeats the three ancestors and divine remembering at the beginning of Israel's rescue from Egypt.

2 Maccabees 1:3
May He give you all a heart to revere Him and do His wishes with a generous heart and a willing soul.
  Deuteronomy 30:6-8 — Heart and obedience: The request for a heart willing to revere God and do His wishes resembles the promised divine cleansing of the heart followed by obedience to His commandments.

2 Maccabees 1:4
May He open your heart to His law and His ordinances, and make peace.
  Deuteronomy 30:6-10 — Heart and law: The prayer for an opened heart toward God's law corresponds to Deuteronomy's connection between divine heart renewal and keeping the written commandments.

2 Maccabees 1:5
May He hear your petitions, be reconciled to you, and not abandon you in an evil time.
  1 Kings 8:49-50 — Prayer for scattered Israel: The request that God hear and be reconciled to the recipients resembles Solomon's petition that God hear His displaced people from heaven and be merciful to their wrongs.

2 Maccabees 1:7
In the reign of Demetrius, in the one hundred sixty-ninth year, we Jews wrote to you in the affliction and the crisis that came upon us in those years, from the time Jason and those with him revolted from the holy land and the kingdom.
  2 Maccabees 4:7-13 — Crisis recalled: The letter traces the crisis to Jason and his associates; the narrative describes Jason's acquisition of the priesthood and his replacement of lawful civic life with Greek customs.

2 Maccabees 1:8
They burned the gateway and poured out innocent blood. We petitioned the Lord and were heard; we offered sacrifice and fine flour, lit the lamps, and set out the loaves.
  2 Maccabees 10:3 — Worship restored: The letter's sacrifices, lamps, and loaves correspond to the specific acts of restored temple worship narrated after the sanctuary's purification.
  1 Maccabees 4:50-51 — Restoration parallel: Both accounts specifically include relighting the lamps and returning the loaves to their proper place in the restored temple.

2 Maccabees 1:9
And now, see that you observe the days of the Festival of Booths in the month of Chislev. In the one hundred eighty-eighth year.
  2 Maccabees 10:5-8 — Festival invitation explained: The invitation to observe Booths in Chislev concerns the purification celebration described as eight days in the manner of Booths and decreed for annual observance.

2 Maccabees 1:18
As we are about to observe the purification of the temple on the twenty-fifth of Chislev, we thought it necessary to explain this to you, so that you yourselves may observe the festival of booths and of the fire, when Nehemiah, who rebuilt the temple and the altar, offered sacrifices.
  2 Maccabees 10:5-8 — Purification anniversary: The proposed observance on the twenty-fifth of Chislev matches the later narrative's purification date and annual festival decree; the Nehemiah fire tradition is additional material in the letter.

2 Maccabees 1:19
For when our fathers were being taken to Persia, the devout priests of that time secretly took some of the altar’s fire and hid it in the hollow of a well-like place without water. They secured it there so that the place was unknown to everyone.
  2 Maccabees 2:1 — Earlier account recalled: The following chapter expressly refers back to the preservation of altar fire at the deportation, adding Jeremiah's instruction to take it.

2 Maccabees 1:20
After many years had passed, when it pleased God, Nehemiah was sent by the king of Persia. He sent the descendants of the priests who had hidden it to fetch the fire. When they reported to us that they had found no fire but thick water, he ordered them to draw some and bring it.
  Nehemiah 2:5-8 — Royal commission compared: Nehemiah's being sent by the Persian king corresponds to his commission to return to Judah and rebuild; Nehemiah's own account does not narrate recovery of thick water or altar fire.

2 Maccabees 1:21
When the sacrificial materials had been brought up, Nehemiah ordered the priests to sprinkle the water on the wood and on what lay upon it.
  1 Kings 18:33-38 — Water and altar fire compared: Sprinkling water on the offering and wood before a remarkable fire resembles Elijah's drenched offering consumed by divine fire; the liquids and narrative explanations differ.

2 Maccabees 1:22
When this was done and some time had passed, the sun, previously hidden by clouds, shone out. A great fire was kindled, so that everyone marveled.
  1 Kings 18:38-39 — Altar fire compared: The unexpected ignition and the spectators' amazement resemble the fire consuming Elijah's wet offering and the people's recognition of God, without equating the two events.

2 Maccabees 1:26
accept the sacrifice on behalf of all Thy people Israel. Protect Thy portion and make it holy.
  1 Kings 8:51-53 — Chosen portion in prayer: Calling Israel God's portion and asking its sanctification resembles Solomon's prayer identifying Israel as God's people and inheritance set apart from the nations.

2 Maccabees 1:27

  Deuteronomy 30:3-5 — Gathering promise invoked: The request to gather the scattered people and release those among the nations corresponds to the law's promise of mercy, gathering, and return to the inherited land.
  Nehemiah 1:8-10 — Gathering prayer compared: The plea for the scattered people to be gathered resembles Nehemiah's prayer invoking Moses' promise that even the farthest dispersed will return to God's chosen place.

2 Maccabees 1:29
Plant Thy people in Thy holy place, as Moses said.”
  Exodus 15:17 — Explicit Mosaic recollection: The request to plant the people in the holy place expressly invokes Moses and corresponds to his song asking God to plant His people on the mountain of His inheritance.

2 Maccabees 1:33
When the matter became known, the king of the Persians was informed that water had appeared in the place where the deported priests had hidden the fire, and that Nehemiah and his companions had purified the sacrificial materials with it.
  2 Maccabees 1:19-22 — Report summarizes event; same-chapter: The report to the Persian king expressly recapitulates the hidden fire, recovered water, and purification described earlier in the letter.

2 Maccabees 2:1

2 Maccabees 2:2

  Deuteronomy 4:9-19 — Law against forgetfulness and images: Jeremiah's reported warning not to forget the ordinances or be led astray by images parallels Moses' linked warnings against forgetting what was taught and making or worshiping visible likenesses.

2 Maccabees 2:3
Saying these and other such things, he urged them not to let the law depart from their heart.
  Deuteronomy 4:9 — Law retained in the heart: The instruction not to let the law depart from the heart resembles Moses' warning that the remembered revelation must not depart from the heart throughout life.

2 Maccabees 2:4
It was also in the writing that, after receiving an oracle, the prophet ordered the tent and the ark to accompany him, as he went out to the mountain where Moses had gone up and viewed God’s inheritance.
  Deuteronomy 34:1-4 — Explicit geographical recollection: The mountain where Moses viewed the inheritance is the setting of Moses' ascent and the Lord's display of the promised land; Deuteronomy does not recount Jeremiah's visit.

2 Maccabees 2:7
When Jeremiah learned this, he rebuked them and said, “The place will remain unknown until God gathers His people together and becomes merciful.
  Deuteronomy 30:3-5 — Gathering and mercy: Jeremiah's reported expectation that God gather His people and become merciful uses the combination of mercy and gathering promised in the law.

2 Maccabees 2:8
Then the Lord will reveal these things, and the Lord’s glory and the cloud will appear, as they were shown in the time of Moses, and as Solomon also prayed that the place might be greatly sanctified.”
  Exodus 40:34-35 — Explicit glory-cloud recollection: The expected glory and cloud expressly recall Moses' time, when the cloud covered the tent and God's glory filled it.
  2 Chronicles 5:13-14 — Explicit temple-cloud recollection: The reference to Solomon's sanctuary is illuminated by the dedication account in which a cloud of the Lord's glory fills the temple and prevents priestly service.

2 Maccabees 2:9
It was also explained how, being wise, Solomon offered a sacrifice for the dedication and completion of the temple.
  2 Chronicles 7:4-5 — Explicit dedication recollection: Solomon's dedication sacrifices are narrated with the offerings of the king and people and the dedication of God's house.

2 Maccabees 2:10
Just as Moses prayed to the Lord, and fire came down from heaven and consumed the sacrificial materials, so Solomon also prayed, and the fire came down and consumed the whole burnt offerings.
  Leviticus 9:23-24 — Explicit sacrificial-fire recollection: The recalled fire in Moses' time corresponds to Moses and Aaron's appearance before the people and the fire from the Lord consuming the altar offerings; Leviticus does not quote the prayer mentioned here.
  2 Chronicles 7:1 — Explicit sacrificial-fire recollection: Solomon finishes praying and fire descends from heaven to consume the offerings, the precise sequence recalled here.

2 Maccabees 2:11
Moses said, “Because the sin offering was not eaten, it was consumed.”
  Leviticus 10:16-18 — Mosaic episode paraphrased: The statement about the uneaten sin offering recalls Moses' discovery that the goat had been burned and his question why the priests had not eaten it; it is a compressed recollection of that exchange.

2 Maccabees 2:12
In the same way Solomon also observed the eight days.
  2 Chronicles 7:8-10 — Dedication duration compared: The recollection of Solomon's eight days corresponds to the seven-day feast with its eighth-day closing celebration; the target preserves the separate day and dismissal notices.

2 Maccabees 2:14
In the same way Judas also gathered all the writings that had been scattered because of the war that befell us, and they are with us.
  1 Maccabees 1:56-57 — Preservation after persecution: Judas's recovery of scattered writings answers the documented crisis in which books of the law were destroyed and possession of the covenant book was punished; scattering and burning are not identical losses.

2 Maccabees 2:16
As we are about to observe the purification, we have written to you. You will do well, then, to observe the days.
  2 Maccabees 1:18 — Festival invitation repeated: The renewed invitation to observe purification repeats the letter's earlier announcement of the twenty-fifth-of-Chislev observance.

2 Maccabees 2:17
Now God, who saved all His people and restored to all the inheritance, the kingship, the priesthood and the sanctification,
  Deuteronomy 30:3-5 — Restored inheritance: The statement that God restored the people's inheritance corresponds to the law's promise to gather them and return them to the land inherited by their fathers; this target does not separately specify restored kingship or priesthood.

2 Maccabees 2:18
as He promised through the law—for we hope in God that He will soon have mercy on us and gather us from under heaven into the holy place, for He rescued us from great evils and purified the place.
  Deuteronomy 30:3-5 — Explicit legal promise: The hope of gathering from under heaven to the holy place expressly appeals to the law and closely follows its promise to gather even those dispersed to heaven's farthest end.

2 Maccabees 2:19
The events concerning Judas Maccabeus and his brothers, the purification of the greatest temple and the dedication of the altar,
  2 Maccabees 10:1-3 — Narrative program fulfilled: The announced topic of Judas's purification of the temple and dedication of the altar is carried out in the subsequent account of recovery, cleansing, and a new altar.

2 Maccabees 2:20
and also the wars against Antiochus Epiphanes and his son Eupator,
  2 Maccabees 10:9-11 — Narrative program organized: The introductory naming of Epiphanes and Eupator corresponds to the later formal transition from Epiphanes' death to the wars under his son Eupator.

2 Maccabees 2:21
and the manifestations from heaven to those who zealously performed brave deeds for Judaism, so that, although few, they plundered the whole land and pursued the barbarian multitudes,
  2 Maccabees 10:29-30 — Manifestations anticipated: The introduction's heavenly manifestations are exemplified by the five mounted figures protecting Maccabeus and striking the enemy during battle.

2 Maccabees 2:22
recovered the temple renowned throughout the inhabited world, freed the city, and restored the laws that were about to be abolished, the Lord having become merciful to them with all gentleness—
  2 Maccabees 11:24-25 — Restored laws documented: The introductory claim that the ancestral laws were restored is supported by Eupator's later letter permitting ancestral customs and restoring the temple to the Jews.

2 Maccabees 2:25
we have taken thought for the pleasure of those who wish to read, for the ease of those eager to commit it to memory, and for the benefit of everyone who encounters it.
  2 Maccabees 15:38-39 — Authorial purpose and conclusion: The author's intention to benefit and delight readers is revisited in the closing assessment of the composition and its comparison of pleasing prose to mixed wine.

2 Maccabees 2:31
But the person making a recasting should be permitted to pursue concise expression and set aside an exhaustive treatment of the subject.
  2 Maccabees 15:38 — Abridger's standard recalled: The stated pursuit of concise expression corresponds to the closing wish that the work be well composed and to the point.

  2 Maccabees 4:1-6 — Peace disrupted: The initial peace under Onias is broken by Simon's continuing accusations and violent rivalry, which lead Onias to seek royal intervention for public peace.

2 Maccabees 3:27
He suddenly fell to the ground, and deep darkness enveloped him. They picked him up and placed him on a litter.

2 Maccabees 3:28
