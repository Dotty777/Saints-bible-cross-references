  1 Chronicles 29:16 — Same-chapter interpretation of the offering's origin: The confession that their gifts already belong to God is restated of the actual building abundance: it comes from His hand and everything is His, grounding the prayer in the materials prepared for the house.
  Deuteronomy 10:14 — Human giving grounded in divine ownership: David explains that the people's offering comes from what is already God's; Deuteronomy's ownership statement extends over heaven, earth, and everything within them, grounding that claim beyond the particular temple collection.

1 Chronicles 29:15
For we are resident strangers before Thee, and sojourners, as all our fathers were: our days upon earth are as a shadow, and there is no endurance.
  Leviticus 25:23 — People as sojourners before the divine owner: David calls the people resident strangers and sojourners before God; Leviticus grounds the people's status as resident foreigners and lodgers before Him in His ownership of the land. Chronicles adds the image of fleeting shadow-like days.

1 Chronicles 29:16
Lord our God, all this abundance that I have prepared for a house to be built for Thy holy name is from Thy hand, and everything is Thine.
  1 Chronicles 29:2 — Same-chapter prayer referring to prepared materials: All this abundance refers to David's earlier enumeration of metals, timber, stones, and marble for God's house; the prayer attributes those concrete preparations to God's hand.

1 Chronicles 29:17
I know, Lord, that Thou examinest hearts and lovest righteousness. In simplicity of heart I eagerly offered all these things. Now I have seen Thy people found here offer eagerly to Thee in gladness.
  1 Samuel 16:7 — God's scrutiny of the heart: David's confidence that God examines hearts grounds his claim of sincere giving; the earlier word to Samuel expressly distinguishes God's regard for the heart from human attention to outward appearance.
  1 Chronicles 28:9 — Examining hearts in charge and prayer: David's prayer that God examines hearts recalls his explicit warning to Solomon that the Lord examines every heart and thought; the prayer applies that conviction to sincere contributions.

1 Chronicles 29:18
Lord, God of Abraham, Isaac, and Israel our fathers, preserve these things for ever in the thought of Thy people’s heart, and direct their hearts toward Thee.
  1 Chronicles 28:9 — Prayer for the hearts required by the charge: David asks that the people's thoughts and hearts remain directed toward God, carrying into prayer the preceding charge to serve God with a whole heart and willing soul under His examination.

1 Chronicles 29:19
Give Solomon my son a good heart to do Thy commandments, testimonies, and ordinances, and to bring the construction of Thy house to completion.”
  1 Chronicles 28:6-7 — Prayer for obedience and completion of Solomon's commission: David asks for Solomon's good heart to keep the commandments and finish the house, joining the building commission and obedience condition stated together in his preceding public address.
  1 Kings 3:9-12 — Solomon's heart sought from God: David prays for Solomon a good heart to obey and finish the house; Solomon later asks God for a hearing heart to judge rightly, and God grants a discerning, wise heart. The requested tasks remain distinct.

1 Chronicles 29:20
David said to the whole assembly, “Bless the Lord your God.” The whole assembly blessed the Lord, the God of their fathers. Bending their knees, they prostrated themselves before the Lord and the king.
  1 Chronicles 29:10 — Same-chapter leader's blessing becomes the assembly's response: David first blessed the Lord before the assembly; after his prayer he commands the whole assembly to bless, and their kneeling and prostration accompany that corporate response.

1 Chronicles 29:21
On the day after the first day David sacrificed sacrifices to the Lord and offered whole burnt offerings to God: a thousand calves, a thousand rams, and a thousand lambs, with their libations, and sacrifices in abundance for all Israel.
  1 Chronicles 29:22 — Same-chapter sacrifices and joyful royal celebration: The abundant sacrifices and thousand each of calves, rams, and lambs precede the people's eating and drinking before the Lord with joy and Solomon's second royal appointment; the linked sequence explains the offerings' assembly setting.

1 Chronicles 29:22
They ate and drank before the Lord that day with joy. They made Solomon son of David king a second time and anointed him for the Lord as king, and Sadok for priesthood.
  1 Kings 1:38-40 — Solomon's anointing compared across accession accounts: Both narratives recount Solomon's anointing and popular joy; Chronicles expressly calls its royal appointment a second time and includes Sadok's priesthood, while Kings narrates Zadok anointing Solomon at Gihon. The occasions are not collapsed.
  1 Chronicles 23:1 — Second royal appointment and earlier accession notice: The earlier narrative introduces Solomon's appointment by David; this source adds a second time and anointing details. The link compares the accession notices without deciding whether the earlier notice summarizes a ceremony later narrated.

1 Chronicles 29:23
Solomon sat upon the throne of David his father and was accepted with favor. All Israel obeyed him.
  1 Kings 2:12 — Solomon succeeding to David's throne: Both accounts place Solomon on his father David's throne and describe favorable establishment; Kings additionally states his age, which Chronicles does not repeat.

1 Chronicles 29:24
The rulers, the powerful men, and all the sons of King David his father submitted to him.
  1 Chronicles 29:23 — Same-chapter scope of obedience to Solomon: All Israel's obedience in the preceding verse is specified by the submission of rulers, powerful men, and all David's sons, showing that the royal household also acknowledged Solomon.

1 Chronicles 29:25
The Lord made Solomon exceedingly great before all Israel and gave him a king’s glory such as had not belonged to any king before him.
  1 Kings 3:13 — Solomon's unequaled royal glory as divine gift: Chronicles attributes Solomon's unprecedented kingly glory to the Lord; Kings records God's gift of wealth and glory so that no man like him had been among the kings.

1 Chronicles 29:26
David son of Jessai reigned over Israel
  1 Chronicles 29:27 — Same-chapter divided regnal summary: David son of Jessai reigned over Israel is completed by the following forty-year total and its seven years in Chebron and thirty-three in Jerusalem.

1 Chronicles 29:27
for forty years: in Chebron seven years, and in Jerusalem thirty-three years.
  1 Kings 2:11 — Parallel regnal totals: Both accounts give David forty years over Israel, divided into seven in Chebron or Hebron and thirty-three in Jerusalem; the source's place spelling is preserved.
  2 Samuel 5:4-5 — Parallel reign summary with preserved six-month detail: Both passages give forty total years and thirty-three in Jerusalem; Chronicles gives seven in Chebron, while Samuel specifies seven years and six months in Hebron over Judah. The difference is preserved rather than numerically reconciled.

1 Chronicles 29:28
He died in a good old age, full of days, wealth, and glory. Solomon his son reigned in his place.
  1 Kings 2:10-12 — David's death and Solomon's succession: Both narratives join David's death to Solomon taking his father's throne; Chronicles emphasizes David's good old age, wealth, and glory, while Kings adds burial in the City of David.

1 Chronicles 29:29
The remaining deeds of King David, the earlier and the later, are written in the records of Samuel the seer, the records of Nathan the prophet, and the records of Gad the seer,
  1 Chronicles 29:30 — Same-chapter scope of the named prophetic records: The records attributed to Samuel, Nathan, and Gad are said to contain David's earlier and later deeds; the next unit specifies his kingdom, dominion, and the times affecting Israel and other kingdoms.

1 Chronicles 29:30
concerning all his kingdom and dominion, and the times that came upon him, upon Israel, and upon all the kingdoms of the earth.
  1 Chronicles 29:29 — Same-chapter grammatical object of the records notice: The phrase concerning all his kingdom and dominion continues the notice of David's deeds in the records of Samuel, Nathan, and Gad, supplying the scope of those records without identifying them with surviving books.
