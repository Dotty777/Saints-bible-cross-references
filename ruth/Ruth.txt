  Deuteronomy 25:5-6 — Marriage and preserving the dead man's name with different kinship scopes: Boaz links acquiring the field and Ruth herself to raising the dead man's name on his inheritance; Deuteronomy addresses a deceased brother's widow and a child's established name, with its brothers-dwelling-together condition kept distinct.

Ruth 4:6
The near kinsman said, “I shall not be able to act as next of kin for myself, lest I destroy my inheritance. Exercise my right as next of kin for yourself, for I shall not be able to do it.”
  Ruth 4:4-5 — Same-chapter refusal follows initial acceptance and the added Ruth condition: The nearer man first says he will act; after Boaz explains acquisition of Ruth and the name-preservation purpose, he declines for fear of harming his inheritance and transfers his right, without inventing the precise financial mechanism.

Ruth 4:7
This was formerly the ordinance in Israel concerning the right of next of kin and exchange, to establish every matter: a man removed his sandal and gave it to his neighbour who was exercising his kinship right. This was testimony in Israel.
  Deuteronomy 25:9-10 — Sandal customs compared with distinct actors and consequences: Ruth explains sandal transfer to establish kinship exchange; Deuteronomy has the widow remove a refusing brother's sandal with spitting and a naming consequence, preserving the different custom, actors and stated assessment rather than adding those sanctions to Ruth.

Ruth 4:8
The near kinsman said to Boaz, “Acquire my kinship right for yourself.” He removed his sandal and gave it to him.
  Ruth 4:7 — Same-chapter the transferred sandal enacts the explained exchange custom: The nearer kinsman transfers his right, removes his sandal and gives it to Boaz; the preceding explanation defines that sandal-giving as testimony establishing a kinship-right exchange.

Ruth 4:9

  Ruth 4:3-4 — Same-chapter witnessed acquisition follows the proposed family-field transaction: Boaz declares acquisition from Naomi of the named family holdings before the assembled witnesses; the preceding proposal introduced Abimelech's field portion and public acquisition before elders, without adding an unstated purchase price.

Ruth 4:10

  Ruth 1:4-5 — Ruth's named deceased husband identified within the earlier marriage losses: Boaz explicitly calls Ruth the Moabite Mahlon's wife; the earlier account introduces Ruth among the Moabite wives and records Mahlon's death, supporting that particular marital identity without naming Orpah's husband by inference.
  Deuteronomy 25:5-6 — Preserving the dead man's name in related widow-marriage provisions: Boaz says acquiring Mahlon's widow as wife prevents the dead man's name from destruction among his brothers and people; Deuteronomy similarly preserves the dead brother's name through the child, while retaining its narrower stated sibling circumstances.

Ruth 4:11

  Genesis 35:23-26 — Rachel and Leah invoked as builders of Israel's house: The gate blessing explicitly invokes Rachel and Leah as building Israel's house; Genesis names sons associated with each and also the maidservants' sons, supplying the family background without assigning every son as biologically born to the two named wives.

Ruth 4:12

  Genesis 38:27-30 — The Perez household blessing recalls his birth to Tamar: The blessing invokes Perez whom Tamar bore to Judah; Genesis's immediate Tamar-and-Judah context leads into the twin birth, Perez's naming and Zerah's birth, preserving that particular ancestral story rather than equating Ruth's circumstances with Tamar's.

Ruth 4:13

  Ruth 4:12 — Same-chapter requested seed and the ensuing conception and son: The elders ask the Lord to give seed through the young woman; the next unit explicitly says the Lord gave Ruth conception and she bore a son, without diagnosing an earlier unreported infertility condition.

Ruth 4:14

  Ruth 4:15 — Same-chapter the women's kinship blessing continued through the child: The women's blessing that Naomi has not been left without a near kinsman continues with life-restoration, old-age nourishment and the one her loving daughter-in-law bore; the actual may-he-call-your-name wording remains unchanged.

Ruth 4:15

  Ruth 1:5 — Household loss contrasted with the women's promise of nourishment: The women speak of Ruth's child restoring Naomi's life and nourishing her age and praise Ruth above seven sons; the earlier account leaves Naomi bereft of husband and sons, preserving the consolation as their particular speech.

  Ruth 4:13 — Same-chapter Naomi's nursing role follows Ruth's actual birth: Naomi receives the child in her bosom and becomes his nurse; the preceding birth account explicitly identifies Ruth as the one bearing the son, distinguishing maternity from the recorded caregiving role.

  Ruth 4:21-22 — Same-chapter Obed's naming and the repeated Jesse-David lineage: The neighbours name Obed, and the narrator identifies him as Jesse's father and David's grandfather; the closing genealogy restates Boaz, Obed, Jesse and David in its explicit father-son sequence.
