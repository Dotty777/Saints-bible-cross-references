1 Samuel 1:1
There was a man from Armathaim Sipha in the hill country of Ephraim. His name was Elkanah, son of Jerahmeel, son of Elihu, son of Thoke, in Nasib Ephraim.
  1 Samuel 1:19 — Same-chapter Elkanah's introduction and return to his named home: Elkanah is introduced from Armathaim Sipha in Ephraim's hill country; the family's return later names Elkanah's house at Armathaim, connecting the journey with its introduced household.

1 Samuel 1:2
He had two wives. One was named Hannah, and the second was named Peninnah. Peninnah had children, but Hannah had no child.
  1 Samuel 1:5-6 — Same-chapter childlessness developed in Hannah's affliction: The household introduction distinguishes Peninnah's children and Hannah's childlessness; the following account describes Hannah's portion, Elkanah's love and her distress because the Lord had closed her womb.

1 Samuel 1:3
The man went up year by year from his city, from Armathaim, to bow down and sacrifice to the Lord God Sabaoth at Shiloh. Eli and his two sons, Hophni and Phinehas, were there as priests of the Lord.
  Joshua 18:1 — Shiloh's worship setting and its earlier tabernacle assembly: Elkanah's annual worship is at Shiloh, where Eli's family serves as priests; Joshua records the congregation assembling and pitching the Tabernacle of Testimony there.

1 Samuel 1:4
When the day came and Elkanah sacrificed, he gave portions to Peninnah his wife and to her sons and daughters.
  1 Samuel 1:5 — Same-chapter sacrificial portions distributed to the two wives: Peninnah and her children receive portions when Elkanah sacrifices; Hannah receives one portion in the next notice, which also states her childlessness and Elkanah's greater love for her.

1 Samuel 1:5
To Hannah he gave one portion, because she had no child. Yet Elkanah loved Hannah more than this other woman, and the Lord had closed her womb.
  1 Samuel 1:2 — Same-chapter Hannah's portion belongs to the stated childless-wife setting: Hannah's one portion and closed womb develop the earlier distinction that Peninnah had children and Hannah had none, preserving the source's stated single portion.

1 Samuel 1:6
For the Lord had not given her a child, in her affliction and in the despondency of her affliction. She was despondent because of this: the Lord had shut her womb so as not to give her a child.
  1 Samuel 1:7 — Same-chapter childlessness and its recurring emotional effects: Hannah's affliction and despondency concern the Lord's not giving her a child; the annual visits then describe her despondency, weeping and not eating without naming a rival's provocation.

1 Samuel 1:7
He would do this year after year when she went up to the Lord’s house, and she would be despondent and weep and not eat.
  1 Samuel 1:8 — Same-chapter weeping and not eating prompt Elkanah's questions: The annual account describes Hannah weeping and not eating; Elkanah then asks why she weeps, does not eat and suffers in heart, responding to those stated signs.

1 Samuel 1:8

  1 Samuel 1:7 — Same-chapter husband's questions address the preceding distress: Elkanah's questions about Hannah's tears and lack of food directly address the preceding description of her weeping and not eating during the yearly visits.

1 Samuel 1:9

1 Samuel 1:10

1 Samuel 1:11
