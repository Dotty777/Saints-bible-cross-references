9:1|Amos 2:14-15|same-book warning that flight and strength cannot save|Amos 9 says no fleeing person will escape and no one seeking safety will be saved. Chapter 2 likewise denies safety to the runner, fighter, archer and horseman. The later vision renews the book's warning that attempted escape and military ability will fail.
9:2|Psalms 138:7-10|heaven and Hades cannot place a person beyond God's reach|Amos names Hades and heaven as extremes from which the Lord will still retrieve fugitives. The psalm likewise asks where one can flee God's presence, then names heaven and Hades and describes His hand's reach. Amos applies the shared spatial contrast to judgment.
9:3|Jeremiah 23:24|hidden places cannot conceal people from the Lord|Amos describes the Lord searching out people hidden on Carmel and reaching those in the sea's depths. Jeremiah likewise asks whether a person hiding in hidden places can remain unseen by the God who fills heaven and earth.
9:4|Jeremiah 21:10|parallel expression of divine attention for evils rather than good|Amos announces eyes fixed upon the people for evils and not for good things. Jeremiah uses the corresponding expression of the Lord's face fixed upon the city for evils and not for good things. The connection is that stated direction of judgment, while the surrounding accounts treat captivity differently.
9:5|Amos 8:8|same-book recurrence of earth's disturbance and Egypt's river|Both passages join disturbance of the earth with its inhabitants mourning and a consummation rising like a river and descending like Egypt's river. Amos 9 explicitly introduces the Lord's touch as the earth is shaken.
9:6|Amos 5:8|same-book repeated description of the Lord's power over waters|Both passages describe the Lord calling for the sea's water and pouring it upon the earth's surface, followed by the declaration that the Lord God Almighty is His name.
9:7|Exodus 20:2|explicit recollection of Israel's departure from Egypt|Amos includes bringing Israel up from Egypt among the Lord's recalled acts. Exodus identifies the Lord as the One who brought the people out of Egypt and the house of bondage.
9:7|Deuteronomy 2:23|parallel recollection of migration from Cappadocia|Amos recalls the foreigners being brought from Cappadocia. Deuteronomy describes Cappadocians coming from Cappadocia and settling in the place of the Hivites as far as Gaza. The connection is the stated movement from Cappadocia, without substituting another geographical name.
9:8|Jeremiah 5:17-18|announced devastation qualified by preservation from a complete end|Amos announces removal of the kingdom of sinners but explicitly limits it by saying Jacob's house will not be completely removed. Jeremiah likewise announces an invading nation's destruction of produce and fortified cities, then promises not to bring the people to a complete end.
9:9|Luke 22:31|winnowing and sifting applied to people|Amos pictures Israel's house among the nations as being winnowed in a basket. Luke describes Satan's demand to sift Simon and those addressed with him like wheat. Both apply agricultural separation imagery to people, with different agents and settings.
9:10|Micah 3:11-12|judgment against sinful people who deny approaching calamity|Amos condemns sinners who insist that evils will not come upon them. Micah likewise presents corrupt leaders claiming that calamities will not come, then announces Zion's devastation because of them.
9:11|Acts 15:15-16|explicit later use of David's fallen-tent promise|Amos promises to raise and rebuild David's fallen tent and demolished parts. Acts introduces a prophetic quotation about rebuilding David's fallen tent and ruins, directly corresponding to that promise.
9:12|Acts 15:17-18|explicit later use of the nations-seeking promise|Amos speaks of remaining human beings and nations over whom God's name is called seeking. Acts' prophetic quotation likewise names the rest of humanity seeking the Lord and nations upon whom His name has been called. Amos's remaining-human-beings wording is preserved.
9:13|Joel 4:18|shared promise of mountains dripping sweetness|Amos pictures renewed agricultural abundance with mountains dripping sweetness. Joel uses the same mountains-dripping-sweetness image alongside hills flowing with milk and abundant water.
9:14|Ezekiel 36:33-36|parallel restoration of cities and cultivated land|Amos promises ruined cities rebuilt and inhabited, with vineyards and gardens yielding food and drink. Ezekiel likewise promises inhabited rebuilt cities and cultivated devastated land, comparing it with a garden of delight.
9:15|Jeremiah 39:40-41|lasting settlement pictured as the Lord planting His people|Amos promises that the Lord will plant the people in their given land without future uprooting. Jeremiah announces an eternal covenant and promises to plant the people in the land in faithfulness, likewise depicting their settlement as the Lord's planting.
