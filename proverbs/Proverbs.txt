Proverbs 1:1
The proverbs of Solomon son of David, who reigned in Israel,
  1 Kings 2:12 — historical identity: Proverbs identifies Solomon as David's son and Israel's king; Kings records Solomon taking the throne of his father David, supplying the royal family setting of this attribution.

Proverbs 1:2
for knowing wisdom and discipline and understanding words of good sense,
  Proverbs 23:12 — wisdom instruction parallel: The opening purpose is to know wisdom and discipline and understand sensible words; the later proverb directs the heart to discipline and the ears to words of discernment.

Proverbs 1:3
for receiving turns of speech, understanding true righteousness, and directing judgment rightly,
  Proverbs 2:9 — literary purpose and outcome: The book promises instruction in righteousness and right judgment; chapter 2 describes understanding righteousness and judgment as the outcome of receiving wisdom.
  1 Kings 3:9-12 — narrative illustration: The stated aim of understanding righteousness and directing judgment has a concrete counterpart in Solomon's request to judge the people in righteousness and God's gift of a discerning, wise heart.

Proverbs 1:4
so that he may give shrewdness to the innocent, perception and thought to a young child.
  Psalms 118:130 — instructional parallel: Proverbs aims to give shrewdness to the innocent and perception and thought to a young child; the psalm says the disclosure of God's words gives light and understanding to the simple. Both link instruction with perception or understanding while preserving the different descriptions of its recipients.

Proverbs 1:5
For a wise person who hears these things will become wiser, and the discerning will acquire guidance,
  Proverbs 9:9 — literary parallel: Both sayings describe growth in an already wise hearer: the wise listener becomes wiser here, and the wise person given a starting point receives more wisdom in chapter 9.

Proverbs 1:6
and understand a comparison and an obscure saying, the utterances of the wise and riddles.
  Sirach 39:2-3 — wisdom literature parallel: Understanding comparisons, obscure sayings and riddles is paired with the sage's study of parables, hidden meanings of proverbs and their riddles in Sirach.

Proverbs 1:7
The fear of God is the beginning of wisdom, and good understanding belongs to all who practice it. Reverence toward God is the beginning of perception, but the ungodly will despise wisdom and discipline.
  Psalms 110:10 — verbal parallel: Both passages call fear of the Lord the beginning of wisdom and associate good understanding with practicing it; the shared formulation links reverence with enacted understanding.
  Proverbs 9:10 — literary repetition: The book later repeats the principle that fear of the Lord begins wisdom, connecting the opening statement with the counsel of holy ones in chapter 9.

Proverbs 1:8
Listen, son, to your father’s discipline, and do not reject your mother’s ordinances,
  Proverbs 6:20 — literary repetition: The paired commands to heed a father's instruction and not reject a mother's ordinances recur in chapter 6, preserving the distinctive instruction from both parents.

Proverbs 1:9
for you will receive a crown of graces for your head and a golden collar around your neck.
  Proverbs 4:9 — adornment imagery: Both passages picture received wisdom as a crown of graces placed on the learner's head; the promised collar here and crown of delight there expand the image of honored instruction.

Proverbs 1:10
Son, do not let ungodly men lead you astray; do not agree if they invite you, saying,
  Proverbs 4:14 — ethical parallel: The warning not to consent when ungodly men invite the son is reinforced by the later command not to enter ungodly ways or imitate lawless conduct.

Proverbs 1:11
Come with us; share in bloodshed. Let us hide a righteous man in the earth unjustly;
  Wisdom of Solomon 2:12-20 — plot against the righteous: Both speeches put a collective plan against a righteous person into the plotters' mouths: Proverbs proposes unjust bloodshed, while Wisdom develops an ambush ending in torture and a shameful death sentence.

Proverbs 1:12
let us swallow him alive like Hades and remove his memory from the earth.
  Numbers 16:30-33 — death imagery: The robbers boast of swallowing someone alive like Hades; Numbers describes the earth swallowing the rebels and their descent alive to Hades. The shared image connects a murderous boast with the account of divine judgment.

Proverbs 1:13
Let us seize his costly property and fill our houses with spoils.
  Micah 2:2 — predatory acquisition: The plan to seize costly property and fill houses with spoils parallels Micah's condemnation of coveting fields and plundering a man, his house and his inheritance.

Proverbs 1:14
Cast your lot among us; let us all obtain a common purse, and let there be one moneybag for us.
  Psalms 49:18 — criminal association: The invitation to cast one's lot with plunderers and share their purse is paired with the psalm's condemnation of running with a thief and placing one's portion among wrongdoers.

Proverbs 1:15
Do not walk in the way with them; turn your foot away from their paths,
  Psalms 1:1 — way and association: Turning one's foot away from the criminals' paths corresponds to the blessed person's refusal to walk in ungodly counsel or stand in the way of sinners.

Proverbs 1:16
for their feet run toward evil, and they are quick to shed blood.
  Isaiah 59:7 — verbal parallel: Both passages describe feet running toward evil and being quick to shed blood, making the same bodily image a description of readiness for violence.
  Romans 3:15 — verbal parallel: Romans includes feet swift to pour out blood in its scriptural description of sinfulness, closely matching Proverbs' feet quick to shed blood.

Proverbs 1:18
For those who share in murder store up evils for themselves, and the overthrow of lawless men is evil.
  Psalms 7:16-17 — reversal of wrongdoing: The murderers store up evils for themselves; the psalm depicts the wrongdoer falling into his own pit and his injustice returning upon his head. Both locate the harm's return upon its planner.

Proverbs 1:19
These are the ways of all who carry out lawless deeds, for through ungodliness they take away their own life.
  Proverbs 15:27 — self-destructive gain: The warning that lawless acquisition costs its practitioners their own life is paired with the later saying that taking bribes destroys the taker.

Proverbs 1:20
Wisdom is celebrated at the exits, and in the squares she speaks openly.
  Proverbs 8:2-4 — personified wisdom's public call: Wisdom speaks openly in the squares here; chapter 8 places her at paths and rulers' gates and has her send her voice to humankind. Both present wisdom as publicly accessible instruction.

Proverbs 1:21
At the tops of walls she is proclaimed; at rulers’ gates she sits nearby, and at city gates she speaks boldly:
  Proverbs 8:3 — shared literary setting: Wisdom's position at rulers' and city gates recurs in chapter 8, where she sits beside rulers' gates and is praised at the entrances.

Proverbs 1:22
As long as the innocent hold to righteousness, they will not be ashamed. But the senseless, desiring insolence, became ungodly and hated perception,
  Proverbs 1:29 — same-chapter literary parallel: The speech's accusation that the senseless hated perception is resumed by the later explanation that they hated wisdom and refused fear of the Lord.

Proverbs 1:23
and became liable to reproofs. Behold, I will send forth to you an utterance of my breath, and I will teach you my word.
  Proverbs 8:6-9 — personified wisdom's teaching: Wisdom promises to send forth an utterance and teach her word; her later speech invites listeners to hear the upright, truthful and righteous words proceeding from her lips.

Proverbs 1:24
Since I kept calling and you did not obey, and kept extending words and you paid no attention,
  Isaiah 65:12 — rejected calls for obedience: Wisdom describes repeatedly calling without obedience; Isaiah's judgment likewise answers the failure to obey when the Lord called and the disregard shown when He spoke.

Proverbs 1:25
but you were making my counsels ineffective and disobeyed my reproofs,
  Psalms 49:17 — rejected instruction: Ignoring wisdom's counsel and reproof corresponds to the sinner's hatred of instruction and casting God's words behind him in the psalm's rebuke.

Proverbs 1:26
therefore I too will laugh at your ruin and exult when destruction comes to you,
  Psalms 2:4-5 — judgment imagery: Wisdom's laughter at the rejecters' ruin parallels the heavenly Lord's laughter and scorn before speaking in anger to rebellious rulers. Both use derision to announce judgment upon resistance.

Proverbs 1:27
and when sudden tumult reaches you and overthrow arrives like a storm, when affliction and siege come upon you, or when destruction comes to you.
  Proverbs 10:25 — storm and ruin: The sudden overthrow arriving like a storm is paired with the later proverb in which the passing storm removes the impious while the righteous is saved.

Proverbs 1:28
For when you call upon me, I will not listen to you; evil people will seek me and will not find me,
  Micah 3:4 — unanswered cry after wrongdoing: Both passages announce that wrongdoers will cry for help and receive no hearing; Micah explicitly links the refusal to their wicked treatment of others.

Proverbs 1:29
for they hated wisdom and did not choose the fear of the Lord,
  Job 28:28 — wisdom and reverence: The rejecters refuse wisdom and fear of the Lord; Job defines reverence for God as wisdom, clarifying the connection between the two things they reject.

Proverbs 1:30
nor did they want to attend to my counsels, but they were sneering at my reproofs.
  Proverbs 1:25 — same-chapter literary refrain: The refusal to attend to counsel and the sneering at reproof repeat the earlier accusation of making counsel ineffective and disobeying reproof within Wisdom's speech.

Proverbs 1:31
Therefore they will eat the fruits of their own way and be filled with their own ungodliness.
  Galatians 6:7-8 — moral harvest imagery: Eating the fruit of one's own way is paired with reaping what one sows; Galatians makes the adverse harvest explicit as corruption from sowing into the flesh.

Proverbs 1:32
For because they wronged the innocent, they will be murdered, and examination will destroy the ungodly.
  Psalms 36:14-15 — violence returning upon attackers: Proverbs says those who wronged the innocent will themselves be murdered; the psalm depicts attackers drawing weapons against the upright and then calls for their own sword to enter their heart.

Proverbs 1:33
But whoever listens to me will dwell in hope and rest fearlessly from every evil.
  Proverbs 3:24-26 — wisdom and freedom from fear: Listening to Wisdom promises fearless rest here; chapter 3 develops secure sitting and sleep, freedom from sudden fright and the Lord's support along one's ways.

Proverbs 2:1
Son, if you receive an utterance of my commandment and hide it within yourself,
  Psalms 118:11 — internalized instruction: Receiving and hiding the commandment within oneself corresponds to the psalmist hiding God's sayings in his heart so that he may not sin.

Proverbs 2:2
your ear will obey wisdom, and you will apply your heart to understanding; you will also apply it to admonishing your son.
  Proverbs 4:20-21 — ear and heart instruction: The earlier assurance that the ear will obey wisdom and the learner will apply the heart to understanding corresponds to the later commands to apply the ear to the father's words and keep them in the heart.

Proverbs 2:3
for if you call upon wisdom and give your voice to understanding, and seek perception with a loud voice,
  James 1:5 — request for wisdom: Proverbs urges the learner to call and give voice for wisdom and understanding; James directs the person lacking wisdom to ask God, who gives it generously.

Proverbs 2:4
and if you seek it like silver and search it out like treasures,
  Proverbs 3:14 — wisdom valued as treasure: Seeking wisdom as silver and treasures is complemented by the later valuation of trading in wisdom above treasures of gold and silver.

Proverbs 2:5
then you will understand the fear of the Lord and find the knowledge of God.
  Job 28:28 — knowledge and reverence: Finding knowledge of God and understanding fear of the Lord is paired with Job's definition of reverence as wisdom and abstention from evil as knowledge.

Proverbs 2:6
For the Lord gives wisdom, and from His presence come knowledge and understanding.
  James 1:5 — God as giver of wisdom: Both passages expressly locate wisdom's gift in God: the Lord gives wisdom here, and James promises God's generous giving to the person who asks for it.
  1 Kings 3:11-12 — narrative illustration: The statement that the Lord gives wisdom is illustrated by His response to Solomon's request for understanding: He gives Solomon a discerning and wise heart.

Proverbs 2:7
He stores up salvation for those acting rightly; He will shield their journey,
  Psalms 36:23-24 — the Lord protecting the journey: The Lord will shield the journey of those acting rightly; the psalm says the Lord directs a person's steps and supports his hand so that when he falls he will not be dashed down. Both connect divine care with the person's course and protection from harm.

Proverbs 2:8
to guard the ways of righteous ordinances, and He will protect the way of those who revere Him.
  Psalms 120:7-8 — divine guardianship: Protecting the way of those who revere God is paired with the Lord guarding the person's soul and every coming in and going out in the ascent song.

Proverbs 2:9
Then you will understand righteousness and judgment, and you will make all good courses straight.
  Proverbs 1:3 — literary purpose and outcome: The promised understanding of righteousness and judgment realizes the book's announced purpose of understanding true righteousness and directing judgment rightly.

Proverbs 2:10
For if wisdom enters your mind and perception seems good to your soul,
  Proverbs 14:33 — wisdom within the person: Wisdom entering the learner's mind and perception pleasing the soul is paired with the later statement that wisdom is in a person's good heart.

Proverbs 2:11
good counsel will guard you, and holy thought will keep you,
  Proverbs 4:6 — wisdom as guardian: Good counsel and holy thought guard the learner here; the later exhortation promises that wisdom will hold fast to and keep the person who loves and retains her.

Proverbs 2:12
so that it may rescue you from an evil way and from a man who says nothing trustworthy.
  Proverbs 6:17-19 — rescue from deceitful wrongdoing: Rescue from the evil way and an untrustworthy speaker corresponds to the condemned combination of an unjust tongue, feet hastening to harm and a witness kindling falsehoods.

Proverbs 2:13
O you who abandon straight ways to walk in ways of darkness,
  Proverbs 4:19 — darkness as the ungodly way: Abandoning straight ways to walk in darkness is paired with the description of ungodly ways as dark and their travelers as unaware how they stumble.

Proverbs 2:14
who delight in evils and rejoice in evil distortion,
  Proverbs 10:23 — pleasure in wrongdoing: Delight and rejoicing in evil correspond to the fool doing evil things in laughter in chapter 10. Both portray pleasure in wrongdoing; the later proverb explicitly identifies its subject as a fool.

Proverbs 2:15
whose paths are crooked and whose tracks are bent,
  Psalms 124:5 — crooked paths: The description of crooked paths and bent tracks is paired with the psalm's people turning aside into tangled ways and being led away with workers of lawlessness.

Proverbs 2:16
to put you far from a straight way and make you alien to a righteous judgment.
  Proverbs 8:20 — wisdom and evil counsel contrasted: Being drawn away from a straight way and righteous judgment contrasts with Wisdom walking in righteousness and in paths of just judgment in her later speech.

Proverbs 2:17
Son, do not let evil counsel seize you, counsel that abandons the teaching of youth and has forgotten a divine covenant.
  Psalms 77:5-10 — abandoned teaching and covenant: Evil counsel abandons youthful teaching and forgets a divine covenant; the psalm recounts instruction passed from fathers to sons and then the failure to keep God's covenant or walk in His law.

Proverbs 2:18
For she has set her house beside death and her courses beside Hades with the earthborn.
  Proverbs 9:18 — wisdom literature death imagery: The house beside death and courses beside Hades with the earthborn correspond to the foolish woman's setting where earth-born ones perish and her visitor reaches Hades' ledge.

Proverbs 2:19
All who travel in her will not return, nor will they reach straight paths, for they are not reached by years of life.
  Proverbs 7:26-27 — fatal paths: The travelers' failure to return or reach life-giving straight paths is paired with the later warning about many murdered victims and a house whose ways descend to Hades and death's chambers.

Proverbs 2:20
For if they had walked good paths, they would have found smooth paths of righteousness.
  Proverbs 4:11 — righteous paths taught by wisdom: The missed prospect of finding smooth righteous paths through walking good paths is paired with the father's teaching of wisdom's ways and leading onto straight tracks.

Proverbs 2:21
Good people will inhabit the land, and the innocent will remain in it, for the upright will dwell in the land and the holy will remain in it.
  Psalms 36:29 — righteous dwelling in the land: Both passages connect righteousness with lasting residence in the land: the good and innocent remain here, and the righteous inherit and dwell upon it in the psalm.

Proverbs 2:22
The ways of the ungodly will perish from the land, and the lawless will be driven out of it.
  Psalms 36:28 — the lawless driven out: Proverbs says the ways of the ungodly will perish from the land and the lawless will be driven out of it. The psalm likewise says the lawless will be driven out, while distinguishing the guarding of God's holy ones and the destruction of the offspring of the ungodly.

Proverbs 3:1
Son, do not forget my ordinances; let your heart keep my words,
  Proverbs 4:5 — literary repetition: The prohibition on forgetting ordinances and the call to keep words in the heart recur in the instruction to keep commandments and not forget or disregard the father's utterance.

Proverbs 3:2
for they will add length of life, years of living, and peace to you.
  Deuteronomy 5:33 — obedience and length of life: The promise of longer life and peace through keeping instruction corresponds to walking God's commanded way so that life in the inherited land may be prolonged and go well.

Proverbs 3:3
Do not let acts of mercy and faithfulness fail you. Fasten them around your neck, and you will find favor.
  Proverbs 6:21 — moral adornment imagery: Mercy and faithfulness are to be fastened around the neck here; the later instruction collars parental laws around the neck and fastens them upon the soul. Both picture what should govern conduct as something worn around the neck.

Proverbs 3:4
And take thought for good things before the Lord and people.
  2 Corinthians 8:21 — verbal reuse: Taking thought for good things before the Lord and people corresponds to Paul's care to do what is honorable before both the Lord and people while administering the gift.
  Romans 12:17 — verbal parallel: The command to take thought for good before people is echoed in Paul's command to take thought for what is honorable before all people, within his instruction against repaying evil.

Proverbs 3:5
Trust God with your whole heart; do not exalt yourself in your own wisdom.
  Jeremiah 17:7-8 — trust in the Lord: Trusting God with the whole heart corresponds to Jeremiah's blessing of the person whose trust and hope are in the Lord, pictured as a well-watered tree secure in drought.
  Proverbs 28:26 — wisdom and misplaced self-trust: The prohibition on exalting oneself in one's own wisdom is paired with the later contrast between trusting a rash heart, which is folly, and walking in wisdom, which brings safety.

Proverbs 3:6
In all your ways acknowledge her, that she may cut your ways straight; and your foot will surely not stumble.
  Proverbs 3:23 — same-chapter literary parallel: The assurance of straight ways and a foot that will not stumble is resumed in the promise of confident, peaceful walking without stumbling after the renewed call to keep counsel.

Proverbs 3:7
Do not be prudent in your own estimation; fear God and turn away from every evil.
  Romans 12:16 — verbal parallel: The warning not to be prudent in one's own estimation corresponds closely to Paul's command not to become wise in one's own eyes.
  Job 28:28 — fear of God and departure from evil: The paired commands to fear God and turn from evil correspond to Job's paired definitions of reverence as wisdom and abstaining from evil as knowledge.

Proverbs 3:8
Then there will be healing for your body and care for your bones.
  Proverbs 4:22 — instruction and bodily healing: Healing for the body and care for the bones is paired with the later description of the father's words as life and healing for all flesh.

Proverbs 3:9
Honor the Lord from your righteous labors, and offer Him firstfruits from your fruits of righteousness,
  Exodus 23:19 — firstfruits offering: Honoring the Lord with firstfruits from righteous labor corresponds to the command to bring the first produce of the land into the Lord's house.

Proverbs 3:10
so that your storerooms may be filled with an abundance of grain and your vats gush with wine.
  Deuteronomy 28:8 — blessing of stored produce: The promised filling of storerooms with grain corresponds to the covenant blessing sent upon storerooms and the work of the people's hands in the land.

Proverbs 3:11
Son, do not make light of the Lord’s discipline or lose heart when corrected by Him,
  Hebrews 12:5 — direct quotation: Hebrews quotes the filial warning against making light of the Lord's discipline or fainting under His reproof, applying the exhortation to its hearers' endurance.

Proverbs 3:12
for the Lord disciplines the one He loves and scourges every son He receives.
  Hebrews 12:6 — direct quotation: Hebrews reproduces the reason for receiving discipline: the Lord disciplines the one He loves and scourges every son He receives.

Proverbs 3:13

  Proverbs 8:34-35 — wisdom and blessed life: The blessing of the person who finds wisdom is paired with Wisdom's later blessing of the hearer who keeps her ways, which lead to life and favor from the Lord.

Proverbs 3:14

  Proverbs 8:19 — literary parallel: Wisdom's greater value in trade is echoed when she describes her harvest as better than gold and precious stone and her produce as better than choice silver.

Proverbs 3:15

Proverbs 3:16

  1 Kings 3:13-14 — narrative illustration: Wisdom's gifts of years, wealth and glory have a concrete counterpart in God's gifts of wealth and glory to Solomon and His conditional promise to multiply Solomon's days if he keeps the commandments.

Proverbs 3:16a
Righteousness proceeds from her mouth, and she carries law and mercy on her tongue.
  Proverbs 31:28 — wise speech and mercy: Wisdom carries law and mercy on her tongue; the final portrait joins a woman's wise, lawful speech with mercy that raised up her children. Both bring lawful speech and merciful conduct together.

Proverbs 3:17

  Proverbs 4:27b — peaceful guidance: Wisdom's good ways and peaceful paths correspond to the assurance that God will straighten the learner's tracks and advance the journey in peace.

Proverbs 3:18
She is a tree of life to all who hold her fast, and secure for those who lean on her as on the Lord.
  Proverbs 11:30 — tree of life imagery: Wisdom is a tree of life for those holding her; the later proverb says a tree of life grows from the fruit of righteousness. Both apply the life-bearing tree image to the moral life.

Proverbs 3:19
God founded the earth by wisdom and prepared the heavens by good sense.
  Jeremiah 10:12 — creation by divine wisdom: Both passages associate God's establishment of the earth with wisdom and His preparation or stretching out of heaven with understanding.
  Proverbs 8:27-30 — creation and personified wisdom: The declaration that God founded earth and prepared heaven by wisdom is developed in Wisdom's account of being beside Him as heaven, clouds, springs and earth's foundations were prepared.

Proverbs 3:20
By perception the deeps were split open, and clouds flowed with dew.
  Proverbs 8:28-29 — creation's waters and wisdom: Divine perception is linked here to the deeps and clouds; Wisdom's later creation account places her beside God as He establishes clouds, the world's springs and the earth's foundations.

Proverbs 3:21
Son, do not drift away; keep my counsel and thought,
  Proverbs 4:13 — holding instruction fast: The call not to drift away but keep counsel and thought corresponds to taking hold of discipline, refusing to release it and keeping it for one's life.

Proverbs 3:22
so that your soul may live and grace be around your neck.
  Proverbs 1:9 — adornment imagery: Grace around the neck, promised alongside life here, recalls the opening promise of a crown of graces and a golden collar received through instruction.

Proverbs 3:22a
There will be healing for your flesh and care for your bones,
  Proverbs 3:8 — same-chapter repeated assurance: This continuation unit repeats the chapter's earlier assurance of healing for the body and care for the bones, preserving the renewed bodily-healing promise as its own source unit.

Proverbs 3:23
so that you may walk all your ways confidently in peace, and your foot will certainly not stumble.
  Proverbs 4:12 — secure travel through wisdom: Confident walking without stumbling is paired with the later promise that one's steps will not be shut in while walking and one will not grow weary while running.

Proverbs 3:24
For if you sit, you will be fearless, and if you sleep, you will sleep pleasantly.
  Psalms 4:9 — peaceful sleep and security: Fearless sitting and pleasant sleep correspond to the psalmist's resolve to lie down and sleep in peace because the Lord made him dwell alone in hope.

Proverbs 3:25
You will not fear an approaching fright or the oncoming assaults of the ungodly,
  Psalms 111:7-8 — steadfastness before threatening news: Freedom from approaching fright and ungodly assaults is paired with the righteous person's lack of fear at an evil report and established heart, with the assurance that he will not fear until he looks upon his enemies.

Proverbs 3:26
for the Lord will be upon all your ways and support your foot so that you are not shaken.
  Psalms 120:3-5 — the Lord as keeper: The Lord's support of the foot so it is not shaken corresponds to the command not to give one's foot over to shaking and the assurance of the wakeful Lord as keeper and shelter.

Proverbs 3:27

  1 John 3:17-18 — material help for a person in need: Helping when one's hand has the means corresponds to John's challenge to a person who has worldly goods yet closes the heart to a needy brother, followed by the call to love in deed.

Proverbs 3:28

  James 2:15-16 — empty dismissal of the needy: Sending a needy person away while help can be given is paired with sending an unclothed or hungry brother or sister away with peaceful words but without providing bodily necessities.
  Proverbs 27:1 — uncertainty of tomorrow: The reason against delaying help until tomorrow is repeated in the later warning not to boast of tomorrow, because one does not know what the coming day will bear.

Proverbs 3:29

Proverbs 3:30

  Proverbs 25:8-10a — needless conflict and its consequences: Avoiding vain enmity lest harm follow corresponds to the later warning against rushing into battle and provoking enduring reproach and enmity, followed by counsel to seek reconciliation.

Proverbs 3:31

Proverbs 3:32
for every lawless person is unclean before the Lord, and he does not sit in council among the righteous.
  Psalms 1:5 — exclusion from righteous company: The lawless person's absence from the council of the righteous corresponds to the psalm's declaration that sinners will not rise in the righteous council.

Proverbs 3:33

  Proverbs 14:11 — contrasting households: The curse upon ungodly houses and blessing upon righteous dwellings correspond to the later contrast between impious houses disappearing and upright tents standing.

Proverbs 3:34
The Lord opposes the proud, but gives grace to the humble.
  James 4:6 — direct quotation: James introduces the same opposition of God to the proud and gift of grace to the humble as scriptural support for humility and submission to God.
  1 Peter 5:5 — direct quotation: First Peter quotes the proud-and-humble contrast while urging mutual humility, retaining God's opposition to pride and His grace toward the humble.

Proverbs 16:25

Proverbs 16:26

Proverbs 16:30

Proverbs 16:31

Proverbs 16:32

  Proverbs 24:5 — parallel moral capacity over physical strength: The source ranks patience and control of anger above strength and capturing a city; the target similarly ranks a wise person above a strong one.

Proverbs 17:3

Proverbs 17:4

Proverbs 17:6

Proverbs 17:7

Proverbs 17:11

Proverbs 19:10

Proverbs 19:11

Proverbs 19:12

Proverbs 19:15

Proverbs 19:20

Proverbs 19:21

Proverbs 20:5
Counsel in a man’s heart is deep water, but an intelligent man will draw it out.

Proverbs 20:6

Proverbs 20:7

Proverbs 20:8

Proverbs 20:9

Proverbs 20:9a

Proverbs 20:9b
A portion eagerly pursued at first will not be blessed at the end.
  Proverbs 13:11 — eager acquisition warning: An eagerly pursued portion ends without blessing in 20:9b; 13:11 likewise warns that eagerly pursued property becomes less, specifically describing that pursuit as lawless.

Proverbs 20:9c
Do not say, “I will punish the enemy.” But wait for the Lord, so that He may help you.
  Romans 12:19 — renunciation of personal vengeance: Proverbs forbids saying one will punish the enemy and directs waiting for the Lord's help; Romans similarly forbids self-avenging and reserves repayment to the Lord.

Proverbs 20:10

  Proverbs 11:1 — honest measures: The rejection of unequal weights and double measures before the Lord corresponds to 11:1's rejection of deceitful scales and approval of a just weight.

Proverbs 20:11

  Proverbs 5:22 — self-entrapment image: In the context of 20:10's false measures, their maker is fettered in his practices; 5:22 similarly pictures a man bound tightly by the cords of his own sins.

Proverbs 20:12
An ear hears and an eye sees; both are works of the Lord.

Proverbs 20:13

  Proverbs 13:3 — speech and preservation: The warning against speaking against others lest one be cut off corresponds to 13:3's connection between guarding one's mouth and preserving one's life.

Proverbs 20:23
A double weight is an abomination to the Lord, and deceitful scales are not good before Him.
  Proverbs 11:1 — honest measures: Both condemn deceitful scales before the Lord; 20:23 also explicitly names the double weight as an abomination.

Proverbs 20:24
A man’s steps are directed by the Lord, but how could a mortal understand his ways?
  Proverbs 15:29b — divine direction of steps: A person's steps directed by the Lord in 20:24 correspond to the steps set right by God in 15:29b, where the heart is told to consider righteous things.

Proverbs 20:25

Proverbs 20:26

Proverbs 20:27

Proverbs 20:28

Proverbs 20:29
Wisdom is an adornment for young men, and gray hair is the glory of elders.
  Proverbs 16:31 — honor of old age: Gray hair as the elders' glory corresponds to old age as a crown of boasting in 16:31; the link concerns the elderly, while 20:29 separately praises young men's wisdom.

Proverbs 20:30
Bruises and crushing wounds meet evil people, and blows reach the store-chambers of the belly.
  Proverbs 19:29 — punishment image: The bruises, wounds and blows assigned to evil people in 20:30 correspond to the whips and punishments prepared for the undisciplined and fools in 19:29.

Proverbs 21:1
As a rush of water, so is a king’s heart in God’s hand; wherever He willingly inclines, there He has bent it.
  Ezra 1:1-2 — historical illustration: The proverb pictures a king's heart inclined by God; Ezra gives a narrative example when the Lord stirs Cyrus's spirit and the king announces the commission to build God's house.

Proverbs 21:2
Every man appears righteous to himself, but the Lord sets hearts straight.
  Proverbs 12:15 — self-assessment warning: A person appearing righteous to himself in 21:2 corresponds to fools' ways appearing upright before them in 12:15, which contrasts that self-assessment with listening to counsel.
  Jeremiah 17:9-10 — human heart and divine scrutiny: Proverbs contrasts human self-approval with the Lord setting hearts straight; Jeremiah contrasts the heart's depth beyond human knowledge with the Lord examining it and judging its ways.

Proverbs 21:3
Doing righteous things and speaking truth are more pleasing before God than the blood of sacrifices.
  Proverbs 16:7 — obedience preferred to sacrifice: Both expressly place doing righteous things above offering sacrifices in God's estimation; 21:3 additionally names speaking truth and the blood of sacrifices.

Proverbs 21:4
One lofty-minded in insolence is rash-hearted, and the lamp of the impious is sin.
  Proverbs 16:5 — pride before God: The insolent, lofty-minded and rash-hearted person in 21:4 corresponds to the high-hearted person declared unclean before God in 16:5.

Proverbs 21:6
One who works up stores with a false tongue pursues empty things toward traps of death.
  Proverbs 13:11 — dishonest acquisition warning: Accumulating stores through a false tongue leads toward emptiness and death traps in 21:6; 13:11 likewise warns that property eagerly pursued with lawlessness diminishes.

Proverbs 21:7
Destruction will lodge with the impious, for they do not wish to do righteous things.
  Proverbs 11:6 — lawlessness and destruction: Destruction comes upon those unwilling to do righteous things in 21:7; 11:6 similarly describes lawless people caught in their own destruction, in contrast to the rescue righteousness brings.

Proverbs 21:8
God sends crooked ways to the crooked, for His works are pure and upright.
  Psalms 17:26-27 — corresponding divine treatment: God sends crooked ways to crooked people while His works remain upright; the psalm similarly describes God's dealings corresponding to the holy, innocent, chosen and twisted person.

Proverbs 21:9
Better to dwell on a corner in the open air than in plastered rooms with injustice and in a shared house.
  Proverbs 25:24 — parallel comparison: Both prefer an exposed corner to a shared house: 21:9 contrasts its corner with injustice in plastered rooms, while 25:24 contrasts a roof corner with an abusive wife.

Proverbs 21:11
When an undisciplined person is penalized, the innocent becomes more resourceful, and a wise person who understands will receive knowledge.
  Proverbs 19:25 — parallel saying: Both describe another person's punishment instructing an observer, then contrast this with the knowledge an intelligent or wise person receives through understanding or reproof.

Proverbs 21:13
One who blocks his ears from hearing the weak will himself also call, and there will be no one listening.
  Proverbs 28:27 — refusal of compassion and consequence: Closing one's ears to the weak results in an unanswered call in 21:13; turning one's eye away from the poor results in great difficulty in 28:27.

Proverbs 21:14
A secret gift overturns anger, but one who is sparing with gifts raises fierce wrath.
  Genesis 32:21 — narrative illustration of appeasement: The gift that overturns anger in Proverbs corresponds to Jacob's stated hope that gifts sent before him will appease Esau and lead Esau to accept him.

Proverbs 21:15
Doing judgment is the joy of the righteous, but a devout person is unclean in the eyes of evildoers.
  Proverbs 29:27 — opposed moral judgments: The devout person regarded as unclean by evildoers in 21:15 corresponds to the straightening way regarded as an abomination by the lawless in 29:27.

Proverbs 21:16
A man who wanders from the way of righteousness will rest in the assembly of giants.
  Proverbs 2:18 — deathward path image: Leaving righteousness leads to rest among the giants in 21:16; 2:18 places the house of personified evil counsel, identified in 2:17, beside death and its courses beside Hades with the earthborn.

Proverbs 21:18
but a lawless person is a cleansing-offering for the righteous.

Proverbs 21:19
Better to dwell in a desert land than with a battling, talkative, and quick-tempered woman.

Proverbs 21:20

  Proverbs 10:20 — valuable speech image: Treasure resting upon the wise person's mouth corresponds to the righteous tongue pictured as fire-tested silver in 10:20. Both give speech a precious-material image.

Proverbs 21:21
A way of righteousness and mercy will find life and glory.
  Proverbs 12:28 — righteous way and life: The way of righteousness and mercy finding life in 21:21 corresponds to the explicit statement in 12:28 that life is in ways of righteousness.

Proverbs 21:22

  Ecclesiastes 9:14-18 — wisdom and military strength: Proverbs describes wisdom overcoming fortified cities; Ecclesiastes describes a wise man delivering a besieged city and judges wisdom better than strength and weapons. The military outcomes differ, but both elevate wisdom over force.

Proverbs 21:23
One who guards his mouth and tongue keeps his life from affliction.
  Proverbs 13:3 — parallel saying: Both directly connect guarding one's mouth with keeping one's life; 21:23 adds the tongue and specifies preservation from affliction.

Proverbs 21:24
A rash, self-willed, boastful person is called pestilent, and one who remembers wrongs is lawless.

Proverbs 21:25

Proverbs 21:26

Proverbs 21:27

Proverbs 21:28
A false witness will perish, but an obedient man will speak with caution.
  Proverbs 19:9 — false-witness warning: The false witness who perishes in 21:28 corresponds to 19:9's warning that a false witness will not escape punishment and that kindled wickedness consumes its instigator.

Proverbs 21:29
An impious man stands his ground with a shameless face, but the upright himself understands his ways.
  Proverbs 14:8 — understanding one's ways: The upright person's understanding of his ways in 21:29 corresponds to the resourceful person's wisdom recognizing his ways in 14:8.

Proverbs 21:30

  Proverbs 14:6 — wisdom absent among evil people: The absence of wisdom and counsel with the impious corresponds to seeking wisdom among evil people and not finding it in 14:6.

Proverbs 21:31

Proverbs 22:1

Proverbs 22:3

Proverbs 22:4

  Proverbs 3:16 — wisdom's benefits: Wisdom's offspring include wealth, glory and life in 22:4; 3:16 likewise places length of life, wealth and glory with wisdom.

Proverbs 22:5

Proverbs 22:8a

Proverbs 22:9

Proverbs 22:9a

Proverbs 22:10

Proverbs 22:11

  Proverbs 11:20 — blamelessness acceptable to God: The Lord's love of devout hearts and acceptance of the blameless correspond to 11:20's acceptance before Him of those blameless in their ways.

Proverbs 22:13

Proverbs 22:14

Proverbs 22:14a

Proverbs 22:19

Proverbs 22:20

Proverbs 22:23

Proverbs 22:25

Proverbs 22:26

Proverbs 22:27

Proverbs 22:28

Proverbs 22:29

Proverbs 23:6

Proverbs 23:9

Proverbs 23:10

Proverbs 23:11

  Proverbs 22:23 — divine defense of the vulnerable: The mighty Lord who redeems orphans and judges their case corresponds to the Lord judging the poor person's case in 22:23.

Proverbs 23:12

Proverbs 23:13

  Proverbs 13:24 — parental discipline: The instruction not to refrain from disciplining a child with a rod corresponds to 13:24's contrast between sparing the staff and the careful discipline associated with parental love.

Proverbs 23:14

Proverbs 23:15

Proverbs 23:17

Proverbs 23:18
for if you keep these things, you will have offspring, and your hope will not depart.
  Proverbs 24:14 — hope preserved: The promise that hope will not depart when the prior instruction is kept corresponds to 24:14's promise that finding wisdom brings a good end and hope that does not abandon the learner.

Proverbs 23:19
Listen, son, and become wise, and straighten the reflections of your heart.
  Proverbs 19:20 — listening to become wise: Both directly address the son and command listening so that he may become wise; 23:19 also calls for straightened reflections, while 19:20 looks to wisdom in later days.

Proverbs 23:20
Do not be a wine-drinker, and do not stretch yourself over contributions and purchases of meat;
  Ephesians 5:18 — wine warning: The warning against becoming a wine-drinker can be read with Paul's prohibition on drunkenness with wine because of its reckless excess; the latter specifically contrasts drunkenness with being filled in the Spirit.

Proverbs 23:21
for every drunkard and frequenter of prostitutes will become poor, and every sleepy person will clothe himself in torn and ragged things.
  Proverbs 29:3 — dissipation and poverty: The prediction that a frequenter of prostitutes becomes poor corresponds to 29:3's statement that tending prostitutes destroys wealth; 23:21 includes drunkards and sleepy people in its warning as well.

Proverbs 23:22
Listen, son, to the father who begot you, and do not despise your mother because she has grown old.
  Proverbs 30:17 — honoring an aging mother: The command not to despise a mother because she has grown old corresponds to 30:17's warning against the eye that dishonors a mother's old age and mocks a father.

Proverbs 23:24
A righteous father brings up well, and his life rejoices over a wise son;
  Proverbs 10:1 — family parallel: The father's rejoicing over a wise son corresponds directly to 10:1's statement that a wise son makes his father glad.

Proverbs 23:25
let father and mother rejoice over you, and let the one who bore you be glad.
  Proverbs 17:21 — family parallel: The call for father and mother to rejoice over the child corresponds to 17:21's contrast between a father's lack of joy over an uneducated son and the intelligent son gladdening his mother.

Proverbs 23:26
Give me your heart, son, and let your eyes keep my ways;
  Proverbs 4:23-26 — heart, eyes and way: Giving the heart and keeping the teacher's ways with one's eyes corresponds to 4:23-26's coordinated commands to guard the heart, direct the eyes toward righteousness and straighten one's tracks.

Proverbs 23:27
for another’s house is a jar with holes, and another’s well is narrow;
  Proverbs 9:18c — foreign-water warning: Proverbs 23:27 describes another's well as narrow alongside the leaking jar image of another's house; 9:18c likewise warns away from foreign water and drinking from a foreign spring.

Proverbs 23:29
Who has woe? Who has uproar? Who has a lawsuit? Who has unpleasantness and chatter? Who has bruises for nothing? Whose eyes are livid?
  Sirach 31:29-30 — drunken strife and wounds: The uproar, lawsuits and gratuitous bruises of 23:29, attributed to drinkers in its following verse, correspond to Sirach's description of excessive wine bringing conflict, anger and wounds.

Proverbs 23:30
Is it not those who linger over wines? Is it not those who track down where drinking parties take place?
  Isaiah 5:11-12 — prolonged drinking: The people lingering over wine and seeking drinking parties correspond to Isaiah's drinkers who pursue strong drink from morning into evening and neglect the Lord's works.

Proverbs 23:31
Do not become drunk with wine, but associate with righteous people, and converse during walks; for if you give your eyes to bowls and cups, afterward you will walk about barer than a pestle.
  Ephesians 5:18 — verbal parallel against drunkenness: Both explicitly command against becoming drunk with wine; Proverbs then urges company with righteous people, while Ephesians directs being filled in the Spirit.

Proverbs 23:32
But at the end he lies stretched out as though struck by a snake, and as though by a horned viper its poison spreads through him.
  Sirach 31:25-30 — bodily harm of drunkenness: In the continuing portrait of the drinker, Proverbs pictures collapse and spreading poison like a snakebite; Sirach likewise warns that wine destroys many and that drunkenness diminishes strength and adds wounds.

Proverbs 23:33
When your eyes see another woman, then your mouth will speak crooked things,
  Proverbs 4:24-25 — crooked speech and directed eyes: The drinker's eyes on another woman and ensuing crooked words contrast with 4:24-25's commands to remove a crooked mouth and direct the eyes toward righteous things.

Proverbs 23:34
and you will lie down as in the heart of the sea, and like a helmsman in heavy surging;
  Psalms 106:25-27 — reciprocal sea and drunkenness imagery: Proverbs compares the drinker's condition to a helmsman in heavy seas; the psalm conversely compares storm-tossed sailors' shaking and loss of wisdom to a drunken man.

Proverbs 23:35
and you will say, “They strike me, and I felt no pain; they mocked me, but I did not know. When will dawn come, so that I may go and seek those with whom I will gather?”
  Proverbs 26:11 — return to harmful folly: After unperceived blows and mockery, the drinker plans to seek his companions again; 26:11 similarly pictures a fool returning to his wicked sin, compared there to a dog encountering its own vomit.
