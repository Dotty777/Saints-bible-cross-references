Song of Songs 1:1
SOURCE: The Song of Songs, which belongs to Solomon.
TARGET 1 Kings 5:12:
1 Kings 5:12 Solomon spoke three thousand parables, and his songs numbered five thousand.
RELATIONSHIP: Solomonic song attribution
RATIONALE: The heading assigns this song to Solomon; Kings explicitly includes songs among Solomon's compositions. This links the attribution without identifying this poem as a particular item in that larger collection.

Song of Songs 2:14 and come, my dove, in the shelter of the rock, close to the outer wall. Show me your face and let me hear your voice, for your voice is sweet and your face lovely.
RELATIONSHIP: Repeated request to hear the beloved
RATIONALE: The garden-dweller is asked to let the speaker hear her voice; the earlier dove address made the same request while praising the voice's sweetness.

Song of Songs 8:14
SOURCE: Flee, my kinsman, and be like a gazelle or a young stag upon mountains of spices.
TARGET Song of Songs 2:17:
Song of Songs 2:17 until the day breathes and the shadows move. Turn back, my kinsman; be like a gazelle or a young stag upon mountains of hollows.
RELATIONSHIP: Repeated mountain-animal appeal
RATIONALE: The final request for the kinsman to be like a gazelle or young stag upon mountains repeats the earlier mountain comparison, changing the request from turning back to fleeing.
