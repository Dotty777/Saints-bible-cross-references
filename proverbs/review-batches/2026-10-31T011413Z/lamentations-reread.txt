Lamentations 1:1
SOURCE: After Israel was taken captive and Jerusalem laid waste, Jeremiah sat weeping and lamented this lament over Jerusalem, saying: How the city once full of peoples sat alone! She has become like a widow, she who was populous among the nations. A ruler among the regions has become subject to tribute.
TARGET 2 Kings 25:8-12:
2 Kings 25:8 In the fifth month, on the seventh of the month, in the nineteenth year of Nabouchodonosor king of Babylon, Nabouzardan, the chief cook who stood before the king of Babylon, came to Jerusalem.
2 Kings 25:9 He set fire to the Lord’s house, the king’s house, and all Jerusalem’s houses; every house was set ablaze by
2 Kings 25:10 the chief cook.
2 Kings 25:11 Nabouzardan, the chief cook, removed the rest of the people left in the city, those who had gone over to the king of Babylon, and the remaining support.
2 Kings 25:12 The chief cook left some of the land’s poor as vinedressers and as gabin.
RELATIONSHIP: Historical setting of Jerusalem's devastation
RATIONALE: The lament's preface names Israel's captivity and Jerusalem's devastation; Kings narrates the burning of Jerusalem and removal of its remaining people. This supplies the historical catastrophe over which the city is personified as bereaved.

Lamentations 1:2
SOURCE: Weeping, she wept in the night, her tears upon her cheeks. Among all those who love her there is no one to comfort her. All her friends have dealt faithlessly with her; they have become her enemies.
TARGET Jeremiah 37:14:
Jeremiah 37:14 All your friends forgot you; they will certainly not inquire. For I struck you with an enemy’s blow, a firm discipline; upon all your injustice your sins multiplied.
RELATIONSHIP: Abandonment by former friends
RATIONALE: Jerusalem's friends act faithlessly and leave her without comfort; Jeremiah describes the afflicted people forgotten by all their friends, who will not inquire after them. Both portray stricken people abandoned by those expected to help.

Lamentations 1:3
SOURCE: Judea was deported because of her humiliation and her great slavery. She dwelt among the nations; she found no rest. All her pursuers overtook her among those who afflicted her.
TARGET Deuteronomy 28:64-65:
Deuteronomy 28:64 And the Lord your God shall scatter you among all the nations, from one end of the earth unto the other; and there you shall serve other gods, wood and stone, which neither you nor your fathers have known.
Deuteronomy 28:65 And among those nations He shall give you no rest, neither shall there be a resting place for the sole of your foot; and the Lord shall give you there a faint heart, and failing eyes, and a wasting soul.
RELATIONSHIP: Exile and restlessness
RATIONALE: Judea lives among nations without finding rest; Deuteronomy warns of scattering among nations with no rest or resting place for the foot. The lament repeats the exile-and-restlessness pattern of the warning.

Lamentations 1:4
SOURCE: Zion’s roads mourn because no one comes at the festival. All her gates are destroyed; her priests groan. Her virgins are led away, and she is embittered within herself.
TARGET Lamentations 2:6:
Lamentations 2:6 He spread out His dwelling like a vine and destroyed His festival. The Lord forgot what He had made in Zion of festival and Sabbath; in His angry indignation He provoked king, priest and ruler.
RELATIONSHIP: Festival life brought to an end
RATIONALE: Zion's roads mourn because nobody comes for the festival; the next lament says the Lord destroyed His festival and forgot festival and Sabbath in Zion. Both describe the loss of the city's gathered worship.

Lamentations 1:5
SOURCE: Those afflicting her have become the head, and her enemies prospered, because the Lord humbled her for her many acts of ungodliness. Her infants went into captivity before the oppressor.
TARGET Deuteronomy 28:41:

RELATIONSHIP: Children taken into captivity
RATIONALE: The lament connects Judah's ungodliness with her infants going into captivity; Deuteronomy warns that sons and daughters will go into captivity rather than remain with their parents.

Lamentations 1:7

RELATIONSHIP: Ridicule of devastated Jerusalem
RATIONALE: Jerusalem's enemies laugh at her deportation; the psalm of the ravaged city describes its people becoming the neighbors' sneering and ridicule. Both add humiliation by onlookers to the city's material losses.

Lamentations 1:9
SOURCE: Her uncleanness is at her feet; she did not remember her last things. He brought her down exceedingly; there is no one to comfort her. See, Lord, my humiliation, for the enemy has grown great!
TARGET Lamentations 1:16:

RELATIONSHIP: Same-chapter uncomforted suffering refrain
RATIONALE: Both verses say the comforter is absent while the enemy has grown strong. The repeated combination links the city's plea to be seen with her later explanation of her tears.

RELATIONSHIP: Nations entering the sanctuary
RATIONALE: The lament sees foreign nations entering the sanctuary and taking desired things; the psalm likewise says nations entered God's inheritance and defiled His holy temple in Jerusalem.

Lamentations 1:11

2 Kings 25:3 On the ninth of the month the famine grew severe in the city, and there were no loaves for the people of the land.
RELATIONSHIP: Jerusalem's famine
RATIONALE: The people seek bread and exchange desired possessions for food; Kings records famine so severe in besieged Jerusalem that no loaves remained for the people. The narrative explains the crisis behind the lament's desperate search for food.

Lamentations 1:12
SOURCE: Not to you, all who pass along the road! Turn and see whether there is pain like my pain, which came to be. Speaking against me, the Lord humbled me on the day of His furious anger.
TARGET Lamentations 2:15:

RELATIONSHIP: Contrasting responses from passersby
RATIONALE: The suffering city asks road-passers to turn and see her pain; later those passing along the road clap, hiss and shake their heads at Jerusalem. The repeated audience responds with mockery rather than the requested recognition of suffering.

Lamentations 1:13
SOURCE: From His height He sent fire; He brought it down into my bones. He spread a net for my feet; He turned me backward. He made me desolate, suffering pain all day long.
TARGET Ezekiel 12:13:

RELATIONSHIP: Divine judgment as a capturing net
RATIONALE: The Lord spreads a net for the lamenting city's feet; Ezekiel says He will spread His net over the ruler and bring him to Babylon. Both represent judgment and capture through a divine net, at different personal scales.

Lamentations 1:14
SOURCE: A watch was kept over my acts of ungodliness; they were intertwined in my hands and rose upon my neck. My strength weakened, because the Lord put pains into my hands; I will not be able to stand.
TARGET Psalms 37:4-5:

Psalms 37:5 For my lawless deeds rose above my head; like a heavy load they weighed upon me.
RELATIONSHIP: Sin as an overwhelming bodily burden
RATIONALE: The lament's acts of ungodliness rise upon the neck as strength fails; the psalm describes lawless deeds rising above the head and weighing like a heavy load. The connection preserves the lament's wording without inserting an unmentioned yoke.

Lamentations 1:15

Lamentations 1:21

SOURCE: Rise, exult in the night at the beginning of your watch! Pour out your heart like water before the Lord. Lift your hands to Him for the lives of your infants, who weaken with hunger at the head of every way out.
TARGET Psalms 61:9:
Psalms 61:9 Hope in Him, all the assembly of the people; pour out your hearts before Him. God is our helper. Diapsalma.
RELATIONSHIP: Pouring out the heart before God
RATIONALE: The lament urges the people to pour out the heart like water before the Lord; the psalm tells the whole assembly to pour out their hearts before Him. Both describe open communal prayer through the same pouring metaphor.

Lamentations 2:20
SOURCE: See, Lord, and look: whom hast Thou gleaned in this way? Will women eat the fruit of their womb? A butcher made a gleaning; will infants nursing at breasts be murdered? Wilt Thou kill priest and prophet in the Lord’s sanctuary?
TARGET Lamentations 4:10:
Lamentations 4:10 The hands of compassionate women boiled their children; they became food for them at the shattering of the daughter of my people.
RELATIONSHIP: The horror of children becoming food
RATIONALE: The lament asks whether women will eat the fruit of their womb; the later lament states that compassionate women's hands boiled their children for food during the people's shattering. The later statement provides the explicit counterpart to the question.

Lamentations 2:21
SOURCE: Child and elder lay at the way out. My virgins and my young men went into captivity. With sword and famine Thou didst kill; on the day of Thine anger Thou didst slaughter without sparing.
TARGET 2 Chronicles 36:17:
2 Chronicles 36:17 He brought against them a king of the Chaldeans, who killed their young men with the sword in the house of his sanctuary and did not spare Zedekiah. They had no mercy on their virgins and carried away their elders. He handed everything into their hands.
RELATIONSHIP: Young and old caught in Jerusalem's fall
RATIONALE: The lament describes child and elder lying at the way out and young people taken captive; Chronicles describes young men killed, virgins shown no mercy and elders carried away. Both portray the catastrophe across age groups while preserving different details.

Lamentations 2:22
SOURCE: He called a festival day of my sojourning from all around. On the day of the Lord’s anger there was neither one rescued nor one left, as I prevailed and multiplied all my enemies.
TARGET Lamentations 1:15:
Lamentations 1:15 The Lord removed all my strong men from my midst. He called a time against me to crush my chosen men. The Lord trod a winepress for the virgin Daughter Judah; for these things I weep.
RELATIONSHIP: A summoned occasion becomes judgment
RATIONALE: A festival day is called, but on the Lord's day of anger nobody is rescued; the earlier lament says He called a time against Judah to crush its chosen men. Both turn the language of a summoned occasion toward the city's calamity.
