Haggai 1:1
In the second year under King Darius, in the sixth month, on the first day of the month, the word of the Lord came through Haggai the prophet, saying: “Speak now to Zerubbabel, the son of Shealtiel, from the tribe of Judah, and to Joshua, the son of Jehozadak, the high priest, saying:
  Ezra 5:1-2 — named prophet and rebuilding leaders in the restoration narrative: Haggai's opening identifies Haggai, Zerubbabel son of Shealtiel and Joshua son of Jehozadak; Ezra names Haggai's prophecy and the rebuilding work of Zorobabel son of Salathiel and Iesous son of Iosedek. The parallel named participants are retained with each text's spellings.
  Ezra 4:24 — second year of Darius as the setting for resumed temple work: Haggai dates his message to Darius's second year; Ezra says the Lord's house remained idle until that second year. The shared regnal year locates the appeal in the narrative's transition from halted work.

Haggai 1:2
‘Thus says the Lord Almighty: These people say, “The time has not arrived to rebuild the house of the Lord.”’”
  Ezra 4:24 — rebuilding delayed before the prophetic appeal: Haggai reports the people's claim that the rebuilding time has not arrived; Ezra records that work on God's house stood idle until Darius's second year. The narrative supplies the prolonged stoppage to which Haggai's appeal speaks, without equating external force with the people's stated excuse.

Haggai 1:3
The word of the Lord came through Haggai the prophet, saying:
  Ezra 5:1 — Haggai explicitly identified as the bearer of divine prophecy: Haggai introduces the Lord's word through the named prophet; Ezra explicitly identifies Haggai as prophesying to the Jews in Judah and Jerusalem in God's name. This links the named prophetic ministry, rather than merely two anonymous revelation headings.

Haggai 1:4
“Is it time for you to live in your houses with recessed ceilings, while this house has been laid waste?
  2 Samuel 7:1-3 — contrast between a settled personal house and God's dwelling: Haggai challenges inhabitants of houses with recessed ceilings while God's house lies waste; David notices that he lives in a cedar house while God's ark remains in a tent. Both contrast domestic comfort with the condition of the divine dwelling, in different historical settings.

Haggai 1:5
And now, thus says the Lord Almighty: Set your hearts upon your ways.
  Lamentations 3:40 — examination of one's way oriented toward the Lord: Haggai commands the people to set their hearts on their ways; Lamentations describes the way being searched and examined and calls for return to the Lord. Both connect reflection on conduct with a response toward God.

Haggai 1:6
You sowed much and brought in little; you ate, but were not satisfied; you drank, but not to intoxication; you clothed yourselves, but were not warmed in them. The one gathering wages gathered them into a bundle with holes.
  Deuteronomy 28:38-40 — much sowing but little harvest and frustrated crop enjoyment: Haggai describes much sowing with little brought in and unsatisfied consumption; Deuteronomy's curse describes much seed with little gathered and denied enjoyment of wine and oil. The connection concerns agricultural futility, without assigning Deuteronomy's named pests to Haggai.
  Leviticus 26:26 — eating without satisfaction as covenant affliction: Haggai says the people ate without being satisfied; Leviticus warns of scarcity in which people eat and are not filled. Both treat food that fails to satisfy as an affliction.

Haggai 1:7
Thus says the Lord Almighty: Place your hearts upon your ways.
  Haggai 1:5 — same-chapter repetition of the command to examine one's ways: The command to place their hearts upon their ways repeats the earlier command to set their hearts upon their ways. The repetition frames the account of disappointing labor before the concrete rebuilding instruction.

Haggai 1:8
Go up onto the mountain, cut wood and rebuild the house. I will take pleasure in it and be glorified, said the Lord.
  Ezra 3:7 — procurement of timber for the Lord's house: Haggai directs the people to cut wood and rebuild the house; Ezra describes provision of payment and supplies to bring cedar timber for the rebuilding setting. Both join obtaining wood to the temple work, without identifying the mountain in Haggai with a specific place.

Haggai 1:9
You looked for much, and it came to little; it was brought into the house, and I blew it away. Therefore, thus says the Lord Almighty: Because My house is desolate, while each of you hurries to his own house,
  Haggai 1:4 — same-chapter return to the contrast between private houses and God's desolate house: Haggai attributes the reduced yield to His desolate house while people hurry to their own houses; the earlier challenge contrasts their finished ceilings with His wasted house. The repeated contrast makes the reason for the shortfall explicit.

Haggai 1:10
therefore heaven will hold back dew, and the earth will withhold its produce.
  Zechariah 8:12 — withheld dew and produce contrasted with restoration: Haggai says heaven withholds dew and the earth its produce; Zechariah promises heaven's dew and the earth's produce to the restored remnant. The same two provisions are denied and then promised in the prophetic rebuilding setting.
  1 Kings 17:1 — withholding of dew announced by divine authority: Haggai announces heaven holding back dew; Elijah announces that no dew or rain will come apart from his prophetic word before the living Lord. Both make withheld dew an announced divine judgment, without equating the episodes.

Haggai 1:11
I will bring a sword upon the land and the mountains, upon grain, wine and oil, and whatever the earth produces, upon people and livestock, and upon all the labors of their hands.”
  Leviticus 26:25 — the Lord bringing a sword as judgment: The native Haggai passage says the Lord brings a sword upon land, produce, people and labor; Leviticus warns that God brings a sword exacting covenant penalty. The correspondence preserves sword language rather than replacing Haggai's word with drought.

Haggai 1:12
Zerubbabel, the son of Shealtiel, from the tribe of Judah, Joshua, the son of Jehozadak, the high priest, and all the remnant of the people listened to the voice of the Lord their God and the words of Haggai the prophet, as the Lord their God had sent him to them. The people feared before the Lord.
  Ezra 5:1-2 — the named leaders respond to prophecy by rebuilding: Haggai says Zerubbabel, Joshua and the remnant listened to the Lord and Haggai; Ezra places the same leaders' beginning to build immediately after Haggai's and Zacharias's prophecy. The connection is the narrative response to prophetic ministry.

Haggai 1:13
Haggai, the messenger of the Lord, said to the people, “I am with you, says the Lord.”
  Joshua 1:9 — divine presence as the basis of encouragement: Haggai delivers the Lord's assurance 'I am with you'; Joshua receives courage because the Lord is with him wherever he goes. The shared assurance supports the work or task given in each setting.

Haggai 1:14
The Lord stirred up the spirit of Zerubbabel, the son of Shealtiel, from the tribe of Judah, the spirit of Joshua, the son of Jehozadak, the high priest, and the spirit of the remnant of all the people. They came in and were carrying out work on the house of the Lord Almighty, their God,
  Ezra 1:5 — God stirring people's spirits to build His house: Haggai says God stirred the leaders' and people's spirits and they worked on His house; Ezra says those whose spirits God stirred arose to go and build the Lord's house. Both attribute willing rebuilding action to God's initiative.
  Ezra 5:2 — rebuilding undertaken by the named restoration leaders: Haggai records Zerubbabel and Joshua coming with the people to work on the Lord's house; Ezra records Zorobabel and Iesous beginning that house's construction with prophetic help. The common names and work establish the narrative parallel.

Haggai 1:15
on the twenty-fourth day of the sixth month, in the second year under King Darius.
  Ezra 4:24 — resumption of work within the specified second year of Darius: Haggai dates the work described immediately before this unit to the twenty-fourth day of month six in Darius's second year; Ezra ends the period of idle work at that same regnal year. Ezra supplies the year, not Haggai's exact month or day.

Haggai 2:1
In the seventh month, on the twenty-first day of the month, the Lord spoke through Haggai the prophet, saying:
  Leviticus 23:34-36 — Calendar correspondence: month seven, day twenty-one within the Feast of Tents: Haggai dates the divine message to the twenty-first day of the seventh month. Leviticus sets the Feast of Tents on the fifteenth day of that same month for seven days, followed by an eighth-day closing observance. Counting the fifteenth as the first festival day places the twenty-first on the seventh day of that prescribed calendar. This is a calendar inference from the stated dates, not a claim that Haggai explicitly reports the audience celebrating the feast or calls its date the eighth day.

Haggai 2:2
“Speak now to Zerubbabel, the son of Shealtiel, from the tribe of Judah, to Joshua, the son of Jehozadak, the high priest, and to all the remnant of the people, saying:
  Ezra 5:2 — identified leaders of the temple rebuilding addressed again: Haggai's renewed instruction addresses Zerubbabel son of Shealtiel and Joshua son of Jehozadak with the remnant; Ezra names Zorobabel son of Salathiel and Iesous son of Iosedek as the men beginning the rebuilding. The link identifies the addressed leaders in the construction narrative.

Haggai 2:3
Who among you saw this house in its former glory? How do you see it now? As though it did not exist before you.
  Ezra 3:12-13 — witnesses of the former house confronted with the new construction: Haggai asks those who saw the former glory how the present house appears; Ezra describes elders who saw the first house weeping as the new foundation is before them. Both make living memory of the first house important to the response to rebuilding.

Haggai 2:4
But now be strong, Zerubbabel, says the Lord; be strong, Joshua, son of Jehozadak, the high priest; let all the people of the land be strong, says the Lord, and work, for I am with you, says the Lord Almighty,
  Joshua 1:9 — strength and courage grounded in the Lord's presence: Haggai commands leaders and people to be strong and work because the Lord is with them; Joshua is commanded to be strong and courageous because the Lord is with him. The matching assurance undergirds action in each commission.
  Zechariah 8:9 — strengthened hands for the temple rebuilding work: Haggai tells the people and leaders to be strong and work; Zechariah tells hearers of the prophets at the founding and building of the temple to have strong hands. Both encourage the rebuilding community's labor.

Haggai 2:5
and My Spirit stands in your midst. Take courage.
  Zechariah 4:6 — the Lord's Spirit as assurance in Zerubbabel's building setting: Haggai assures the community that God's Spirit stands in their midst and calls for courage; Zechariah's word to Zerubbabel locates effectiveness in God's Spirit rather than great power or strength. The Spirit's presence or agency supports the work in both.

Haggai 2:6
For thus says the Lord Almighty: Once more I will shake heaven and earth, the sea and the dry land.
  Hebrews 12:26-27 — later citation of the promise to shake heaven and earth once more: Haggai promises once more to shake heaven and earth, sea and dry land; Hebrews quotes the promise to shake earth and heaven once more and reflects on the removal of shaken things. The explicit repeated wording establishes the citation relationship.

Haggai 2:7
And I will shake all the nations, and the choice things of all the nations shall come; and I will fill this house with glory, says the Lord Almighty.
  Isaiah 60:5-7 — nations' valuable offerings and the glorification of God's house: Haggai says the nations' choice things will come and His house will be filled with glory; Isaiah describes nations' wealth, gold and offerings arriving, followed by the glorification of God's house. The relationship follows the native plural choice things rather than substituting a singular personal title.

Haggai 2:8
The silver is Mine and the gold is Mine, says the Lord Almighty.
  1 Chronicles 29:14-16 — all temple resources belong to God: Haggai declares silver and gold His; David acknowledges that everything given for God's house comes from God's own hand and belongs to Him. The surrounding temple donations include gold and silver, making the ownership statement materially specific.

Haggai 2:9
For the latter glory of this house will be greater than the former, says the Lord Almighty; in this place I will give peace, says the Lord Almighty, and peace of soul as a possession for everyone building to raise this sanctuary.”
  Ezekiel 43:4-5 — the Lord's glory filling His house: Haggai promises greater glory for the house; Ezekiel sees the Lord's glory enter the house and fill it. The shared house-and-glory image does not establish that Ezekiel's vision is the exact building phase Haggai compares.
  John 14:27 — divine gift of peace extending to inner life: Haggai promises peace in the place and peace of soul for those building the sanctuary; Jesus gives His peace and tells the disciples not to have troubled or afraid hearts. The thematic parallel concerns inward peace as a divine gift, not identification of the builders with the disciples.

Haggai 2:10
On the twenty-fourth day of the ninth month, in the second year under Darius, the word of the Lord came to Haggai the prophet, saying:
  Haggai 2:18 — same-chapter date connecting the law inquiry and the turning-point exhortation: The oracle is dated to the twenty-fourth of the ninth month; the later instruction repeats that date as the day from which the people should direct their hearts. The repeated date connects the current message with its stated turning point.

Haggai 2:11
“Thus says the Lord Almighty: Ask the priests about the law, saying:
  Leviticus 10:10-11 — priests charged to distinguish holiness and teach the law: Haggai asks the priests for a legal ruling; Leviticus charges the priests to distinguish holy from profane and clean from unclean and teach the Lord's statutes. This explains their role in the following holiness questions.

Haggai 2:12
If someone takes holy meat in the edge of his garment and the edge of his garment touches bread, cooked food, wine, oil or any food, will it become holy?” The priests answered, “No.”
  Leviticus 6:18-20 — distinction between direct holy-flesh contact and indirect contact through a garment: Haggai's priests deny that food becomes holy when touched by the garment edge carrying holy meat; Leviticus calls the sacrifice most holy and says one touching its flesh is sanctified. The comparison carefully distinguishes direct contact with flesh from the mediated contact asked about in Haggai.

Haggai 2:13
Haggai said, “If one defiled through a dead person touches any of these, will it be defiled?” The priests answered, “It will be defiled.”
  Numbers 19:11-13 — corpse contact as a source of uncleanness: Haggai asks about someone defiled through a dead person and the priests affirm resulting defilement on touch; Numbers says touching a dead person makes one unclean and explains the need for purification. This supplies the legal basis of the person's condition.
  Numbers 19:22 — uncleanness transmitted by an unclean person's touch: Haggai's priests affirm that what a corpse-defiled person touches is defiled; Numbers explicitly says whatever the unclean person touches becomes unclean. This is the contact rule applied in Haggai's question.

Haggai 2:14
Haggai answered, “So is this people, and so is this nation before Me, says the Lord; so are all the works of their hands, and whoever approaches there will be defiled because of their morning gains. They will suffer because of their labors, and you hated those who rebuked at the gates.
  Amos 5:10 — hatred of rebuke at the gates: Haggai's expanded native reproof says the people hated those rebuking at the gates; Amos says they hated one reproving at the gates. The unusually particular phrase connects the two rebukes without omitting Haggai's wider defilement application.

Haggai 2:15
And now, please set your hearts on the time before this day, before stone was placed upon stone in the sanctuary of the Lord,
  Haggai 2:18 — same-chapter contrast between reflection before and from the rebuilding turning point: Haggai first asks the people to set their hearts on the time before stone was placed in the sanctuary; the later instruction asks attention from the stated day and the sanctuary's founding onward. The paired directions structure the before-and-after reflection.

Haggai 2:16
on how things were with you: when you put twenty sata of barley into a bin, there were ten sata of barley; when you went to the wine vat to draw fifty metretai, there were twenty.
  Haggai 1:6 — same-book concrete illustration of reduced agricultural return: Haggai's reduced barley and wine measures specify the earlier complaint that much was sown but little brought in. The numerical shortfalls make the earlier agricultural futility concrete without changing the native units of measure.

Haggai 2:17
I struck you with barrenness, damage from wind and hail—all the works of your hands—and you did not turn to Me, says the Lord.
  Amos 4:9 — divine agricultural blows failing to produce return: Haggai says God struck the people's labor with barrenness, wind damage and hail, yet they did not turn to Him; Amos describes burning, jaundice and devoured plantings, also followed by failure to turn. The shared rebuke concerns unheeded discipline, not identical agents of crop damage.

Haggai 2:18
Please direct your hearts from this day onward; from the twenty-fourth day of the ninth month and from the day the sanctuary of the Lord was founded, put it in your hearts:
  Zechariah 8:9 — the temple foundation as a prophetic point of reference: Haggai directs attention from the sanctuary's founding and the named day; Zechariah addresses those hearing prophetic words from the day the Lord's house was founded. Both use the foundation as a reference point for the rebuilding community's response.

Haggai 2:19
whether it will yet be recognized on the threshing floor, and whether the vine, the fig tree, the pomegranate and the olive trees still bear no fruit. From this day I will bless.”
  Zechariah 8:11-13 — blessing replacing the former lack of agricultural fruit: Haggai asks about the still-unfruitful plantings and promises blessing from this day; Zechariah contrasts former treatment of the remnant with vine fruit, earth's produce and blessing. Both place renewed fruitfulness within a reversal from earlier want.

Haggai 2:20
The word of the Lord came a second time to Haggai the prophet on the twenty-fourth day of the month, saying:
  Haggai 2:10 — same-chapter second revelation on the earlier stated day: Haggai calls this a second word on the twenty-fourth day; the earlier heading specifies the twenty-fourth day of month nine in Darius's second year. The headings link the successive law-and-blessing and Zerubbabel messages within the chapter's dated sequence.

Haggai 2:21
“Speak to Zerubbabel, the son of Shealtiel, from the tribe of Judah, saying: I am shaking heaven and earth, the sea and the dry land,
  Haggai 2:6 — same-book repetition of the shaking announcement in the word to Zerubbabel: The renewed word to Zerubbabel repeats the shaking of heaven, earth, sea and dry land announced earlier to the rebuilding community. The repeated fourfold scope joins the final oracle to the earlier promise.

Haggai 2:22
and I will overturn the thrones of kings and destroy the strength of the kings of the nations. I will overturn chariots and riders; horses and their riders will fall, each with a sword against his brother.
  Ezekiel 38:21 — judgment in which a man's sword turns against his brother: Haggai says riders will fall with each one's sword against his brother; Ezekiel announces a man's sword against his brother as part of God's judgment. The precise fraternal combat image supports the parallel without equating the armies.
  Daniel 2:21 — God's sovereignty over the removal of kings: Haggai promises to overturn royal thrones and destroy kings' strength; Daniel says God transfers kings and appoints them. Both place royal power under God's authority to remove it.

Haggai 2:23
On that day, says the Lord Almighty, I will take you, Zerubbabel, the son of Shealtiel, My servant, says the Lord, and make you like a signet, for I have chosen you, says the Lord Almighty.”
  Jeremiah 22:24 — contrasting treatment of a named Judean figure through signet imagery: Haggai promises to make the chosen servant Zerubbabel like a signet; Jeremiah says Jeconiah would be pulled off even if he became a signet on God's right hand. The shared signet image is used for selection in one and removal in the other, without resolving all dynastic implications.
