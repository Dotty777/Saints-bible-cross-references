Genesis 1:1
In the beginning God made heaven and earth.
  John 1:1-3 — Thematic connection: John places the Word at the beginning and says that all things came into being through Him; Genesis opens with God's making heaven and earth.
  Hebrews 11:3 — Thematic connection: Hebrews describes the worlds as formed by God's word, explaining the creation by divine speech narrated in Genesis.
  2 Maccabees 7:28 — Thematic connection: The mother appeals to God's making heaven, earth and humanity as the ground for trust in His creative power.
  Psalms 32:6-9 — Thematic connection: The psalm attributes the heavens and their host to the Lord's word and command, corresponding to creation by divine speech.

Genesis 1:2
Now the earth was unseen and unformed, and darkness was over the deep; and the Spirit of God was moving over the water.
  Wisdom of Solomon 11:17 — Shared imagery: Wisdom describes God's powerful hand creating the world out of unformed matter, corresponding to the unformed earth in Genesis.
  Psalms 103:30 — Thematic connection: The psalm associates God's sending His spirit with the creation of living beings and renewal of the earth; it illuminates the life-giving role of the Spirit.
  Genesis 8:1 — Narrative parallel: God sends a wind over the flooded earth and the waters subside; both scenes place divine action over the waters before habitable land appears, without identifying wind and Spirit.

Genesis 1:3
And God said, “Let there be light.” And there was light.
  2 Corinthians 4:6 — Explicit reuse: Paul recalls God's command for light to shine out of darkness and applies that creative act to illumination in the heart.
  Psalms 32:9 — Thematic connection: The psalm's statement that God spoke and things came to be matches the effective command that produces light.
  Hebrews 11:3 — Thematic connection: The worlds' formation by God's word corresponds to the effective divine speech that brings light into being.

Genesis 1:4
And God saw that the light was good, and God divided the light from the darkness.
  Isaiah 45:7 — Shared imagery: Isaiah pairs God's forming light with making darkness, matching the created distinction between light and darkness.
  Job 38:19-20 — Shared imagery: God questions Job about the dwelling places and boundaries of light and darkness, corresponding to their ordered distinction in Genesis.
  John 1:4-5 — Thematic connection: John uses the contrast of light and darkness for the life-giving light associated with the Word; this is a theological analogy, not an account of the first day.

Genesis 1:5

  Genesis 1:13 — Literary parallel: The third-day conclusion repeats the evening-and-morning structure introduced at the close of the first day.
