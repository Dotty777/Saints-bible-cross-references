Genesis 1:1
In the beginning God made heaven and earth.
  John 1:1-3 — Thematic connection: John places the Word at the beginning and says that all things came into being through Him; Genesis opens with God's making heaven and earth.
  Hebrews 11:3 — Thematic connection: Hebrews describes the worlds as formed by God's word, explaining the creation by divine speech narrated in Genesis.
  2 Maccabees 7:28 — Thematic connection: The mother appeals to God's making heaven, earth and humanity as the ground for trust in His creative power.
  Psalms 32:6-9 — Thematic connection: The psalm attributes the heavens and their host to the Lord's word and command, corresponding to creation by divine speech.

Genesis 1:2
Now the earth was unseen and unformed, and darkness was over the deep; and the Spirit of God was moving over the water.
  Wisdom of Solomon 11:17 — Shared imagery: Wisdom describes God's powerful hand creating the world out of unformed matter, corresponding to the unformed earth in Genesis.
  Psalms 103:30 — Thematic connection: The psalm associates God's sending His spirit with the creation of living beings and renewal of the earth; it illuminates the life-giving role of the Spirit.
  Genesis 8:1 — Narrative parallel: God sends a wind over the flooded earth and the waters subside; both scenes place divine action over the waters before habitable land appears, without identifying wind and Spirit.

Genesis 1:3
And God said, “Let there be light.” And there was light.
  2 Corinthians 4:6 — Explicit reuse: Paul recalls God's command for light to shine out of darkness and applies that creative act to illumination in the heart.
  Psalms 32:9 — Thematic connection: The psalm's statement that God spoke and things came to be matches the effective command that produces light.
  Hebrews 11:3 — Thematic connection: The worlds' formation by God's word corresponds to the effective divine speech that brings light into being.

Genesis 1:4
And God saw that the light was good, and God divided the light from the darkness.
  Isaiah 45:7 — Shared imagery: Isaiah pairs God's forming light with making darkness, matching the created distinction between light and darkness.
  Job 38:19-20 — Shared imagery: God questions Job about the dwelling places and boundaries of light and darkness, corresponding to their ordered distinction in Genesis.
  John 1:4-5 — Thematic connection: John uses the contrast of light and darkness for the life-giving light associated with the Word; this is a theological analogy, not an account of the first day.

Genesis 1:5
And God called the light Day, and the darkness He called Night. And there was evening, and there was morning: one day.
  Psalms 73:16-17 — Thematic connection: The psalm assigns day and night to God and speaks of His establishing their lights and the earth's seasons.
  Genesis 8:22 — Thematic connection: The promise that day and night will continue preserves the ordered cycle first named in Genesis 1.
  Genesis 1:13 — Literary parallel: The third-day conclusion repeats the evening-and-morning structure introduced at the close of the first day.

Genesis 1:6
And God said, “Let there be a firmament in the midst of the water, and let it divide water from water.” And it was so.
  Psalms 103:2-3 — Shared imagery: The psalm pictures God spreading heaven and placing His upper chambers in the waters, corresponding to the heaven-and-water ordering in Genesis.
  Psalms 148:4 — Shared imagery: The call to waters above the heavens corresponds to the upper waters distinguished by the firmament.
  Genesis 1:7 — Narrative continuation: The next verse records the making of the firmament and the separation commanded in this verse.

Genesis 1:7
And God made the firmament, and God divided the water beneath the firmament from the water above the firmament.
  Psalms 148:4 — Shared imagery: The psalm explicitly names waters above the heavens, corresponding to the upper waters separated in Genesis.
  Psalms 103:3 — Shared imagery: God's upper chambers in the waters provide a poetic counterpart to Genesis's arrangement of heaven and the upper waters.
  Proverbs 8:27-28 — Shared imagery: Wisdom describes God's preparing heaven and establishing the clouds and deep, paralleling the ordering of heaven and waters.

Genesis 1:8
And God called the firmament Heaven. And God saw that it was good. And there was evening, and there was morning: a second day.
  Psalms 18:2 — Thematic connection: The psalm presents the heavens and firmament as declaring God's work, corresponding to the firmament named Heaven in Genesis.
  Genesis 2:1 — Narrative continuation: The later summary names heaven and earth and their ordered host as completed, including the heaven named here.
  Exodus 20:11 — Explicit reuse: The Sabbath command summarizes God's making heaven, earth and sea in six days, including the work of the second day.

Genesis 1:9
And God said, “Let the water beneath heaven be gathered into one place, and let the dry land appear.” And it was so. And the water beneath heaven was gathered into its gathering places, and the dry land appeared.
  Job 38:8-11 — Shared imagery: God describes bounding the sea and limiting its advance, paralleling the gathering of waters so that dry land appears.
  Psalms 32:7 — Shared imagery: The psalm speaks of God's gathering the sea's waters and storing the depths, corresponding to the gathering command in Genesis.
  Psalms 103:6-9 — Shared imagery: The psalm describes waters retreating from the mountains and receiving a boundary so that they do not cover the earth.

Genesis 1:10
And God called the dry land Earth, and the gathered waters He called Seas. And God saw that it was good.
  Psalms 94:5 — Thematic connection: The psalm assigns the sea and dry land to God's making, matching the two domains named Earth and Seas.
  Exodus 20:11 — Explicit reuse: The Sabbath command names earth and sea among the domains God made, corresponding to the named domains in Genesis.
  Psalms 103:24-25 — Thematic connection: The psalm praises God's abundant works on earth and in the great sea, the two created domains identified here.

Genesis 1:11
And God said, “Let the earth bring forth vegetation sowing seed according to its kind and likeness, and fruit-bearing trees upon the earth, each yielding fruit with its seed within it, according to its kind.” And it was so.
  Genesis 1:29 — Narrative continuation: God later assigns seed-bearing plants and fruit-bearing trees as human food, reusing the plant categories introduced here.
  Psalms 103:14 — Thematic connection: The psalm attributes growing grass and cultivated plants to God's provision, corresponding to the earth's producing vegetation.
  Mark 4:28 — Thematic connection: In Jesus' parable the earth produces successive stages of grain growth, drawing on the productive earth and seed-bearing vegetation; it is an analogy rather than a creation quotation.

Genesis 1:12
And the earth brought forth vegetation sowing seed according to its kind and likeness, and fruit-bearing trees upon the earth, each yielding fruit with its seed within it, according to its kind. And God saw that it was good.
  Genesis 1:11 — Narrative fulfilment: The earth's producing vegetation and fruit-bearing trees fulfils the command immediately preceding it.
  Genesis 2:9 — Thematic connection: The garden account describes God causing pleasing, food-bearing trees to grow from the earth, corresponding to the vegetation produced here.
  Psalms 103:14 — Thematic connection: God's making plants grow for human use corresponds to the successful production of vegetation in Genesis.

Genesis 1:13
And there was evening, and there was morning: a third day.
  Genesis 1:5 — Literary parallel: The third-day conclusion repeats the evening-and-morning pattern established for the first day.
  Genesis 1:19 — Literary parallel: The fourth-day conclusion continues the same evening-and-morning structure used for the third day.
  Exodus 20:11 — Explicit reuse: The six-day creation summary in the Sabbath command includes this third day within the sequence of God's work.

Genesis 1:14
And God said, “Let there be lights in the firmament of heaven to give light upon the earth and to divide the day from the night. Let them serve for signs and seasons, for days and years.
  Psalms 103:19 — Thematic connection: The psalm connects the moon with appointed times and the sun with its setting, matching the lights' time-marking function.
  Psalms 135:7-9 — Thematic connection: The psalm names the great lights and their rule over day and night, corresponding to the lights' appointed ordering function.
  Sirach 43:6-7 — Thematic connection: Sirach describes the moon's role in seasons and festival times, corresponding to the luminaries' service for signs and seasons.

Genesis 1:15
And let them be lights in the firmament of heaven, to shine upon the earth.” And it was so.
  Psalms 135:7-9 — Thematic connection: God's making the great lights, sun, moon and stars corresponds to the appointed lights in the firmament.
  Psalms 148:3 — Thematic connection: The summons to sun, moon and stars of light identifies the heavenly lights commanded to shine in Genesis.
  Genesis 1:17 — Narrative fulfilment: The later verse records God's placing the lights in heaven to shine on earth, fulfilling this command.

Genesis 1:16
And God made the two great lights: the greater light to rule the day, and the lesser light to rule the night; He made the stars also.
  Psalms 135:8-9 — Explicit reuse: The psalm repeats the sun's rule over the day and the moon and stars' rule over the night.
  Psalms 148:3 — Thematic connection: Sun, moon and stars are summoned to praise God, matching the three groups of heavenly lights made here.
  Sirach 43:1-8 — Thematic connection: Sirach praises the sun and moon and their ordered functions, expanding on the heavenly lights described in Genesis.

Genesis 1:17
And God set them in the firmament of heaven to shine upon the earth,
  Psalms 8:4 — Thematic connection: The psalm describes the heavens, moon and stars as God's established works, corresponding to His placing the lights in heaven.
  Psalms 148:3 — Thematic connection: The sun, moon and shining stars addressed by the psalm are the heavenly lights set in place in Genesis.
  Genesis 1:15 — Narrative fulfilment: The placing of the lights so that they shine on earth carries out the command given two verses earlier.

Genesis 1:18
to rule the day and the night, and to divide the light from the darkness. And God saw that it was good.
  Psalms 73:16 — Thematic connection: The psalm assigns both day and night and their lights to God, matching His ordering of those domains by the luminaries.
  Psalms 135:8-9 — Explicit reuse: The psalm repeats the distinct rule of the sun by day and the moon and stars by night.
  Genesis 1:4 — Literary parallel: The division of light from darkness recalls the first-day separation, now connected with the lights' appointed rule.

Genesis 1:19
And there was evening, and there was morning: a fourth day.
  Genesis 1:5 — Literary parallel: The fourth-day refrain repeats the evening-and-morning pattern first used for day one.
  Genesis 1:13 — Literary parallel: The fourth-day refrain follows the same literary pattern as the preceding third-day conclusion.
  Genesis 1:23 — Literary parallel: The next day's conclusion continues the evening-and-morning structure and advances the day count.

Genesis 1:20
And God said, “Let the waters bring forth living creatures that move, and birds that fly above the earth across the firmament of heaven.” And it was so.
  Psalms 103:24-26 — Thematic connection: The psalm praises God's teeming sea creatures and the sea's great creature, corresponding to the waters' living inhabitants.
  Psalms 148:7-10 — Thematic connection: The psalm includes sea creatures and winged birds among the created beings called to praise God.
  Genesis 2:19 — Thematic connection: The garden account names birds among the creatures God forms and brings to the human, corresponding to the birds introduced here.

Genesis 1:21
And God made the great sea creatures, and every living creature that moves, which the waters brought forth according to their kinds, and every winged bird according to its kind. And God saw that they were good.
  Psalms 103:25-26 — Thematic connection: The psalm describes the sea's many living creatures and the great sea creature God formed, corresponding to Genesis's sea life.
  Psalms 148:7 — Thematic connection: The call to great sea creatures and the depths corresponds to the great sea creatures made in Genesis.
  Genesis 6:20 — Narrative parallel: The ark instructions preserve birds and animals according to their kinds, continuing the creation categories used for living creatures.

Genesis 1:22
And God blessed them, saying, “Grow and multiply, and fill the waters in the seas; and let the birds multiply upon the earth.”
  Genesis 8:17 — Explicit reuse: At the release of the ark's creatures, God renews the growth and multiplication language on earth; the command in this reading is addressed to Noah.
  Genesis 9:1 — Literary parallel: God's blessing on Noah and his sons repeats the command to grow and multiply, now addressed to humanity.
  Genesis 1:28 — Literary parallel: The human blessing repeats the growth and multiplication language first addressed to sea creatures and birds.

Genesis 1:23
And there was evening, and there was morning: a fifth day.
  Genesis 1:5 — Literary parallel: The fifth-day conclusion repeats the evening-and-morning structure introduced on day one.
  Genesis 1:19 — Literary parallel: The fifth-day refrain follows the fourth-day refrain with the same structure and an advancing count.
  Genesis 1:31 — Literary parallel: The sixth-day conclusion continues the same evening-and-morning pattern used here.

Genesis 1:24
And God said, “Let the earth bring forth living creatures according to their kind: four-footed animals, creeping things, and wild beasts of the earth according to their kind.” And it was so.
  Genesis 2:19 — Thematic connection: The garden account describes God forming the land's animals, corresponding to the land creatures called forth in Genesis 1.
  Genesis 6:20 — Narrative parallel: The ark instructions preserve animals according to their kinds, carrying forward the categories used in the creation command.
  Psalms 148:10 — Thematic connection: The psalm names wild animals, cattle and creeping things, corresponding to the land-animal groups listed in Genesis.

Genesis 1:25
And God made the wild beasts of the earth according to their kind, and the cattle according to their kind, and all the creeping things of the earth according to their kinds. And God saw that they were good.
  Genesis 7:14 — Narrative parallel: The creatures entering the ark are listed by kind in groups corresponding to the land animals made in Genesis 1.
  Psalms 148:10 — Thematic connection: The psalm's wild animals, cattle and creeping things match the groups made in this verse.
  Genesis 1:24 — Narrative fulfilment: The making of land creatures according to kind fulfils the command of the preceding verse.

Genesis 1:26
And God said, “Let Us make man in Our image and likeness; and let them rule over the fish of the sea, the birds of heaven, the cattle, all the earth, and every creeping thing that creeps upon the earth.”
  Psalms 8:5-9 — Thematic connection: The psalm joins humanity's dignity with rule over animals, birds and fish, corresponding to the dominion entrusted in Genesis.
  Wisdom of Solomon 9:1-3 — Thematic connection: Wisdom recalls humanity's appointment to rule God's creatures and administer the world in holiness and justice.
  Sirach 17:1-4 — Explicit reuse: Sirach connects humanity's creation, God's image and authority over living creatures, bringing together the principal elements of this verse.

Genesis 1:27
And God made man; in the image of God He made him; male and female He made them.
  Genesis 5:1-2 — Explicit reuse: The genealogy's introduction restates creation in God's image and the making and blessing of male and female.
  Matthew 19:4 — Direct quotation: Jesus cites the Creator's making humanity male and female as part of His teaching on marriage.
  James 3:9 — Explicit reuse: James grounds respect for human beings in their being made according to God's likeness, recalling the image language of Genesis.

Genesis 1:28
And God blessed them, saying, “Grow and multiply, and fill the earth and subdue it. Rule over the fish of the sea, the birds of heaven, all the cattle, all the earth, and every creeping thing that creeps upon the earth.”
  Genesis 9:1-2 — Explicit reuse: God renews the multiplication blessing after the flood and describes humanity's relation to the earth's animals, birds and fish.
  Psalms 8:7-9 — Thematic connection: The psalm describes creatures being placed under human authority, corresponding to the dominion given here.
  Wisdom of Solomon 9:2 — Thematic connection: Wisdom identifies humanity's vocation to rule God's creatures, corresponding to the task assigned in Genesis.

Genesis 1:29
And God said, “Behold, I have given you every plant sowing seed upon all the earth, and every tree that bears fruit containing seed. These shall be yours for food.
  Genesis 2:16 — Thematic connection: God's permission to eat from the garden's trees corresponds to the gift of seed-bearing plants and fruit trees as human food.
  Genesis 9:3 — Contrast: The later permission to eat living creatures explicitly compares that gift with the earlier gift of green plants, marking an expansion of the food allowance.
  Psalms 103:14-15 — Thematic connection: The psalm describes vegetation, bread and other produce as God's provision for human nourishment.

Genesis 1:30
And to every wild beast of the earth, every bird of heaven, and every creeping thing upon the earth that has life within it, I have given every green plant for food.” And it was so.
  Psalms 103:14 — Thematic connection: God's growing grass for cattle corresponds to the provision of green plants as animal food.
  Psalms 103:27-28 — Thematic connection: The psalm describes living creatures depending on God for food, corresponding to His assigning their food in Genesis.
  Genesis 9:3 — Contrast: The later gift of living creatures as food to humanity differs from the plant provision assigned to living beings in Genesis 1.

Genesis 1:31
And God saw all that He had made, and behold, it was very good. And there was evening, and there was morning: a sixth day.
  Psalms 103:24 — Thematic connection: The psalm praises the wisdom and abundance of all God's works, corresponding to the completed creation's goodness.
  Sirach 39:16 — Thematic connection: Sirach declares all the Lord's works very good, echoing the approval of all creation in Genesis.
  Genesis 2:1-3 — Narrative continuation: The next passage summarizes the completion of heaven and earth and God's rest after the sixth day's finished work.
