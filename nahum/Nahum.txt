Nahum 1:1
An oracle concerning Nineveh; the book of the vision of Nahum the Elkoshite.
  Jonah 1:1-2 — prophetic messages directed to Nineveh: Nahum identifies his book as an oracle concerning Nineveh. Jonah receives the Lord's command to go to that named city and proclaim because its wickedness has come before Him. Both explicitly identify Nineveh as the destination of prophetic address.

Nahum 1:2
The Lord is a jealous and avenging God, the Lord avenging with fury, the Lord avenging Himself on His adversaries, and He Himself removes His enemies.
  Exodus 34:14 — the Lord explicitly described as jealous: Nahum calls the Lord a jealous and avenging God. Exodus likewise names Him Jealous and calls Him a jealous God when forbidding worship of another god. The shared description is explicit, while Nahum emphasizes vengeance upon adversaries.
  Deuteronomy 32:35-36 — vengeance and judgment belonging to the Lord: Nahum presents the Lord avenging Himself upon adversaries and removing enemies. Deuteronomy likewise places repayment in His day of vengeance and announces His judgment, while also speaking of concern for His weakened servants.

Nahum 1:3
The Lord is patient, and His strength is great; the Lord certainly will not acquit. His way is in consummation and shaking; clouds are the dust of His feet.
  Exodus 34:6-7 — patience joined to the refusal to acquit guilt: Nahum places the Lord's patience beside the declaration that He certainly will not acquit. Exodus likewise describes Him as slow to anger and merciful while saying that He does not cleanse the guilty. Neither passage makes patience an absence of judgment.

Nahum 1:4
He threatens the sea and dries it up, and makes all the rivers desolate. Bashan and Carmel have dwindled, and Lebanon’s flowering things have failed.
  Isaiah 50:2 — the Lord's threat dries seas and rivers: Nahum says the Lord threatens the sea and dries it, making rivers desolate. Isaiah likewise describes His threat making the sea desolate and turning rivers to deserts. Both expressly connect desiccated waters with His power.
  Exodus 14:21-22 — the Lord makes the sea dry: Nahum describes the Lord drying the sea. Exodus narrates a particular occasion when He drives the sea back and makes it dry so that Israel can pass through. The historical scene supplies an explicit example of power over the sea, without identifying it as Nahum's only referent.

Nahum 1:5
Mountains shook before Him and hills were shaken; the whole earth recoiled before Him, and all who dwell in it.
  Psalms 113:4-7 — mountains and earth shaken before the Lord: Nahum describes mountains and hills shaken and the earth recoiling before the Lord. The psalm likewise depicts mountains and hills moving and expressly says the earth was shaken before Him. The images concern creation's response to His presence.

Nahum 1:6
Who will stand before His anger? Who will resist His furious wrath? His fury melts rulers, and rocks were crushed before Him.
  Malachi 3:2-3 — the question of standing before divine appearance and melting imagery: Nahum asks who can stand before the Lord's anger and says His fury melts rulers. Malachi likewise asks who will stand at the coming appearance and describes smelting and melting the sons of Levi. The shared question and melting imagery have different subjects and purposes in their respective contexts.

Nahum 1:7
The Lord is good to those who wait for Him in a day of affliction, and He knows those who revere Him.
  Lamentations 3:25-26 — the Lord's goodness to those who wait for Him: Nahum says the Lord is good to those waiting for Him in affliction. Lamentations likewise explicitly calls Him good to those who wait for Him and describes enduring quietly for His salvation. The connection preserves Nahum's waiting language rather than importing a stronghold image.

Nahum 1:8
With an advancing flood He will make a complete end of those who rise up, and darkness will pursue His enemies.
  Isaiah 8:7-8 — an overwhelming advance represented by powerful waters: Nahum pictures an advancing flood making an end of opponents. Isaiah likewise pictures strong river water advancing over walls and filling the country, explicitly identifying its force with the Assyrian king. The image is shared, while Assyria's role and the people threatened differ.

Nahum 1:9
What do you devise against the Lord? He will make a complete end; He will not exact vengeance twice upon the same thing in affliction.
  Isaiah 8:10 — counsel opposed to God cannot endure: Nahum asks what is devised against the Lord before announcing His complete end. Isaiah likewise says that the Lord will scatter devised counsel and that the spoken word will not endure. The connection concerns failed opposition, not a claim that Isaiah repeats Nahum's statement about vengeance twice.

Nahum 1:10

  Isaiah 10:16-18 — destructive burning pictured through vegetation: Nahum compares consumption with an entwining thorn-vine and dry stubble. Isaiah describes burning against glory and woodland consumed like grass, extending to mountains, hills and forests. Both use combustible vegetation to picture devastating judgment, with different plants named.

Nahum 1:11

  Nahum 1:9 — same-chapter recurrence of schemes against the Lord: Nahum 1:11 identifies a scheme devising evil in opposition to the Lord. The earlier question in 1:9 asks what is devised against Him and announces His response. The repeated devising language develops the same accusation within the chapter.

Nahum 1:12

  Psalms 28:3 — the Lord's authority over many waters: Nahum introduces the Lord as the One who rules many waters. The psalm likewise places the Lord over many waters and His voice over the waters. The shared description identifies the authority of the speaker, without making the subsequent threats identical.

Nahum 1:13

Isaiah tells the people not to fear the Assyrian's rod and promises the removal and destruction of his yoke. Both describe liberation through the removal of instruments of oppression.

Nahum 1:14

Micah likewise announces the Lord's destruction of carved images and pillars and the end of bowing to human handiwork. The shared action concerns images, while the addressed settings differ.

Nahum 2:1

  Isaiah 52:7 — shared announcement of good news and peace upon the mountains: Nahum calls attention to the feet on the mountains of one bringing good news and announcing peace. Isaiah likewise combines mountains, feet, good news and a report of peace, alongside salvation and God's reign. The distinctive cluster of images directly connects the announcements.
  Romans 10:15 — later scriptural use of the good-news messenger's feet: Nahum depicts the feet of one bringing good news and peace. Romans introduces written Scripture about the beautiful feet of those announcing good things within its discussion of sent proclaimers. The shared messenger image is retained without claiming Romans names Nahum as its source.

Nahum 2:11

Nahum 2:14
