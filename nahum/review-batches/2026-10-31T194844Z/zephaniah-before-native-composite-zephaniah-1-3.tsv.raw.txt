Zephaniah 1:1	2 Kings 21:24-26	named royal setting	Zephaniah dates the word to Josiah son of Amon; Kings recounts Amon's death and the people's installation of Josiah his son, establishing the named royal succession.
Zephaniah 1:2	Genesis 6:7	divine announcement of sweeping earthly destruction	Zephaniah announces the disappearance of everything from the earth; Genesis announces the removal of humanity together with animals and birds. The shared scope is a judgment pattern, without identifying the two events.
Zephaniah 1:3	Hosea 4:3	judgment affecting land creatures, birds and fish	Zephaniah includes humanity, livestock, birds and fish in the announced disappearance; Hosea describes the land mourning with beasts, birds and even sea fish failing. Both extend judgment beyond human society.
Zephaniah 1:4	2 Kings 23:4-5	removal of Baal worship and its priesthood	Zephaniah announces removal of Baal's names and the priests' names from Judah and Jerusalem; Kings describes Josiah removing Baal's objects and abolishing the priests of illicit worship. The correspondence concerns the named cult and priesthood.
Zephaniah 1:5	Jeremiah 19:13	rooftop worship of the heavenly host	Zephaniah identifies those worshiping heaven's army on rooftops; Jeremiah identifies roofs where offerings were made to heaven's army. The link concerns rooftop worship, not an identification of Zephaniah's separate oath by their king.
Zephaniah 1:6	Hosea 7:10	failure to return to or seek the Lord	Zephaniah condemns turning away and failing to seek or hold fast to the Lord; Hosea says the people did not return to the Lord or seek Him while announcing the humbling of their arrogance.
Zephaniah 1:7	Isaiah 34:6-8	divine judgment portrayed as the Lord's sacrifice	Zephaniah announces the near day and a sacrifice prepared by the Lord; Isaiah presents the Lord's sacrifice in Bosor in the setting of His judgment day. The sacrificial imagery belongs to judgment in both.
Zephaniah 1:8	Jeremiah 22:1-5	judgment addressing the royal household	Zephaniah directs judgment against rulers and the king's house; Jeremiah addresses Judah's king and household and warns that the house will become desolate if justice is refused. The link does not interpret the foreign clothes in Zephaniah.
Zephaniah 1:9	Jeremiah 7:9-11	impiety associated with the Lord's house	Zephaniah condemns those filling the Lord their God's house with impiety and deceit; Jeremiah condemns theft and falsehood followed by standing in God's house, which they treat as a robbers' cave.
Zephaniah 1:10	Amos 5:16-17	public cries and mourning during divine judgment	Zephaniah locates cries, wailing and crashing in the city's quarters and hills; Amos announces lamentation in public ways and streets when the Lord passes through. Both make judgment audible in communal spaces.
Zephaniah 1:11	James 5:1-3	lament over wealth that cannot preserve its owners	Zephaniah calls for lament because those exalted by silver are destroyed; James tells the rich to weep over coming miseries and describes gold and silver becoming testimony against them.
Zephaniah 1:12	Psalms 9:32-34	denial of divine concern or accountability challenged	Zephaniah exposes hearts saying the Lord will do neither good nor harm before announcing His searching judgment; the psalm challenges the wicked person's belief that God forgets and will not call to account and calls on God to remember the needy.
Zephaniah 1:13	Deuteronomy 28:30	covenant curse of building without inhabiting and planting without enjoyment	Zephaniah says houses will be built without being inhabited and vineyards planted without their wine being drunk; Deuteronomy's curse likewise denies enjoyment of a built house and planted vineyard.
Zephaniah 1:13	Amos 5:11	prophetic repetition of uninhabited houses and undrunk vineyard wine	Zephaniah's denial of dwelling in newly built houses and drinking planted vineyards' wine closely matches Amos's judgment against oppressors who built houses and planted desirable vineyards.
Zephaniah 1:14	Joel 1:15	nearness of the Lord's day	Zephaniah declares the Lord's great day near and very swift; Joel laments that the Lord's day is near and comes with affliction. The correspondence concerns imminence and distress.
Zephaniah 1:15	Joel 2:2	the Lord's day described by darkness, gloom, cloud and mist	Zephaniah calls the day one of darkness and gloom, cloud and mist; Joel uses the same cluster of dark-weather descriptions for the approaching day.
Zephaniah 1:16	Joel 2:1	trumpet alarm associated with the near day	Zephaniah describes a day of trumpet and shouting against fortifications; Joel orders a trumpet in Zion and a proclamation on the holy mountain because the Lord's day approaches. Both connect alarm with the day, without equating their city details.
Zephaniah 1:17	Deuteronomy 28:28-29	punitive blindness and helpless movement	Zephaniah says sinners will walk like the blind; Deuteronomy's curse includes blindness and groping as a blind person does. Both portray impaired movement as judgment.
Zephaniah 1:18	Ezekiel 7:19	silver and gold rendered useless in judgment	Zephaniah says silver and gold cannot rescue on the wrath day; Ezekiel describes silver thrown into streets and gold disregarded, unable to satisfy life or fill bellies. Both expose precious metals' failure under judgment, though Ezekiel's stated failure concerns sustenance.
Zephaniah 1:18	Zephaniah 3:8	same-book repetition of universal judgment by jealous fire	The opening oracle says the earth will be consumed in the fire of God's jealousy; the later oracle repeats that all the earth will be consumed by His jealous fire when nations are assembled for wrath.
Zephaniah 2:1	Joel 2:15-17	summoning the people to gather before the Lord	Zephaniah orders the undisciplined nation to gather and bind together before wrath arrives; Joel calls for a gathered, consecrated congregation and intercession that the Lord spare His people.
Zephaniah 2:2	Isaiah 40:6-8	the passing flower as an image of transience	Zephaniah urges action before becoming like a passing flower and before wrath; Isaiah likens human glory to grass's flower that falls while God's word remains. The shared flower image emphasizes the fragility underlying Zephaniah's urgency.
Zephaniah 2:3	Amos 5:14-15	seeking good and practicing judgment in hope of mercy	Zephaniah calls the humble to seek the Lord and righteousness and practice judgment in hope of shelter; Amos urges seeking good and restoring judgment in hope of the Lord's mercy upon the remnant.
Zephaniah 2:4	Amos 1:6-8	parallel judgment against the named coastal cities	Zephaniah names Gaza, Ashkelon, Ashdod and Ekron in judgment; Amos's oracle names Gaza, Ascalon, Azotus and Accaron. The overlapping set of coastal cities is preserved with each passage's different native spellings and punishments.
Zephaniah 2:5	Ezekiel 25:15-17	judgment against Cretans and coastal inhabitants	Zephaniah addresses the coastal Cretans and announces destruction of their dwelling; Ezekiel announces the destruction of Cretans and the coast's remaining inhabitants. Both locate the threatened population by the coast.
Zephaniah 2:6	Isaiah 17:1-2	ruined territory becoming pasture for flocks	Zephaniah says Crete will become pasture and a sheep enclosure; Isaiah describes ruined Damascus as an abandoned resting place for flocks. The connection is the reversal from inhabited strength to grazing land, not identity of the places.
Zephaniah 2:7	Amos 9:14	reversal of captivity associated with restored habitation	Zephaniah assigns the coast and houses of Ashkelon to the remnant and says the Lord visits and turns captivity; Amos promises to reverse Israel's captivity so they inhabit rebuilt cities. The shared restoration does not erase Zephaniah's unusual lodging clause.
Zephaniah 2:8	Ezekiel 25:3-11	Moab and Ammon condemned for hostility toward God's people	Zephaniah cites Moab's reproaches and Ammon's blows and boasts against the people's borders; Ezekiel condemns Ammon's rejoicing over Israel's devastation and Moab's disparaging comparison of Israel and Judah with other nations.
Zephaniah 2:9	Genesis 19:24-25	Sodom and Gomorrah as a named destruction comparison	Zephaniah says Moab will become like Sodom and Ammon like Gomorrah; Genesis records the Lord overthrowing those two cities and their surrounding country. This explains the named comparison without assigning every additional Zephaniah clause to Genesis.
Zephaniah 2:9	Isaiah 17:1-2	parallel announcement of Damascus becoming an abandoned ruin	The native Zephaniah unit explicitly includes Damascus becoming abandoned and vanishing; Isaiah announces Damascus's removal from cities into an enduring abandoned ruin. The Damascus clause is retained rather than omitted to match another edition.
Zephaniah 2:10	Isaiah 16:6-7	Moab's arrogance followed by judgment and lament	Zephaniah attributes the preceding punishment to arrogance and reproach against the Lord; Isaiah likewise names Moab's excessive insolence and arrogance before announcing Moab's lamentation.
Zephaniah 2:11	Malachi 1:11	worship of the Lord among nations in their own places	Zephaniah says the nations' islands will worship the Lord, each from its place; Malachi says His name is glorified among the nations and offerings are brought in every place. Both extend worship beyond one local population.
Zephaniah 2:12	Ezekiel 30:3-6	Ethiopia included in the sphere of divine military judgment	Zephaniah addresses Ethiopians as slain by God's sword; Ezekiel places turmoil in Ethiopia alongside the sword against Egypt and falling allies. The parallel is regional judgment involving Ethiopia, while Ezekiel explicitly places its wounded men in Egypt.
Zephaniah 2:13	Nahum 3:7	Nineveh named as the object of devastating judgment	Zephaniah announces Assyria's destruction and Nineveh's waterless desolation; Nahum depicts observers recoiling from miserable Nineveh and finding no consolation for her. Both directly name Nineveh's collapse.
Zephaniah 2:14	Isaiah 34:10-15	deserted built places occupied by wild creatures	Zephaniah describes grazing animals, hedgehogs and ravens among architectural remains; Isaiah describes deserted territory and fortifications becoming dwellings for hedgehogs, ravens and other creatures. The shared ruin scene does not identify the locations or every creature.
Zephaniah 2:15	Isaiah 47:8-11	self-sufficient city boast overturned by sudden ruin	Zephaniah's confident city says 'I am, and there is no one after me' before becoming a wasteland; Isaiah's confident city says 'I am, and there is no other' before unexpected destruction. Both expose the same kind of exclusive boast.
Zephaniah 3:2	Deuteronomy 9:23-24	refusal to hear and trust the Lord	Zephaniah says the city did not heed the voice or trust the Lord; Deuteronomy recalls disobeying God's word, not believing Him and not hearkening to His voice. The corresponding failures are hearing and trust.
Zephaniah 3:3	Ezekiel 22:25-27	predatory lions and wolves used for civic leaders	Zephaniah compares rulers to roaring lions and judges to Arabian wolves; Ezekiel compares leaders to roaring lions and rulers to prey-seizing wolves. Both expose leadership as predatory while retaining Zephaniah's Arabian designation.
Zephaniah 3:4	Ezekiel 22:26-28	priests profaning holy things and prophets failing their office	Zephaniah condemns priests who profane holy things and act impiously toward the law, alongside contemptuous prophets; Ezekiel condemns priests setting aside the law and profaning holy things, and prophets speaking without the Lord's word.
Zephaniah 3:5	Deuteronomy 32:4	God's righteousness excludes injustice	Zephaniah says the righteous Lord does no injustice and brings His judgment to light; Deuteronomy calls His ways judgments and denies unrighteousness in Him. Both ground judgment in God's own righteousness.
Zephaniah 3:6	Leviticus 26:31-33	divine devastation of cities and land	Zephaniah describes roads desolate and cities without inhabitants through God's action; Leviticus warns that God will desolate cities and land and scatter their people among nations.
Zephaniah 3:7	Proverbs 3:11-12	call to accept the Lord's discipline	Zephaniah presents God's appeal to fear Him and accept discipline so destruction may be averted; Proverbs exhorts the son not to despise the Lord's discipline because He disciplines those He loves.
Zephaniah 3:8	Joel 4:2	assembly of nations for divine judgment	Zephaniah announces God's gathering of nations and kings to pour out wrath; Joel says God gathers all nations into the valley to enter judgment with them. Both make the international assembly an act of the judging Lord.
Zephaniah 3:9	Genesis 11:6-9	divine alteration of human speech with contrasting communal outcomes	Zephaniah promises a changed tongue so all call on the Lord and serve under one yoke; Genesis describes God confounding shared language and scattering people. The contrast concerns divine action on speech and communal direction, without claiming an explicit quotation or identifying the precise change of language.
Zephaniah 3:10	Isaiah 18:1-7	offerings to the Lord in a setting beyond Ethiopia's rivers	Zephaniah says sacrifices will be brought from the far reaches of Ethiopia's rivers; Isaiah opens with a land beyond those rivers and concludes with gifts brought to the Lord's named place on Zion. The common geography and offering motif support the link.
Zephaniah 3:11	Isaiah 2:11-12	removal of human arrogance before the Lord	Zephaniah promises to remove arrogant contemptuous behavior so there is no boast on His holy mountain; Isaiah announces a day humbling the insolent and elevated, leaving the Lord alone exalted.
Zephaniah 3:12	Isaiah 14:32	humble people preserved by the Lord in Zion's setting	Zephaniah promises a meek and humble people remaining and revering the Lord's name; Isaiah says the Lord founded Zion and that through Him the humble among the people will be saved.
Zephaniah 3:13	Micah 4:4	rest without anyone causing fear	Zephaniah's remnant will graze and lie down with no one frightening them; Micah promises rest under vine and fig tree with no one causing fear. Both give concrete peaceful rest as a feature of restoration.
Zephaniah 3:14	Zechariah 9:9	daughter Zion and Jerusalem summoned to rejoice	Zephaniah calls daughter Zion and Jerusalem to rejoice and proclaim wholeheartedly; Zechariah similarly addresses daughter Zion and Jerusalem with rejoicing and proclamation as their king comes.
Zephaniah 3:15	Zechariah 2:14	the Lord dwelling within rejoicing Zion	Zephaniah grounds Zion's security in the Lord, Israel's King, being within her; Zechariah tells daughter Zion to rejoice because the Lord is coming to dwell within her.
Zephaniah 3:16	Isaiah 35:3-4	encouragement and strengthened hands in anticipation of salvation	Zephaniah tells Zion to take courage and not let her hands loosen; Isaiah strengthens limp hands and urges courage because God comes to save. Both oppose physical slackness and fear with divine rescue.
Zephaniah 3:17	Isaiah 62:5	the Lord's delight over His restored people	Zephaniah says the Lord will bring gladness, renew in love and rejoice with delight; Isaiah compares the Lord's rejoicing over His people to a bridegroom's joy over a bride. The connection concerns divine delight, without importing quietness or singing into Zephaniah.
Zephaniah 3:18	Micah 4:6-7	gathering the crushed into a restored remnant	Zephaniah promises to gather the crushed; Micah explicitly promises to gather the crushed one and make that one a remnant. The shared gathering addresses people in a condition of affliction.
Zephaniah 3:19	Micah 4:6-7	reception of rejected and afflicted people	Zephaniah promises to save the oppressed and receive the rejected; Micah promises to receive the expelled and rejected and make them a strong nation. Both reverse exclusion through God's reception.
Zephaniah 3:20	Jeremiah 40:7-9	restoration bringing public praise among the earth's peoples	Zephaniah promises returned captivity and renown among all peoples; Jeremiah promises to bring back Judah's and Israel's exiles and make the restoration a cause of praise and greatness before the earth's people.
